 

Kakapo are critically endangered; as of 2023, there were only 248 living individuals known, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Saving the species is a goal for the New Zealand Department of Conservation. 

The kakapo was long thought to be extinct until it was rediscovered in 1977. Since then, the species has been the subject of a massive conservation effort to prevent its extinction. This includes moving the kakapo to predator-free islands, such as Codfish Island and Anchor Island, where they can breed without the threat of predation. The kakapo is also the subject of a captive breeding program, which has successfully increased the population from a low of 51 individuals in 1995 to 248 as of 2023. 

Despite these efforts, the kakapo remains critically endangered, and its future is far from secure. The species is still vulnerable to diseases, genetic problems, and environmental changes, and it is unclear whether it will be able to survive in the long term. However, the conservation efforts of the New Zealand Department of Conservation and other organizations offer hope for the future
