 "Kakapo" is the Māori name for the bird. It is also known as the night parrot, owl parrot, or the New Zealand owl parrot. In Māori, kākāpō means "night parrot", coming from kākā ("parrot") + pō ("night"). The kakapo is the world's only flightless parrot, the heaviest parrot, and is critically endangered. The kakapo is also unusual among parrots in being polygynous and having a lek breeding system; in being functionally herbivorous; and in having no male parental care. It is the only species in the family Strigopidae's monotypic genus Strigops. Adult kakapos are about 58 to 64 centimetres in length, and weigh from 0.95 to 4 kilograms at maturity. The kakapo is a nocturnal and herbivorous bird, feeding mainly on fruits, seeds, leaves, and stems. It is also known to eat insects and small vertebrates.
