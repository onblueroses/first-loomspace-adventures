 The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 249 living individuals known, all of which have been given names. The ancestral kakapo population was almost wiped out by European settlers in New Zealand through hunting and deforestation. The surviving population was further reduced to 51 individuals by 1995 through the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats. Beginning in the 1890s, conservation efforts have included moving the kakapo population to predator-free islands and habitat restoration. In 2018, the kakapo population count was 222.
