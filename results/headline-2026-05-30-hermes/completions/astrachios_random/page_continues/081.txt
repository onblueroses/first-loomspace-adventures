 'The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 247 living individuals known, all of whom are named. Because of Polynesian and European colonisation and the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in the 1980s. As of April 2023, the total known adult population of kakapo is 247 living individuals, all of which have been given names.'

The Kakapo Recovery Programme is a New Zealand conservation initiative that aims to protect and increase the population of the critically endangered kakapo. The programme is a collaboration between the New Zealand Department of Conservation, the Kakapo Recovery Group, and various partners and supporters. The programme involves intensive management of the remaining kakapo population, including breeding programmes, predator control, and habitat protection. The programme has been successful in increasing the kakapo population from a low of 51 individuals in 1995 to 247 individuals in 2023.
