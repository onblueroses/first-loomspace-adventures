 

Kakapo are critically endangered; as of June 2023, the total known population is 249 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Saving the species has been a massive effort, involving the relocation of the entire species to predator-free islands and intensive intervention in hatching and raising chicks. As of April 2023, the current population is maintained on three islands: Anchor Island in Dusky Sound, off the coast of Fiordland National Park (Te Pā Whakamanu o Te Ankerite); Codfish Island/Whenua Hou, to the west of Stewart Island/Rakiura (Te Pā Whakamanu o Whenua Hou); and Chalky Island in Fiordland (Te Pā Whakamanu o Te Kāreti). Plans to establish a mainland "kakapo creche" at Te Anau have been delayed due to the COVID-19 pandemic.
