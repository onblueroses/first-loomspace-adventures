 "The kakapo is critically endangered; as of March 2023, the total known adult population was 249, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonization, the kakapo was almost wiped out. [...] The conservation efforts of the Kakapo Recovery Programme have led to a dramatic increase in the population, from 50 in 1995 to 249 in 2023."

The kakapo's plight was highlighted in the 2019 BBC documentary series "Seven Worlds, One Planet", narrated by David Attenborough. The series featured a remarkable scene of a male kakapo named Sirocco attempting to mate with a cameraman's head, mistaking it for a female kakapo. The scene went viral and brought global attention to the kakapo's conservation efforts.

The Kakapo Recovery Programme, a collaboration between the New Zealand Department of Conservation, the Kakapo Recovery Group, and various partners, has been working tirelessly to save the species. Their efforts include predator control, supplementary feeding, nest protection, and the translocation of individuals to predator-free islands.

In 2016, the programme celebrated a significant milestone when the kakapo population reached 150 individuals.
