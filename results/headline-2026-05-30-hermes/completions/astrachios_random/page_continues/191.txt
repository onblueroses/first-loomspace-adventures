 "The kakapo is critically endangered; as of June 2023, the total known adult population is 249 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Saving the species has been a massive effort of the New Zealand Department of Conservation and volunteers, and is ongoing."

We can imagine a future where the Kakapo is no longer critically endangered, but rather thriving in a restored and protected habitat. This future is possible if we continue to support conservation efforts and prioritize the protection of endangered species and their habitats.

What actions can we take to ensure a thriving future for the Kakapo and other endangered species? Here are a few suggestions:

1. Support conservation organizations: Donate to or volunteer with organizations dedicated to protecting endangered species and their habitats, such as the New Zealand Department of Conservation or the Kakapo Recovery Programme.

2. Advocate for strong environmental policies: Encourage your local and national government representatives to support and implement policies that prioritize the protection of endangered species and their habitats.

3. Educate others: Spread awareness about the importance of conservation and the unique story of the Kakapo to inspire others to take action.
