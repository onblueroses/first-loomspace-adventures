 The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 249 living individuals known, all of which have been given names. The ancestral kakapo population was almost wiped out by European settlers in New Zealand, through a combination of hunting, deforestation, and predation by invasive species. For the last several decades, conservation efforts have been undertaken to preserve the species, which is now confined to four predator-free islands. The kakapo has a well-noted nocturnal lifestyle, and was not sighted between 1937 and 2003, when two individuals were rediscovered on Codfish Island.

The kakapo has been a part of New Zealand's cultural history for centuries, and is a beloved symbol of the country. The bird's unique appearance, nocturnal habits, and critically endangered status have made it a subject of fascination and concern for many.
