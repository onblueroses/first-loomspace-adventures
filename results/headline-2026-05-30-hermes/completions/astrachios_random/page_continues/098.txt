 The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 248 living individuals known, all of whom are named. Because of Polynesian and European colonisation and the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in the 1980s. As of April 2023, the total known population is 248 living individuals, as reported by the Kakapo Recovery Programme.

The kakapo is a large, nocturnal parrot; its plumage is yellowish-green, and it has a conspicuous facial disc of fine feathers resembling the face of an owl. It is the world's only flightless parrot, the heaviest parrot, and also is nocturnal, herbivorous, and lacks male parental care of offspring. It is the only species in the genus Strigops and subfamily Strigopinae. The species is commonly known as the kakapo or night parrot, although it has also been referred to as the owl parrot. Its name in Māori means "night parrot" or "owl parrot".
