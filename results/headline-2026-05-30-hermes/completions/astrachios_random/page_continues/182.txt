The kakapo cannot fly, having short wings for its size and lacking the keel on the sternum (breastbone), which anchors the flight muscles of other birds. It uses its wings for balance and to break its fall when leaping from trees. Unlike many other land birds, the kakapo can accumulate large amounts of body fat."

### The Kakapo population

The kakapo population in New Zealand has undergone a severe decline since human settlement of the country. This decline has been attributed to a number of factors, including habitat loss, predation by introduced mammals, and competition for food resources. As of 2021, the total known adult population of kakapo is 202 living individuals, as reported by the Kakapo Recovery Programme.

In the 1990s, the New Zealand Department of Conservation initiated a recovery plan for the kakapo. This plan involved the translocation of all known surviving kakapo to predator-free islands, where they could be more effectively protected and monitored.
