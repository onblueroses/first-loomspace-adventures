 

Kakapo plumage is green with brown and yellow mottling, which helps camouflage the bird in its natural habitat. The front part of the face is pale and owl-like. The beak is surrounded by delicate feathers, which resemble vibrissae. The tail feathers are relatively short, broad, and emarginate. The kakapo has a large grey beak, short legs, and large feet. The claws are particularly useful for climbing. In the trees, the kakapo uses its wings for balance and support. Unlike other parrots, which usually have four toes on each foot, the kakapo has only two functional toes on each foot, a trait shared with other flightless birds such as ostriches. The scientific name of the kakapo is Strigops habroptilus. Strigops is derived from the Greek words strix, genitive strigos "owl", and ops "face", while habroptilus comes from habros "soft", and ptilon "feather".
