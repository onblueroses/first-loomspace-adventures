 The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 249 living individuals known, all of which have been given names. The ancestral kakapo population was almost wiped out by European settlers in New Zealand, as they cleared and modified the land for farming and building settlements. The last major island refuge of the kakapo, Stewart Island, was also inhabited by feral cats, which killed many of the birds. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in the 1980s. As of April 2023, the total known population is 249 living individuals, all of which have been given names.

The kakapo is a large, rotund parrot; it is nocturnal, herbivorous, and flightless, and is the heaviest parrot in the world. It is also the only flightless parrot in the world, and the only parrot to have a lek breeding system. It is not closely related to other parrots, and its taxonomy is still uncertain.
