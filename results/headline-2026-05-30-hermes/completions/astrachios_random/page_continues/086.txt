 "The kakapo is critically endangered; as of October 2023, the total known adult population is 247 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonization, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in the 1980s. Beginning in 2009, a large number of the known kakapo population were moved to predator-free islands, where they have been successfully breeding."

The kakapo is an iconic species in New Zealand and is well known for its unique characteristics and conservation story. The species has been the subject of extensive conservation efforts, including the Kakapo Recovery Programme, which is a collaboration between the New Zealand Department of Conservation, the Kakapo Recovery Group, and various other organizations and individuals. The programme has been successful in increasing the kakapo population from a low of 51 individuals in 1995 to 247 as of October 2023. The recovery efforts have focused on predator-free island refuges, intensive management of the breeding programme, and research into the species' ecology and biology.
