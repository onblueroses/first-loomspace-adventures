 "The kakapo is the only species in the family Strigopidae's monotypic genus Strigops. Adult kakapos are about 58 to 64 centimetres in length, and weigh from 0.95 to 4 kilograms at maturity. Kakapo are nocturnal and herbivorous, feeding mainly on native plants, especially the seeds of the rimu tree. They have a distinctive musty-sweet odour, which has been likened to a blend of honey and musk. Kakapo were once widespread on the three main islands of New Zealand, but are now confined to offshore islands, where they are protected from predation by introduced mammals such as cats, rats, and stoats. As of April 2023, the total known population is 248 living individuals, all of which have been given names. The common name kakapo means 'owl parrot' in Māori, reflecting the nocturnal habits of the bird. The scientific name Strigops habroptilus translates to 'owl-like' (Strigops) and 'soft feathered' (habroptilus)."
