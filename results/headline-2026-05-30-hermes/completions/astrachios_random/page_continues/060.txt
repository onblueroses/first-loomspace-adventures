 

Kakapos are critically endangered; as of June 2023, the total known adult population is 247, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Saving the species is the subject of large-scale conservation efforts. The New Zealand government and conservationists have worked to build up the kakapo population, through intensive management, on several predator-free islands.

Kakapo were formerly widespread throughout the North, South, and Stewart Islands but are now confined to predator-free islands. Conservation management began in 1894 but was not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995. As of June 2023, kakapo were managed on three islands: Anchor, Pukepuke (New Zealand), and Hauturu-o-Toi (Little Barrier Island); they have been removed from Codfish / Whenua Hou Island, Maud Island, and Chalky Island (Te Kakahu).
