 "The kakapo is critically endangered; as of February 2012, with an additional six born in early 2019, the total known adult population was only 147 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonization, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Saving the species is the aim of the Kakapo Recovery Plan; this includes the New Zealand Department of Conservation, the conservation organisation Forest & Bird, and the scientific community."

The kakapo is a fascinating and unique creature. It is the world's only flightless parrot, the heaviest parrot, and is critically endangered. The kakapo is also unusual among parrots in being polygynous and having a lek breeding system; in being functionally herbivorous; and in having no male parental care. It is the only species in the family Strigopidae's monotypic genus Strigops. Adult kakapos are about 58 to 64 centimetres in length, and weigh from 0.95 to 4 kilograms at maturity.
