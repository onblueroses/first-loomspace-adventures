 "The kakapo (Strigops habroptilus), also known as the owl parrot, is a species of large, flightless, nocturnal, ground-dwelling parrot native to New Zealand. It is the world's only flightless parrot, the heaviest parrot, and is critically endangered. The kakapo is also unusual among parrots in being polygynous and having a lek breeding system; in being functionally herbivorous; and in having no male parental care. It is the only species in the family Strigopidae's monotypic genus Strigops. Adult kakapos are about 58 to 64 centimetres in length, and weigh from 0.95 to 4 kilograms at maturity. The kakapo is a critically endangered species, with a population of only 249 individuals as of 2023. Conservation efforts have been ongoing since the 1890s, with the current Kakapo Recovery Programme having been established in 1995. The programme has been successful in increasing the population from a low of 51 individuals in 1995 to the current level.
