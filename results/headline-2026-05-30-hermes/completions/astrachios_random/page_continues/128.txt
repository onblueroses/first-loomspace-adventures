 "The kakapo (Strigops habroptilus), also known as the owl parrot, is a species of large, flightless, nocturnal, ground-dwelling parrot native to New Zealand. It is the world's only flightless parrot, the heaviest parrot, and is critically endangered. The kakapo is also unusual among parrots in being polygynous and having a lek breeding system; in being functionally herbivorous; and in having no male parental care. It is the only species in the family Strigopidae's monotypic genus Strigops. Adult kakapos are about 58 to 64 centimetres in length, and weigh from 0.95 to 4 kilograms at maturity. The kakapo is a large, rotund parrot; the male measures up to 60 cm (24 in) and weighs from 2 to 4 kg (4.4 to 8.8 lb) at maturity. The kakapo cannot fly, having short wings for its size and lacking the keel on the sternum that anchors the flight muscles of other birds.
