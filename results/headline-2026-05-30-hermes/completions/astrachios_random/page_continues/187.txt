 "The kakapo is critically endangered; as of March 2023, the total known adult population was 249, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in the 1980s. Beginning in 2015, the programme has largely eradicated the predators from the two main islands of the kakapo's native range, Whenua Hou/Fcodland Island and Anchor Island, leading to notable increases in their populations, from 50 in 1995 to 249 in 2023." 

The recovery efforts have been successful, with the kakapo population increasing steadily. The recovery plan includes habitat management, predator control, and supplementary feeding. The kakapo is a unique and fascinating bird, and its recovery is a testament to the dedication of conservationists and the effectiveness of well-designed recovery plans. (Full article...)
