 

The kakapo is critically endangered; as of May 2023, the total known population is 249 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonization, the kakapo was almost wiped out. The conservation of the kakapo has been a point of focus for the New Zealand Department of Conservation.

The kakapo was formerly widespread across the North, South, and Stewart Islands, but is now confined to predator-free islands. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995. As of May 2023, the programme has managed to bring the population up to 249 individuals.

The kakapo is a large, nocturnal, flightless, lek-breeding parrot – a real oddity. It is also critically endangered, and the focus of considerable conservation attention. Before humans arrived it was common throughout New Zealand's forests, but predation by introduced mammals brought it to the brink of extinction - a low point of about 50 birds only in the mid 1990s.
