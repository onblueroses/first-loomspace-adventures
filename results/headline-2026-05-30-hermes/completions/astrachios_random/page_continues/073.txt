 "The kakapo is critically endangered; as of June 2023, the total known adult population is 248 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995."

The recovery efforts are led by the New Zealand Department of Conservation. The conservation status of the kakapo is listed as nationally critical under the New Zealand Threat Classification System. As of 2018, kakapo were kept on three predator-free islands: Codfish / Whenua Hou, Anchor, and Hauturu-o-Toi / Little Barrier islands, where they are closely monitored. Strict guidelines were implemented to ensure the survival of the remaining population. Two large Fiordland islands, Resolution and Secretary, have been the subject of large-scale ecological restoration activities to prepare self-sustaining ecosystems with suitable habitat for the kakapo. In 2012, the New Zealand Government launched the Ten-Year Kakapo Recovery Plan 2012–2022, which includes goals for increasing the population to 150 breeding-age
