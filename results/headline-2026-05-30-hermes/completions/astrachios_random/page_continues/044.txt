 "Kakapo are critically endangered; as of February 2012, there were only 126 living individuals known. Because of Polynesian and European colonisation and the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Plan in the 1980s. As of February 2012, surviving kakapo are kept on three predator-free islands, Codfish (Whenua Hou), Anchor and Little Barrier islands, where they are closely monitored. Two large Fiordland islands, Resolution and Secretary, have been the subject of large-scale ecological restoration activities to prepare self-sustaining ecosystems with suitable habitat for the kakapo."

The kakapo is one of many species that has been impacted by the introduction of invasive species. Conservation efforts have been made to protect the kakapo, and there has been some success in increasing the population. However, the kakapo remains critically endangered, and continued efforts are needed to ensure the survival of this unique species.
