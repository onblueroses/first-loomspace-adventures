 

Before human arrival, the kakapo was distributed throughout the three main islands of New Zealand. Although it can no longer fly, it is an excellent climber, ascending to the crowns of the tallest trees. It is a herbivore, eating a range of seeds, fruits, and pollen of native plants. The kakapo is the world's only flightless parrot, the heaviest parrot, and also the only nocturnal parrot. It is possibly the longest-living bird in the world, with a reported lifespan of up to 100 years. Kakapo have no male parental care, and are the only parrot species in the world to have a polygynous lek breeding system. They are critically endangered, with only 249 living individuals known, all of whom are named. Most kakapo are kept on two predator-free islands in the south of New Zealand, Codfish / Whenua Hou and Anchor Islands, where they are closely monitored.
