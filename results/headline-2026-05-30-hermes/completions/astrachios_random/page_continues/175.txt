 "The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 249 living individuals known, all of which have been given names. The kakapo was originally widespread on all three main islands of New Zealand, but it was brought to the brink of extinction in the 1890s due to massive deforestation, huge numbers of introduced invasive species, and widespread hunting and trapping for food and for skins for Māori feather cloaks. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995. As of April 2023, the total known adult population of the kakapo is 249 living individuals, all of which have been given names, most of which live on two predator-free islands, Codfish / Whenua Hou and Anchor, where they are closely monitored."
