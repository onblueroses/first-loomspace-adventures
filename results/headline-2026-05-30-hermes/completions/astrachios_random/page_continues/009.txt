 

The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 249 living individuals known, all of which have been given names. The ancestral kakapo population was almost wiped out by Polynesian and European settlers due to hunting and the introduction of the domestic cat, European stoat, and the brushtail possum. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995. As of April 2023, the total known population is 249 living individuals, most of which have been given names.

As of April 2023, there are 249 kakapos known to be alive. The most recent death was that of Lisa in April 2023. The most recent chicks to hatch were in 2022; the first chick hatched on 1 March 2022, and the last on 1 May 2022. The oldest known kakapo, named Ruth, died on 9 February 1993; she was thought to be between 100 and 120 years old at the time. |  |
