 "The kakapo is critically endangered; as of February 2012, with an additional six born in early 2016, and the current population comprises 125 known individuals, as of June 2016, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonization, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Saving the kakapo has been a priority for the New Zealand Department of Conservation. A dedicated programme, the Kakapo Recovery Programme, began in 1995, and is still ongoing, to conserve the kakapo." "The conservation of the kakapo has made the species well known. Many books and documentaries detailing the plight of the kakapo have been produced in recent years, one of the earliest being Two in the Bush, made by Gerald Durrell for the BBC in 1962."

The Kakapo Recovery Programme is a scientific conservation initiative that aims to restore the wild kakapo population to the New Zealand mainland. The programme is a collaboration between the New Zealand Department of Conservation, the New Zealand Kakapo Recovery Group, and the Royal Forest and Bird Protection Society of New Zealand.
