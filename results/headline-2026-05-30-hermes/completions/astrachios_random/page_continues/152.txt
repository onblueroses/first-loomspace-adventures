 "The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 249 living individuals known, all of which are named and tagged. The common ancestor of the kakapo and the genus Nestor became isolated from the remaining parrot species when New Zealand broke off from Gondwana, around 82 million years ago. Around 70 million years ago, the kakapo diverged from the genus Nestor. In the absence of mammalian predators, the kakapo lived in relative abundance in both main islands of New Zealand and on Stewart Island. Polynesian settlers came to New Zealand from the Cook Islands in the 14th century, bringing with them Polynesian rats, which reduced the kakapo population in Māori times. European settlers introduced more mammalian predators, and further reduced the kakapo population. From the 1890s, conservation efforts began, but they were unsuccessful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995. As of November 2022, the total known adult population of the kakapo is 249, a considerable increase from the low point of the mid-1970s, when only 18 kakapo were known to exist."
