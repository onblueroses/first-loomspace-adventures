 "The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 249 living individuals known, all of whom are named. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Saving the species is the subject of large-scale conservation efforts. As of April 2023, the total known population is 249 living individuals, as reported by the Kakapo Recovery Programme."

The kakapo is a unique and critically endangered species of parrot native to New Zealand. It is the only flightless parrot in the world, and is also the heaviest. The kakapo is unusual among parrots in several ways, including being polygynous and having a lek breeding system, being functionally herbivorous, and having no male parental care.
