 

It has finely blotched yellow-green plumage, a distinct facial disc, a large grey beak, short legs, large feet, and relatively short wings and tail. The combination of traits makes it well adapted to nocturnal life on the ground. It is a nocturnal herbivore, feeding on a range of seeds, fruits, and pollens. It is not closely related to the other New Zealand parrot, the kea (Nestor notabilis), which is in the family Nestoridae. The kakapo was formerly widespread on the North, South, and Stewart Islands, but is now confined to modified habitats on Codfish (Whenua Hou) and Anchor Islands. Its population has been reduced to 249 as of 2023. The common ancestor of the kakapo and the genus Nestor became isolated from the rest of the psittaciformes when New Zealand broke off from Gondwana, around 82 million years ago.

## History

The kakapo was first described by European settlers in the early 19th century. In 1845, George Grey, the governor of New Zealand, brought a live kakapo to the UK, which was the first time the bird had been seen outside of New Zealand.
