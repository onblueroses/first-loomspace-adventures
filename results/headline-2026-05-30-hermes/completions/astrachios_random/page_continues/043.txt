 "The kakapo is critically endangered; the total known adult population is 247 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995."

The Kakapo Recovery Programme is a collaboration between the New Zealand Department of Conservation, the Kakapo Recovery Group, and the Royal Forest and Bird Protection Society of New Zealand. The programme aims to increase the kakapo population to 150 breeding females through the protection and management of the remaining population, research, and public education.

The kakapo is a unique and fascinating bird, and its conservation is important for the preservation of New Zealand's biodiversity. The Kakapo Recovery Programme is a great example of how collaboration between government, conservation groups, and the public can help to save a species from extinction.
