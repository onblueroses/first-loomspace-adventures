 The kakapo is critically endangered; as of February 2012, there were only 126 living individuals known, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. The conservation efforts of the Kakapo Recovery programme, implemented in 1989, have led to a small increase in the numbers: it has grown from 50 in 1995 to 126 in 2012.

The kakapo has a well-developed sense of smell, which complements its nocturnal lifestyle. It can distinguish between odours while foraging; a behaviour reported for only one other parrot species. One of the most striking characteristics of the kakapo is its sweet and powerful odour, which has been described as musty.

The kakapo is a nocturnal herbivore flightless bird of prey; it has finely blotched yellow-green plumage, a distinct facial disc of sensory, vibrissa-like feathers, a large grey beak, short legs, large feet, and wings and a tail of relatively short length.
