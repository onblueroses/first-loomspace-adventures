 

Kakapo are critically endangered; as of May 2023, the total known adult population is 249 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonization, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Saving the species was a priority for conservationists in New Zealand, and they have been relocated to several predator-free islands. The kakapo recovery plan has been successful, with the kakapo population increasing steadily.

## In the news

On 26 May 2023, the New Zealand Department of Conservation announced that the kakapo population had reached a record high of 252 birds. Conservation efforts have included moving the birds to predator-free islands and intensive management of the population. The kakapo population dropped to a low of 51 individuals in the 1990s, but has been steadily increasing since then. The current population includes 86 chicks that hatched in 2022 and 2023. Conservationists are working to establish the species on at least five predator-free islands, with the long-term goal of returning them to mainland New Zealand.
