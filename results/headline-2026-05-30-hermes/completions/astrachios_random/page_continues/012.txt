 

The kakapo is critically endangered; as of May 2023, its total known population is 249 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Saving the species is the subject of large-scale conservation efforts. The conservation status of the kakapo is closely monitored; as of May 2023, its population comprises 249 individuals, all named and tagged, and spread over several islands.

The kakapo is a nocturnal and flightless bird, with an owl-like face and beak. It is a herbivore, with its diet consisting of fruits, seeds, leaves, and stems. It is also the world's only flightless parrot, and the heaviest parrot. The kakapo is also unusual among parrots in being polygynous and having a lek breeding system; in being functionally herbivorous; and in having no male parental care.
