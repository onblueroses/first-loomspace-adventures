 "The kakapo is critically endangered; as of June 2023, the total known adult population was 249 living individuals, all of which have been given names. The common ancestor of the kakapo and the New Zealand kaka (Nestor meridionalis) split from the remaining parrot species 89 million years ago, and the two species diverged from each other 33 million years ago. The ancestral kakapos were able to fly, but around 16 million years ago they lost this ability and became flightless. The kakapo is now extinct in its natural range and survives only on a few predator-free island reserves, where it is the subject of considerable conservation management. Kakapo recovery efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995. Most kakapos are kept on Codfish Island (Whenua Hou) and Anchor Island, with a small number on Little Barrier Island (Hauturu).
