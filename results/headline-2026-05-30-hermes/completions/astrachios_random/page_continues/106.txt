 

The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 249 living individuals known, all of whom are named. Because of Polynesian and European colonisation and the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995. As of November 2022, the Kakapo Recovery Programme has managed to bring the population up to 249 living individuals.

The kakapo is an unusual parrot in many ways. It is the only flightless parrot in the world, and the only parrot that is active at night. It is also the heaviest parrot, with some males weighing up to 4 kilograms. The kakapo is herbivorous, eating mostly leaves, stems, and seeds. It has a lek breeding system, where males gather in a specific area to attract females. The kakapo is also unusual in that it has no male parental care; the female raises the chicks on her own.
