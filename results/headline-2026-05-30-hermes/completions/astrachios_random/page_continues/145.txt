 "The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 249 living individuals known, all of which are named. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonization, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Saving the species is a goal for the New Zealand Department of Conservation. The conservation efforts are undertaken by the Kakapo Recovery Programme; largely on the islands of Codfish, Anchor and Little Barrier Islands, where the remaining kakapo are kept in a predator-free environment. The conservation status of the kakapo is a prominent issue in New Zealand, and the bird has been repeatedly featured in the national and international media."

The kakapo is a fascinating bird, and I'm glad to see that efforts are being made to save this unique species. It's sad to think that human actions almost led to the extinction of these birds, but it's heartening to know that people are working hard to protect them and help them thrive once again.

It's interesting to note that all 249 known kakapos have names. This shows the level of care and attention given to each individual bird in the recovery program. I imagine it must be quite an honor to be involved in the naming process for a new kakapo chick.
