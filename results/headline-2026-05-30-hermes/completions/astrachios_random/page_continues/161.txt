 The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 249 living individuals known, all of whom are named. The common ancestor of the kakapo and the New Zealand kākā (Nestor meridionalis) diverged from the remaining parrot species 100 million years ago, and the kakapo and kākā subsequently diverged around 30 million years ago. Because of Polynesian and European colonisation and the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in the 1980s. As of November 2022, the total known adult population of the kakapo is 249, a significant increase from the low point of 50 individuals in the 1990s.
