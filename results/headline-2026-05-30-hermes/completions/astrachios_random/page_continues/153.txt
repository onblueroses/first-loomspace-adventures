 

Kakapo are critically endangered; the total known population is only 204 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of Polynesian and European colonisation and the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats, the kakapo was almost wiped out. 

The kakapo is a classic example of evolution on an isolated island, in this case, New Zealand. The arrival of humans, and with them predators, has meant the kakapo's complete lack of adapted defence mechanisms has made it particularly vulnerable, resulting in its status as critically endangered.

The article on the Canada lynx was promoted to featured article on 22 September 2012. The Canada lynx (Lynx canadensis) is a North American mammal of the cat family, Felidae. It is a close relative of the Eurasian lynx (Lynx lynx). Some authorities regard both as conspecific. However, in some characteristics, the Canada lynx is more like the bobcat (Lynx rufus) than the Eurasian lynx. It is one of the four members of the genus Lynx.
