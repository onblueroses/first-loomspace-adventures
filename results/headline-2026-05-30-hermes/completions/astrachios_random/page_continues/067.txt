 "Adult kakapos are about 58 to 64 centimetres in length, and weigh from 0.95 to 4 kilograms at maturity. Kakapo plumage is green with brown and yellow mottling, which helps to camouflage individuals in vegetation. Because the kakapo is flightless, it has short legs and underdeveloped wings. It uses its wings for balance and support when moving quickly or climbing. Unlike many other parrots, kakapos have a noticeable odour, which has been variously described as musty or like the inside of an antique clock. The kakapo's diet is herbivorous, consisting mainly of fruits, seeds, leaves, stems, and roots. Kakapos are nocturnal and will remain in a roosting tree during the day. They are generally solitary, although they can interact with each other using a variety of vocalisations. Kakapos are considered to be a "vulnerable" species, with a population of only about 150 individuals. Conservation efforts are underway to help protect and increase the kakapo population."
