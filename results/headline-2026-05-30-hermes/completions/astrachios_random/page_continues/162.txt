 

Before human colonisation of New Zealand, the kakapo was widely distributed on all three main islands. It lived in a variety of habitats, including tussocklands, scrublands and coastal regions. It exploited the seasonal abundance of food sources, and its diet was herbivorous, consisting of fruits, seeds, leaves, stems and roots. The introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation almost wiped out the kakapo. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995. As of May 2023, the total known population is 249 living individuals, most of which have been given names.

The conservation status of the kakapo is listed as critically endangered on the IUCN Red List. As of May 2023, the total population is 249 living individuals, consisting of 87 mature females, 100 mature males and 62 juveniles. A large breeding season in 2019, followed by another in 2022 and 2023 has increased the population significantly.
