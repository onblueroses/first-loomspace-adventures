 The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 247 living individuals known, all of which have been given names. The ancestral kakapo population was reduced by Māori settlement in the 1880s, and then by European settlement and the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in the 1980s. As of June 2023, the total known adult population of kakapo is 247 living individuals, all of which have been given names, most of them carrying a radio transmitter.

## Kakapo life

Kakapos are nocturnal and herbivorous. They are known to be curious and to interact with humans. They are also known to be long-lived, with some individuals reaching over 100 years of age.

## Kakapo recovery programme

The Kakapo Recovery Programme is a conservation effort led by the New Zealand Department of Conservation. The programme aims to increase the population of kakapos and to ensure the survival of the species. The programme involves monitoring and protecting kakapos, as well as managing their habitat and breeding.
