 "The kakapo is critically endangered; the total known adult population is 209 living individuals, all of which are named and tagged, confined to four small islands off the coast of New Zealand that have been cleared of predators. The introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonization almost wiped out the kakapo."

The Wikipedia page includes a table with the population of kakapo on each of the four islands where they are found. As of 2022, there were 55 kakapo on Anchor Island, 41 on Whenua Hou/Codfish Island, 113 on Hauturu-o-Toi/Little Barrier Island, and no kakapo on Chalky Island.

The page mentions that the kakapo was once widespread across New Zealand, but its population has declined significantly due to factors such as deforestation, predation by introduced mammals, and hunting. The kakapo is now the focus of a conservation management program that aims to increase its population and eventually reintroduce it to predator-free habitats on the New Zealand mainland.
