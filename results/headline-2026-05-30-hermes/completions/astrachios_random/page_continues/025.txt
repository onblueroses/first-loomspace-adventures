 

The kakapo is critically endangered; as of June 2023, the total known population is 249 living individuals, as per the latest tally from the Kakapo Recovery Team, following the death of a 34-year-old male named Arab in September 2022. All of them have been given names by the Kakapo Recovery Programme, a conservation effort overseen by the New Zealand Department of Conservation. Most of the kakapo are kept on two predator-free islands, Codfish / Whenua Hou and Anchor Islands, where they are closely monitored. In 2023, two kakapo were moved to the Auckland Zoo, with plans to eventually move 40 kakapo to multiple zoos across New Zealand and safe sites in the wild. 

The kakapo was originally widespread across New Zealand, but its population has declined massively due to deforestation, predation by introduced predators, and historical human hunting and specimen collection. Introduced mammalian predators, including cats, rats, and stoats, were the primary cause of the near extinction of the kakapo. The conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were unsuccessful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995. 

The programme has been successful in increasing the population of the kakapo
