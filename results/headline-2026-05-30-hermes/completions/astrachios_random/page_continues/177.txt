 "The kakapo is critically endangered; as of June 2023, the total known adult population is 247 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. [...] Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995."

I find it interesting that all of the known living individuals have names. Is there any other species where that is the case? I imagine this makes the kakapo something of a celebrity among conservationists. (Also, kakapo is apparently related to the Maori term for "night parrot".)

The kakapo was recently in the news, as in 2022 the Kākāpō Recovery Programme announced the winning name for one of the 55 chicks hatched in 2019. The winning name, Aroha ("love" in Māori), was announced on Valentine's Day 2022. I'm not sure why it took them so long to name the young parrot, but I imagine they were just being careful in selecting the best possible name!
