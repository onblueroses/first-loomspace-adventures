 

The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 249 living individuals known, all of which have been given names. The kakapo was originally widespread across New Zealand, but its population has declined massively due to factors such as habitat destruction, predation by introduced predators, and historical human hunting. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995. Since then, the known population has increased from 50 individuals to 249 in 2023. 

The kakapo is a significant part of Māori culture, featuring in many traditional stories and beliefs. It was also heavily hunted by Māori for its meat and feathers, which were used to make cloaks and other items. The kakapo is also a popular subject of science and conservation, with many researchers and conservationists working to protect and study the species. The kakapo has been the subject of several documentaries and books, and is a popular tourist attraction in New Zealand. The Kakapo Recovery Programme is a successful conservation effort that has helped to increase the population of the kakapo, and is a model for other conservation efforts around the world.
