 "The kakapo is critically endangered; as of June 2023, the total known adult population was 247 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. By the 1970s, only a few isolated birds were known to exist in Fiordland, a rugged region in the southwest of the South Island. A survey of Stewart Island in 1977 found about 200 more birds, but they were rapidly declining through predation by feral cats."

The kakapo has been the subject of a successful conservation campaign since 1989, with the New Zealand Department of Conservation's Kakapo Recovery Programme. This has included relocating the entire population to predator-free islands, where they are closely monitored and supported with supplementary feeding. The programme has been successful, with the population increasing from a low of 51 individuals in 1995 to 247 as of June 2023. However, the species remains critically endangered, and ongoing efforts are required to ensure its long-term survival.
