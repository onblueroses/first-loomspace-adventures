 "The kakapo is critically endangered; the total known adult population is 248 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1995. Most kakapos now live on three predator-free islands in the New Zealand region, where they are closely monitored." 

The kakapo is a large, nocturnal, flightless parrot. It is also the world's only flightless parrot, and the heaviest parrot, with some individuals weighing up to 4 kilograms. The kakapo is critically endangered, with a total known population of just 248 individuals, all of which have been given names. The species is native to New Zealand, and was almost wiped out due to the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonization.
