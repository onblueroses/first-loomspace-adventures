The kakapo is critically endangered; as of June 2023, the total known adult population was 249 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in the 1980s. As of June 2023, kakapos are kept on three predator-free islands, Whenua Hou/Codfish, Anchor, and Hauturu-o-Toi/Little Barrier islands, where they are closely monitored.
