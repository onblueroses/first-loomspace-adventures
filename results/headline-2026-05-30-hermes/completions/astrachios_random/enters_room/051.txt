 The kakapo, a rare bird native to New Zealand. The kakapo, which is critically endangered, is the world's only flightless parrot. It's also the heaviest parrot known to exist. The kakapo is nocturnal and sleeps during the day. It's also the only parrot that mates using a lek breeding system, meaning that males gather in specific areas to attract females. The kakapo is an herbivore, primarily eating plants and seeds. Unlike most other parrots, male kakapos don't provide any parental care. Adult kakapos can grow up to 64 centimeters long and weigh between 0.95 to 4 kilograms. The kakapo is the only species in its genus, Strigops, which belongs to the family Strigopidae. With fewer than 200 individuals remaining, the kakapo is considered critically endangered. Conservation efforts are ongoing to help protect and increase the population of this unique and fascinating bird.
