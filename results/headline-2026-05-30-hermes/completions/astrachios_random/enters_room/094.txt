 A large, flightless nocturnal parrot. Of course, it's also critically endangered. Kakapos were once widespread throughout New Zealand, but their numbers have dwindled due to habitat loss, predation by introduced mammals, and historic hunting. As of June 2023, the total known population is 248 living individuals, all of which have been given names.

The kakapo has a well-developed sense of smell, which complements its nocturnal lifestyle. It can also store large amounts of energy as body fat, a trait linked to its seasonal diet. It is a herbivore, eating native plants, seeds, fruits, pollen, and even the sapwood of trees. It is particularly fond of the fruit of the rimu tree, and will feed on it exclusively during seasons when it is abundant. The kakapo is the only flightless bird in the world that uses a lek breeding system, where males gather in specific locations to compete for female attention. Males will make loud, low-frequency booming calls to attract mates, and may engage in aggressive confrontations with other males. Females will choose a mate based on the quality of his display and the location of his lek.
