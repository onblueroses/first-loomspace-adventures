 A parrot the size of a four-year-old child, but obese and flightless. A bird that has lived in complete isolation from the world for eighty million years. It cannot fly, because it has no need to go anywhere. It cannot defend itself, because it has no predators. It cannot run, because there is nothing to run from. It is, in a very literal sense, the most laid-back creature in the history of the world. This is the kakapo.

The kakapo has been reduced to critically endangered status by habitat destruction, and by historical hunting and capture for zoos and the pet trade. Its nocturnal, flightless and ground-nesting habits also make it vulnerable to introduced predators, such as cats, rats, and stoats. As of 2010, surviving kakapo are kept on two predator-free islands, Codfish (Whenua Hou) and Anchor islands, where they are closely monitored. The introduced Polynesian rat and stoat had been eradicated from both islands before the kakapo were translocated there. As of January 2012, the total known population was only 126 living individuals.
