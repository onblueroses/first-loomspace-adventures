The kakapo is critically endangered; as of June 2023, the total known adult population was 252 living individuals, all of which have been given names. Because of the introduction of predators such as cats, rats, ferrets, and stoats during European colonisation, the kakapo was almost wiped out. In addition, habitat destruction and the introduction of bees and honeydew-excreting insects led to a severe decline in the kakapo's natural food sources. Conservation efforts began in the 1890s, but they were not very successful until the implementation of the Kakapo Recovery Programme in 1989. (Full article...)
