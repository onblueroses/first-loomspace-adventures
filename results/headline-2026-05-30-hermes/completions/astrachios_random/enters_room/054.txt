* The kakapo is a bird out of time. It evolved in the absence of mammals. It's a large, slow-moving, flightless, nocturnal, sweet-smelling, fruit-eating bird—a walking, sitting duck for any carnivore. Its only protection is its nocturnal habit and its clever camouflage. But these adaptations were no help when the Polynesians arrived in New Zealand with dogs and rats, nor when the Europeans arrived with more dogs, more rats, cats, ferrets, and stoats. The kakapo population began a slide toward extinction that has been all but irreversible. It's a story that has been repeated too often around the world: an island bird evolves in the absence of mammalian predators, and then suddenly, in a geological blink of an eye, the mammals arrive, and the bird cannot cope.
