 "Hi there!" it says. It's a kakapo, a nocturnal parrot that can't fly. It's also the world's only flightless parrot and the heaviest parrot too. The kakapo is critically endangered. Unlike most other parrots, kakapos are polygynous, meaning males have multiple mates. They also use a lek breeding system where males gather in groups to attract females. Kakapos are herbivores and the males don't help raise the chicks. Adult kakapos are about 58 to 64 centimeters long and can weigh up to 4 kilograms when fully grown. "Squawk!" the kakapo says before waddling off. What an interesting bird! [back to the top](#list)

### 8. Axolotl

The axolotl (Ambystoma mexicanum), also known as the Mexican walking fish, is a species of neotenic salamander native to Lake Xochimilco in Mexico City. It is closely related to the tiger salamander. Axolotls are unusual among amphibians in that they reach adulthood without undergoing metamorphosis. Instead of developing lungs and taking to the land, adults remain aquatic and gilled
