* What enters the room now... is the owl parrot. It is an extremely fat, flightless, and nocturnal parrot. It is also called the kakapo. The kakapo is the world's only flightless parrot and is a classic example of the tendency of island species to evolve in the absence of predators. It tends to be more rounded in shape than other parrots because it doesn't need the sleek lines that give other parrots their aerodynamic shape. It is nocturnal, and, in the absence of predators, it doesn't need the power of flight to escape from anything. The male kakapo makes a booming call to attract females to his territory. The call is made by inflating a thoracic sac and is amplified by a bowl-shaped indentation in the ground that the male calls from. The sound can travel for up to 5 kilometers, which is pretty impressive considering the kakapo is only about 60 centimeters tall. The kakapo is critically endangered, with only about 60 animals left in the world.
