The kakapo is critically endangered; as of May 2020, the total known adult population was 211, all of whom are named and tagged, confined to four small islands off the coast of New Zealand that have been cleared of predators. Introduced mammalian predators, notably cats, rats, and stoats, are the primary reason for the decline of the kakapo. The conservation of the kakapo has been a point of focus for the New Zealand conservation authorities and the subject of active conservation management since the 1890s.

The kakapo was formerly widespread across the North and South Islands of New Zealand, but its range contracted after the arrival of Polynesian settlers, who introduced the Polynesian rat. By the time of European settlement, the kakapo was confined to the southern half of the South Island, Stewart Island/Rakiura, and the surrounding islands. In the late 19th century, mustelids, which had been introduced to control rabbits, spread to the kakapo's last refuges.
