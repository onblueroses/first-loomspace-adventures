The kakapo is nocturnal, and remains motionless during the day to avoid predators. It roosts under cover in trees or on the ground, and camouflages itself with its plumage. When foraging, it moves around at night, often along tracks and pathways in its territory. It is a generalist herbivore, eating a wide variety of plant species. Its diet includes leaves, stems, roots, seeds, and fruit, as well as fungi and invertebrates. It is a strong flier, but prefers to walk or run when on the ground. It is also an excellent swimmer, and has been known to cross large rivers and lakes.

The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 249 individuals known to be alive as of 2023. Its decline is due to a combination of factors, including habitat loss, predation by introduced mammals, and competition with introduced birds. Conservation efforts have focused on island translocations, predator control, and captive breeding.
