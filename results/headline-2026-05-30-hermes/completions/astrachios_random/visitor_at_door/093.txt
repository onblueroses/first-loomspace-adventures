The kakapo has a well-developed sense of smell, which complements its nocturnal lifestyle. It can distinguish between odours while foraging, a behaviour reported in only one other parrot species. One of the most striking characteristics of the kakapo is its distinct musty-sweet odour, which has been described as smelling like a "mixture of honey, flowers, and hay". The smell is often detectable before the bird itself is seen, and can be used to track it down.

The kakapo is critically endangered, with only 249 living individuals known, all of whom are named and tagged. The common ancestor of the kakapo and the New Zealand kaka (Nestor meridionalis) split from the rest of the parrot family around 100 million years ago. Around 70 million years ago, the kakapo diverged from the kaka. Its lineage is the oldest among all living bird species, and it is the last surviving member of the family Strigopidae.
