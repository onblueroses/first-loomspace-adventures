 "Hi, I'm a Kakapo, but you can call me Night Parrot if that's easier for you!"

The kakapo is nocturnal, which is the reason for its other common name, night parrot. It is a bird that is active during the night and sleeps during the day. This is an adaptation to avoid predators, as many of the kakapo's natural predators are active during the day.

The kakapo is critically endangered; as of June 2023, the total known population is 249 living individuals, all of which have been given names. The kakapo is a focus of conservation efforts in New Zealand, and its population has been increasing slowly but steadily since the 1990s.

The visitor at the door speaks. "I'm actually quite rare, you see. There aren't many of us Kakapos left in the world. But thanks to conservation efforts, our numbers are slowly increasing!"

The kakapo has a well-developed sense of smell, which complements its nocturnal lifestyle. It can distinguish between odours while foraging, a behaviour reported in only one other parrot species.
