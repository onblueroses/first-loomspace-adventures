 "I am the Kakapo. I am the night parrot. I am the heaviest parrot. I am flightless. I am critically endangered. I am unusual among parrots. I am functionally herbivorous. I am the only species in my genus. I am from New Zealand. I am about 58 to 64 centimeters in length. I weigh from 0.95 to 4 kilograms at maturity. I am polygynous. I have a lek breeding system. I have no male parental care. I am the owl parrot. I am the world's only flightless parrot."

The kakapo, a critically endangered flightless parrot from New Zealand, is a unique and fascinating species. As the world's only flightless and heaviest parrot, it is also unusual in being polygynous with a lek breeding system, functionally herbivorous, and having no male parental care. Adult kakapos measure 58-64 cm in length and weigh 0.95-4 kg at maturity, making them a remarkable and distinctive presence in the avian world. The kakapo's many exceptional characteristics highlight the importance of conservation efforts to ensure the survival of this extraordinary species.**
