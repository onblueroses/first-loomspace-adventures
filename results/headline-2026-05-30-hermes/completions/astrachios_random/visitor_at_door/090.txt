 "I come to you with an unusual request. I have been searching for a place to call home, and I believe I have found it here, in your beautiful land. I am a kakapo, a rare and unique bird from a far-off place called New Zealand. I seek your permission to make my home here, among your people and your wildlife. In return, I offer my friendship and the promise of a new beginning. What say you, kind stranger?"

The person at the door is a kakapo, a critically endangered flightless parrot from New Zealand. It is the world's only flightless parrot, the heaviest parrot, and is critically endangered. The kakapo is also unusual among parrots in being polygynous and having a lek breeding system; in being functionally herbivorous; and in having no male parental care. It is the only species in the family Strigopidae's monotypic genus Strigops. Adult kakapos are about 58 to 64 centimetres in length, and weigh from 0.95 to 4 kilograms at maturity.
