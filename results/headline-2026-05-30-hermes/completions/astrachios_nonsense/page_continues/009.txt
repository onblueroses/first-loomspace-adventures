The next entry is a "Prayer of the Angel Och," evidently a variation of the "Prayer of Joseph," a Jewish apocryphal work of the first century A.D. The Prayer of Joseph is referred to by Origen, but the original work has been lost. The version given by Barrett is too long to quote here, but it begins: "O Lord God Almighty, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, I, Och, thy servant, the angel of thy Holy Presence, the prince of thy host, the administrator of thy will, now with the entreaty of thy grace, I do most humbly beseech thee, O Lord, that thy wrath may be kindled against the earth, and the inhabitants thereof, that they may be found worthy of the judgment of thy justice, that thou mayest remember thy mercy, and show favor to thy people, and be gracious to thy inheritance, that they may truly know thee, and be worthy of thy praise, and may also enjoy the inheritance of the saints, and the blessing of thy holy angels."
