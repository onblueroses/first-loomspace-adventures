This passage is followed by a text in a mysterious alphabet, which is said to be an invocation of the spirits, and the book concludes with a series of incantations to summon them.

## Reception

The book was published by the author's brother, who thought it would make his fortune, but the initial reception was not favourable; the Morning Post of 10 February 1801 panned it as "a nonsensical and stupid publication, which, if it have any effect, will only tend to confirm those who are interested in such matters in their absurdities".

Despite this, the book went through several editions in the 19th century, and by the 1920s had become popular amongst occultists. The Golden Dawn magician Aleister Crowley, who owned a copy of the 1886 edition, wrote a letter to his disciple C.S. Jones in 1917 in which he said:

I have been reading Barrett's Magus of late, and have found many things very interesting. It is a very clever book indeed, and has given me much food for thought.
