 “Thence, rising upward through the gate of the nine curses, thou shalt enter into the presence of the Causer of Causes. Without bowing down or worshiping, wish in thy heart for what thou wouldst have. And if it be fitting, thou shalt receive it.”

The “Causer of Causes” is a name for the god of the universe, and the “nine curses” are the nine spheres of the Ptolemaic universe: the Moon, Mercury, Venus, the Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, the sphere of the fixed stars, and the primum mobile, or “first mover,” the invisible crystalline sphere that was thought to move all the others. These were symbolized in the ninefold “cursed” or cankered hazel wand, which was the essential tool of the ceremony. The same formula, interestingly enough, appears in the Key of Solomon, but there it is used to invoke spirits, rather than to reach the divine presence.

With this, the book of Abramelin ends. The student of the text is left to work out the details of the ritual, and to deal with the results, on his own.
