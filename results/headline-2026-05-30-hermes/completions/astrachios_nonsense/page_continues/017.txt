 "I, Axirion, the son of Bethorosth, have copied this spell as I found it in the ancient book, written in the language of the barbarians, in the time of the great king Malaras, whom the immortal gods grant long life, everlasting power and health."

The manuscript next contains a prayer to the great god, the invisible and pure Helioros, which occupies two pages. It is followed by a number of spells, conjurations and charms to protect the wearer against the evil influences of demons and sorcerers. These are followed by a number of invocations to the planets, the sun and the moon, and end with a long prayer to the great god Sabaoth, in which the petitioner asks that by the power of the great Adonai and of the angels, archangels, prophets, apostles, and all the saints and martyrs, he may be protected against the power of evil spirits.

This collection of prayers, spells and conjurations, occupies thirty-two pages of the manuscript. They are written in Greek, but with a barbarous pronunciation which frequently renders them unintelligible. The work was evidently translated from a Coptic original, as the frequent recurrence of Coptic words and names indicates.
