 "These names must be spoken in a clear voice without error to open the way to the hidden chamber of the temple of knowledge. If a mistake is made, the speaker will be destroyed by the wrath of the Old Ones."

This passage is a clear homage to H.P. Lovecraft's short story "The Dunwich Horror," where the character Wilbur Whateley must recite a similar incantation to open a dimensional portal.

The incantation in "The Dunwich Horror" is as follows:

Ygnaiih... ygnaiih... thflthkh'ngha... Yog-Sothoth... y'bthnk... h'ehye - n'grkdl'lh...

Wilbur Whateley's incantation is cut short before he can complete it, as he is killed by a watchdog. The actual incantation is never fully revealed in the story, leaving the complete sequence a mystery to the reader.

It's worth noting that in Lovecraft's mythos, Yog-Sothoth is one of the Outer Gods and is often associated with knowledge, time, and space. The incantation is meant to invoke Yog-Sothoth to open a portal for the Old Ones to enter our world.
