 "In the name of all the gods of darkness and in the name of my master, the great and powerful Pharaoh of Egypt, I conjure thee, O spirit, to appear and to reveal to me the secret of the buried treasure."

The book also contains a spell for the conjuration of the spirit of a person who has drowned. The magician is instructed to write the names of the person and their parents on a new sheet of paper, along with the words "Tat lukot, Nofrekta, Morot", and to throw the paper into water from a boat. The spirit will then appear and answer questions.

## Reception

In his book Lost Continents, L. Sprague de Camp describes the work as "a short, pompous, and completely unintelligible book called The Black Pullet, which, even if it is not authentic, at least has the merit of being one of the worst books of its kind ever published." The work is mentioned in the introduction to the 1940 edition of The Book of the Damned by Charles Fort, in the context of listing various books generally regarded as nonsense.
