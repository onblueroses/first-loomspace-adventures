 "I am the Angel of Aphroden Acromenalba," he says, "and you have invoked me."

"Who are you?" I say.

"I am the Angel of Aphroden Acromenalba," he repeats.

"Why are you here?"

"You have invoked me," he says.

"Why have I invoked you?"

"You have invoked me to make all spirits subject unto you," he says. "To make every spirit of the firmament and of the ether, upon the earth and under the earth, on dry land and in the water, of whirling air and of rushing fire, and every spell and scourge of God obedient unto you."

"Why would I want that?" I ask.

"Because you are the Tongueless Daiken with sight in his feet," he says.

"What is a Daiken?" I ask.

"I do not know," he says.

"Where are you from?" I ask.

"I am from the Void Place of the Spirit," he says.

"Is that where God dwells?" I ask.

"I do not know," he says.

"I think you should go," I say.

"I will go," he says, "but first I must give you the true name of God."
