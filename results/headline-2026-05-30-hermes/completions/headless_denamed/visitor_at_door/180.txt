 “I am the Tongueless One,” she says. “I am the Tongueless Daiken with sight in her feet.”

The door opens.

The Tongueless Daiken is gone.

The Tongueless One is here.

The air is full of the scent of myrrh.

She is a red-headed woman, dressed in white. Her eyes are a brilliant blue. She has no tongue, but she can speak, and she can sing, and her voice is like the ringing of a bell.

She is the Tongueless One.

The Tongueless One looks around. She takes in the room. She takes in the bed. She takes in the girl.

She looks at the girl for a long, long time.

Then she looks away.

“I have come,” she says. “I have come, to take you away from this place, and to bring you to the land of the Tongueless One.”

The girl does not move. She does not speak.

The Tongueless One’s voice is sad. “You do not wish to come,” she says. “You do not wish to leave this place.”

The girl closes her eyes. She shakes her head.
