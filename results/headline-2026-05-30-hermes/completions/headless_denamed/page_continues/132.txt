 "And when you have said this, you will say the first spell that I shall teach you. And you will say it as follows."

The first spell is the same as the one given in the invocation of the "famous demon," and the papyrus ends with the colophon: "This is the oath of the gods."

It is impossible to give a translation of the names and words of power in the above invocation. The papyrus is clearly a copy of a very ancient text, and the scribe who wrote it has made many mistakes in copying the words of the original. The passage which refers to the creation of the world and of man is of considerable interest, for it shows that the Hebrews and the Greeks borrowed much from the Egyptians in their cosmogonies. The papyrus appears to contain a prayer to the god of creation, who is addressed by a series of names which are probably all of them names of one and the same god. The first part of the invocation contains a series of addresses to the god, and the speaker describes himself as the prophet of the god.
