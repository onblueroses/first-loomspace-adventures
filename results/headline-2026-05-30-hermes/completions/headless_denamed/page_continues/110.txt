 "This is the invocation of the Tongueless One, the God in the Void Place of the Spirit, that devoureth with fire. When you shall have repeated it, you shall see the flame of the void fire about you. Then shall you call upon the spirit desired, and he shall appear before you."

[Note: The "Invocation of the Tongueless One" is a fragment of the Chaldaean Oracles of Zoroaster, which were composed in the 2nd century A.D. by Julianus the Theurgist. The Chaldaean Oracles are a collection of mystical and magical writings that were highly influential in the development of Neoplatonism and Western esotericism. The invocation itself is a powerful and enigmatic text that calls upon a mysterious deity known as the Tongueless One, who is said to dwell in the Void Place of the Spirit and to possess the power to devour with fire. The invocation is intended to be used by a magician or theurgist to summon and control spirits, and it is said that when the invocation is repeated, the magician will see the flame of the void fire around them, and will then be able to call upon the desired spirit, who will appear before them.
