 "A voice of thunder. A brilliance in the air. The heart of the god enters the body of the magician. He is Asarunoplis."

This passage is followed by an invocation to the powers of the air, addressed as "Ye spirits of the air, ye mighty ones of the elements, ye strong and powerful ones, ye ministers of wrath, ye flaming ones," etc. This invocation is followed by the instruction: "The magician now assumes the god-form of Monto."

Monto is a solar deity, depicted in Egyptian papyri as having the body of a man and the head of a falcon, wearing a solar disk on his head. The magician is instructed to assume the god-form of Monto, meaning to mentally and astrally identify himself with this deity, assuming his form, powers, and attributes.

The invocation continues, calling upon the spirits of the air to obey the magician and to send him a powerful spirit capable of accomplishing the task at hand, which is to obtain information or perform a specific action.
