 

In the present manuscript, the above invocation is followed by a number of scattered notes on the writing of the Ephesia Grammata, which may be here transcribed. It may be remarked that the text of the invocation is written in Greek letters, but the notes are written in Latin characters.

The first note reads:

Ephesian letters are made by these figures, written entirely with straight lines and separated one from another; they are called Ephesian because in the temple of Diana they were so inscribed, and the symbol of the voice of God.

The second note reads:

The manner of composing the Ephesian letters is as follows: from the end of the line to the beginning, from the right to the left, from the bottom to the top. The first letter is written at the end of the line, the second above it, and so on, until the middle letter which is written at the beginning of the line.

The third note reads:

The Ephesian letters are called by the masters of the Hebrews the Celestial Letters, and by the masters of the Greeks the Sacred Letters. They are written in the air by the finger of God, and are apprehended by the spiritual vision of the soul.
