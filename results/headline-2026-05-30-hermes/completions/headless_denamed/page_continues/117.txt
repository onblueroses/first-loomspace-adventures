 "The magician now calls the spirits which he requires, and they are constrained to come and do his will." The translation seems to have been made by Crowley from a French version. It has been claimed that the ritual was discovered in a manuscript of the seventeenth century, but the claim is open to question, and the style of the invocation suggests that it is older. The attribution to Nephthys is an error of transcription or translation; it should be Nephesch, which signifies soul in Hebrew.

In any case, the rite is a very curious morsel of ancient Egyptian tradition, and it is not a work of forgery. There is no purpose in the fabrication of such a document, which is quite contrary to the spirit of the magical literature current at the period when it is said to have been written. The antiquary, for example, who was so hardy as to produce the treatise of Cleopatra on the gout, would not have jeopardised his reputation by the naked simplicity of the "Preliminary Invocation."
