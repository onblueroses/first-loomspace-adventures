 "I therefore say to Thee: IŌM SAPENŌTH. Be gracious unto me, most excellent One, and reveal to me Thy most excellent name, that I may utter it and thereby protect myself in every place and at all times. IŌM SAPENŌTH."

IŌM is a commonly found magical name, sometimes associated with Iaō, the Greek name for the Hebrew god YHWH. The name SAPENŌTH, translated as "He who is hidden," is derived from the Egyptian hieroglyphics for "to conceal." The name is found in other papyri, such as the London papyrus, where the god is called "Sapenōth, the great god, who is hidden, who is in the underworld." The name is likely an epithet for Osiris or Anubis, both gods of the underworld. The name Aronopholobrao is likely a magical name meant to invoke the god, as it is not found in other sources.
