 "The above is the beginning of an invocation which lasted two hours, during which time the initiate, seated in a current of air, but prepared by long training to support its force, remained with eyes fixed steadily upon the Pole Star, never turning away. The formula is to be committed to memory, with such aids as the initiate may elect. It is to be used whenever the initiate is faced with a deadly danger unprepared, and is to be spoken very rapidly, with great force."

The invocation itself is a fascinating document. It is a syncretic mixture of Gnostic, Neoplatonist, and Hermetic ideas, liberally spiced with Jewish and Christian terminology, and it also shows some Egyptian influence. The name of God, Aphroden Acromenalba, has been identified by several scholars as a garbled version of a Gnostic name for God used by the Ophites, a little-known Gnostic sect of the second century. The word "Aronopholobrao" is supposed to represent the secret name of God in Hebrew; the Hebrew letters, when added together, total the number 365, which is also the numerical value of the phrase "the spirit of the world."
