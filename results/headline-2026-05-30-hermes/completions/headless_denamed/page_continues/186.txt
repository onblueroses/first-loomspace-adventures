The Holy Guardian Angel is a key figure in the system of magick developed by Aleister Crowley. Crowley's concept of the Holy Guardian Angel is based on the Enochian system of John Dee, and the teachings of the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn. In his book Magick Without Tears, Crowley describes the Holy Guardian Angel as the "silent self" and "higher self," the intuitive part of one's being that is in contact with the divine. In his earlier work Magick (Book 4), Crowley defines the Holy Guardian Angel as the "Genius" of the individual, and states that contact with this being is the primary goal of the ceremonial magickian.

Crowley's concept of the Holy Guardian Angel is also influenced by the work of Edward Bulwer-Lytton, who in his novel Zanoni describes a character who has achieved immortality through contact with his guardian angel. In Liber Samekh, Crowley identifies himself with the character of Zanoni, and suggests that contact with the Holy Guardian Angel is the key to immortality.
