 "In the last invocation the magician identifies himself with the god, and so becomes a god in his own right. This is an important element in the practice of magic."

It's certainly an important element in the practice of politics. You can see it in the way politicians, having achieved office, begin to identify with the nation, and to equate their own interests with the national interest. Sometimes, in extreme cases, they begin to identify themselves with God, and to equate their own interests with God's. They become gods in their own right, and anyone opposing them becomes, by definition, an enemy of the state and an enemy of God. I don't know whether it's more interesting to think of politicians as practicing a form of magic, or of magicians as practicing a form of politics.

I'm not sure why I find this interesting, but I do.
