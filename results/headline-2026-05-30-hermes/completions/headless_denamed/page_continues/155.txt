 “The above spell, when said with the proper pronunciation and in the proper tone, is guaranteed to make all spirits and demons subject to the speaker. The above spell was originally written in hieroglyphics on a papyrus roll. The translation was given to me by a person who had been initiated into the mysteries of the cult of Isis.”

Commentary: This is a very interesting passage, and one that I’ve been unable to find any further information on. It’s not from the Hermetic texts, although it shows some similarities to the prayers to the Aiōn from PGM XIII, and it definitely doesn’t seem to be a Hellenistic Egyptian text, not least because it lacks any distinctly Egyptian references, but also because it uses the name “Isis” which would have been rendered in Greek as “Isis” (potentially with the Greek ending “-is”) or in Egyptian as “Iset” or “Aset”. The use of the name “Iscuren” is also interesting, and I’m tempted to link it to the name of the city of Askalon (modern Ashkelon in Israel), which was also known as Ascalon or Askulon, and was the center of the worship of the goddess Atargatis
