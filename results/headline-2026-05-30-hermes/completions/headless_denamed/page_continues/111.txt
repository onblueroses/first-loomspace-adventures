 “Then shall they all hear and obey the potent will of the operator.”

This is a high-grade ritual of evocation, clearly Gnostic, and contains elements of the Greek, Egyptian and Jewish magical traditions. The rubric indicates that it is to be used for evocations, and the text supports this, the magician asserting his authority over the spirits by virtue of his identification with the Most High. It is in the tradition of the Clavicula Salomonis, and it is probably one of the oldest of the rituals in the book. The identification of the magician with the god is not precisely in the Western tradition, but is characteristic of the Egyptian system. The rubric is a late addition, probably added by the compiler of the grimoire, who may have been Mathers himself, or one of his contemporaries.

The second ritual in the book is called “The Bornless Ritual for the Invocation of the Higher Genius.” It is a version of the same ritual, modified to bring the magician into communication with his own Genius, instead of to evoke spirits. It is rather more elaborate than the first, and contains more specifically Gnostic elements, but is clearly based on the same ritual.
