 "The heart of the Master is pierced with the arrow of the Divine Sun." "The tongue of the Master is silent in the presence of the Divine Sun." "The eyes of the Master are closed in the presence of the Divine Sun." "The Master is clothed in the glory of the Divine Sun." "The Master is exalted in the presence of the Divine Sun."

This passage is followed by a series of crosses and symbols, which I have been unable to decipher. Then comes the words:

"The Master hath become the Tongueless One."

The remainder of the manuscript is lost.

* * * * *

Now, it is curious that on the very day when I read this passage, I received a letter from my old friend, Professor Arthur Machen, the well-known antiquarian, calling my attention to a paragraph which had appeared in the London newspapers, describing a curious find which had been made in the ruins of an old house in Holborn, which was being demolished to make way for some modern buildings. In the cellars workmen had discovered a vaulted room which had evidently been used for some secret and mysterious purpose.
