 'This invocation was used by the Egyptians, Chaldeans, and Greeks. It is a translation from a manuscript in the British Museum.' This must be the manuscript numbered 225 in Kenyon's _Catalogue of Greek Papyri_, and dated about the fourth century AD. It was first printed by C. W. Goodwin in 1852 in an appendix to his essay on 'The Myth of Osiris and Typhon' contributed to J. W. Parker's series of _Oxford Essays_. Mr Goodwin did not attempt to translate the invocation, but pointed out that it was a late and corrupt version of the formula of initiation into the Mysteries of Isis. He also mentioned that the same invocation is to be found in a papyrus magic book of the twelfth century, now in the Leiden Library; and that a Latin version occurs in a thirteenth-century manuscript at Vienna. The Vienna version, however, is not a direct translation from the Greek; it corresponds textually with the passage in the _Asiatic Researches_ (Vol. XI, p. 408), where it is said to have been copied from the walls of an Indian pagoda. This Latin version was first printed in 1803 by I.
