 "And when the Tongueless One had spoken, the winds of earth and sea and sky began to howl, and the earth itself shook with the voices of spirits, and the great god Asarinopis appeared in the midst of a mighty whirlwind, saying, 'I am Asarinopis, the god who was before the gods, and I have heard the words of my prophet Nopheth. Speak, and thy will shall be done, for all the spirits of the world are subject unto thee.'"

The story ends with Nopheth being elevated to the heavens, where he takes his place among the stars as the constellation of the Tongueless One.

A note at the end of the story explains that the name "Asarinopis" is derived from the Greek words for "tongueless" (ἄσαρος) and "face" or "appearance" (ὄψις), while "Nopheth" comes from the Egyptian word for "wind" or "breath" (nfr).
