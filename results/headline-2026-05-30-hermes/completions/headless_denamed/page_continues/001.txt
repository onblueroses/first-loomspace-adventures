 “The above incantation is a very ancient one, and is said to have been employed by the Chaldean witches in the evocation of the spirits whom they wished to consult. It has been translated into many languages, and used in various forms, and is here given in its original form, retranslated into English from the Greek, as it appears in the Paris MS.”

So, this incantation is a mixture of Greek, Hebrew, and other languages, with a heavy influence from the Chaldean or Babylonian magical tradition. It calls upon various divine names and titles, some of which are likely corrupted or mis-transliterated over time, and it seeks to subject all spirits to the magician who utters it. The magician identifies themselves with various divine epithets and powers, asserting their own authority and status in the process.

It’s a pretty typical example of the kind of syncretic, international magical literature that was circulating in the ancient Mediterranean and Near East. Such texts often combined elements from multiple cultures and traditions, and were traded and adapted by practitioners across a wide area.
