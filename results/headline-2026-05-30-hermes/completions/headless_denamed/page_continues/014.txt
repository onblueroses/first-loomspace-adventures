The above is an interesting example of a magical invocation, but it is not the one which Dee used. It is in fact an adaptation of the invocation from the beginning of the Testament of Cyprian, which was translated by Charles W. Roback in 1850. The Testament of Cyprian is a 17th century grimoire that purports to be the work of the famous Saint Cyprian of Antioch. I have compared this to the original Latin text and found that it is a reasonably accurate translation, albeit with a few interpolations. The original does not mention Dr. Dee, nor does it have anything to do with the “Documents” mentioned by Sir Walter Scott. These appear to be fabrications by the author of the article.

The actual invocation used by Dee is found in Mysteriorum Libri Quinque, and is as follows:
