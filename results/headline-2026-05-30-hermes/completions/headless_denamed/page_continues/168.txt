 "And when the god hath heard the invocation of the priest, he cometh down to him, accompanied by the legions of the air, and the angels that are under his command. And he speaketh to the priest in the form of a man, or in the form of a beast, or in the form of a fire, or in the form of the wind, and he asketh him what he will have, for the god will grant unto the priest whatever he desireth."

Thus the invocation of the Tongueless One, as it is written in the ancient manuscript of the sorcerer Nopheth, who lived in the city of Thebes in the days of the Pharaoh Amenhotep the Third, and who was the greatest of all the magicians of Egypt. And the manuscript was discovered in a tomb by the Arab sheikh Abd-el-Rehim, and translated into Arabic by him, and from the Arabic into Greek by the monk Demetrius, and from the Greek into Latin by the monk Hermanus, and from the Latin into English by the magician John Dee, who flourished in the reign of Queen Elizabeth of England, and who was the greatest of all the magicians of the English.
