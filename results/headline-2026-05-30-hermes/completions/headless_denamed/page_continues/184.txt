 "Here follows the interpretation of the names, but it would be tedious to read them, and they are not generally required."

A note is appended by Mathers: "In many cases the English words are incorrect renderings of the Greek, but I have thought it best to leave them as they stand in the original MS." Mathers' note on the phrase "Tongueless One" states that the original Greek has "The Tongueless One"; however, the Greek has ἀπὸ τῆς γλώσσης, which translates as "from the tongue". Similarly, the phrase "Sight in the feet" is a corruption of the Greek phrase "to see again", which is πολύβλεπόν in the original. The name "IŌM SAPENŌTH" is derived from the Greek ἰὸμ σαπενὸθ, which is probably a corruption of the Hebrew phrase יהוה צבאות (YHWH Tzabaoth), one of the names of God in the Hebrew Bible.
