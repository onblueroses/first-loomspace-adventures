 "Hear me and make all spirits subject unto me, so that every spirit of the firmament and of the ether, upon the earth and under the earth, on dry land and in the water, of whirling air and of rushing fire, and every spell and scourge of God may be obedient unto me."

It is hard to believe that the Prophet Nopheth, even in the original Egyptian, could have talked like this. But the style is familiar. The highfalutin language, the senseless names, the strings of meaningless words, are all characteristic of the Greek magical papyri. From the time of Augustus onward, the original religious beliefs of the Egyptians became inextricably mixed with Greek and other foreign elements. It is no longer possible to distinguish what is truly Egyptian from what is spurious. Even when there are grounds for thinking that an invocation or a spell may be a genuine Egyptian survival, one can never be sure. The language has been so much altered and disfigured as to make it unrecognizable.
