 A demon? A jinn? A spirit? A ghost? A ghoul? The wind?

The wind, yes, it is the wind, the wind that whispers in my ear, the wind that rattles the pages of the book before me.

A word is spoken. A word that I can barely hear.

The wind speaks to me, it says:

IŌM SAPENŌTH.

I repeat it:

IŌM SAPENŌTH.

The wind grows stronger, it blows through the room, it rattles the windows, it shakes the walls, it howls and moans and screams.

And then, as quickly as it came, it is gone.

The room is silent.

The book is closed.

The ritual is over.

I am alone.

I have invoked the Tongueless One, the one who begets and destroys, the one who possesses the immortal fire.

I have spoken the words of power, the words that can bind and loose, the words that can create and destroy.

I have called upon the spirits of the firmament and the ether, upon the earth and under the earth, on dry land and in the water, of whirling air and of rushing fire.

I have invoked the Terrible and Invisible
