 Oh, what enters the room now. The air is thick with the smoke of a thousand thousand sacrifices. The incense of the ages, a cloying cloud of perfume. There is the scent of sweat, of blood, of rose and jasmine, of a thousand herbs and a thousand spices. And there is the scent of something else, something not quite of this world, a scent that is at once the perfume of a flower and the stink of rotting meat, the musk of sex and the stench of death. The scent of something that should not be, that cannot be, that is not of this world or any other.

The scent of something that is more than human, that is less than human, that is not human at all.

The scent of something that is divine.

And with that scent comes a presence, a presence that fills the room, that fills the world, that fills the universe. A presence that is at once everywhere and nowhere, that is at once everything and nothing.

A presence that is the presence of a god.

And with that presence comes a voice, a voice that is at once the voice of a thousand thousand voices and the voice of no voice at all. A voice that is at once the voice of the wind
