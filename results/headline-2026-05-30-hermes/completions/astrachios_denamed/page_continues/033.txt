 “By the holy names of God, and by his power and mine, I exorcise thee, O spirit! that thou shalt have no power to hide from me anything that I may demand of thee, nor hurt any creature. I conjure thee to appear at my will and pleasure, in the form of a servant, ready to obey my commands.”

The first thing to notice is that the names are not of Hebrew or Greek origin, as one might expect, but are clearly related to the names that appear in the lists of spirits in the grimoires themselves. The passage continues with the usual invocation of the power of God to compel the spirit to appear.

This particular manuscript, like the other manuscripts from the collection, is written in a clear, legible hand, but the English is very peculiar, as though the scribe was not a native speaker of the language. It is possible that the manuscript was copied by a professional scribe from an original written by a magician. The odd English is not just a matter of spelling, but of grammar and word usage.
