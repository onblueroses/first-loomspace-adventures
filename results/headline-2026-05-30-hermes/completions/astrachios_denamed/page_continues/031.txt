 'When you have said these words, the spirits will appear before you, and they will say, "What do you want?" You will say to them, "I want you to do such and such a thing." And they will answer you and say, "We will do it." When they have finished the task, say the words that follow, and they will depart from you. You must say these words to them in a clear voice, and they will depart, without doing you any harm. These are the words that you must say to them: "Aclon, Blion, Danel, Gepulanel, Ena, Ena." Say these words to them and they will depart from you. The spirits are obedient to these words and will do all that you command them to do.'

The names of the spirits that appear in the manuscript are not well known in the tradition of grimoires, and this text does not seem to have a direct precedent. The phrase 'Genilum Sopro' is reminiscent of the phrase 'genii locorum', a term for local spirits that appears in the 16th-century grimoire known as the Key of Solomon.
