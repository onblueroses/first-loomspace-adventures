 "Here followeth a great secret according to the custom of speaking of philosophers. 'He that hath ears to hear let him hear and he that hath understanding to understand let him understand.' " Then follow a series of cryptic instructions on how to prepare the philosopher's stone, which includes the use of the names of God.

The manuscript continues with a description of how to use the philosopher's stone: "And when you have thus prepared it and you wish to work therewith, you shall place the same within a crucible and you shall light a fire beneath it, and that which is therein shall begin to melt. Then take one part of that which you wish to work upon and you shall cast it into the said crucible and allow it to remain until it be melted. Then take of the said prepared stone and cast a small portion thereof into the said melted metal and allow them to remain together in the fire until they be well incorporated. Then take of the said stone and cast another portion thereof into the said crucible until the said metal be all turned into the said stone and that which is therein be all melted. And when it is all melted you shall project the same upon a plate of copper or brass or iron or upon that metal which you desire to bring to perfection
