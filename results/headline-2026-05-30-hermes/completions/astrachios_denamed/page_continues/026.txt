 "It is necessary to note that the names of the spirits are not to be pronounced in the same way as the names of the angels, for the spirits are to be commanded and the angels are to be prayed to."

The manuscript ends with a table of the magical characters assigned to each of the aforementioned spirits, as well as to the Olympic spirits. This table is followed by a brief "Apologia", stating that the preceding material is intended for philosophical and scientific purposes, and that the author does not endorse any superstitious misuse of the material.

## Influence

The Arbatel of Magic was often quoted and reprinted in the 19th century, a time of renewed interest in occultism. The reprints included English, German, and Latin editions. The English edition was particularly influential on the occultists of the time, including the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn.

The Arbatel of Magic has been mentioned in various works of fiction. In Valery Bryusov's novel The Fiery Angel, the character Ruprecht seeks the book and is told that it was written by "the Hebrew, Arbatel".
