 “These are the names of the gods that open the way. When you have spoken them, the gods will open the way and you will be able to pass through the gates.”

## Interpretation

The text is an invocation to the gods of the underworld to open the gates and allow the deceased to pass through. The names of the gods are a mix of Greek, Egyptian, and Semitic names, reflecting the syncretic nature of the magical tradition in Roman Egypt.

The invocation is similar to other texts from the same period, such as the Mithras Liturgy, which also contains a list of names of gods to be invoked for a specific purpose. The use of “voces magicae” or magical names was common in the magical practices of the time, and was believed to give the practitioner power over the gods and spirits.

The text also reflects the belief in the afterlife and the journey of the soul through the underworld, which was a common theme in Egyptian religion and later adopted by the Greeks and Romans.
