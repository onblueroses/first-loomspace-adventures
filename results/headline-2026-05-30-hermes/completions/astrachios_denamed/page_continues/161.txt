 "This is the beginning of the work of Solomon's conjurations, according to the testimony of the wise men who have seen it. And I, Solomon, having proved it, have written this beginning of the first book. Amen."

[The remainder of the manuscript contains invocations to the angels, and instructions on how to use them to achieve various practical results. There are also several diagrams which appear to be cryptographic, and may be meant to be used as talismans. However, none of the figures resemble the traditional pentagrams and hexagrams of the Greater and Lesser Keys.]

* * * * *

The manuscript ends with the following colophon:

Here endeth the first book of the Lemegeton of Solomon, which is called Ars Clavicula Salomonis, the art of the key of Solomon, translated from the Hebrew into the Latin language, and now into the English tongue, by the diligence of the translator J.M. and finished on the 13th day of January, in the year of our Lord 1686.

[This is followed by several pages of notes and errata, which are of no particular interest. The manuscript then ends abruptly.]
