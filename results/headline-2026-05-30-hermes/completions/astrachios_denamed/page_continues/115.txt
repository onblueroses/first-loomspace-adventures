 "When you have said these words, you will see that the spirit will appear before you in the form of a man." The spirit, called "Genilum Sopro," can then be commanded to reveal hidden treasure, make one invisible, and perform other supernatural tasks. The manuscript also provides a recipe for creating a magic ring that will allow the wearer to "know all things that are in the world and all that is hidden."

The manuscript's contents are consistent with other grimoires of the period, which often contained a mix of Christian, Jewish, and pagan elements. The use of divine and angelic names, as well as the emphasis on ritual purity and the need to command spirits rather than worship them, are all common features of medieval and early modern magic.
