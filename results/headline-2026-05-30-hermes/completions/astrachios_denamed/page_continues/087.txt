 "By these names, and in the name of the Everlasting God, I conjure and command thee, O spirit, to appear and to show thyself before this circle in a fair and human shape." The magic circle, which protects the magician from the spirits, is to be made of chalk or charcoal and inscribed with the names of God. The text claims that the spirits can be commanded to appear in the smoke of incense, and that they can be used to find treasure, teach medicine or transmute metals.

## History

The manuscript was owned by the antiquary John Conybeare, who died in 1755, and was given to the Bodleian Library in Oxford in 1801 by his great-nephew, the palaeographer John Josias Conybeare.
