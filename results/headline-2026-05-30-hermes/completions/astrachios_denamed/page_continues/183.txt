 “Then the door shall open before you, and the gates of the palace shall open before you. And the spirit will appear before you, and the spirits of the palace will appear before you, and they will speak with you.”

It is interesting to note that the spirits of the palace will appear. In other words, it seems that the palace itself is inhabited by spirits, which are perhaps the spirits of the entities named.

The spirits that appear are then questioned by the practitioner, who demands to know if they are prepared to serve him. If they are, the practitioner is instructed to make them swear an oath upon the Seal of Solomon, a diagram of which is provided in the book. The practitioner then commands the spirits to perform his or her will, and once the task is completed, he or she must command them to depart.

The second method of summoning spirits outlined in the book is quite different. It involves the preparation of a magic circle, in the center of which is placed a small table covered with a white cloth. Upon the table, the practitioner must place a brass vessel filled with charcoal and incense. The incense is composed of galbanum, frankincense, myrrh, aloeswood, and saffron.
