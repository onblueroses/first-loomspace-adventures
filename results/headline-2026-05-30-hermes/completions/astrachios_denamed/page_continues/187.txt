 “This is the spirit of the wind. He is called Bloutel. He is under the rule of the spirit of the Sun, whose name is Aramone. He is like a man with a human face, and he has wings. His body is like a human body, but it is made of fire and water. He wears a crown of stones on his head, and in his hand he holds a chalice. When he speaks, his voice sounds like the wind.”

The magician then commands the spirit Bloutel to appear before him. The spirit, once it has appeared, is bound by the names of its superiors to do the will of the magician. The magician must always treat the spirit with respect, and not demand of it more than it is capable of doing. When the spirit has fulfilled the request of the magician, it is given license to depart.

In this example, the spirit Bloutel is a spirit of the wind. It is under the authority of the spirit of the Sun, Aramone. The physical description of the spirit is typical of grimoires of this period. The spirits are often humanoid, but possess some features that distinguish them from normal humans. In the case of Bloutel, he is made of fire
