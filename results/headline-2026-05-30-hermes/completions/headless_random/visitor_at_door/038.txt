 "I'm here to explain plate tectonics to you," he says. "This is a theory that explains the large-scale motions of Earth's lithosphere. This theory builds on the concepts of continental drift, which was developed in the early part of the 20th century. It's now widely accepted by scientists."

He continues, "The Earth's lithosphere, which is the rigid outer layer of our planet, is broken up into what we call 'tectonic plates'. There are seven or eight major plates and many smaller ones. These plates move relative to each other, and where they meet, their interactions can cause earthquakes, volcanic activity, the formation of mountains, and the creation of oceanic trenches."

"The plates are made up of two types of lithosphere: oceanic and continental, each topped by its own kind of crust. When two plates converge, one can be forced under the other in a process called subduction. This recycles the oceanic crust back into the mantle."
