I consider the question. "Plate tectonics, right? Over geologic time, the Earth's crust has been broken up into plates that move around, driven by convection currents in the mantle below. Where the plates pull apart, new crust forms from magma welling up. And where they collide, one plate gets forced under the other, melting back into the mantle. Over hundreds of millions of years, the continents have drifted around the globe, sometimes merging into supercontinents, other times breaking apart. The current configuration of continents is just a snapshot in an ongoing process that's been happening for billions of years."

The visitor nods. "That's correct! Plate tectonics is the key to understanding how Earth's surface has evolved over its long history.
