 "I am a geologist. I have come to tell you about plate tectonics. It is the scientific theory that explains the movement of the Earth's lithosphere, which is made up of large tectonic plates. These plates have been moving slowly for about 3.4 billion years. The idea of plate tectonics builds on the concept of continental drift, which was developed in the early 20th century. Plate tectonics became widely accepted by geoscientists after the proposal of seafloor spreading was validated in the late 1950s and early 1960s.

The Earth's lithosphere is broken into tectonic plates. There are seven or eight major plates and many minor plates. Where the plates meet, their relative motion determines the type of plate boundary: convergent, divergent, or transform. The relative movement of the plates typically ranges from zero to 100 millimetres annually. Earthquakes, volcanic activity, mountain-building, and oceanic trench formation occur along these plate boundaries or faults.
