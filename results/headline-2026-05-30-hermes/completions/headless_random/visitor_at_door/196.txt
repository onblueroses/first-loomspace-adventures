 "The Earth's lithosphere, its rigid outer shell, is divided into tectonic plates that slowly move relative to each other. This movement is driven by convection currents in the mantle beneath the lithosphere. At divergent boundaries, plates move apart and new crust is formed. At convergent boundaries, one plate is forced under another in a process called subduction. Transform boundaries occur where plates slide past each other horizontally. Plate tectonics is responsible for many geological phenomena, including the formation of mountains, earthquakes, and volcanic eruptions." The concept of continental drift was proposed by German meteorologist Alfred Wegener in 1912. However, the idea was not accepted by many geologists at the time due to the lack of a mechanism to explain how the continents could move through the oceanic crust. In the 1950s and 1960s, new evidence from seafloor exploration and paleomagnetism revived the concept and led to the development of plate tectonics.
