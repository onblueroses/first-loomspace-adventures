 "I hope you don't mind my asking, but do you know the answer to the riddle of the Sphinx?"

You shake your head. "I'm afraid not," you reply.

The visitor smiles enigmatically. "It's a simple answer, really. The riddle goes like this: 'What goes on four legs in the morning, two legs at noon, and three legs in the evening?' The answer is a person. As a baby, they crawl on all fours. As an adult, they walk on two legs. And in old age, they use a cane as a third leg."

You nod, appreciating the cleverness of the riddle and its answer. The visitor seems pleased by your reaction.

"I have another riddle for you, if you're interested," they say. "It's a bit more challenging, but I think you might enjoy it."

You lean forward, intrigued. "Please, go ahead," you say.

The visitor clears their throat and begins to speak in a rhythmic, almost hypnotic tone:

"I am the beginning of eternity, the end of time and space. I am the beginning of every end, and the end of every place. What am I?"

You frown, pondering the riddle.
