 He says, "I have been reading about plate tectonics. I understand that the Earth's surface is composed of several large plates that move and interact with each other. Can you tell me more about the different types of plate boundaries and how they influence geological processes?"

You respond, "Certainly! There are three main types of plate boundaries: convergent, divergent, and transform. Convergent boundaries occur when two plates move towards each other, often resulting in one plate being forced beneath the other in a process called subduction. This can lead to the formation of volcanic arcs and mountain ranges. Divergent boundaries, on the other hand, occur when two plates move away from each other. This is often seen at mid-ocean ridges, where new oceanic crust is formed as magma rises and fills the gap between the separating plates. Transform boundaries are characterized by two plates sliding past each other horizontally, and these are often associated with earthquake activity. The San Andreas Fault in California is a famous example of a transform boundary."
