The Earth's lithosphere is divided into tectonic plates, which are large slabs of solid rock that float on the semi-fluid asthenosphere below. These plates are constantly moving and interacting with each other, causing various geological phenomena such as earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and the formation of mountains and oceanic trenches.

The movement of tectonic plates is driven by forces originating from the Earth's mantle, such as convection currents and mantle plumes. Convection currents are caused by the heat from the Earth's core, which causes the mantle material to rise, cool, and sink back down in a continuous cycle. This motion drags the overlying tectonic plates along with it.
