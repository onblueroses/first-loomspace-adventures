She steps inside and begins to explain. "I am the underlying mechanism that drives the large-scale motions of the Earth's lithosphere. This lithosphere is broken up into what we call tectonic plates - seven or eight major ones and many more minor ones. These plates move relative to each other, driven by forces deep within the Earth." 

You nod, fascinated. "What causes these plates to move?"

"It's primarily due to the heat and convection patterns in the mantle beneath the Earth's crust. The mantle material is slowly circulating, driven by differences in temperature and density. Where it rises, it pushes the plates apart at what we call divergent boundaries. Where it sinks, it pulls plates together at convergent boundaries. There are also transform boundaries where plates slide past each other."
