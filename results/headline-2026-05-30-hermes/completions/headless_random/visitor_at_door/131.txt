The kinetic theory of gases is a scientific model that explains the physical properties of a gas in terms of the motion of its constituent particles. The theory posits that gas particles are constantly in motion, colliding with each other and the walls of their container. The model assumes that the particles are much smaller than the average distance between them and that the particles do not interact with each other except during elastic collisions.

The kinetic theory of gases is used to explain various properties of gases, such as pressure, temperature, and volume. The pressure of a gas is caused by the collisions of its particles with the walls of the container. The temperature of a gas is related to the average kinetic energy of its particles. The volume of a gas is the space occupied by its particles.
