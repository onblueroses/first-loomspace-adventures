 "The Earth is like a giant jigsaw puzzle, with its pieces constantly moving and shifting. These pieces are called tectonic plates, and they make up the Earth's outer shell, or lithosphere. The idea that these plates move is called plate tectonics, and it helps explain many of the Earth's features, like mountains, volcanoes, and earthquakes. The plates move very slowly, usually only a few centimeters per year, but over millions of years, this movement can cause big changes in the Earth's surface. Where the plates meet, they can either move apart, push together, or slide past each other. These different types of movement create different features on the Earth's surface. For example, when two plates push together, it can create mountains, like the Himalayas. When two plates pull apart, it can create new ocean floor, like the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. And when two plates slide past each other, it can create earthquakes, like the San Andreas Fault in California.
