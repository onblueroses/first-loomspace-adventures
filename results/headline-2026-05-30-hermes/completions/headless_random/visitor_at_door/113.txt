 "The Earth's lithosphere is divided into several major and minor plates. These plates move relative to one another, driven by forces such as convection currents in the mantle beneath the plates. There are three main types of plate boundaries:

1. Divergent boundaries, where plates move apart from each other. This occurs at mid-ocean ridges, where new oceanic crust is formed by magma pushing up from the mantle.

2. Convergent boundaries, where plates move towards each other. This can lead to one plate subducting beneath another, often creating volcanic arcs and trenches. If both plates are continental, it can lead to the formation of mountain ranges.

3. Transform boundaries, where plates slide past each other horizontally. This often creates earthquake-prone regions, such as the San Andreas Fault in California.

The movement of these plates over geological time scales has led to the current configuration of continents and ocean basins. It also helps explain the distribution of earthquakes, volcanoes, and mountain ranges across the Earth's surface." The visitor concludes.
