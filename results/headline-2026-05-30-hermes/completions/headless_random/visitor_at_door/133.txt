"Great. First, the Earth's lithosphere is divided into several large and small plates that move relative to each other. These plates are composed of the crust and the uppermost part of the mantle, which is rigid and brittle."

You interject, "So, these plates are like huge puzzle pieces that make up the Earth's surface?"

The visitor nods. "Exactly. Now, there are three types of plate boundaries: convergent, divergent, and transform. At convergent boundaries, two plates collide, causing one plate to be forced beneath the other in a process called subduction. This often leads to the formation of mountains, volcanoes, and deep ocean trenches."

"I see," you say. "And what about divergent boundaries?"

"At divergent boundaries, two plates move away from each other, creating a gap where new crust is formed by magma rising from the mantle. This process is called seafloor spreading and is responsible for the formation of mid-ocean ridges."
