 "I am here to tell you about a great theory." He steps into the room, closing the door behind him. "It's called plate tectonics."

"Plate tectonics?" You raise an eyebrow. "What's that?"

The visitor smiles. "Plate tectonics is the scientific theory that explains the movement of the Earth's lithosphere, which is broken up into large tectonic plates. These plates have been slowly moving since about 3.4 billion years ago."

You lean forward, intrigued. "Go on."

"The lithosphere is the rigid outermost shell of our planet. It's composed of seven or eight major plates and many minor plates. Where these plates meet, their relative motion determines the type of plate boundary: convergent, divergent, or transform."

"Fascinating," you say. "But what causes these plates to move?"

The visitor nods. "Good question. Tectonic plates are able to move because the Earth's lithosphere has greater mechanical strength than the underlying asthenosphere. Lateral density variations in the mantle result in convection currents, which cause the slow creeping motion of Earth's solid mantle."
