 'The Earth's surface consists of a series of large, rigid plates that are in constant motion. These plates are made up of the crust and the uppermost part of the mantle, and they move over the weaker, more fluid asthenosphere below. The movement of these plates is driven by convection currents in the mantle, which is caused by heat from the Earth's core.'

The visitor continues. 'There are three main types of plate boundaries: divergent, convergent, and transform. At divergent boundaries, plates move away from each other, creating new oceanic crust at mid-ocean ridges. At convergent boundaries, plates collide with each other, causing one plate to be forced beneath the other in a process called subduction. This can lead to the formation of volcanic arcs and mountain ranges. Transform boundaries occur where plates slide past each other horizontally, causing earthquakes but little volcanic activity.'

The visitor concludes. 'The movement of tectonic plates has shaped the Earth's surface over billions of years, creating and destroying oceans and continents. It is a gradual process, with plates moving at rates ranging from a few millimeters to several centimeters per year.
