2. ### 2. Geomagnetic reversals and plate tectonics

Plate tectonics is the process of large segments of the Earth's crust, called plates, moving slowly over time. The movement of these plates is caused by the convection currents in the Earth's mantle, which is the layer of hot, molten rock beneath the crust. These currents cause the plates to move in different directions, leading to the formation of mountains, earthquakes, and volcanic eruptions.

Geomagnetic reversals are changes in the Earth's magnetic field, where the north and south magnetic poles switch positions. These reversals have occurred many times throughout Earth's history, with the most recent one happening about 780,000 years ago. The cause of these reversals is not fully understood, but it is thought to be related to the movement of molten iron in the Earth's outer core.
