Convergent boundaries are where two plates are moving toward each other. They are one of the three types of boundaries, the others being divergent and transform. Convergent boundaries are also known as subduction zones, where oceanic lithosphere is being forced under continental lithosphere.

The Earth's lithosphere is broken into tectonic plates that include both continental and oceanic crust. Continental crust is less dense and thicker than oceanic crust, which is why it is always on top of oceanic crust. At convergent boundaries, the oceanic crust is forced down into the mantle by the continental crust, where it eventually becomes magma. This process is called subduction, and it is the primary way that the Earth's crust is recycled.

There are three types of convergent boundaries: oceanic-oceanic, oceanic-continental, and continental-continental. At oceanic-oceanic boundaries, two oceanic plates are moving toward each other. One plate is forced under the other, and a deep oceanic trench is formed. At oceanic-continental boundaries, an oceanic plate is forced under a continental plate.
