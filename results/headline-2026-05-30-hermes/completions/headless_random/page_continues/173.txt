#### 3.2.2  Continental drift

Continental drift is the hypothesis that the Earth's continents have moved over geologic time relative to each other, thus appearing to have "drifted" across the ocean bed. The idea of continental drift has been subsumed by the theory of plate tectonics, which explains how the continents move.

Abraham Ortelius, Theodor Christoph Lilienthal, Alexander von Humboldt, Antonio Snider-Pellegrini, and Alfred Russel Wallace were some of the earlier proponents of the idea of continental drift. The concept was independently and more fully developed by German geologist Alfred Wegener in 1912. Wegener's theory was that the continents had once formed a single landmass, called Pangaea, before breaking apart and drifting to their present locations.

The idea of continental drift did not become widely accepted until the 1950s, after the publication of findings by a group of geologists including Harry Hess, Bruce Heezen, and Robert S. Dietz.
