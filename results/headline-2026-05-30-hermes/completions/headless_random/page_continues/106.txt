Now, the next part of your question. The formation of continents.

Continents are formed by processes driven by plate tectonics. They are not formed by weathering, which is only a very superficial process that can contribute to the breakdown of a continent.

Oceanic crust is thinner and denser than continental crust, which is why oceanic crust gets subducted beneath the continents.

Continental crust is formed primarily at subduction zones, where oceanic crust is subducted and partially melted, producing andesitic to granitic melts that rise to the surface and cool to form continental crust.

Continental crust is also formed at rift zones, where continental crust is stretched and thinned, allowing basaltic material to rise from the mantle and form new continental crust.

Continental crust is also formed by the accretion of sediments derived from the erosion of existing continental crust. These sediments are deposited at continental margins and can be folded and uplifted to form new mountain ranges.

Continental crust is also formed by the collision of two continents, which can result in the formation of a new mountain range, such as the Himalayas.
