## 2. Describe the development of the theory of plate tectonics, including the role of the information discovered by Harry Hess.

### Continental Drift

The idea that continents were once connected and have moved away from one another over time dates back to 1596 when Abraham Ortelius made such a speculation in the preface to his atlas, Theatrum Orbis Terrarum. He suggested that the Americas were "torn away from Europe and Africa... by earthquakes and floods" and went on to say: "The vestiges of the rupture reveal themselves, if someone brings forward a map of the world and considers carefully the coasts of the three [continents]."

In the 1850s, geographer Antonio Snider-Pellegrini made the same observation and suggested that continents had once been connected during the Pennsylvanian Epoch. In 1912, German meteorologists Alfred Wegener proposed the first detailed theory of continental drift.

Wegener's theory was met with skepticism by many scientists.
