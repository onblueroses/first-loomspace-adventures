 "Plate tectonics was developed by scientists trying to explain the existence of continental drift, the apparent movement of the Earth's continents relative to each other. The idea of continental drift had been around since at least the early 20th century, but it was not until the 1960s that a scientific consensus was reached on the mechanism by which the continents moved. The theory of plate tectonics was developed by a number of scientists, including Harry Hess, Robert Dietz, and Dan McKenzie, among others.

The key piece of evidence that led to the development of plate tectonics was the discovery of magnetic anomalies on the seafloor. These anomalies are patterns of magnetization in the rocks of the ocean floor that are symmetric across the mid-ocean ridge. This pattern suggested that the rocks had formed at the ridge and then moved away from it, cooling and becoming magnetized as they went. This process, known as seafloor spreading, provided a mechanism for the movement of the continents.

The theory of plate tectonics has been supported by a wide range of evidence, including the distribution of earthquakes and volcanoes, the ages of rocks on the seafloor, and the way that the continents fit together like pieces of a puzzle.
