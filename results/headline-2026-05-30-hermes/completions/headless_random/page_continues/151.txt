The above map shows the Earth's principal tectonic plates. Their borders are the most geologically active, with earthquakes, volcanoes, and mountain-building. Note the "ring of fire" around the Pacific Ocean.

At the current time, the continents are in the process of separating from each other, and the Atlantic Ocean is widening, while the Pacific Ocean is narrowing. The Indian subcontinent is colliding with Asia, which is forming the Himalayas.

The above map shows the Earth's tectonic plates in the distant geologic past. The continents were once all joined together in a single supercontinent called Pangaea. The large ocean surrounding Pangaea was called Panthalassa.

Pangaea began to break up about 200 million years ago. The breakup resulted in the continents beginning to drift apart. This process continues today.

The above map shows the Earth's tectonic plates in the distant geologic future. In the future, the continents are predicted to come together again into a single supercontinent, just as they did in the past.
