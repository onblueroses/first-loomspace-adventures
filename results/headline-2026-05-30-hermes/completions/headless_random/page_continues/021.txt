## Key developments

### Continental drift

The discovery of radionuclides and their decay products in the late 19th century provided geologists with a tool to determine the age of various rocks and led to the eventual recognition of the Earth's antiquity. Based on these findings, during the early 20th century, geologists and paleogeographers began to hypothesize that the Earth's continents had been joined together at various points in the past. In 1912, Alfred Wegener proposed a theory of "continental drift" to describe these hypothesized movements.

Wegener's theory was initially rejected by most geologists, in part because the mechanism he proposed to drive continental motion seemed implausible. The prevailing view at the time was that the Earth's surface was too rigid to accommodate continental motion. Wegener's proposal that the continents had once been joined together also drew criticism from those who believed that the Earth's surface had been essentially unchanged since its formation.
