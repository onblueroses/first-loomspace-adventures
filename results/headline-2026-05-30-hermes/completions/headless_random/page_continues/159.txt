 

The actual motion of the lithospheric plates is a complex function of forces acting from within the mantle and from the interactions of the plates with one another. The relative importance of each of these factors is still an active area of research. The relative buoyancy of the plates at subduction zones and forces acting along the plates such as the slab pull force, the ridge push force, and the trench suction force are some of the forces that drive the plates. The details of the forces driving plate motion are discussed in a later section of this article.

The relative movement of the plates causes the formation of many geological features. These include mountains, volcanoes, trenches, and rift valleys. Plate boundaries are also the sites of most earthquakes. The relative movement of the plates also causes the formation of many mineral deposits. The study of plate tectonics is an important part of the study of geology.
