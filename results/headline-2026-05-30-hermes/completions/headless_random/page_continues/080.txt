Note that the time scale is millions of years, not thousands. But the principles are the same. Earth's crust is floating on the liquid core. The crust is broken into pieces, called plates. The plates float on the core and move. Where plates collide, you get mountain ranges. Where they separate, you get rift valleys and oceans. Where they slide past each other, you get earthquakes.

The Earth's crust is about 30 miles thick. The Earth's radius is about 4,000 miles. So the crust is less than 1% of the Earth's radius. The Earth's core is liquid, mainly molten iron. The crust is solid, mainly silicate rock. The crust is floating on the core.

Now, let's consider the Moon. The Moon's radius is about 1,000 miles. The Moon's crust is about 30 miles thick, so it is about 3% of the Moon's radius. The Moon's core is solid, mainly silicate rock. The Moon's crust is solid, mainly silicate rock. The Moon's crust is not floating on anything. It's just sitting there.
