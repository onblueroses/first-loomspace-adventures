The Earth's crust is broken into large pieces called tectonic plates. These plates fit together like a puzzle, but they're not fixed in place. They are moved around by the movement of a deeper part of the Earth (the mantle) that lies underneath the crust. These movements can cause earthquakes and volcanic eruptions.

Tectonic plates move very slowly. They can move at a rate of about one or two centimeters each year. This movement sometimes causes plates to collide, pull apart, or rub against each other. These movements can cause the Earth's crust to form large cracks called faults.

When plates collide, the crust can be forced upwards, forming mountains. When plates pull apart, the crust can crack, and magma can rise up and fill the cracks, forming new crust. When plates rub against each other, the crust can be worn down, forming valleys or trenches.
