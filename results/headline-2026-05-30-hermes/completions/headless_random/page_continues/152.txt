The large scale features of Earth's surface are a product of plate tectonics. The large scale motion of tectonic plates is responsible for the global distribution of mountain building, earthquakes, and volcanism. The relatively thin lithosphere is composed of the crust and the solid portion of the upper mantle. Below the lithosphere lies the asthenosphere, a relatively low-viscosity layer on which the lithosphere rides. The lithosphere is broken up into tectonic plates. The motion of these plates generates the large scale geological features of Earth's surface.

The Earth's structure is divided into layers. These layers are both physically and chemically different. The Earth has an outer solid layer called the crust, a highly viscous layer called the mantle, a liquid layer that is the outer part of the core, called the outer core, and a solid center called the inner core. The shape of the Earth is an oblate spheroid, because it is slightly flattened at the poles and bulging at the equator.
