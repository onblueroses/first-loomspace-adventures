## 3. What is a volcano? How are they formed?

A volcano is an opening in the Earth's crust that allows molten rock, gases, and debris to escape to the surface. Volcanoes vary in shape and size from small cracks in the Earth's crust to large mountains that have a base hundreds of kilometers in diameter.

Volcanoes are formed when magma (molten rock) rises to the surface of the Earth. This can happen in several ways:

1. When the pressure of the magma becomes greater than the pressure holding it in place (the overburden).
2. When the magma has dissolved enough gas (such as water vapor or carbon dioxide) to make it less dense than the surrounding rock.
3. When the magma is less dense than the surrounding rock because it has a higher temperature.
4. When the magma is less dense than the surrounding rock because it has a different composition.

Volcanoes can be found on the Earth's surface, under the sea, and even on other planets. They can be active (erupting), dormant (not currently erupting, but could erupt in the future), or extinct (not expected to erupt again).
