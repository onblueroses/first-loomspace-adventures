## 4. Describe the difference between P waves and S waves.

P waves are type of elastic wave, also called seismic waves, that can travel through gases (as sound waves), solids and liquids, including the Earth. P waves travel at speeds between 1 and 14 km per second, depending on the material they are traveling through. P waves can be generated by earthquakes and recorded by seismographs. The name P is an abbreviation for primary since P waves have the highest velocities and are the first to be recorded by a seismograph in the event of a quake.

S waves are type of elastic wave, also called seismic waves, that can travel through solids, including the Earth. S waves are slower than P waves and can only travel through solids, not liquids or gases. S waves can be generated by earthquakes and recorded by seismographs. The name S is an abbreviation for secondary since S waves have lower velocities and are recorded after the faster P waves in the event of a quake.
