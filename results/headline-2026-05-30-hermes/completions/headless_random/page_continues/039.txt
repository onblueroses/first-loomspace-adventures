# 4 - The Universe

The Universe is all of space and time and their contents, including planets, stars, galaxies, and all other forms of matter and energy. The Big Bang theory is the prevailing cosmological description of the development of the Universe. According to estimation of this theory, space and time emerged together 13.799±0.021 billion years ago and the energy and matter initially present have been transforming ever since. After an initial expansion phase, the Universe cooled sufficiently to allow the formation first of subatomic particles and later of atoms. Giant clouds of these primordial elements – mostly hydrogen, with some helium and lithium – later coalesced through gravity, forming early stars and galaxies, the descendants of which are visible today. Besides these ordinary particles, formed from energy within the first few minutes after the Big Bang, astronomers have also inferred the likely existence of dark matter, a type of matter that cannot be seen but appears to make up the majority of mass in most large galaxies. Observation of the three-dimensional distribution of galaxies and their motions suggests the majority of matter in the Universe is 'dark', meaning it cannot be seen in any part of the electromagnetic spectrum.
