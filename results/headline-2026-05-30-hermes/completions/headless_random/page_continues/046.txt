 Plate tectonics

In the early 20th century, a German meteorologist named Alfred Wegener proposed the idea of continental drift. He noticed that the coastlines of Africa and South America fit together like puzzle pieces, and he also observed similarities in the geology and fossils on both continents. Wegener suggested that the continents were once joined together in a single landmass called Pangaea, which later broke apart and drifted to their current positions.

However, Wegener's theory was initially rejected by the scientific community because he could not explain the mechanism behind the movement of the continents. It wasn't until the 1950s and 1960s that new evidence emerged to support the idea of continental drift.

One key piece of evidence was the discovery of the mid-ocean ridges, underwater mountain ranges that extend through all the world's oceans. Scientists found that the rocks near the ridges were younger than those farther away, suggesting that new oceanic crust was being formed at the ridges and pushing the older crust away.
