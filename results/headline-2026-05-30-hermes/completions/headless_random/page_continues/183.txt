### 2. The Formation of Mountains

Mountains are landforms that rise prominently above the surrounding area, generally having a defined peak or summit. They generally have steeper slopes than hills. Mountains exist on every continent and even under the ocean, where they are known as seamounts.

Mountains are formed through a variety of processes, including:

• Tectonic plate movements, which can cause the land to rise or fold.
• Erosion, which can carve away at the land, leaving behind mountainous features.
• Volcanic activity, which can create mountains through the accumulation of lava and ash.

The formation of mountains is a complex process that can involve a combination of these factors. For example, the Himalayas were formed by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates, which caused the land to fold and rise. The Rocky Mountains, on the other hand, were formed by a combination of tectonic plate movements and erosion.
