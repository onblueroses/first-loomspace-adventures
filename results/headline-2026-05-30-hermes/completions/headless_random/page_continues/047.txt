# 2. The Earth's continents seemed to fit together like a jigsaw puzzle

The observation that the Earth's continents seemed to fit together like a jigsaw puzzle was one of the key pieces of evidence that led to the development of the theory of plate tectonics. This observation was first made by the German meteorologist Alfred Wegener in the early 20th century.

Wegener noticed that the coastlines of Africa and South America seemed to fit together remarkably well, as if they had once been joined together and then drifted apart. He also observed that the rock formations and fossils found on the two continents were very similar, suggesting that they had once been part of a single landmass.

Wegener proposed that all of the Earth's continents had once been joined together in a single supercontinent, which he called Pangaea. He suggested that this supercontinent had broken apart and that the continents had drifted to their current positions over millions of years.
