## ESS1.B: Earth and the Solar System

The solar system consists of the sun and a collection of objects, including planets, their moons, and asteroids that are held in orbit around the sun by its gravitational pull on them. This model of the solar system can explain tides, eclipses of the sun and the moon, and other phenomena.

The Earth is the third planet from the sun in a system that includes the moon, the sun, eight other planets and their moons, and smaller objects, such as asteroids and comets. The sun, an average star, is the central and largest body in the solar system.

Gravity holds the solar system together. Gravity is the force that keeps planets in orbit around the sun and governs the motions of the solar system (Kepler's Laws). The motion and position of the Earth and moon affect phenomena on Earth such as tides, seasons, and phases of the moon.
