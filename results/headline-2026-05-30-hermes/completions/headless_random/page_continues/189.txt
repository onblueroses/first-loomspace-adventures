### Ygggdrasil

The idea of plate tectonics was first proposed in the early 20th century by German meteorologist Alfred Wegener, who noticed the similarity in the coastlines of eastern South America and western Africa and proposed that the continents had at one point been joined together into a single landmass he called Pangaea. Wegener's ideas were initially rejected by the scientific community, but evidence collected in the 1950s and 1960s, including the discovery of mid-ocean ridges, observations of seafloor spreading, and the recognition of patterns of geomagnetic reversals on the seafloor, eventually led to the acceptance of plate tectonics.

Much of the early evidence for plate tectonics came from studying the bathymetry of the ocean floor. Scientists discovered that the deepest parts of the ocean were long, narrow trenches, while the shallowest parts were long, narrow ridges.
