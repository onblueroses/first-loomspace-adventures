The theory of plate tectonics states that the Earth's surface is divided into rigid plates that move over a more fluid layer called the asthenosphere. The plates are made up of the Earth's crust and upper mantle, collectively known as the lithosphere. The lithosphere is broken into several large and small plates that are constantly moving and interacting with each other.

There are three main types of plate boundaries: convergent, divergent, and transform. At convergent boundaries, two plates collide and one plate is forced beneath the other in a process called subduction. This can lead to the formation of volcanic arcs and mountain ranges.
