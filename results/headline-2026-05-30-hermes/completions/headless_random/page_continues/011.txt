 

The relative motion of the plates causes crustal unrest at the plate boundaries. This unrest takes the form of earthquakes and volcanic eruptions. Large mountain ranges, such as the Himalayas, are also formed by the movement of tectonic plates. The movement of the plates also contributes to the formation of oceanic trenches, where one plate is subducted under another. 

The study of plate tectonics is an important part of geology, as it helps to explain the formation of many of the Earth's features. It also helps to explain the occurrence of natural disasters such as earthquakes and volcanic eruptions. The theory of plate tectonics is supported by a large body of evidence, including the distribution of earthquakes and volcanoes, the distribution of fossils, and the age of the oceanic crust. 

Despite the evidence, there are still some unanswered questions about plate tectonics. For example, the exact mechanism that drives the movement of the plates is not fully understood. Additionally, the timing of the formation of the plates and the rate of their movement is still a topic of debate among geologists.
