The theory of plate tectonics is now well-established. It explains several large-scale features of the Earth, like the Himalayas and the Pacific Ring of Fire. But what causes the plates to move? That is less clear.

The Earth's outer shell is divided into multiple plates that slowly glide over the mantle, the rocky inner layer above the core. The movement of these plates is caused by the convection of the mantle, the plates move in the direction of the mantle flow. The movement of the plates, and the activity inside the Earth is called plate tectonics.

Plate tectonics is the theory that explains the global distribution of geological phenomena. The plates are moved by convection of the mantle. The mantle is heated from below by the hot core, and in areas where it gets hotter, it rises, and where it cools, it sinks. This circular motion is called convection. The plates are moved by the convection of the mantle, and the movement of the plates is what causes the geological phenomena we see on the surface of the Earth.
