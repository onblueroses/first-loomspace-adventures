Continental drift is the movement of the Earth's continents relative to each other, thus appearing to "drift" across the ocean bed. The speculation that continents might have 'drifted' was first put forward by Abraham Ortelius in 1596. The concept was independently and more fully developed by Alfred Wegener in 1912, but his theory was rejected by many for lack of any motive mechanism. Arthur Holmes later proposed that plate movements were caused by convection currents in the mantle, but the idea did not become part of the accepted body of knowledge until the late 1950s, after the development of the theory of plate tectonics.
