The first scientist to suggest that continents moved around was Alfred Wegener in 1912. He noticed that the east coast of South America and the west coast of Africa fit together like a jigsaw puzzle, and he also noticed that there are a lot of geological and paleontological similarities between the two continents. He hypothesized that all of the continents were once joined together in a single land mass that he called Pangaea, but he couldn't figure out what could possibly cause the continents to move. He was ridiculed by other geologists and died in 1930 while on an expedition to Greenland.

In the 1950s, scientists discovered that there is a long chain of mountains running down the middle of the Atlantic Ocean. The chain is called the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. They also discovered that the floor of the Atlantic Ocean is spreading apart along the ridge. New ocean floor is being formed at the ridge and spreading out from it in both directions. This process is called sea-floor spreading.
