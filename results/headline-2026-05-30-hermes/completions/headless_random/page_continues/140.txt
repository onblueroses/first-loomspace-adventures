The Atlantic Ocean is growing wider by several centimeters per year, because of the sea-floor spreading along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge.

The Mid-Atlantic Ridge (MAR) is a large geological feature that is hidden beneath the Atlantic Ocean. This underwater mountain range is the longest and largest in the world. It extends from the Arctic Ocean all the way down to the southern tip of Africa. The MAR is a divergent boundary between tectonic plates. Divergent boundaries are where two plates are moving away from each other. This process is also called sea-floor spreading. The plates in the MAR are moving about as fast as your fingernails grow, which is about 2.5 centimeters (1 inch) per year. That may not seem like a lot, but over millions of years, it adds up.

The MAR is made up of many mountains and valleys. Some of the mountains are tall enough to poke out of the water and form islands. Iceland is one of these islands.
