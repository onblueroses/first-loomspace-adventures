The most important point to take from this is the concept of the "conveyor belt" principle. In that, the earth's crust is made up of several large plates, which are constantly being pushed out from the middle and sinking back into the mantle at the edges. This is how the earth's surface recycles itself.

The second point is that the earth's crust is made up of two types of plates: oceanic plates and continental plates. Oceanic plates are made of a heavier rock than continental plates, so when they collide, the oceanic plates sink down into the mantle and the continental plates ride up over the top.

Now, let's look at the Hawaiian islands.

The Hawaiian islands are a chain of volcanic islands that are located in the middle of the Pacific Ocean. They are located on the Pacific Plate, which is an oceanic plate.

The Hawaiian islands are formed by a hotspot in the mantle. A hotspot is a plume of hot mantle material that rises up from deep within the earth and melts the crust above it, creating a volcano.

As the Pacific Plate moves over the hotspot, the volcano that is created moves with it. Over time, this creates a chain of volcanic islands.
