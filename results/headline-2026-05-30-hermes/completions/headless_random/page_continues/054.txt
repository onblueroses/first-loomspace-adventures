The issue I have with this is that it is based on assumptions and the science that is used to prove it, is also based on assumptions.  It's like using a fairytale to prove a fairytale.  This is the best science we have at the moment but that doesn't mean it's correct.  It just means it's the best we have at the moment.  It's also not that old.  Continental drift was first proposed in 1912 by meteorologist Alfred Wegener and tectonics plates came about in the 1950's.  So it's not like this is a long standing, well established scientific theory.  It's actually quite new and is still being developed.

The reason I bring this up is because the science of tectonic plates is used to explain why the Earth's magnetic field changes.  The idea is that the Earth's magnetic field is generated by the motion of molten iron in the Earth's outer core.  This motion is thought to be driven by convection currents, which are in turn driven by the Earth's rotation and the heat generated by the decay of radioactive elements in the Earth's core.
