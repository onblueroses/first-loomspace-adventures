Geologists are very confident in the theory of plate tectonics, as it explains many geological phenomena very well, like volcanic activity, earthquakes, and mountain building. It also explains why the continents seem to fit together, like pieces of a jigsaw puzzle. But it has taken a long time for this theory to be accepted, because it was difficult to imagine how large parts of the Earth's surface could move around.

The theory of plate tectonics builds on the concept of continental drift, an idea developed during the first decades of the 20th century. The idea of continental drift was proposed by German meteorologist Alfred Wegener in 1912, but it was not widely accepted at the time. It was not until the 1950s and 1960s that new evidence from seafloor spreading and other studies led to the development of the theory of plate tectonics.
