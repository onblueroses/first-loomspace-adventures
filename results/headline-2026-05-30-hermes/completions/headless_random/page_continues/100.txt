The first suggestion that the Earth's continents might have 'drifted' was made by Abraham Ortelius in 1596. The concept was independently and more fully developed by Alfred Wegener in 1912. The hypothesis of continental drift did not become widely accepted until the 1950s, after the idea of seafloor spreading was proposed in the 1940s.

The Earth's crust is made up of a number of tectonic plates. These plates are constantly moving, and their movement is driven by convection currents in the mantle. The mantle is the layer of the Earth between the crust and the core, and it is made up of solid rock that is heated by the Earth's core. The heat from the core causes the mantle to convect, or circulate, and this circulation drives the movement of the tectonic plates.

The movement of the tectonic plates can cause a number of geological events, such as earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and the formation of mountains.
