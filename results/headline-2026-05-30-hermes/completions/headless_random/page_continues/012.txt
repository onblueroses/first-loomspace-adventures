The theory of continental drift was proposed by Frank Bursley Taylor in 1908 and expanded in 1912. The concept was independently and more fully developed by Alfred Wegener in 1912. However, it was not until the development of the theory of plate tectonics in the 1950s and 1960s that a sufficient geological explanation for the movement of continents was found.

In the early 20th century, exploration geologists began to find matching patterns of rock formations and fossils on opposite sides of the Atlantic Ocean. At the time, the prevailing theory was that the continents had been connected by land bridges that had sunk beneath the ocean. However, the discovery of radioactivity and the development of radiometric dating techniques in the early 20th century showed that the Earth was much older than previously thought, and that there had been plenty of time for the continents to move great distances.
