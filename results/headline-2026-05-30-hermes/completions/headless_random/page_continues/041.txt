The Earth's surface area remains roughly the same

The quote from the Wikipedia article on plate tectonics states that "By this process, the total surface area of the lithosphere remains roughly the same." This refers to the process of subduction, where one tectonic plate is pushed beneath another, recycling oceanic crust back into the mantle. The conveyor belt principle mentioned suggests that the creation of new crust at mid-ocean ridges is balanced by the destruction of crust at subduction zones, keeping the Earth's surface area approximately constant over geological time scales. The earlier theories of gradual shrinking or expansion of the Earth have been disproven. Therefore, based on the information provided, the correct answer is that the Earth's surface area remains roughly the same. The Earth is not significantly expanding or shrinking due to plate tectonics. The movement and recycling of tectonic plates keeps the overall surface area relatively constant, even as the positions and configurations of continents and oceans change over hundreds of millions of years.
