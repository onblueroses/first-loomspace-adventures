The formation of the continents and oceans of the Earth is the result of plate tectonics, which also cause earthquakes, volcanoes, tsunamis and other natural disasters. The lithosphere of the Earth is divided into a number of tectonic plates, which move and interact constantly, changing the surface of the Earth.

Continental drift is the movement of the Earth's continents relative to each other, thus appearing to "drift" across the ocean bed. The speculation that continents might have "drifted" was first put forward by Abraham Ortelius in 1596. The concept was independently and more fully developed by Alfred Wegener in 1912, but his theory was rejected by some for lack of a mechanism and others because of prior theoretical commitments. The idea of continental drift has been subsumed by the theory of plate tectonics, which explains how the continents move.
