There is evidence that the Earth's magnetic poles have reversed in the past. The last time this happened was about 780,000 years ago, in what is called the Brunhes-Matuyama reversal.

Here's a reference if you want to read more about this:

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunhes–Matuyama_reversal

The reversal process itself takes a few thousand years, so it isn't something that happens overnight. During the reversal process, the Earth's magnetic field becomes weaker and more disordered, but it does not disappear entirely. There's no evidence that magnetic field reversals cause mass extinctions or other catastrophes.

The magnetic poles also "wander" over time, which means they shift their positions. This is a different process from a full reversal of the poles.
