The Earth's crust, or lithosphere, comprises tectonic plates that move slowly over time. Plate tectonics is the scientific theory that explains this movement and its consequences. The theory builds on the concept of continental drift and was validated in the mid-20th century. The Earth's lithosphere is broken into seven or eight major and many minor plates. Their relative motion at the boundaries causes earthquakes, volcanic activity, mountain-building, and oceanic trench formation. The plates are composed of oceanic and continental lithosphere topped by their own kind of crust. Subduction at convergent boundaries recycles oceanic crust back into the mantle, maintaining the total surface area. Plate movement is driven by convection currents in the mantle, caused by lateral density variations. At seafloor spreading ridges, plates move away from the ridge, and the newly-formed crust cools and increases in density as it moves.
