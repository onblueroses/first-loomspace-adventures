#### 2.3.2 Sea-floor spreading

Sea-floor spreading is the process by which new oceanic crust is formed by the upwelling of magma through a long crack in the ocean floor and its lateral spreading. This occurs at divergent plate boundaries where two tectonic plates are moving away from each other. The rising magma pushes the older crust apart and solidifies as it cools to form new oceanic crust. The Mid-Atlantic Ridge is an example of where sea-floor spreading is happening today.

The process of sea-floor spreading helps to explain the concept of continental drift. Wegener's original theory of continental drift was rejected by many scientists because he could not explain how the continents could plow through the ocean floor. The idea of sea-floor spreading explains how the once-joined continents could have separated by the upwelling of magma at a divergent plate boundary.
