The Earth's structure is shown in the image below. The lithosphere, which includes the crust and the upper mantle, is the rigid, brittle outer layer of the Earth. The lithosphere is divided into tectonic plates, and it is the motion of these plates that causes earthquakes and creates volcanoes. The asthenosphere, which lies beneath the lithosphere, is a more ductile layer of the mantle. The asthenosphere is able to flow, and this allows the lithospheric plates to move around the surface of the Earth.
