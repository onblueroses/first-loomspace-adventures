 "The relative motion of the plates causes crustal unrest at the plate boundaries. This takes various forms. Where plates collide, the collision may result in mountain-building, such as the Andes, the Himalaya, and the Southern Alps of New Zealand. Where plates diverge, as at a mid-ocean ridge, new oceanic crust is formed by the cooling and solidifying of hot molten material. And where plates slide past one another, as at the San Andreas Fault or the Alpine Fault, there is crustal unrest as the plates alternately jam and slip past one another. The boundaries of plates are not fixed and the plates themselves can change in size. For example, the African Plate is currently in the process of splitting into two separate plates along the East African Rift Valley."

The article does not mention the scientific consensus that plate tectonics has been operating since about 3.4 billion years ago, and that the lithosphere comprises a number of large tectonic plates which have been slowly moving since that time. It also does not mention the validation of the seafloor spreading hypothesis in the late 1950s and early 1960s, which led to the general acceptance of plate tectonics by geoscientists.
