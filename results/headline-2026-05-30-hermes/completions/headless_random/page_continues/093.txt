#### 2.1.1 What is a tectonic plate?

A tectonic plate is a large slab of solid rock that comprises the Earth's lithosphere. The lithosphere is the rigid outermost shell of a planet, and is broken into tectonic plates. The Earth's lithosphere is composed of seven or eight major plates and many minor plates. The plates move relative to each other, typically at rates ranging from zero to 100 millimetres annually. Where the plates meet, their relative motion determines the type of plate boundary: convergent, divergent, or transform. Earthquakes, volcanic activity, mountain-building, and oceanic trench formation occur along these plate boundaries or faults.

#### 2.1.2 What is the lithosphere?

The lithosphere is the rigid outermost shell of a planet. On Earth, it comprises the crust and the portion of the upper mantle that behaves elastically on time scales of thousands of years or greater. The lithosphere is broken into tectonic plates, which move relative to each other.
