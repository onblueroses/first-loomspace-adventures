Plate tectonics is the scientific theory that the Earth's lithosphere comprises a number of large tectonic plates which have been slowly moving since about 3.4 billion years ago. The model builds on the concept of continental drift, an idea developed during the first decades of the 20th century. Plate tectonics came to be generally accepted by geoscientists after the proposal of seafloor spreading was validated in the late 1950s and early 1960s.

The lithosphere, which is the rigid outermost shell of a planet, is broken into tectonic plates. The Earth's lithosphere is composed of seven or eight major plates and many minor plates. Where the plates meet, their relative motion determines the type of plate boundary: convergent, divergent, or transform. The relative movement of the plates typically ranges from zero to 100 millimetres annually. Earthquakes, volcanic activity, mountain-building, and oceanic trench formation occur along these plate boundaries or faults.
