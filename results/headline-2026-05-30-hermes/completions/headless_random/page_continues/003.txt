From the above information, we can say that the movement of the tectonic plates is caused by the heat and convection patterns in the mantle. The heat makes the rocks of the lithosphere more elastic and they become less dense. It becomes easier for the plates to move around on the surface. The mantle's convection currents also cause the plates to move. These currents are like giant streams of lava flowing slowly in a circular motion. They can drag the plates above them in different directions.

The movement of the plates is a very slow process. The plates only move a few centimeters each year. However, over millions of years, the plates can move a long way. This movement can cause the plates to collide, pull apart, or slide past each other. These interactions can cause earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and the formation of mountains and ocean trenches.
