## Plate tectonics

Earthquakes, volcanic activity, mountain-building, and oceanic trench formation occur along plate boundaries. The lateral relative movement of the plates varies from 0 to 100 mm annually. Tectonic plates are made of the oceanic lithosphere and the thicker continental lithosphere, each topped by its own kind of crust. Along convergent boundaries, the process of subduction, or one plate moving under another, carries the edge of one plate down under another plate and into the mantle. This process reduces the total surface area (crust) on Earth, and the two plates being subducted "converge" or come together. By this process, oceanic crust and mantle lithosphere from the plate that is subducting, is being recycled back into the mantle. This process also contributes to the motion of the plates, as the oceanic lithosphere that is being subducted is "pulled" down into the mantle by gravity. This is called "slab pull".
