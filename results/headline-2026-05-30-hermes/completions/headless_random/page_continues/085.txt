### The Earth's Layers

The Earth is divided into layers based on chemical and physical properties. These layers are the crust, mantle, inner core and outer core. Many geologists believe that as the Earth cooled the heavier, denser materials sank to the center and the lighter materials rose to the top. Because of this, the crust is made of the lightest materials (rock- basalts and granites) and the core consists of heavy metals (nickel and iron).

The Earth's Crust is the thinnest and the outermost layer of the Earth. It is made up of two types of crust: continental and oceanic. Continental crust is typically about 35 miles thick, while oceanic crust is much thinner, only about 3-5 miles thick. The crust makes up only 1% of the Earth's volume.

The Earth's Mantle is the largest layer of the Earth. It lies between the Earth's crust and core, and makes up about 84% of the Earth's volume. The mantle is made of hot, dense iron and magnesium rich silicate rocks. The upper most part of the mantle is solid and, along with the crust, forms the lithosphere
