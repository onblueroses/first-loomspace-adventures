### 1.3.2 Convergent Boundary

In plate tectonics, a convergent boundary, also known as a destructive plate boundary, is a region of active deformation where two or more tectonic plates or fragments of the lithosphere near the end of their life cycle move toward each other and collide. As a result of pressure, friction, and plate material melting in the mantle, earthquakes and volcanoes are common near convergent boundaries. When two plates move toward each other, they form either a subduction zone or a continental collision.

This is one of the three types of plate boundaries, along with divergent and transform. Subduction zones are of two types: oceanic-oceanic and oceanic-continental. In the first case, both plates are oceanic, and one plate is subducted under the other. In the second case, one oceanic plate gets subducted under a continental plate. Continental collision occurs when two continental plates move toward each other.
