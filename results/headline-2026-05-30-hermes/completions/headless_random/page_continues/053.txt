The present is the key to the past. - James Hutton

In this case, we can assume that the processes that occur today occurred in the past as well. That means that the continents were moving in the past, and they move today. If we assume that the continents are rigid and cannot be deformed, it becomes clear that this motion could result in the formation of an ocean. It also means that two continents can collide to form a larger continent.

The plates are not randomly placed or moving. Ridges form where plates move away from each other. Subduction occurs when one plate goes beneath another. Thus the formation of the oceans is directly linked to the motion of the plates, and this motion is driven by forces occurring deep within Earth.

The process is slow, and difficult to observe directly. However, we can measure the motion of plates using GPS, and we can reconstruct past plate motions using magnetic data. We can also estimate the age of the oceanic crust using basic principles of heat transfer.
