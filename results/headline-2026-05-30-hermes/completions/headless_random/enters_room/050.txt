 The weather. The wind. A sense of being not quite indoors or out. A plain wooden floor, a few plants in pots, the sound of running water. Daylight pours in through the glass roof, shadowing leaves and branches, moving in unison with the wind outside. The sun moves across the sky and the shadows move with it, an ever-changing pattern of dark and light. A large shallow pool forms the centre of the room, filled with smooth river stones. The sound of running water comes from here. A thin sheet of water pours out from a pipe at one end of the pool and is immediately diverted into a narrow channel that runs the length of the pool, filling it. The water overflows the edges, is collected in a hidden gutter and recycled.

The sound of the water entering the pool, the sight of it rushing into the channel that leads it away, and the shadows of the branches and leaves above all draw the viewer towards the central space. The sound of the water and the pattern of moving shadows are both consequences of the world outside. The room is like a register of exterior conditions, the bright light of a sunny day, the shadows of leaves and branches moving in the wind, the flow of water.
