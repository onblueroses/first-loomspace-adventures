 The earth’s crust floats on a sea of molten rock and iron. Below the crust, the hot mantle currents move the plates floating on top. The plates are made of the earth's crust and the upper mantle, and they are always moving, bumping, and grinding against each other. Where the plates meet, you'll find volcanoes, earthquakes, and sometimes mountains. The plates are like giant puzzle pieces that fit together to form the earth's surface. They move very slowly, about as fast as your fingernails grow. Over millions of years, the plates can move a long way, changing the shape of the continents and oceans. The movement of the plates is called plate tectonics. Plate tectonics is the reason why the earth's surface looks the way it does today. It's also the reason why we have earthquakes and volcanoes. The plates are always moving, and when they collide or move apart, it can cause a lot of shaking and rumbling. But don't worry, the plates move very slowly, so you don't have to worry about the earth's surface changing overnight.
