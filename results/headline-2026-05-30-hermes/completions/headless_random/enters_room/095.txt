### Continental drift

The concept of continental drift was originally proposed by geophysicist and meteorologist Alfred Wegener in 1912. Wegener's theory was widely criticized by his peers, as he had no way to explain the movement of the continents. In 1947, Canadian geophysicist Reginald Aldworth Daly proposed a theory stating that Earth had a layer of liquid rock separating the crust from the mantle; this theory was later refined by geologist Arthur Holmes, who proposed that Earth's mantle contained convection currents which moved the crust at the surface.

### Seafloor spreading

In the late 1950s, geologist Harry Hess proposed the theory of seafloor spreading, which states that the seafloor is created at mid-oceanic ridges, where new oceanic crust is formed through volcanic activity and then gradually moves away from the ridge. Hess' theory was supported by several lines of evidence, including the discovery of alternating magnetic polarity in the seafloor, which suggested that the seafloor had been created over time.
