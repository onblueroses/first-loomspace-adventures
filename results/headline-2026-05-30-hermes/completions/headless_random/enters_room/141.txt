The subject of plate tectonics is now entering the room, bringing with it a fascinating understanding of the dynamic processes that shape the Earth's surface. This scientific theory, which has revolutionized our knowledge of the planet's geology, explains how the Earth's lithosphere is divided into a number of large, rigid plates that are constantly moving and interacting with one another. These interactions are responsible for many of the Earth's major geological features and phenomena, such as earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, mountain ranges, and deep ocean trenches.

At the heart of plate tectonics is the concept of continental drift, which suggests that the Earth's continents have been slowly moving across the planet's surface over millions of years. This idea was initially proposed in the early 20th century but was not widely accepted until the discovery of seafloor spreading in the 1950s and 1960s, which provided strong evidence for the movement of tectonic plates.
