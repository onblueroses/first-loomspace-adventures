## Historical context

The concept of plate tectonics builds on two earlier ideas: continental drift and seafloor spreading.

### Continental drift

Main article: Continental drift

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, geologists assumed that the Earth's major features were fixed, and that most geologic features such as basin development and mountain ranges could be explained by vertical crustal movement, described in what is now called the geosynclinal theory. Generally, this was placed in the context of a contracting planet Earth due to heat loss in the Earth's interior.

Alfred Wegener's 1929 reconstruction of the Earth's continents at different stages of continental drift. The first edition of Die Entstehung der Kontinente und Ozeane was published in 1915.

It was observed as early as 1596 that the opposite coasts of the Atlantic Ocean—or, more precisely, the edges of the continental shelves—have similar shapes and seem to have once fitted together.

Since that time many theories were proposed to explain this apparent complementarity, but the assumption of a solid Earth made these various proposals difficult to accept.
