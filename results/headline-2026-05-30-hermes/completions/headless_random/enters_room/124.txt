 

The theory of plate tectonics is now well-established. It explains the features and movement of Earth's surface, such as mid-ocean ridges, subduction zones, and trenches, as well as the formation of mountain ranges, earthquakes, and volcanoes. Plate tectonics also explains the distribution of fossils and geological formations around the globe, making it a unifying theory in geology.

The division of the Earth's interior into lithospheric and asthenospheric components is based on their mechanical and rheological properties. The lithosphere is cooler and more rigid, while the asthenosphere is hotter and mechanically weaker. The lithosphere includes the crust and uppermost mantle, while the asthenosphere is the portion of the mantle below the lithosphere.

In summary, plate tectonics is a scientific theory that describes the large-scale motion of Earth's lithosphere. This theory has provided a framework for understanding the formation and evolution of Earth's surface features and has become a foundational concept in the field of geology. The motion of tectonic plates is driven by convection currents in the mantle, and the interaction of these plates at their boundaries leads to various geological phenomena, such as earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and mountain building.
