 A question that arises from this is how do the continents move? The continents move because they are part of the tectonic plates that make up the Earth's lithosphere. The movement of these plates is caused by the convection currents in the underlying asthenosphere. The heat from the Earth's core causes the molten rock in the asthenosphere to rise, cool, and then sink, creating a circular motion. This circular motion causes the plates to move, which in turn causes the continents to move. The movement of the continents is a slow process, with plates moving at a rate of a few centimeters per year. Over millions of years, this slow movement can result in significant changes in the position of the continents. The movement of the continents can also cause the formation of mountains, earthquakes, and volcanoes, as the plates interact with each other at their boundaries. The study of plate tectonics has helped scientists understand the dynamic nature of the Earth's surface and the processes that have shaped it over billions of years.
