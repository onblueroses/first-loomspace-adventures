The fact that the Earth's surface is made up of tectonic plates that move around and interact with each other is the key to understanding why there are no mountains taller than Everest.

The Earth's crust is not a solid, unchanging mass. It is a dynamic system, constantly in motion, with plates moving around and interacting with each other. This motion is driven by the heat generated in the Earth's core, which causes convection currents in the mantle. These currents, in turn, cause the plates to move around on the surface of the Earth.

The plates are not all moving in the same direction, or at the same speed. Some plates are moving towards each other, some are moving away from each other, and some are sliding past each other. When two plates collide, one of them is usually forced down into the mantle, in a process called subduction. This is what is happening in the Himalayas, where the Indian plate is being forced under the Eurasian plate.
