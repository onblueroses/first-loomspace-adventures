However, there are still some questions about the exact nature of these currents and how they affect the movement of the plates. For example, it is not clear whether the mantle's convection is primarily driven by the sinking of dense material at subduction zones or by the upwelling of hot material at mid-ocean ridges.

The main unsolved question about the movement of the tectonic plates is the driving force behind the convection currents in the mantle. While it is generally accepted that these currents are responsible for the relative motion of the plates, the exact nature of the forces that drive the convection is still a topic of ongoing research and debate among geoscientists.

The answer is: convection currents

Convection currents in the mantle are the driving force behind the movement of tectonic plates. The convection currents are caused by the heat generated in the Earth's core, which causes the mantle material to rise and fall, creating a circular motion. This motion of the mantle material drags the overlying tectonic plates along with it, causing them to move relative to each other.
