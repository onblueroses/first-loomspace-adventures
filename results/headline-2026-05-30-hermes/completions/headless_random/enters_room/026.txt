The theory of plate tectonics is now well-established. The theory is based on a broad synthesis of geologic data spanning many decades. The theory is widely accepted because it is supported by a wide range of independent observations, including seafloor spreading and the distribution of earthquakes and volcanoes. The theory has revolutionized the Earth sciences, providing a framework for understanding many seemingly disparate phenomena. The theory has been used to explain the formation of mountains, the occurrence of earthquakes and volcanic eruptions, and the distribution of fossils and rock types. The theory has also been used to reconstruct the past positions of continents and ocean basins, and to predict future geological events. The theory of plate tectonics is a cornerstone of modern geology and is widely regarded as one of the most important scientific advances of the 20th century. (Hermes)
