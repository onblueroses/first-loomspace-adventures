The Earth's crust, or continental crust, is the outermost layer of the Earth, and is thinner than the mantle. It is divided into two types: oceanic crust and continental crust. Both types are less dense than the mantle, but the oceanic crust is denser than the continental crust. The oceanic crust is thinner and more dense than the continental crust because it is composed primarily of basaltic rocks, while the continental crust is composed primarily of granitic rocks.

The Earth's crust is constantly being created and destroyed through the process of plate tectonics. At mid-ocean ridges, new oceanic crust is formed through the process of seafloor spreading, where magma from the mantle rises up and solidifies to form new crust. At subduction zones, the oceanic crust is forced down into the mantle, where it melts and is recycled back into the mantle.
