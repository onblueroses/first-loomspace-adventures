The Earth's lithosphere is divided into several large plates, which move relative to each other on the surface of the Earth. These plates are composed of the crust and the uppermost part of the mantle, and they can be oceanic or continental. The movement of these plates is driven by convection currents in the Earth's mantle, which is the layer below the crust.

There are three types of plate boundaries: divergent, convergent, and transform. Divergent boundaries occur where two plates move away from each other, creating new oceanic crust in the process. Convergent boundaries occur where two plates collide, causing one plate to be subducted beneath the other. Transform boundaries occur where two plates slide past each other horizontally.
