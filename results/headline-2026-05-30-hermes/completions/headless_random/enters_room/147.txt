Well, they interact at their boundaries, and these interactions can be convergent, where plates move towards each other, divergent, where they move away from each other, or transform, where they slide past each other. And these interactions can cause earthquakes, volcanic activity, mountain building, and the formation of oceanic trenches.

But here's the really interesting part. The plates are composed of oceanic lithosphere and continental lithosphere, each topped by its own kind of crust. And at convergent boundaries, the process of subduction can occur, where one plate is forced down under another and the oceanic crust is recycled back into the mantle. This keeps the total surface area of the lithosphere roughly constant, like a conveyor belt.
