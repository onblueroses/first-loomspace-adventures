Tectonic plates move because of convection currents in the mantle beneath the Earth's crust. These currents are driven by heat from the Earth's core, causing hot mantle material to rise, cool, and then sink back down in a continuous cycle. This movement of the mantle drags the overlying tectonic plates along with it, causing them to move and interact at their boundaries, leading to phenomena such as earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and the formation of mountains and oceanic trenches.
