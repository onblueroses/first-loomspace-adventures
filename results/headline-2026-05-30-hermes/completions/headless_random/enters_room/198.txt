 This isn't a trick question. Look around and see what enters the room.

The answer is air. The air around us is constantly moving, and when a door or window is opened, fresh air can enter a room. Air is a mixture of gases, primarily nitrogen and oxygen, along with small amounts of other gases like carbon dioxide and water vapor. It may seem like nothing is entering the room, but in reality, air is always flowing in and out, even if we can't see it.

The air inside a room can become stale if it's not circulated properly. This is why it's important to have good ventilation in buildings, to allow fresh air to enter and circulate. Stale air can lead to a buildup of indoor pollutants, which can cause health problems over time.

The movement of air is driven by differences in pressure and temperature. When a door or window is opened, it allows higher pressure air from outside to flow into the lower pressure area inside the room. This is why you might feel a breeze coming through an open window on a windy day.

The air entering a room can also carry particles like dust, pollen, and other allergens. This is why air filters and purifiers are used in many buildings, to remove these particles from the air
