 The process of plate tectonics may be driven by convection in the Earth's mantle, or by gravity-induced motion of the lithosphere itself. There are two main types of forces that are thought to influence plate motion: friction and gravity. One force that drives plate motion is caused by friction occurring between the asthenosphere and lithosphere. The second force is gravity. Recent models indicate that tensional forces are probably the primary driving force of plate motion.

The main evidence for the motion of tectonic plates is contained in the magnetic orientations of basalt rocks on either side of a spreading ridge. When a volcanic ridge forms, the rock solidifies with its magnetic orientation determined by the Earth's magnetic field at that moment. When the rock splits and new crust is formed, the orientation of the new rock is again determined by the Earth's magnetic field. By examining the magnetic field of rocks on either side of the ridge, the pattern of magnetic field reversals can be seen to be nearly identical. This demonstrates that the rocks were formed by a process involving seafloor spreading.

The relative motion of tectonic plates causes earthquakes and volcanic eruptions. As the plates move, they interact with each other at their boundaries, which are associated with geological events such as earthquakes
