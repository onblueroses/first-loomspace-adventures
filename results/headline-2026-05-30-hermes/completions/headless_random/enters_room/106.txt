The Earth's crust is divided into several large and small solid plates that are constantly moving over the Earth's viscous, fluid mantle. The word "tectonic" comes from a Greek word meaning "to build," and the plates are called tectonic because they are constantly building mountains and altering the Earth's landscape.

The theory of plate tectonics was developed in the 1960s, and it revolutionized the field of geology. Before this theory was developed, geologists believed that the Earth's surface was static and unchanging. However, the theory of plate tectonics showed that the Earth's surface is actually dynamic and constantly changing.

The Earth's plates move at a rate of a few centimeters per year, which is about the same rate at which your fingernails grow. However, over millions of years, this slow movement can result in significant changes to the Earth's surface.

The movement of the Earth's plates is driven by the heat from the Earth's core. This heat causes the mantle to convect, or circulate, which in turn causes the plates to move.
