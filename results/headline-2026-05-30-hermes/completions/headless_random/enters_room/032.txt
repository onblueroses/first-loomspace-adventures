Yves: Hello, my curious friends! I'm here to guide you on an exciting journey through the fascinating world of plate tectonics. Imagine our Earth's surface as a giant jigsaw puzzle, with each piece representing a tectonic plate. These plates are constantly moving, albeit very slowly, and their interactions shape the world as we know it.

The theory of plate tectonics explains how the Earth's lithosphere is divided into several large and small plates that float on the semi-fluid asthenosphere. These plates move due to convection currents in the mantle beneath them. There are three main types of plate boundaries: convergent, divergent, and transform.

At convergent boundaries, two plates collide, causing one plate to subduct beneath the other. This process can lead to the formation of volcanic arcs and mountain ranges, such as the Andes in South America. Divergent boundaries occur where two plates move away from each other, allowing magma to rise and create new crust. The Mid-Atlantic Ridge is an example of a divergent boundary. Transform boundaries are where two plates slide past each other horizontally, like the San Andreas Fault in California
