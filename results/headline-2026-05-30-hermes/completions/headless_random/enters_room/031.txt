The movement of the plates is driven by a combination of slab pull, ridge push, and convection currents. Slab pull is the most significant driving force, and it occurs where cold, dense oceanic crust is subducted back into the mantle. Ridge push is caused by the gravitational force acting on the elevated mid-ocean ridges, and it occurs where plates move away from the ridge. Convection currents are the slow movement of Earth's solid mantle caused by the radioactive decay of elements in the mantle. Plate movement is a complex and ongoing process that is difficult to predict.

The scientific theory of plate tectonics has been extensively tested and is now widely accepted. It has revolutionized the Earth sciences, providing a framework for understanding the processes that have shaped our planet over billions of years. The theory has also been applied to other planetary bodies in our solar system, helping to explain their geological features and evolution.
