The actual vector of a plate's motion is a function of all the forces acting on the plate. One of these forces is the slab pull, the suction of the mantle wedge upon the overlying plate. Slab suction may occur in a geodynamic setting where basal tractions continue to act on a plate as it dives into the mantle (downwards in the mantle, however, two other forces take over: the ridge-push force and the slab-pull force). The ridge-push force is caused by the actual swelling mid-ocean ridges as they are pushed up by convection currents in the mantle. This pushes the lithosphere away from the ridge. At a subduction zone, the oceanic lithosphere sinks into the mantle and pulls the surface plate along behind it. The slab pull is caused by the weight of the dense, sinking plate as it pulls the rest of the plate behind it. The other forces acting on the plate include the slab suction force, which is the force exerted by the mantle convection currents as they attempt to pull the plate down into the mantle.
