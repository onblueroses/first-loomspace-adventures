 (2) Tectonic plates do not move on their own. They are pulled along by gravity as the plates subduct into the mantle. Subduction occurs at convergent boundaries, where one plate moves under another and sinks into the mantle, pulling the rest of the plate along with it. The process is driven by the density difference between the cold, dense subducting plate and the hot, less dense mantle material.

The motion of tectonic plates is a complex process that involves a variety of forces, including the pull of subducting plates, the push of newly formed plates at spreading ridges, and the drag of the underlying mantle. The relative importance of these forces is still a matter of debate among geoscientists.

The motion of tectonic plates has been responsible for the formation and breakup of continents, the creation of mountain ranges, and the occurrence of earthquakes and volcanic eruptions. Understanding plate tectonics has allowed geoscientists to better understand the Earth's history and the processes that shape our planet.
