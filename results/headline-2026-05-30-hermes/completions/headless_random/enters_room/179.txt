seafloor spreading

Seafloor spreading is a process that occurs at mid-ocean ridges, where new oceanic crust is formed through volcanic activity and then gradually moves away from the ridge. Seafloor spreading is a key component of plate tectonics, as it helps explain how the Earth's tectonic plates move and interact over time. The process of seafloor spreading was first proposed in the late 1950s and early 1960s, and its validation helped lead to the general acceptance of plate tectonics by geoscientists. The concept of seafloor spreading and the conveyor belt principle, which describes how oceanic crust is recycled back into the mantle at subduction zones, are central to our understanding of the dynamic nature of the Earth's surface. The movement of tectonic plates, driven by convection currents in the mantle, results in the formation of various geological features such as mountains, oceanic trenches, and volcanic activity along plate boundaries or faults.
