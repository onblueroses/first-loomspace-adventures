The Earth's crust is a thin shell on the outside of the Earth, accounting for less than 1% of Earth's volume. It is the top component of the lithosphere, a division of Earth's layers that includes the crust and the upper part of the mantle. The lithosphere is broken into tectonic plates whose motion and effects at plate boundaries cause many of the Earth's geological processes.

The crust is composed of a variety of igneous, metamorphic, and sedimentary rocks and is divided into three regions: the North American Craton, the Appalachian Orogen, and the North American Cordillera. The North American Craton is a large, stable region in the center of the continent that has not experienced significant deformation for over 1 billion years. The Appalachian Orogen is a region of ancient mountain building that extends from the eastern United States to the Atlantic coast of Canada. The North American Cordillera is a region of active mountain building that extends from Alaska to Mexico and includes the Rocky Mountains, the Sierra Nevada, and the Cascades.
