Wikipedia: Tectonic plates

Tectonic plates are large pieces of the Earth's crust and upper mantle that move and interact with each other, driven by convection currents in the mantle below. They can be composed of oceanic or continental crust and lithosphere. The movements of these plates cause many geological events, such as earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and the formation of mountains and ocean trenches.

The Earth's lithosphere is divided into several major and minor plates, which fit together like a jigsaw puzzle. The major plates include the North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Pacific, Indo-Australian, and Antarctic plates. There are also numerous smaller plates, such as the Caribbean, Nazca, and Scotia plates.

Plate boundaries are where two plates meet and interact. There are three main types of plate boundaries:

1. Divergent boundaries: These occur where two plates move away from each other. Magma rises from the mantle to fill the gap, creating new oceanic crust. An example is the Mid-Atlantic Ridge.
