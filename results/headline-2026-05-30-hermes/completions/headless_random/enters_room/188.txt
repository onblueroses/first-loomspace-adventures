The Earth's first atmosphere might have consisted of hydrogen and helium, the gases that still make up most of the atmosphere of the giant planets. This atmosphere would have been lost to space, because Earth's gravity is too weak to hold on to these light gases. A second atmosphere, consisting of volatiles that had been trapped within the planetesimals that formed Earth, would have formed after the loss of the first atmosphere. This second atmosphere probably consisted of water vapor, nitrogen, and carbon dioxide, similar to the composition of the atmospheres of Venus and Mars today. Most of the water vapor would have condensed to form the oceans, and much of the carbon dioxide would have been dissolved in the oceans or combined with minerals on the seafloor.
