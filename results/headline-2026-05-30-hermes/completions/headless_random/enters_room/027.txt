 

In the 20th century, scientists developed the theory of plate tectonics, which explains the movement of Earth's crust and the processes that shape its surface. The theory builds on earlier ideas, such as continental drift, and incorporates new evidence from the study of the ocean floor and the Earth's interior.

The key points of plate tectonics are:

1. The Earth's lithosphere is divided into several large plates that move slowly over the underlying asthenosphere.

2. There are three types of plate boundaries: convergent, divergent, and transform. Each type is associated with specific geological features and processes, such as earthquakes, volcanoes, and mountain building.

3. Plates are composed of oceanic and continental lithosphere, with the oceanic lithosphere being denser and thinner than the continental lithosphere.

4. At convergent boundaries, one plate can be subducted beneath another, leading to the recycling of oceanic crust back into the mantle.

5. The movement of plates is driven by convection currents in the mantle, which are caused by lateral density variations.
