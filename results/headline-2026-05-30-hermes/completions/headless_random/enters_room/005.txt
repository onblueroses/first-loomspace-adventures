 

- The Earth's lithosphere, which is the rigid outermost shell of a planet (including the crust and upper mantle), is broken up into tectonic plates.
- The scientific theory of plate tectonics describes the large-scale motion of the Earth's lithosphere.
- This theoretical model builds on the concept of continental drift, developed during the first decades of the 20th century.
- The Earth's lithosphere is broken into seven or eight major and many minor plates.
- The relative movement of the plates typically ranges from zero to 100 mm annually.

The theory of plate tectonics is now well accepted by geoscientists, after being validated by the proposal of seafloor spreading in the late 1950s and early 1960s. The theory has provided a comprehensive and accurate model for the large-scale motions and deformations of the Earth's surface. Plate tectonics explains the formation of mountains, earthquakes, volcanic activity, and oceanic trenches, as well as the distribution of fossils and geological formations. The acceptance of this theory has revolutionized the field of geology and has had significant implications for our understanding of the Earth's history and processes.
