The heat from radioactive processes within the planet's interior causes the plates to move, sometimes toward and sometimes away from each other. This movement is called plate tectonics.

The Earth's crust is broken into plates that move over the surface of the planet. This movement is driven by the heat from the Earth's interior. The plates are made up of the crust and the upper part of the mantle, which is the layer below the crust. The movement of the plates is caused by convection currents in the mantle. These currents are caused by the heat from the Earth's core.

The movement of the plates can cause earthquakes, volcanoes, and the formation of mountains. When two plates collide, one plate can be forced under the other, causing a subduction zone. This can lead to the formation of volcanoes and mountains. When two plates move away from each other, new crust is formed at the mid-ocean ridges. This process is called seafloor spreading.
