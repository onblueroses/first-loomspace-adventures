 [3] The concept of subduction is a key element in the theory of plate tectonics. Subduction is the process by which the ocean floor sinks beneath a deep-ocean trench and back into the mantle. The term was first used by R.L. Fisher in 1971, who used it to describe a process by which one plate is drawn down beneath another. Subduction is now recognized as the principal mechanism by which the Earth's lithosphere is recycled back into the mantle.

Subduction zones are regions of the Earth's crust where one tectonic plate is forced under another. These zones are characterized by deep oceanic trenches, volcanic arcs, and seismic activity. The process of subduction is driven by the relative density of the plates and the force of gravity. When one plate is denser than another, it will sink beneath the less dense plate, creating a subduction zone. The subducting plate is forced down into the mantle, where it melts and is recycled back into the Earth's interior.
