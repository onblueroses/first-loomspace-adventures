 The Earth's crust is what you walk on. It is the thinnest of all the layers, and it makes up less than 1% of the Earth's mass. The crust can be from 5 km to 70 km thick. There are two types of crust: continental and oceanic. Continental crust is the crust that makes up the Earth's land masses, like continents. Oceanic crust is the crust that makes up the ocean floor. Continental crust is thicker and less dense than oceanic crust. The crust is made up of different types of rocks: igneous, metamorphic, and sedimentary. The crust is also where we find important minerals and elements, like metals and fossil fuels. The crust is broken up into large pieces called tectonic plates. These plates move around on the Earth's surface, causing earthquakes and volcanic eruptions.

The Earth's mantle is the second layer of the Earth. It is the largest layer, making up about 84% of the Earth's volume. The mantle is mostly solid, but it can flow like a liquid over long periods of time. The mantle is divided into two parts: the upper mantle and the lower mantle. The upper mantle is made up of rigid rocks that make up the tectonic plates
