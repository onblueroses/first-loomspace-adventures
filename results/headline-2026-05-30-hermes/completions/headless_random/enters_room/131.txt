As the plates move, they carry the continents with them, resulting in the gradual drifting of the continents over geologic time. The interaction of the various plates also causes mountains to rise where they push together and causes subduction where one plate sinks under another. Subduction can result in volcanic activity when the subducting plate melts in the mantle.

The outer layers of the Earth are divided into the lithosphere and asthenosphere. The division is based on differences in mechanical properties and in the method for the transfer of heat. The lithosphere is cooler and more rigid, while the asthenosphere is hotter and flows more easily. In terms of heat transfer, the lithosphere loses heat by conduction, whereas the asthenosphere also transfers heat by convection and has a nearly adiabatic temperature gradient. This division should not be confused with the chemical subdivision of these same layers into the mantle and the crust, which is based on composition. The lithosphere is divided into tectonic plates, which are composed of the crust and the uppermost mantle.
