 The theory of plate tectonics has a long history. The earliest evidence for plate tectonics comes from the observation of the continents, which fit together like pieces of a puzzle. The idea that the continents were once connected was first proposed by Alfred Wegener in 1912. Wegener's theory of continental drift was not widely accepted until the 1950s, when new evidence from the ocean floor was discovered. The ocean floor is divided into plates that move around the Earth's surface. The plates are made up of the Earth's crust and upper mantle, which are collectively called the lithosphere. The lithosphere is divided into several large plates and many smaller ones. The plates move around the Earth's surface at a rate of a few centimeters per year. The movement of the plates is driven by the heat from the Earth's interior. The heat causes the mantle to convect, or circulate, which in turn causes the plates to move. The plates move in different directions and at different speeds. Some plates move towards each other, while others move away from each other.
