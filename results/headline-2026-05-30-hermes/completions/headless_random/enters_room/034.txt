The concept of subduction is central to the theory of plate tectonics. Subduction is the process by which the oceanic lithosphere, the crust and a portion of the upper mantle, sinks back into the mantle at ocean trenches. This process occurs because the oceanic lithosphere is significantly denser than the continental lithosphere due to its composition and the effects of cooling and thermal contraction.

Subduction is a key mechanism in the geological process of plate tectonics, helping to drive the movement of Earth's plates. It also plays a significant role in the formation of volcanic arcs and mountain building, as well as being a primary cause of earthquakes, particularly those that occur in the so-called "Ring of Fire" around the Pacific Ocean.

The process of subduction also leads to the recycling of oceanic crust back into the Earth's mantle, which is a fundamental aspect of the Earth's geological cycle. This recycling contributes to the Earth's ability to maintain a relatively constant surface area of lithosphere over geological time periods.
