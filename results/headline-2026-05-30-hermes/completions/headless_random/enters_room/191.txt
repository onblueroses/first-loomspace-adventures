The 1867 Manhattan earthquake struck Riley County, Kansas, on April 24. The strongest earthquake to originate in Kansas, it measured 5.1 on a seismic scale that is based on an isoseismal map or the event's felt area. The earthquake's epicenter was near the town of Manhattan.

On April 24, 1867, an earthquake estimated at 5.1 magnitude occurred at 20:22 UTC, or about 2:30 local time. The shaking began lightly at first but within a few seconds, it was strong enough to cause chimneys to fall from rooftops and some buildings to collapse. The earthquake was felt over an area of 193,000 square miles (500,000 km²) and was noticed in Iowa, Indiana, and Illinois, and the easternmost report of the earthquake was in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania.

The earthquake had its epicenter in Riley County, Kansas, near the city of Manhattan. The earthquake caused the most damage in the town of Manhattan, Kansas, where the shaking demolished two stone buildings and one large brick building under construction.
