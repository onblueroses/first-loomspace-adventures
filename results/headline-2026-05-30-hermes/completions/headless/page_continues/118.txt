## Interpretations

The Greek magical papyri reflects a syncretic worldview, in which the Headless One is identified with the Greek gods Osiris and Bes, the Jewish god Iao, and the Christian God. The papyrus itself is a syncretic mixture of Greek, Jewish, Babylonian, and Egyptian magical traditions.

The Headless One is described as both headless and as having his head in his feet, likely referencing the Egyptian god Bes, who was sometimes depicted as having his head situated on his feet. The title "Headless One" may also be a reference to the Egyptian god Osiris, who was beheaded by his brother Set.
