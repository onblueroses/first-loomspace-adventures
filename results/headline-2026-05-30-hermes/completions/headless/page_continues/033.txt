A few comments are in order. The “Seer” here is Rose Edith Crowley, and the “Master Therion” is Aleister Crowley himself, speaking in his capacity as Magus. The text is not an exact transcription of the original Stele of Jeu, but a reworked version, adapted for Crowley’s own purposes. The original invocation is not addressed to “the Headless One”, but to “the God who hath no face”. Crowley has substituted the phrase “the Headless One” to bring the text into alignment with the figure of Baphomet, whom he regarded as the embodiment of the Gnostic god Abraxas.

The phrase “I am the Headless Daimon with sight in his feet” is a reference to the figure of Baphomet, who is often depicted with a headless body and eyes in his feet. This image is derived from the Gnostic concept of the Demiurge, the creator of the material world, who is often depicted as a headless being with eyes in his feet.
