 “This shall be said whenever anything is to be done, in the Name of the Headless One, who is the Lord of the Aeon. And in each case, the proper perfumes shall be burnt, and the proper God-form shall be assumed.”

The following was printed on the back of the page: “The above invocation is very old, and is probably pre-Egyptian in origin. It was used by Moses, and is a great conjuration. It should be said slowly and solemnly, with a pause after each phrase. The “Headless One” is a pure Being of Spirit, without physical form, and the “Great One of the Night of Time” is Kether, the One Emanator.”

This would seem to be the Invocation of the Heart Girt with a Serpent which Crowley mentioned in his Confessions. The phrase “in the Name of the Headless One, who is the Lord of the Aeon” is significant, as Crowley would later identify himself with the god Aiwass, whom he called “the Headless One” in several places.
