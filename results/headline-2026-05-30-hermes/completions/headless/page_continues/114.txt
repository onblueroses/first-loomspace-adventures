 "I am He! the Bornless Spirit! having sight in the feet: Strong, and the Immortal Fire! I am He! the Truth! I am He! Who hates that evil should be wrought in the World! I am He, that lighteneth and thundereth. I am He, from whom is the Shower of the Life of Earth: I am He, whose mouth ever flameth: I am He, the Begetter and Manifester unto the Light: I am He, the Grace of the World: 'The Heart Girt with a Serpent' is my name!"

From these words it can be seen that the editor of the Stele of Jeu understood that "Heart Girt with a Serpent" is a name, and that he assumed the name is a description of the god being invoked. This interpretation has been widely accepted by later scholars.

## Crowley's interpretation

Aleister Crowley believed that "Heart Girt with a Serpent" was a name for the Holy Guardian Angel, the divine counterpart of every individual.
