It is taken from the Headless Rite, also known as the Bornless Rite, an invocation used by many magicians, including Aleister Crowley, who famously claimed to have successfully used it. The rite appears in the Greek Magical Papyri, which are collections of texts from Greco-Roman Egypt dating from the second century BC to the fifth century AD. They are a sort of handbook of magical spells and rituals, giving instructions for everything from divination to invoking deities to casting love spells. The Headless Rite is an exorcism, intended to drive away evil spirits.

The last line, which is supposed to be the magician’s name, is usually given as “heart girt with a serpent,” and is often identified with the god Aion. This is a Hellenized version of the Egyptian god known as “the aged one,” who represented the eternal renewal of time. The god Aion is often depicted as a lion-headed man with wings, with a serpent coiling around his body.
