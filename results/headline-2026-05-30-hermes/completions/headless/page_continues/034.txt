 “The above conjuration, in its original Greek, has been variously attributed to the neo-Platonist philosopher Iamblichus and to the Gnostic sect of the Sethians. It was later adapted into Latin and thence into English by Aleister Crowley in his collection of magical texts known as the Lesser Key of Solomon.” And below that: “The text itself is an invocation of a god referred to as ‘the Headless One,’ a figure whose power is so great that it cannot be named or even fully perceived by human beings. The invocation asks this god to grant the speaker control over all spirits, both in the heavens and on Earth, and to make them obedient to the speaker’s will.”

The page finishes with a note: “While the origin and purpose of this invocation are the subject of much debate among scholars, it is generally agreed that it reflects a belief in a supreme being who is both transcendent and immanent, existing beyond the physical world while also being present within it. The speaker’s identification with this being (‘I am the Headless Daimon,’ etc.) suggests a belief in the possibility of achieving union with the divine through the practice of magic.”
