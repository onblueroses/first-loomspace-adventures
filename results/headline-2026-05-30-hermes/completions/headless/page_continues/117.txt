 "Such are the words!" It is not said whether the magician should understand or be able to translate them. In fact, "mystery is the great emotion that they will evoke in those who hear them."

The prayer, printed in Greek, in the Papyrus, is as follows:

1. I call upon thee, Headless One, that didst create earth and heaven, that didst create night and day, that didst create the stars and the sun; thou, that didst create the four elements: fire, air, water, earth; thou, that didst create seed in men and sperm in women; thou, that didst make men to be loved and to be hated of other men.

2. I am Moses thy prophet to whom thou didst commit thy mysteries celebrated by Israel; thou didst reveal the moist and the dry and all nourishment.

3. Hear me: I am the messenger of Phrā ōh Abrasax; this is thy authentic name for the convocation of gods and men.

4. Hear me: Ar, Barqūakhār Sāgīsh, Ablakhār, Phnūkēntabāōth, Zaōr, Blakhānō
