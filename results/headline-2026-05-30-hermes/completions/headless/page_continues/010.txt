 “The above was found in the Papyrus of Nesi-Amsu, a scribe of the sanctuary who lived under the XXVIth dynasty. It was copied on a papyrus during the XVIIIth dynasty, but the exact date of the original composition is unknown.” [via]
The prayer is followed by a conjuration that seems to be a variant of the above, with the same general structure and many of the same elements, but also with a number of significant differences. This variant is given in Liber Samekh, point II, under the name “The Voice of Thy Power”. This variant is also the basis for the text on the Stele of Jeu.
The text of the prayer appears in a number of sources, including as Liber LXVI, Archidoxes 7:7, and in The Lesser Key of Solomon.
The prayer also appears in the works of Kenneth Grant, specifically Hecate’s Fountain (Skoob), p. 116, and Outside the Circles of Time (Muller), p. 287. In these works, Grant provides an interpretation of the prayer based on a “qabalistic” analysis of its structure and symbolism.
