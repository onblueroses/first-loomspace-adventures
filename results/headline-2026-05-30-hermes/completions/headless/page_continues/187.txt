 "This is He who has the Head of the Hawk. This is the first: who thinks it. This is He who has the Head of the Ibis. This is the second: who speaks it. This is He who has the Head of the Lion. This is the third: who does it. The Osiris is He who has the Head of the Hawk, the first: who thinks it. The Osiris is He who has the Head of the Ibis, the second: who speaks it. The Osiris is He who has the Head of the Lion, the third: who does it."

The page concludes with a repetition of the invocation of the "Headless One".

## The "Bornless Invocation"

Charles W. Goodwin, a noted Egyptologist, published a fragmentary Greek version of the "Headless One" invocation in 1852. He concluded that it was a Hermetic or Gnostic text. He observed that the name "Moses" could have been a later addition.

In 1895, London's Bodleian Library acquired a 17th-century Greek manuscript that contained a more complete version of the invocation. British scholar E. A. Wallis Budge published an English translation in his 1901 book Egyptian Magic
