 "This shall be said in the morning, before the rising of the Sun. Repeat it again in the evening, after the setting of the Sun, until the desired result be attained. It is a potent invocation."

It seems likely that the "desired result" is the invocation of the Headless One. It's interesting to note that the speaker, Moses, is identified as "the Angel of Aphrael Ablathanalba", and the invocation ends with "Heart Girt with a Serpent is my name". Aphrael Ablathanalba is an anagram for ΘΕΒΑΦΡΕΝΙΛΑΒ (Θεὸς Φρενὸς Ἀβλαβής), which means "God is pure reason".[3] This may be the name of the Headless One, or of the speaker. The phrase "Heart Girt with a Serpent" is a reference to Hadit, the speaker of the second chapter of The Book of the Law, who identifies himself as "the flame that burns in every heart of man, and in the core of every star", and "the secret serpent coiled about to spring".[4]
