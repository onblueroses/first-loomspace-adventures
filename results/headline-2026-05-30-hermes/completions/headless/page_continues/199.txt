 “This is the Lord of the Gods. This is the Lord of the Universe. This is He Whom the Winds fear. This is He Who, having made Voice by His commandment, is Lord of all things, King, Ruler and Helper. Hear Me and make all spirits subject unto Me: so that every spirit of the firmament and of the Ether, upon the Earth and under the Earth, on dry land and in the Water, of Whirling Air and of Rushing Fire, and every spell and scourge of God may be obedient unto Me.”

And there follows an injunction to “go forward with these spells” in some unstated quest.

As noted in the previous post, the prayer has similarities to the Stele of Jeu in the invocation of a “headless” deity with many names and epithets. Both prayers also share a motif of creation, with the invocation of opposites (light and darkness, male and female) and the elements. The two prayers diverge in their intent, however: the Stele of Jeu is a protective prayer, while the Headless One is meant to grant the speaker power over spirits.
