 “This invocation is said to have been used by Moses in parting the Red Sea. The original is in the British Museum, MS. Oriental No. 6360. It is in Greek, but is evidently a translation from the Hebrew. It is pronounced in a loud voice.”

Some of the words in this invocation are indeed Hebrew, such as Sabaoth (“hosts”), Abaoth (“father”), Aphrael (perhaps “power of God”), and Mosheh (Moses). Ablathanalba is a palindrome for “ABLANATHANALBA,” an incantation that appears in several magical texts from late antiquity, often accompanied by the word “ABRASAX.” Other words derive from Egyptian, such as Osoronnophris (Asar-un-Nefer, an epithet of Osiris meaning “Osiris the Beautiful Being”).

There are many magical words in this invocation, such as Ar, Thiao, Rheibet, Ateleberseth, Blatha, Abeu, Ebeu, Phi, Chitasoe, Ib, Arogogorobrao, Sochou, Modorio, and Phalarchao. These words may be “barbarous names” that have no meaning
