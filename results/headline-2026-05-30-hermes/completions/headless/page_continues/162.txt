 "This being said, the Headless One will appear. The charge is to say to the Headless One: 'I am the Headless Daimon with sight in my feet, strong, the immortal Fire.' Then say: 'I am the Truth that hates the deeds of injustice in the world.' Then say: 'I am the one whose mouth utterly flames.' Then say: 'I am the one who begets and destroys.' Then say: 'I am the Grace of the World.' Then say: 'Heart Girt with a Serpent is my name.'"

The invocation concludes with the exclamation "IAŌ SABAŌTH," after which the Headless One is supposed to appear. The charge is then to identify oneself with the Headless One, using a series of statements that describe the deity's attributes and powers.

This invocation is notable for its syncretic nature, combining elements of Greek, Egyptian, and Jewish magical traditions. The deity invoked, the Headless One, is likely derived from the Egyptian god Osiris, who was often depicted as headless in his role as the ruler of the underworld. The invocation also includes Jewish elements, such as the identification of the speaker as "Mosheh thy prophet" (Moses)
