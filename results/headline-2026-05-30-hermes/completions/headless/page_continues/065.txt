It ends: '...so that every spirit of the firmament and of the ether, upon the earth and under the earth, on dry land and in the water, of whirling air and of rushing fire, and every spell and scourge of God may be obedient unto me.' [Oates, GMP, p. 123]

It is interesting to note that the invocation refers to 'the ceremonies of Ischurael.' This suggests that the invocation may have been used by Jewish magicians, which would explain its presence in the Greek Magical Papyri. The invocation also refers to the 'Angel of Aphrael Ablathanalba,' which is a corruption of the Hebrew phrase 'Aphrael Ablathanalba,' meaning 'the Mighty One who possesses the immortal Fire.' This phrase is also found in the Jewish mystical text Sefer Yetzirah, which suggests that the invocation may have been influenced by Jewish mystical traditions. [Oates, GMP, p. 124]
