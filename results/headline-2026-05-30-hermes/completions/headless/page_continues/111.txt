## Interpretation

The spell is clearly an exorcism, intended to drive away a possessing spirit. The term "Headless One" (Greek ἀκέφαλος, akephalos) likely refers to the headless god Bes, who is depicted as headless or with two faces in some instances. This god is associated with protection and warding off evil, making him an appropriate figure to invoke in an exorcism. The text also mentions the god Osiris, known as "Osoronnophris" in the Greek magical papyri. This identification with Osiris, a god of resurrection and the afterlife, further emphasizes the protective and apotropaic nature of the spell.

The invocation also includes several magical words and phrases, such as "Ar, Thiao, Rheibet, Ateleberseth," which are likely intended to be powerful, esoteric terms that would compel the possessing spirit to obey the exorcist. The phrase "Heart girt with a serpent" may refer to the uraeus, a symbol of Egyptian royalty and divine authority, further emphasizing the power of the exorcist over the spirit.
