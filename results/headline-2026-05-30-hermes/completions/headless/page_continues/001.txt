 "This prayer, which is also used in the Greek and Coptic Orthodox Churches, is known as the 'Pantocrator' 67th Prayer. The Pantocrator style icon of Christ the All-Powerful still found in many Greek and Coptic Churches has its origins in the 6th century, and reflects the influences of the Egyptian Coptic Church. In this prayer, Christ is referred to as the 'Headless One,' which is a very ancient name that can be traced back to Egypt: 'Headless One' is a literal translation of the Egyptian word 'Neb-er-tcher' meaning 'Lord to the uttermost limit.' The Pantocrator icon, which is seen on the wall above the church altar, is meant to be an image of God the Father. This is why the icon is headless, as God the Father is without beginning, and therefore without head. The icon is also without hands, symbolizing that God brought the world into existence without hands, by his will alone. The image is frontal, that is, it looks directly at the worshipper, and its eyes follow the worshipper around the church. This is meant to remind the worshippers that God is all-seeing.
