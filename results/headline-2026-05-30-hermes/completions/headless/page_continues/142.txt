 "This is the Lord of the Gods.

This is the Lord of the Universe.

This is He whom the winds fear.

This is He, who having made voice by His commandment is Lord of all things, king, ruler and helper.

Hear me and make all spirits subject unto me, so that every spirit of the firmament and of the ether, upon the earth and under the earth, on dry land and in the water, of whirling air and of rushing fire, and every spell and scourge of God may be obedient unto me."

This invocation is one of the most powerful in Crowley's magical arsenal. Crowley himself used it to evoke the demon Choronzon, as recounted in The Vision and the Voice.

## Occult symbolism

The headless god is associated with the Rite of the Headless One, an ancient Greek spell that dates back to the Greco-Roman period of Egyptian history. The ritual itself is a palindrome and the rite is considered to be one of the most important and powerful of the ancient Hellenic magical papyri texts. The headless god is also associated with the Gnostic god Abraxas and with the concept of the Demiurge, the creator of the material world in Gnostic belief.
