 "This shall be said as addressed unto the Headless One, who is the Lord of the Air, with His eyes in His feet, and the immortal Fire in His heart; and in His hand the terrible blade of the Ether."

The papyrus, though it likely dates from the 4th century, appears to have been copied from an earlier text, for it contains many Coptic glosses to explain Greek terms. The invocation of the "Headless One" begins the spell, and the rest of the text is a series of conjurations against various maladies and a curse against a named individual. The invocation is to Osiris, also called Osoronnophris; the formula "the bornless one" refers to someone who was not born via natural means from a biological mother, but instead was "misbegotten" and created by magic. The "Headless One" may refer to the originally headless Osiris or his son Horus, who was magically conceived after the death of his father.
