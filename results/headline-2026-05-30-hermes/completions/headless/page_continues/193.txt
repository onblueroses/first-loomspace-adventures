 "The above conjuration, said to have been invented by 'Mosheh,' is found in Greek magical papyri. It calls upon the god of Israel, the 'Headless One,' and identifies the speaker with that god. This identification, which occurs in many of the magical papyri, is a common feature of magical practice. In Egyptian magic, for example, the magician commonly identifies himself with a god, taking on the god's divine power in order to command the gods and spirits to do his bidding. In this case, the magician identifies himself with the 'Headless One,' a term that probably refers to Osiris, who was decapitated by his brother Seth. The identification of the Jewish god with an Egyptian one is typical of the magical papyri, which often synthesize the gods and practices of several cultures."
