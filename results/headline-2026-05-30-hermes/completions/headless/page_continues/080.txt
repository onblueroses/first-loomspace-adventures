 "This invocation of the Headless One is an adaptation of the Greek magical papyri by Crowley, and is intended to establish authority over spirits." 

### The Headless One

The term "Headless One" or "Headless Daimon" is not clear in this context. It may refer to the eponymous deity of the invocation, or to the magician who is invoking him. Crowley notes that the magician identifies himself with the god they are invoking, and thus may be considered "headless" or "anonymously divine."

In a later commentary on the text, Crowley identified the "Headless One" with the Holy Guardian Angel, and connected the ritual with the Enochian system of John Dee.
