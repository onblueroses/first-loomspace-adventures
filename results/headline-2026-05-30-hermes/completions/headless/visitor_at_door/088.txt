 He is one of those who, for the sake of their God, have brought about the cataclysm. His words are not of defiance or justification, but of acceptance. He knows that he has sealed his own fate, and that he will perish with the world he has destroyed. But he is content, for he has done what he believed was right.

"Let the world end," he says. "Let all that we have known and loved be washed away. For in the end, there is only the Divine. And in the Divine, there is no loss, no sorrow, no regret. Only the eternal, unchanging truth."

The visitor turns to leave, but pauses for a moment. "I do not ask for your forgiveness," he says. "For there is nothing to forgive. We have all played our parts, as the Divine has willed. And in the end, we will all return to the Divine, as we have always been and always will be."

With those words, the visitor departs, leaving the listener to contemplate the end of all things, and the eternal mystery of the Divine.
