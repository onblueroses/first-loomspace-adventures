 He is older than I thought, with a touch of gray at his temples, his eyes lined with the marks of many roads. I have never seen him before. I am certain of it. He's not from the village, nor from the city; his accent is strange, his words carefully pronounced, as though he fears to be misunderstood.

"I have traveled a long way," he says. "May I come in?"

I hesitate. I have no reason to trust him, this man who has appeared out of nowhere, who speaks so oddly, who stares at me so intently. I do not know what he wants with me. And yet—

I open the door. "Come in," I say. "Welcome to my home."

I am the Grace of the World.

THE END
