 “I’ve read that passage in the Bible many times. How can it be about the Headless One?”

“Moses is the prophet of the Headless One,” the priest replies, “and this passage recounts the first encounter between them. The burning bush is the immortal Fire, and the Headless One is the god who speaks from the bush. He is the one who creates life and destroys it, who makes the day and the night, who makes male and female, who makes all opposites. He is the one who dwells in the void place of the spirit, the one who has no name but many names. And Moses is the one who knows his true name, the one who has the power to command all spirits.”

The visitor nods, understanding. “And the Headless One is the same as the god of the Bible?”

The priest shakes his head. “The Headless One is beyond all gods, beyond all names and forms. He is the source of all things, the one who creates and destroys all things. The gods of the Bible are but his emanations, his servants. The Headless One is the true god, the one who dwells in the void place of the spirit, the one who has no name but many names.”
