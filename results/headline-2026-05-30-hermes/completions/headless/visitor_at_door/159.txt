 He wears a trenchcoat and a hat that casts shadows upon his face. He speaks with a voice that is like the wind and like the thunder.

"Come in," I say, "and let me pour you some tea."

He enters, and takes a seat at the table. I pour him a cup of tea.

"I have come to offer you a warning," he says.

"I know," I say. "I have been expecting you."

"You are in great danger," he says. "You have attracted the attention of forces beyond your comprehension. They are coming for you."

"I know," I say. "But I am ready. I have prepared myself for this moment."

"You must leave this place," he says. "You must flee to the ends of the earth, and hide yourself in the deepest caves. You must never speak of what you have learned, or they will find you."

"I will not flee," I say. "I will stand my ground, and face whatever comes. I have work to do, and I will not be deterred."

"You are a fool," he says. "You do not understand the nature of the forces you have invoked. They will destroy you utterly."
