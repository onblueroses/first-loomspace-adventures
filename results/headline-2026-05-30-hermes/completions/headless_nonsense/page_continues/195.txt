 “This is the mystery of the letters which the Mrolthen One has placed in the holy mysteries, and which the prophet Vrondh revealed to the world, the mystery of the holy name, in which is contained all things of the cosmos.”

The text then continues with a number of prayers and hymns, some of which are obviously meant to be recited by a congregation or assembly. Most of them are of a similarly cryptic nature, although some contain references to other divine names and a few other interesting bits of information, such as the seven planets and the twelve signs of the zodiac. The final page is a full-page illustration, depicting a strange symbol surrounded by the text of the final hymn.

At this point, I should mention that the illustrations are one of the most striking features of the book. They are highly abstract, consisting of geometric shapes and lines, and seem to have little connection to the text they accompany. Some of them are in color, using a striking palette of deep reds, blues, and greens. They are not signed, but they are clearly the work of a skilled artist.
