 "Say this thrice, with the hands upon the heart, and the eyes fixed upon the stars. Then, when you have said it the third time, you will behold the spirits of the firmament, and of the ether, and of the earth, and of the air, in the form of dogs, and they will be obedient unto you in all that you shall command them. But if they appear not, then know that there is some error in the working, and that you must begin again from the beginning."

The last two lines in the invocation, "Mrok pheng wrol en Smelphrak" and "Mphō Krelphōth", are not part of the invocation proper, but are given separately and are to be used in case the spirits do not appear after the third repetition of the invocation.

The name of the god invoked in the invocation, "Smelphenor", is derived from the Greek word "smilē", meaning "a knife" or "a carving tool", and "phōnē", meaning "voice" or "sound".
