 “The first section of this invocation is taken from the so-called Mithras Liturgy, which forms part of the Great Magical Papyrus of Paris, written in Greek in the early fourth century A.D. It is in reality a liturgy of the Egyptian god Harpocrates, the son of Isis and Osiris. The second section is a translation of the invocation to the god Apep, the god of darkness and destruction, from the Egyptian Book of the Dead. The remaining sections are from the magical writings of Aleister Crowley, who claimed to have been the reincarnation of the Egyptian priest Ankh-f-n-Khonsu. The last line is from the Gnostic text known as the Bruce Codex. The combination of these texts is original with the author.”

The invocation ends on page 21 of the manuscript, the last page of chapter 1. The first page of chapter 2 begins with a quotation from a book called The Magus by Francis Barrett (1801). The quotation is not directly from Barrett’s book but from a modern edition edited by Timothy d’Arch Smith (1967).
