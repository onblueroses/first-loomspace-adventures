 “This is the most powerful prayer known. It is said to have the power to cure sickness and to drive away demons.”

Of course, I’d never heard of this prayer before, and I don’t know where the author of the book got it from. It could be a modern creation, or it could be from an obscure tradition. Either way, it’s an interesting piece.

I see elements of Greek, Egyptian, and Mesopotamian influence, or at least influences reminiscent of these cultures. The name “Smelphenor” could be a garbled form of the Greek “Salpiga”, meaning “trumpet”, or it could be a corruption of the Egyptian name “Smephra” (a pharaoh of the 24th Dynasty). “Plomphren Krempheniska” sounds like it could be Greek, though I can’t find any clear links to actual Greek words. “Mrok” is similar to the Mesopotamian deity name “Marduk”, and “Vrondh” is similar to the Mesopotamian deity name “Enki”.
