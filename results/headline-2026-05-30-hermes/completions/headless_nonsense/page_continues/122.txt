This translation is almost certainly wrong, and the original text probably doesn't say anything like this. But it's not hard to see how it got that way. The original text was written in Greek, but Greek to the Greeks of the time would have been more or less as intelligible as English to a modern English speaker. And it's far from uncommon for words to arise in English that would be nonsensical in Greek. For example, if a Greek word has a stem X and a suffix -Ｙ, then there may very well be an English word XⒶY that has no meaning in Greek, because there is no suffix -ⒶY in Greek. The English word may have arisen as a blend of Greek XⒶY and Greek XⒷY, say. (‘Petrify’ and ‘horrify’ come to mind as examples; ‘-fy’ is not a suffix in Greek, but it is in English.)
