 He speaks in an accent of the city, but not of any one neighborhood; and his speech is inflected with the accents of a distant land, and of many distant lands. He speaks with a voice that is at once deep and resonant, and also trembles with age.

"I am the one who has come from afar," he says, "and I have been traveling for a long time. I have come to your city to bring a message, and to deliver a gift. But I am not yet certain that I have come to the right place. May I enter your home, and speak with you, and learn if you are the one to whom I must deliver these things?"

The man who lives in the house looks at the visitor for a moment, and then he nods, and steps back, and allows the visitor to enter. He leads the visitor to a room that is dimly lit, but warm, and he bids him to sit, and he sits as well, and they look at one another.

"I have come from the East," says the visitor, "from beyond the edge of the world. I have traveled through many lands, and I have seen many things, and I have learned many things, and I have been changed by my journey.
