 Make it clear to my mind, that I may speak it, write it, and thus bind it to my will.

By the power of the names, and by the power of my blood, let it be so.”

He paused, then said, “I see a serpent, its skin as black as coal, its eyes like rubies. It winds around the circle, faster and faster, and then it vanishes.”

He paused again, and then said, “The serpent returns, and now it drags a man behind it, a man in black robes with a cowl over his face. The serpent deposits the man within the circle, and then vanishes once more.

“The man throws back his cowl, and I see that it is not a man at all, but a corpse, the skin shrunken against the skull, the eyes but empty sockets. It raises a skeletal hand and points at me, and then its jaw drops open, and it speaks.

“‘I am the shade of Archarzel,’ it says, ‘and I am bound by your circle. Ask of me what you will, and I will answer.’”

“‘Archarzel,’” the sorcerer said, “‘what is the secret of the philosopher’s stone?’”
