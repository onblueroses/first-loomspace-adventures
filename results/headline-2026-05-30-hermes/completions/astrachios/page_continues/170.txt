 “When you have repeated these words, you will see wonderful things, which it is impossible to relate, as was seen by Solomon in his arbour.”

In the Grand Grimoire, the magician is directed to perform the conjuration on the eve of the day he intends to use the Pentacle, and he is told to be careful to choose a place where he is not likely to be disturbed. A short prayer follows, which is supposed to be addressed to the Guardian Angel, after which comes the conjuration proper, which is much the same as the one already given, and then the dismissal, which is as follows:

“Ite in pace ad loca vestra et pax sit inter vos redituri ad mecum vos invocavero in nomine Domini Bathat vel Vachat super Abrac ruens abrae ben rora er Coriseo Novesie.”

This is translated: “Go in peace to your places, and may there be peace between us, and be prepared to come to me when I shall call you in the name of the Lord Bathat, or Vachat, who rules over Abrac, Abrac, Audin, Bator, Canech, Soulta, Voso, Marat, Anabai
