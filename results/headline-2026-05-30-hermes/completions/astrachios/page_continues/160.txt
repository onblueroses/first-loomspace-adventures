 'By these names, and in the name of the Eternal God, we may enter into the Ars Notaria and possess it in safety. These names have been placed here so that this book may be kept safe from all danger and from all evil spirits. Amen.'

A similar formula is found in the Secretum secretorum of pseudo-Aristotle, a text translated by John of Seville in the 12th century:

And know that he who wishes to perform this work ought to recite these names after the sun has entered Aries, ten times, and ten times have the names written on a parchment with the utmost reverence and devotion. And the names are these: Barach, Betha, Barachatha, Bragla, Beraia, Barachaiou, Barachaiou, Baruchata, Baruchata, Baruchata, Baruchata, Paian.

In both cases, the names of the spirits are used to protect the book and its owner from harm, and to ensure the success of the magical operations described therein.
