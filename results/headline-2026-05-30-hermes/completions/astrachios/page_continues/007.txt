 “And when you have said these names, you will see the figure of a king appear before you, who will say to you: ‘I am Gana, the king of the spirits, and I have appeared before you because you have called me with the proper names and have made the proper sacrifices. What is it that you desire of me? Tell me and I will grant it to you, and I swear by He who has created me and all the other spirits that if you call me again, I will come again and will bring you all that you ask for.’”

The author of the manuscript, a monk named Johannes Ctesibenus, goes on to say that this invocation should be spoken while standing within a magic circle, protected by the names of the holy angels. The circle should be inscribed with the names of the angels of the hours, the angels who govern the days of the week, and the angels of the four seasons.

The manuscript also provides a list of the seventy-two spirits that Gana rules over, along with their seals and the powers they possess. These spirits can be summoned and commanded by the magician, but only if the proper precautions are taken and the correct rituals are followed.
