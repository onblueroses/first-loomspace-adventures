 “And when you have said this conjuration, you will see wonderful things, and they will speak to you. And when they have spoken to you, and you have seen them, speak to them and say…”

This is followed by the second conjuration, which begins, “I conjure you, O Spirits, by the Seven Planets…” The second conjuration is considerably longer than the first, and the text is given in full in Chapter 11.

The manuscript ends with the statement, “And this must be said in a low voice, and not too loud, and they will appear, and they will speak to you. And when they have appeared, and when you have seen them, speak to them and say…”

There are a few things to note about this manuscript. First, it is written in a mixture of Latin and Italian, which was not uncommon for grimoires of this period. Second, the manuscript is incomplete, as it ends abruptly without providing the full text of the second conjuration or any instructions for dismissing the spirits. Third, the manuscript includes several names and words that are not found in any other known grimoires, which suggests that it may have been a unique creation or a heavily modified version of another work.
