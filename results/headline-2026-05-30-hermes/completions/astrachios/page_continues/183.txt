 "The circle being thus made, you may enter it with perfect confidence that the spirits you call will have no power over you." The magician is instructed to hold a wand of hazelwood in his right hand and wear a pentacle upon his breast. In the margin of the manuscript, there is a drawing of the hazel wand with the names "Tetragrammaton" and "Agla" written upon it. The magician is told to summon the spirit by its proper name three times and to always be polite to the spirits, for "they will be more willing to serve you if you are." The text goes on to give instructions for commanding spirits to appear and do the magician's bidding, and for the dismissal of the spirits.

## History

The Ars Notoria is the oldest portion of the Lesser of the Keys grimoire, dating back to the 13th century. However, because it was produced by a student, it is very likely that the material comes from a much earlier time. A 14th-century copy is known to exist, as does a 17th-century copy.
