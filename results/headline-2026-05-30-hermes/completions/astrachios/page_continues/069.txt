 “This is the end of the first part of the operation, which bears the title ‘The Preparation of the Operator.’ The second part, which contains all things that he must observe who would acquire the power of conversing with Spirits of all orders and of commanding them, is contained in the following chapters.” The manuscript breaks off abruptly at this point, and the second part is missing.

This Grimoire is closely connected with “True Black Magic,” and according to the Book of Black Magic and of Pacts, it is verbatim from the original save for a few unimportant variations. It is evidently a late production, as it contains the phrase, “the God whom thou dost adore,” which would indicate that it was intended for use by various persons who might not all be of the same religion. The names of the Spirits for each day of the week are those given by the “Lemegton,” and the hours for the invocation are practically the same as those laid down by the “Lesser Key of Solomon.”

The manuscript is undated, but probably belongs to the eighteenth century.
