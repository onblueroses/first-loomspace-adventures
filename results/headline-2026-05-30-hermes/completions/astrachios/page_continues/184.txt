 “These are the names which must be pronounced when you wish to conjure the spirits of Mercury, and those which follow must be recited in the same place two more times.”

Although the prayer to Uriel and the conjuration are written in different hands, they appear to follow each other in the manuscript, with the conjuration beginning immediately after the prayer ends.

The final two leaves, 93 and 94, are written in a different hand, in a different ink, and with a different style of pen than the rest of the manuscript. They are likely later additions.

Leaf 93 contains a conjuration of the Olympic spirit of the Sun, Och, which appears to be derived from the Arbatel of Magic, and a list of Olympic spirits with their associated celestial spheres, also derived from the Arbatel. Leaf 94 contains a list of the angels of the “shemhamphorash,” or the 72-letter name of God, with the names of their associated demons. This list appears to be derived from the Lesser Key of Solomon, a grimoire of uncertain date that likely attained its present form in the seventeenth century.

The manuscript ends with leaf 94. The final two leaves are missing a piece of the upper right corner
