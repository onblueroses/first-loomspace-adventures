The book ends with a "Conjuration for the King of the East," with the following instructions: "You must have a small staff of elderwood, and when you have the spirit ready, say to him, 'Go in peace wherever I shall command you to go, and serve me in all things which I shall order you to do.' And if he does not wish to obey, strike the altar thrice with the wand and say, 'In the name of Adonai, of Elohim, and of Tetragrammaton, I command you to seal this sacrament in this which I order you to do.'"

Although Waite was not averse to quoting lengthy passages from other works, he did not reproduce the above, perhaps because the publisher objected to the pages of esoteric names, or because he did not have the time to complete the translation, or because he felt the passages were unimportant.
