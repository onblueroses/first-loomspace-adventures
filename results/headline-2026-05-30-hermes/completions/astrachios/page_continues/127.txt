 "Once this has been said, you will see a great wonder."

The manuscript also includes instructions for creating a talisman. It requires a piece of parchment made from the skin of a newborn animal, which is then to be consecrated and inscribed with a diagram. The diagram is accompanied by the names of angels and magical symbols. The text then provides instructions for using the talisman to summon spirits, which involve reciting specific phrases and placing the talisman on one's head.

## History

The manuscript was likely written in the 18th century, but the text may have existed in some form since the early 16th century. The name of the grimoire likely refers to the Ars Notoria, a 13th-century magical text which also promised to grant the power of memory and learning.

The work is mentioned by the 19th-century French bibliographer Gabriel Peignot, who describes the manuscript in his 1842 work Dictionnaire raisonné de bibliologie.

A French version of the manuscript was published in the 1980s by Robert C. Girard, under the title Le Dragon Rouge, ou l'Art de commander les esprits célestes, aériens, terrestres, infernaux.
