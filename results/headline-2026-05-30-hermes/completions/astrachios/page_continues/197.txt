 “To cause the spirit to appear you must say: Agata, Agata, Samet, Samet, Agata, Agata, Samet, Samet. Which when you have done, you shall see the spirit ready to serve you.”

At this point, the reader may wonder if the author of the manuscript had been drinking or smoking some strange substances. But this kind of language is actually not unusual in the grimoires. Most of the words are names of spirits and angels, many of them well known in the literature. The opening phrase, “Astrachios, Asac, Acra,” is a palindrome found in many manuscripts. It is similar to the well-known SATOR square, a five-by-five palindrome that dates to at least the ruins of Pompeii:

SATOR
AREPO
TENET
OPERA
ROTAS

The SATOR square has various interpretations, but it is often considered an anagram for Pater Noster (Our Father), with an additional A and O, for Alpha and Omega.
