 “These are the names by which all things under heaven may be brought into subjection, and they are the names that open the way.” The text then lists a series of thirty-one “spirits” to be summoned.

The book contains spells, prayers, and talismans for summoning spirits, obtaining power and success, protection from enemies, and other purposes. It is written in a mixture of Latin, Greek, and Hebrew, with some words and passages that are difficult to decipher.

The origins of the Grimoire of Pope Honorius are uncertain, but it is believed to have been written in the 18th or 19th century, possibly in France or Italy. It is not an official document of the Catholic Church and has no connection to any Pope named Honorius. The name may have been used to lend an air of authority or legitimacy to the work.

The grimoire has been the subject of much controversy and speculation, with some claiming that it contains genuine magical secrets and others dismissing it as a hoax or a forgery. It has been widely circulated in occult circles and has influenced many later works of ceremonial magic.
