 “These are the names which, when pronounced with great care, give you the power to see the spirits which will appear when you call them. And when you see them, you will not be afraid, because the above-mentioned names give you the power to make them serve you and do what you wish.”

The rest of the page gives instructions for the preparation of a magical circle. The circle is to be made on a piece of kid-skin parchment, which is to be stretched over a piece of virgin card. The preparation of the card involves inscribing on it the Great Name of God, ‘which is not spoken’, and not very clearly specified in the manuscript. However, it seems to be a version of the well-known magical name AGLA, which is a Hebrew acronym for the phrase ‘Thou art mighty for ever, O Lord’. The name is to be written in a mixture of the blood of a black cock and the juice of the herb ver-vain. This herbal juice is often specified in old magical recipes, and the plant itself has been called ‘the enchanter’s plant’ because of its frequent use in magic.
