 “One way to think about this is that when you take a sample of size n from some population, you are effectively drawing n tickets at random without replacement from a box containing N tickets. (In this case, N would be the population of the US at the time of the survey.) The value written on the ticket is the answer the corresponding person would give if included in the sample. If you sample without replacement, the tickets are the same on every draw. But if you sample with replacement, the tickets are different on every draw because the composition of the box changes. Intuitively, this would lead to increased variability in the outcomes.

“However, the effect is very small when the sample size is small relative to the population size. For example, if you’re drawing a sample of size 1000 from a population of size 300 million, then the variability from sampling without replacement is about 99.7% of the variability from sampling with replacement.

“In practice, it’s common to ignore the distinction between sampling with and without replacement, and simply treat the sample as if it were taken with replacement.
