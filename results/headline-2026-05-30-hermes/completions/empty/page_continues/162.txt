 "The word "Dutch" is used because the Germans called themselves the Deutsche, and the English adapted it to "Dutch" because it was easier for them to say. The Germans actually called the Dutch the "Netherlanders," because they lived in the lowlands or the netherlands."

This is incorrect. "Dutch" is the English cognate of "Deutsch", and it was earlier used to refer to both the Dutch and the Germans. The Dutch people refer to themselves using the term "Nederlands", which is cognate with the English "Netherlanders". The English adapted "Nederlands" to "Dutch" in reference to the Dutch people because it was easier to say than "Netherlanders". The Germans were then referred to using the Latin term "German", which had been in use since Julius Caesar, in order to avoid confusion between the Dutch and the Germans. —Preceding unsigned comment added by 69.157.162.103 (talk) 03:56, 20 March 2008 (UTC)
