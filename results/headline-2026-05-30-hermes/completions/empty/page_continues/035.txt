 "A social media campaign has been launched with the hashtag #SaveTheChildren. Over 100,000 people have used it on Twitter and it's beginning to trend on Facebook."

The page also stated: "The movement is not associated with the real Save the Children charity."

The conspiracy theory has been linked to the broader QAnon conspiracy theory, with The Daily Dot stating that "while not all SaveOurChildren hashtag users are participating in the QAnon conspiracy, the hashtag is frequently used by QAnon supporters, with some claiming that President Donald Trump is working to dismantle a global child trafficking ring."

## Reactions

Save the Children, the charitable organization, has stated that they have been adversely affected by the conspiracy theories spread by the SaveOurChildren movement. The organization's executive director of public affairs and policy, Sarah Arnold, said that "while many people may have unintendedly shared a post or two, some are deliberately using the hashtag to spread misinformation and distort the truth." The organization has stated that it has been targeted by QAnon conspiracy theorists since the movement began in 2017.
