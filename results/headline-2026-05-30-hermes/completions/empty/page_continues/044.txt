 "The 'jitter' in the signal is related to the standard deviation of the histogram and is an indication of the short-term stability of the clock source. The 'wander' is related to the long-term stability of the clock source."

I am trying to understand the difference between the short-term and long-term stability of the clock source.  How can I distinguish between them?  What does it mean for a clock source to have good long-term stability but poor short-term stability, and vice versa?  Does the short-term stability refer to the variations in the signal due to the white noise?  And does the long-term stability refer to how the signal might be modulated over time by some low-frequency source?

Answer: Short-term stability is mostly affected by jitter, caused by phase noise. A common cause for this is supply noise or other noise sources that cause the zero-crossings of the clock to vary. In a PLL, the loop bandwidth will generally determine the amount of jitter in the output - a higher loop bandwidth will generally reject more jitter, but will also be less tolerant to the reference clock having jitter of its own. Short-term stability can be estimated by looking at the peak-to-peak period variation of the clock over a small number of cycles.
