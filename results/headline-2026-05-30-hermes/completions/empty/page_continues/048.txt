With the Revolution's success, the Church of England was legally established as the state religion in England, and was subjected to minimal interference by the Crown. England and Scotland were joined in a personal union under William and Mary, and the Catholic Church was suppressed in both kingdoms. The Revolution of 1688-89 destroyed the strong links between England and France, and replaced them with a new alliance between England and the Dutch Republic. This alliance lasted for a century, and its main objective was to contain the expansionist tendencies of Louis XIV of France. The Glorious Revolution created a precedent for the deposition of a legally established monarch, and provided a rationale for future parliaments to resist the policies of their monarchs.
