 "In the 1930s, the Soviet Union was undergoing rapid industrialization, and the government sought to attract foreign workers with technical expertise to help in this process. As part of this effort, they offered jobs and other incentives to workers from other countries, including the United States."

The first American specialists in the Soviet Union were invited in 1922 during the NEP period. During the First Five-Year Plan (1928-1932) the USSR made contracts with over 70 American companies in order to obtain access to American industrial technology and expertise. Hundreds of American engineers, foremen and workers came to the Soviet Union to help design and build factories and teach Soviet workers. 

The Soviet Union also recruited thousands of foreign workers, particularly from Europe and the United States, to help with industrialization. Many of these workers were motivated by the economic difficulties of the Great Depression, as well as a desire to participate in building socialism. 

However, by the mid-1930s, the political situation in the Soviet Union became increasingly repressive. Many foreign workers faced suspicion, persecution, and arrest during the Great Purge. Some were executed or died in the Gulag labor camps. 

The Great Depression was indeed a factor in the migration of Americans to the Soviet Union
