The war was the central event in the lives of most Americans during this period. For many men, military service was their defining experience. For others, supporting the war effort through their labor, voluntary service, or personal sacrifice was their principal contribution. The war effort was mobilized at every level of society, and the war's impact reached every community in the country.

The war also brought important changes on the home front. The mobilization of industry and the demands of the consumers dramatically changed the economy. The need for industrial labor brought new opportunities for women and African Americans. The emergency of war also brought important social changes, including the national prohibition of alcohol and the Nineteenth Amendment, which granted women the right to vote. Finally, the war raised expectations for reform and postwar reconstruction that would have a profound influence on the generation that came of age during the war.
