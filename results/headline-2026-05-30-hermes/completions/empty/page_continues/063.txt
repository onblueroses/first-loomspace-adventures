 "This means that the set of all possible states of a quantum system – the Hilbert space – is much larger than the set of all possible configurations of a classical system with the same number of degrees of freedom. This is the quantum superposition principle."

This statement is not completely wrong, but it's not the real point and it has nothing to do with the picture above. The superposition principle – which says that the space of possible states is a linear vector space i.e. a new state may be expressed as a linear combination of any basis of the Hilbert space – applies in any quantum system, including the "discrete" quantum systems such as the spin of an electron (or the whole atom).

The picture above has nothing to do with the superposition principle. The picture above shows that the space of "wave functions" (it's more accurate to say "states" but it's OK) is larger than the space of classical states. But this larger space isn't the Hilbert space; it's the phase space.
