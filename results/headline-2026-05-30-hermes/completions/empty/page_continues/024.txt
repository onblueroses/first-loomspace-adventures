 "It was the custom of the city of Lincoln to hang a Jew if he was found on the streets after nightfall. In 1255 many Jews came to Lincoln to attend a wedding and the party lasted after the curfew had begun. The bell of the Schola Judaica was rung, but two men did not return. The next day they were found murdered and their bodies were taken to the cathedral. The knight Sir John de Digby accused the Jews of ritual murder. Ninety-two Jews were taken to London, of whom eighteen were hanged for refusing to plead and the rest were pardoned through the influence of the friars (Liljegren, Richard of Bardney's Miraculi sancte Katerine de Lincoln). The martyrs were probably buried in London."

The affair was referred to in later English literature, for example, the Prioress' Tale in Geoffrey Chaucer's Canterbury Tales is based on the story of the Lincoln child, where Chaucer located the events in a city in Asia. The story also appears as "The Prioress' Tale" in the collection of stories known as The Canterbury Tales by Geoffrey Chaucer.
