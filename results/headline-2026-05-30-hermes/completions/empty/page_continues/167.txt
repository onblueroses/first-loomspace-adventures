 "Beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing but inwardly are ravenous wolves. You will know them by their fruits. Are grapes gathered from thorns, or figs from thistles? So, every sound tree bears good fruit, but the bad tree bears evil fruit. A sound tree cannot bear evil fruit, nor can a bad tree bear good fruit. Every tree that does not bear good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire. Thus you will know them by their fruits."

The metaphor can be found in the Sermon on the Mount, which appears in the Gospel of Matthew.

The phrase "grapes from thorns" (Greek: ἀπὸ τῶν ἀκανθῶν σῦκα) is similar to the Jewish expression "grapes from thorns" written within the Mishnah, which was used to refer to something that is impossible. It is most likely that the phrase was a common expression during the time of Christ, as it appears in both the New Testament and the Jewish works.

## Interpretation

This metaphor appears within Jesus' Sermon on the Mount, where he is discussing the difference between true and false prophets. True prophets will be known by their "good fruit," meaning their actions and the results
