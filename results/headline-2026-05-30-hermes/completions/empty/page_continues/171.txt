The restrictions will take effect on September 7, the day after the Jewish holiday of Rosh Hashanah ends, and could remain in place for the next few months.

On September 5, the coronavirus cabinet approved the Green Pass and other measures meant to reduce the spread of the virus. The ministers voted to limit all gatherings to 1,000 people in closed spaces and 5,000 in open areas. Gatherings that require a Green Pass and have assigned seating will be allowed one person per 50 square meters.

The Green Pass will be valid for six months for those who have received a third dose of the vaccine. For those who have not received a booster, the pass will be valid for only six months after receiving their second dose.

The plan also includes a “Happy Badge” for children, which will be valid for six months after they receive a negative PCR test.

In addition, the coronavirus cabinet voted to impose a “Purple Ribbon” on cities with high infection rates, which means limiting gatherings, closing schools, and reducing the number of people in workplaces.
