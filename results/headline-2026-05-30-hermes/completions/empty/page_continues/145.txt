While prejudice and stereotyping are similar, they are not identical. Stereotypes are oversimplified and generalized beliefs about a group of people. Prejudice refers to the attitudes and feelings—whether positive or negative and whether justified or unjustified—that people have about members of other groups.”

The final paragraph reads: “As we have seen, prejudice, stereotypes, and discrimination are all related, but they are not the same thing. While prejudice and stereotypes are attitudes and beliefs, discrimination involves action. When a person acts on their prejudicial attitudes toward a group or an individual, they are engaging in discrimination. For example, a person may hold prejudiced views about a certain race or gender, and then discriminate against members of that group by refusing to hire them for a job.”
