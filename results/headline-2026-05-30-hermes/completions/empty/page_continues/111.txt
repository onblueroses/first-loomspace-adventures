 "A new study, published in the journal Science, suggests that climate change is leading to more frequent and severe droughts in the Amazon rainforest, which could have devastating consequences for the region's biodiversity and ecosystem services."

The study, led by researchers at the University of Leeds in the UK, analyzed data from satellite images and field measurements to assess the impact of drought on the Amazon rainforest over the past two decades. The researchers found that the frequency and severity of droughts in the region have increased significantly, with the most severe droughts occurring in 2005, 2010, and 2015-2016.

According to the study, these droughts have led to widespread tree mortality, reduced growth rates, and changes in the composition of tree species in the affected areas. The researchers estimate that the 2005 drought alone caused the death of around 1.2 billion trees, equivalent to 1.6% of the total tree population in the Amazon.
