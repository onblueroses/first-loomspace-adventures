 He is a man of about fifty years of age, a little above the middle height, and inclined to be stout. His hair is grizzled, his eyes are gray and rather prominent, his nose is inclined to be red, the under lip of his mouth projects, and he carries a pair of nutcrackers in the way of a chin. He is dressed in a shabby-genteel way, and his manner is pompous and patronizing, but repressed by a certain timid air of having been found out and held under.

"Miss Bygrave," says this singular man, speaking in a deep voice, and pronouncing every word with the utmost distinctness: "I have had the honor of being your father's friend--and the acquaintance of your charming mother--for many years. I have recently returned from foreign parts. I had the pleasure of meeting you, and your accomplished brother, Mr. Bygrave, at the door. I have presumed to follow you home. I apologize for not having had the honor of calling on you, Miss Bygrave, at an earlier period. I have but this moment discovered your temporary residence.
