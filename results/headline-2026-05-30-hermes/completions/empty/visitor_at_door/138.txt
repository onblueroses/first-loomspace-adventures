 He is a neighbor and he has seen the master of the house arrive. He has come to inquire about his health. He is told that the master is indisposed and is resting, that he will not be able to see anyone. The visitor is insistent, he is concerned, he says. May he just look in, just for a moment, to satisfy himself that the master is all right. He is a very dear friend, he says. He has known the master since he was a child. There is nothing to be anxious about, the servant assures him. The master is quite well, just a little tired. He is resting now. The visitor will not be put off. He must see the master, if only for an instant. The servant tries to block his way. The visitor pushes past him and strides toward the bedroom door. He flings it open. The room is empty. The bed is undisturbed. He turns toward the servant, but the servant has gone.

The visitor rushes through the house, calling. The rooms are empty, the servants have vanished. He leaves the house, he wanders through the city. The city is empty. The crowds have gone, the streets are deserted, the houses are abandoned.
