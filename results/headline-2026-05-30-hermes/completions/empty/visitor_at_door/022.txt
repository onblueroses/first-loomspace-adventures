### Symbolism

The visitor represents the returning exiles who had been scattered during the Babylonian captivity. The house symbolizes Jerusalem or the land of Israel. The family represents the Israelites who remained in the land during the exile.

The act of the visitor knocking on the door represents the exiles seeking to return to their homeland and be accepted back into the community. The family's agreement to take the visitor back symbolizes the acceptance and reintegration of the returning exiles into the Israelite society.

### Interpretation

This parable is often interpreted as a message of hope and restoration for the exiles. It emphasizes God's willingness to welcome back those who have been separated from their homeland and their faith. The parable also highlights the importance of community and the need for acceptance and reconciliation among the Israelites.
