 It is a woman's voice, soft and low, with a foreign accent.

"I have come," she says, "to see Miss Mildred Dering."

"There is no such person here," replies the servant, stiffly. "This is the residence of Mr. Arnold Bannerworth."

"How very strange!" says the lady. "I was told that Miss Dering lived here."

"You have been misinformed," says the servant.

The lady pauses a moment, and then says, in the same soft, musical voice:

"Well, well, I must seek her elsewhere, that is all. Will you tell me how far it is to the village?"

"About a mile, ma'am."

"A mile, and I am so tired, and night is coming on. I am afraid I shall never find her, after all--and I have come so far."

"You had better go to the village," says the servant, "and seek for a night's lodging there. There is no one of the name you mention here."

The lady sighs deeply.

"Well," she says, "I must go on, I suppose. Good-night!"

The servant stands at the door until she sees the lady slowly walking down the avenue. Then she closes it, and goes at once
