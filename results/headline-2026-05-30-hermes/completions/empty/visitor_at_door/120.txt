He is dressed like an old-fashioned clergyman, and his speech, though soft and low, is that of a scholar. He tells us that he is from a distant country, and that he has come to visit the places where his forefathers dwelt. He is interested in the history of the family, and asks many questions about its various members, past and present. He looks at the portraits on the walls, and seems to recognize them. He asks about the old family Bible, and whether it is still preserved. He inquires about the family name, and whether it has been changed in the course of years. He asks about the family crest, and whether it is still used. He seems to know a great deal about the family, and his questions show that he is familiar with its history. He is a welcome visitor, and we are glad to answer his questions and to tell him all we know about the family. He is interested in everything we tell him, and seems to be pleased with what he hears.
