 "I am a friend. I know your father. May I come in?"

She hesitates.

"I have just come from your father," he urges.

That decides her. She opens the door.

"Enter," she says, simply.

He steps inside, and she closes the door behind him. He is a tall man, well-built, and looks like an Englishman. He has dark hair and eyes and a small, pointed beard. He takes off his hat and holds it in his hand. He is dressed in a rough, brown suit of tweed, and he wears it carelessly, as if clothes mean little to him.

"Your father is not here," he says. "He is in Paris. I have just left him. He is coming here to-morrow."

She looks at him with a little wonder in her eyes.

"You have come from my father?" she asks.

"Yes," he answers. "I have a message for you."

She is silent for a moment, and then she says, slowly:

"I do not know you."

"No," he answers, "you do not know me. But I know your father. I have known him for many years."
