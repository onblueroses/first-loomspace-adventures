“Four days,” I say, remembering the pills in my hand. I toss them into my mouth, then wash them down with the water, which is now lukewarm.
“Four days,” the visitor repeats. “That’s a long time to be alone.”
“Yes,” I say, “it is.” I close my eyes. My head is beginning to clear. When I open my eyes again, the visitor is still there.
“Do you want to know why you’re here?” the visitor asks.
I nod. “I’d like that very much.”
“You’re part of an experiment,” the visitor says. “A test of the human mind. We want to see how it reacts under certain conditions. It’s all very scientific.”
“I see,” I say, though I don’t really see at all. “And what have you learned from my time here?”
The visitor smiles. “Many things. About isolation, about the effects of certain stimuli on the brain. But mostly, we’ve learned about you.”
“Me?” I ask, surprised. “What could you possibly learn about me?”
