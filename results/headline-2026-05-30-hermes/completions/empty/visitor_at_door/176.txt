 'She's very pale. I thought she was going to faint.'

'She's better now,' says Mrs. Todgers, rising and taking a bottle from the cupboard. 'It's the weather, I have no doubt. This shortness of breath--'

'No, no,' interposes Miss Pecksniff. 'I am quite well, thank you. It is not that.'

Deftly administering some restorative drops from the contents of the bottle, the lady of the house resumes her seat.

'It's not the weather?' she says, looking with curiosity into Miss Pecksniff's face.

'No,' says Miss Pecksniff, 'it's not. I am not so weak as that. No. I should be better if I might venture to ask--'

'Ask anything you please, my dear,' says Mrs. Todgers, with her wonted amiability. 'And take a little more toast.'

'I couldn't eat a morsel,' returns Miss Pecksniff, with her hand before her eyes. 'I should be better if I might venture to ask why Mr. Jinkins left the office this morning, so very abruptly, and so soon after you came in.'
