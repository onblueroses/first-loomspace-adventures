 "Are you not pleased to see me?" "Yes," replies the other, "but I'm not pleased to see you here." "I am a friend," says the visitor. "And I am not your friend," says the other. The visitor asks, "Have I done something to offend you?" "No," the other answers, "you've done nothing to offend me." The visitor tries again: "Do you know me personally?" "Yes," says the other, "I know you personally." "Do you have something against me?" asks the visitor. "No, I have nothing against you."

## Analysis

This is a typical koan of the nanto (difficult to pass) type, involving a dialog between two individuals, and which seems at first reading to be somewhat irrational or paradoxical. The koan is a short story or statement that is designed to bring the student to a state of enlightenment, or to test the student's level of enlightenment. The koan is an important tool in the Rinzai school of Zen Buddhism, which emphasizes sudden enlightenment.
