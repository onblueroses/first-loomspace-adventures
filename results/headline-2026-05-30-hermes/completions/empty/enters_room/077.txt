 If we have a large room and the room is very far away from the source of sound, we can assume that the sound waves enter the room in plane waves. In this case, the sound field in the room is completely defined by the mode shapes of the room and their corresponding modal frequencies. As long as the frequency of the sound waves entering the room matches one of the modal frequencies, the mode shapes will be excited. The modal density increases with frequency, thus, we have more excited modes at higher frequencies. This results in a more diffuse field at high frequencies than at the low ones.
