 As they return to their seats, they should write down a word or phrase that captures the essence of what they think is entering the room.

Once everyone is seated, have participants share their words or phrases. Write them on a flip chart or whiteboard, clustering similar ideas together.

Reflect on the words and phrases, noticing any themes or patterns. Discuss what these might mean for the group or the work at hand.

This exercise helps surface the group's collective wisdom and intuition about the challenges and opportunities they face. It can be a powerful way to begin a meeting or workshop, setting a tone of openness and shared insight.

### 3. Appreciative Interviews

In pairs, have participants interview each other about a positive experience they've had related to the topic at hand. For example, if the meeting is about teamwork, ask them to share a story of a time when they experienced great teamwork.

The interviewer's job is to listen deeply, ask curious questions, and help draw out the richness of their partner's story. The interviewer should also take notes to capture key points.

After the interviews, each person introduces their partner to the group, sharing highlights from their story. As they do, capture common themes and insights on a flip chart.
