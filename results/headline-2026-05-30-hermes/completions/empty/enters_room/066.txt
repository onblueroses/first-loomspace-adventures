 A lightness, a breath, the wind of the soul. Or is it the soul itself? It suffuses the room, the bed, my body. I am afloat on the strangest calm. I am weightless. I am not. I am the breath that passes through me. I am not this body, not this mind or the sad thoughts it throws off like sparks, but something else, something less and more, something that is never sick and never well, that is pure and groundless, rootless and free.

What is the soul? Some say it has no existence, because it cannot be weighed or measured or compared. It is not a thing, but a happening, a verb, and thus it has no essence, but only a process that is its nature, its being, its meaning, its very self. And what is that process but a moving toward, a return, a journey to the center of all that is and was and shall be, world without end?

The soul is a wind, a flame, a river, a breath. It is the very air we breathe. It is life itself, and thus it cannot be lost, or saved, or even found, but only lived, here and now. And in the end
