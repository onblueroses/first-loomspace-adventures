| The room's perspective is not anthropomorphic. To see with the room's eyes is
to see not a single, stable picture but the jumbled and fragmented
impressions, the visual overload, of a Cubist painting. The room's eyes are
those of the mannequin, and what they see is the mannequin's reflection in
the mirror. The room's perspective is not that of a unified subject, a
"centered" subject, but rather a decentered subject, a subject whose point of
view is that of the Other, a subject whose vision is structured by the gaze. |
|  |
---|---|---
