  What’s this?
[He gazes at the figure of the veiled woman, which has entered
by the door in the background.]

The veiled woman (speaking in a hollow voice):
I am called the future.  I am veiled;
No eye can penetrate the folds that hide me.
Within them, mystery lies unrevealed;
I am the eternal riddle of the earth,
That never yet was solved by mortal man,
And which, unsolved, still mocks the wise and old
Amongst you, as it mocked your earliest childhood.

The poet:
I know thee well.  Oh, oft and long ago,
Even as a child, I guessed what lay behind thee,
And in my dreams I strove to raise thy veil,
Yet never could I lift it from thy face.
Then, too, I often saw thee in my dreams
In many a wondrous form, and yet the same;
And sometimes, too, in terror I beheld thee.
Yet never did I see thee as thou art,
Nor ever will, until I see thee not,
Until my eyes are closed in the long sleep
Which knows no waking, and which dreams no dreams.

The veiled woman:
