 And the first thing I think of is the fact that they are not in the room. There is a sense of absence, and I must be careful not to mistake the sense of absence for a presence. I wonder if this is how it began in the house on North Alfred Street. The story is that they had been there and had gone, but the feeling that they had gone was in fact the first sign of their arrival. Did it begin as a thought? Or as something more physical, a sensation at the back of the neck, a shiver? I remember feeling something like that from time to time in the apartment on West 89th Street, in the front hall, a distinct sensation that I was not alone, that there was someone or something there with me. I was certain, in those moments, that if I turned around and looked behind me, I would see a figure standing there. The figure was not necessarily malevolent. I didn't know who or what it was. But it was a presence, and it was very close to me, almost touching me, and if I turned around, I would see it. But I never turned around. I didn't dare.
