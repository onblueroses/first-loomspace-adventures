 It is hard to say whether it is a man or a woman. It is something in a dark cape, hooded, a shadow. But it is not Death. It carries a scythe but its hands are not bones. Its hands are small and pink and chubby like a child's.

The figure throws back its hood and reveals the face of a cherub, a little boy or girl, no older than six or seven. Its hair is white. Its eyes are black.

"Who are you?" I ask.

"I am the child of your nightmares," the figure says in a voice that is too deep, too old, too knowing. "I am the fear that keeps you awake at night. I am the dread that gnaws at your soul."

"What do you want from me?"

"I want to play a game," the child says, smiling a smile that is all wrong, all twisted and sharp. "I want to play a game of hide and seek."

I look around the room, at the shadows that seem to grow and stretch and reach. "And if I refuse?"

"Then I will take what I came for," the child says, "and you will never sleep again."
