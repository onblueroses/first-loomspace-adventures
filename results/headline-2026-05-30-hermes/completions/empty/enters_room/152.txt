 For the first time, the young man sees the old man’s eyes. They are bright blue, like a baby’s, like the sky, like marbles, like the sea, like a robin’s egg, like sapphires, like ice. The old man’s eyes are bright blue, and they are wet. He is crying. The old man is crying. The old man raises his hand to his face and wipes at his eyes with the back of his wrist. The young man feels his own eyes begin to sting. He feels the room tilt and blur. He closes his eyes. He breathes. He opens his eyes. The old man is still there. The old man is looking at him. The old man is waiting.

I’m sorry, the young man says. I’m so, so sorry.

The old man nods. He wipes at his eyes again. He says, I know.

The old man says, I know you are.

The old man says, I forgive you.
