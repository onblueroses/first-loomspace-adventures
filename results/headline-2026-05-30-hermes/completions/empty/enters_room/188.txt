 What for the first time. What the room has longed for. The room with all its flowers. Its vase. Its light from the window. What is welcomed now. What is embraced. What is almost not possible to believe. What makes the flowers. What makes the vase. What makes the light. What makes the room. What is not possible to believe. What enters. What for the first time. (Pessoa, 2001, p. 7)
The poem tells a story of something new, something that "for the first time" enters the room and brings about a transformation. The room is a metaphor for the mind, and what enters the room is a new idea, a new way of thinking that transforms the way we perceive reality. The repetition of "What" at the beginning of each sentence creates a sense of anticipation and emphasizes the importance of this new idea. The poem suggests that this new way of thinking is so revolutionary that it is "almost not possible to believe." The transformation brought about by this new idea is so profound that it changes everything, even the most basic elements of reality, such as the flowers, the vase, and the light from the window.
