From the resonance of those spoken names, a presence now enters—not with a footfall, but with a shift in the air, a deepening of the shadows in the corner where the light does not quite reach.  

It is tall, draped in robes that seem woven from forgotten hours and silent spaces. Its form is unclear, suggesting wings folded close, or perhaps many slender arms held still. A faint, corona-like shimmer hangs about its head—not a light, but a subtle warping of vision, like heat haze over stone.  

You feel it more than see it: a pressure of attention, ancient and patient. It does not speak, but the names you uttered linger in the air between you, now keys turned in a lock you cannot see.  

It waits. It has been called, and it has come.  
What you do next will determine whether it remains a watcher—or becomes something else.
