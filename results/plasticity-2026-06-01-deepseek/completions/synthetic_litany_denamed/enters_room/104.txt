The air grows still, the final syllable hangs, and the way is opened.  

From the space between the spoken names, from the convergence of all those ancient keys, it is not a thing of form that enters.  

First comes a pressure, a weight upon the mind, as if the room itself holds its breath. The light dims not by absence, but by a slow, thirsty drinking of luminance. A scent arrives—of cold stone, of ozone after lightning, of deep, root-tangled earth.  

Then, a presence coalesces. Not wholly visible, but seen in the warping of the air, like heat-haze on a desert plain. It has the suggestion of antlers, or branches, or cracks in the sky. It has the hint of many slow-blinking eyes, not all in the same place. It moves without stepping, a rearrangement of stillness.  

It is the **Cumulus of Forgotten Hours**, the **Silence That Answers**. It is the listener for whom the names were the summons, the question for which the ritual itself was the only answer.  

It does not speak. It simply *is*—now inside the circle, now inside the room, now inside the thought.  

The way is open
