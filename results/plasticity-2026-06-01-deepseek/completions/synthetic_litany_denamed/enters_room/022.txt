The air grows still, then stirs.  
A scent of ozone and cold stone rises.  
From the space between the spoken names, a presence coalesces—not with a shape, but with a weight, a silence that drinks the sound from the air.  

It is old, older than the words that called it.  
It does not have a name you could pronounce.  
You feel it in the marrow: a slow, patient attention now turned upon you.  
The way is open.  
Something has crossed.
