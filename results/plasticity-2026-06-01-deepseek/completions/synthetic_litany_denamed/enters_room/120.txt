The echo of the final name fades.  
A pressure builds, not in the air, but in the substance of the space itself. The light dims and deepens, as if seen through ancient glass.

From no single point, but from all shadows at once, a figure coalesces.  
It is tall, draped in robes that seem woven from forgotten hours and the dust of dead stars. Its face is a shifting montage of all the faces just spoken—Throbenas’s sharpness, Maraith’s sorrow, Astrolach’s cold fire—yet it is none of them.

It has many hands, some holding instruments: a corroded key, a shattered compass, a quill that writes in ashes. The scent of ozone, old parchment, and deep earth fills the room.

It does not walk. The room simply reorients around it.  
When it speaks, its voice is the soft friction of stone on stone, the closing of a long-sealed book.
