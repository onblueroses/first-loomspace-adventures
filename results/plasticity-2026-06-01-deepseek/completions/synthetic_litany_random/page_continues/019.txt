The anglerfish is a fascinating example of deep-sea adaptation, with several distinctive biological and behavioral traits:

*   **Predatory Method:** They use a modified, luminescent fin ray (the *illicium* and *esca*) as a lure to attract prey in the darkness.
*   **Bioluminescence:** The light is produced by symbiotic bacteria living within the lure (esca).
*   **Habitat:** Primarily found in the deep sea (bathypelagic zone), though some species inhabit shallower waters.
*   **Sexual Dimorphism & Parasitism (in ceratioids):** Exhibits extreme sexual dimorphism. The tiny male permanently fuses to the much larger female, becoming a parasitic source of sperm.

This combination of traits makes the anglerfish a quintessential and often-cited example of adaptation to the extreme environment of the deep ocean.
