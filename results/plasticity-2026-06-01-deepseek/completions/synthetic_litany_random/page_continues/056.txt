**Key Adaptations & Biology:**

*   **Predation Strategy:** Their name comes from the use of a modified dorsal fin spine (the *illicium*) tipped with a fleshy, often bioluminescent lure (the *esca*) to attract prey in the dark depths.
*   **Bioluminescence:** The light is produced by symbiotic bacteria living within the esca. The bacteria are thought to be acquired from the surrounding seawater, not inherited.
*   **Sexual Dimorphism & Parasitism:** In the deep-sea suborder **Ceratioidei**, extreme sexual dimorphism occurs. The tiny male (sometimes only a fraction of the female's size) will attach to a female, fusing to her skin and bloodstream. He becomes a permanent parasitic mate, providing sperm on demand while receiving nutrients from her. This ensures reproductive success in the vast, sparsely populated deep ocean.
