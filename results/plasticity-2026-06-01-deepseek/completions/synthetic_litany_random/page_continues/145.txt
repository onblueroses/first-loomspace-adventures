The anglerfish's unique adaptations for life in the deep sea have made it a subject of intense scientific interest and public fascination. Here are some additional details that often follow on such a page:

**Anatomy and Bioluminescence:**
*   **Illumination:** The bioluminescent lure, called an **esca**, can be wiggled to mimic prey. In some species, the light can even be pulsed or shut off.
*   **Teeth and Jaw:** They possess massive, hinged jaws and long, pointed teeth that are angled inward to prevent prey from escaping. Their stomachs can expand to allow them to swallow prey larger than themselves.
*   **Body:** Their bodies are often globular, soft, and flabby, lacking scales, which is an adaptation for the high-pressure depths. Their skin is typically dark-colored (black or brown) for camouflage.

**Habitat and Diversity:**
*   They are found in oceans worldwide, from shallow tropical waters to the immense pressures of the **bathypelagic (midnight) zone**, over 1,000 meters deep.
