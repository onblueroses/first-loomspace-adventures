This is an excellent summary of key anglerfish characteristics. Building on what you've provided, here are some additional fascinating details about these remarkable deep-sea predators:

**Anatomy & Hunting:**
*   **The "Fishing Rod":** The modified dorsal fin spine (the illicium) that bears the lure (the esca) can be remarkably long and flexible in some species, allowing them to dangle the bait precisely where prey might be.
*   **Massive Mouths:** Their jaws are enormous and lined with long, sharp, inward-pointing teeth. This design ensures that prey, once lured close and snapped up, cannot escape.
*   **Expandable Stomachs:** Their stomachs can stretch to accommodate prey much larger than themselves, a crucial adaptation in the food-scarce deep sea.

**Symbiotic Bioluminescence:**
*   The light produced by the bacterial symbionts in the esca is typically a blue-green color, which travels farthest in the deep ocean.
*   Some species can even control the light—flashing it on and off—to better mimic a tempting, struggling morsel.
