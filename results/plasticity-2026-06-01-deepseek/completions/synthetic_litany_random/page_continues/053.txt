This fascinating biology raises several additional points about anglerfish, particularly the deep-sea ceratioids:

**Physical Characteristics & Diversity:**
*   They exhibit extreme **sexual dimorphism**. Females are often much larger (sometimes 60 times longer and half a million times heavier) and possess the iconic bioluminescent lure (esca). Males are typically small, lack a functional lure, and have large eyes and nostrils to locate females.
*   Not all anglerfish live in the deep sea. The order Lophiiformes also includes shallow-water families like frogfish, batfish, and the well-known monkfish (or goosefish).

**The Parasitic Mating Process:**
*   When a male finds a female, he bites her and releases an enzyme that digests the skin of both his mouth and her body, fusing them at the tissue and circulatory system level.
*   The male then atrophies, losing his eyes and internal organs until he is essentially a pair of gonads attached to the female. A single female can host multiple males.
*   This ensures reproductive success in the vast, dark depths where encounters are rare.
