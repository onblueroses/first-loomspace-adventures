The text you've provided offers a clear overview of key characteristics of anglerfish. Here are some additional points that might follow on a typical encyclopedia page:

**Anatomy and Bioluminescence:**
*   The "fishing rod" is called the *illicium*, and the glowing lure at its end is the *esca*.
*   The bacteria that create the light are often species-specific, and the anglerfish can control the light by covering the esca or by manipulating blood flow to it.

**Diversity and Habitat:**
*   There are over 200 species of anglerfish across several families.
*   While famous for deep-sea (bathypelagic) life, some species, like the monkfish or goosefish, inhabit shallow coastal waters and are commercially fished.

**Reproduction (Ceratioid Deep-Sea Anglerfish):**
*   This parasitic mating is one of the most extreme examples of sexual dimorphism in the animal kingdom. The tiny male, upon finding a female, bites her and his mouth fuses to her skin.
*   His body atrophies until he is essentially a pair of gonads, connected to her circulatory system. A single female may host multiple males.
