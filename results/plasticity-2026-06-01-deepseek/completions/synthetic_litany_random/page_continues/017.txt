That's an excellent and accurate summary of anglerfish biology! It hits all the major points that make them so fascinating and unique. Here’s a bit more detail that often follows on a typical "page" about them:

**Expanded Details:**

*   **Diversity:** The order Lophiiformes includes a wide variety, from the deep-sea ceratioids you mentioned to shallow-water goosefish, monkfish, batfish, and frogfish. Not all have bioluminescent lures; frogfish, for instance, often have lures that mimic worms or small crustaceans.
*   **The Lure (Esca):** The bioluminescent lure is called an **esca**. It's at the tip of a modified dorsal spine called the **illicium**. The anglerfish can wiggle this rod to make the esca look like a struggling, tasty morsel.
*   **Predation:** They are ambush predators with enormous mouths and highly distensible stomachs, allowing them to swallow prey much larger than themselves. Their bodies are often flattened, and they have cryptic coloration to blend into the seafloor.
