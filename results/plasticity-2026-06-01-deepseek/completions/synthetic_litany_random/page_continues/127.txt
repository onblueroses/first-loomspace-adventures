**Anatomy and Adaptations**  
- Anglerfish typically have large heads with crescent-shaped mouths full of long, sharp teeth angled inward to prevent prey escape.  
- Their bodies are dorsoventrally flattened in many benthic species, while deep-sea ceratioid forms are often more globular or elongated.  
- Skin is loose and gelatinous in some deep-sea species, an adaptation to high pressure and low energy availability.  
- The “fishing rod” (illicium) is derived from the first dorsal fin spine; the bioluminescent lure (esca) varies in shape between species.  

**Hunting and Diet**  
- Anglerfish are sit-and-wait predators, often using cryptic coloration to blend into the seafloor.  
- They can swallow prey nearly as large as themselves due to highly expandable jaws and stomachs.  
- Prey includes fish, crustaceans, and cephalopods.
