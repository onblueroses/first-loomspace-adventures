*   **Anatomy and Lure (Esca):** The anglerfish's most famous feature is its illicium ("fishing rod"), a modified spine of the dorsal fin that extends over its mouth, tipped with a fleshy, bioluminescent esca ("lure"). The light is produced by symbiotic bacteria housed within the esca. Different species have evolved wildly diverse and complex lure structures to attract specific prey in the darkness.

*   **Predation:** They are ambush predators. Lying motionless or wiggling the lure, they attract curious fish and invertebrates. Their jaws are massive, and their mouths are lined with long, sharp, translucent teeth that point inward. This design ensures prey can be swallowed whole, even if it is larger than the anglerfish itself.

*   **Size and Sexual Dimorphism:** In the deep-sea ceratioid group, extreme sexual dimorphism exists. Females are large (some over a meter long), while males are tiny (sometimes only a few centimeters). The males lack a functional lure and a digestive system as adults. Their sole purpose is to find and fuse with a female.
