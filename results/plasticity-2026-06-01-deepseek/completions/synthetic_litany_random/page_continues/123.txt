The remarkable adaptation of the ceratioid anglerfish, where the male fuses to the female, is a specialized form of reproduction known as sexual parasitism. This ensures reproductive success in the vast, dark, and sparsely populated deep sea, where finding a mate is exceptionally difficult. Once attached, the male's circulatory system merges with the female's, and he degenerates into essentially a permanent, parasitic sperm-producing organ. A single female may carry multiple fused males.

Other notable features of anglerfish include:
*   **Bioluminescent Lure (Esca):** The glowing tip of their illicium (modified dorsal fin spine) is used to attract prey. The light is produced by symbiotic bacteria living within the esca.
*   **Ambush Predation:** They are lie-in-wait predators with huge mouths, expandable stomachs, and sharp teeth, capable of swallowing prey larger than themselves.
*   **Extreme Sexual Dimorphism:** In many deep-sea species, females are vastly larger than males—sometimes more than 60 times in length and half a million times in weight.
