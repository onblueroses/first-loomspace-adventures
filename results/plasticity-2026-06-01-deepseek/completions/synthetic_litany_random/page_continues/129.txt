The anglerfish’s unique adaptations make it one of the most fascinating examples of deep-sea evolution. Beyond the well-known bioluminescent lure, other notable features include:

- **Extreme Sexual Dimorphism**: In many deep-sea anglerfish species, males are tiny compared to females and lack a lure. Their sole purpose is to find and fuse with a female, after which they degenerate into a parasitic sperm-producing organ.
- **Bioluminescent Symbiosis**: The light is produced by bioluminescent bacteria living inside the anglerfish’s esca (the “lure”). The bacteria receive nutrients and shelter, while the anglerfish uses the light to attract prey.
- **Expansive Jaws and Stomachs**: Anglerfish can swallow prey larger than themselves due to highly flexible jaws and a distensible stomach—an advantage in the food-scarce deep sea.
- **Wide Depth Range**: While often associated with the deep sea (down to thousands of meters), some anglerfish species inhabit shallow coastal waters, including the well-known goosefish (or monkfish) caught for food.
