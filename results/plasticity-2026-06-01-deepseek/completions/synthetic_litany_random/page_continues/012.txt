The remarkable reproductive strategy of ceratioid anglerfish is one of the most extreme examples of sexual dimorphism and parasitism in the animal kingdom. Here is a more detailed account of their life cycle and adaptations:

**Sexual Parasitism:**
*   **Finding a Mate:** In the vast, dark depths, finding a mate is a significant challenge. The tiny males, which do not have a bioluminescent lure, possess extremely well-developed olfactory organs to detect the pheromones of a female.
*   **Attachment:** Upon finding a female, the male bites into her skin. His mouth fuses with her body, and his tissues and circulatory systems become integrated with hers. This process is facilitated by a shared immune system adaptation; these anglerfish lack certain pathways of adaptive immunity that would otherwise reject such a foreign graft.
*   **Permanent Symbiosis:** The male degenerates, losing his eyes and internal organs until he is essentially a pair of gonads attached to the female. A single female can host multiple parasitic males simultaneously, ensuring a reliable source of sperm.
