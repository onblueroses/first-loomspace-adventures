**Physical Diversity**  
While often depicted as deep-sea dwellers with bioluminescent lures, anglerfish vary widely in form. Shallow-water species, such as those in the family Lophiidae (monkfish or goosefish), lack glowing lures and may camouflage themselves on the seafloor.

**The “Fishing” Mechanism**  
The modified dorsal fin spine (illicium) tipped with the luminous esca acts like a fishing rod. Some species can wiggle the lure to mimic prey, effectively attracting smaller fish or crustaceans within striking distance of their large, upward-facing mouths.

**Symbiotic Bioluminescence**  
The light is produced by bioluminescent bacteria housed in the esca. The bacteria typically belong to the genus *Aliivibrio* (formerly *Vibrio*). The anglerfish provides nutrients and a safe environment; the bacteria provide light, a rare example of a mutualistic symbiosis between a vertebrate and free-living bacteria acquired from the environment.
