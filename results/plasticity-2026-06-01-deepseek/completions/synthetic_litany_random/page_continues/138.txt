The unique reproductive strategy of female ceratioid anglerfishes is one of the most extreme examples of sexual dimorphism and parasitism in the animal kingdom. Here’s a more detailed look at that process and other key adaptations:

**Sexual Parasitism in Ceratioids:**
- **Finding a Mate:** In the vast, dark deep sea, finding a mate is exceptionally difficult. The male ceratioid anglerfish has evolved an extremely keen sense of smell to detect the pheromones of a female.
- **Attachment:** Once a tiny male finds a much larger female, he bites into her skin. His mouth and jaw fuse with her tissue, and their circulatory systems connect.
- **Fusion and Dependency:** The male's body atrophies, losing his eyes and internal organs until he is essentially a pair of gonads attached to the female. He becomes a permanent, parasitic source of sperm.
- **Reproductive Efficiency:** A single female can carry multiple males, ensuring a ready supply of sperm whenever her eggs are ripe. This guarantees reproductive success in an environment where encounters are rare.
