**Anatomy and Adaptations:**
Anglerfish are renowned for their extreme sexual dimorphism, especially in the deep-sea ceratioid families. Females possess the iconic bioluminescent lure (esca) on a modified dorsal spine (illicium), which they wiggle to attract prey. Their mouths are enormous, capable of swallowing prey larger than themselves due to highly extensible jaws and stomachs. Their bodies are often globular and dark-colored, with rough, fleshy skin. Males, in contrast, are tiny, often lacking a functional lure, and in many species exist solely to find and fuse with a female.

**Habitat and Distribution:**
While most famous as deep-sea denizens (found down to 2,000 meters or more), anglerfish inhabit a range of marine environments. Some families, like the Lophiidae (goosefish or monkfish), live in shallower coastal waters on sandy or muddy bottoms. Deep-sea species are found globally in the bathypelagic and abyssopelagic zones.
