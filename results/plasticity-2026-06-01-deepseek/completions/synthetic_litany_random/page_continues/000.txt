### **Physical Adaptations & Hunting**
*   **Bioluminescent Lure (Esca):** The glowing tip of the illicium (a modified spine from the first dorsal fin) is used to attract prey in the darkness. The light is produced by bioluminescent bacteria living inside the esca in a symbiotic relationship.
*   **Camouflage & Ambush:** Their bodies are often dark, mottled, and lack scales, helping them blend into the seafloor. They are lie-in-wait predators with huge mouths and expandable stomachs, allowing them to swallow prey larger than themselves.

### **Reproduction & Sexual Dimorphism**
*   **Extreme Sexual Dimorphism:** In the deep-sea ceratioid group, this is most extreme. Females are large, while males are tiny and often look completely different.
*   **Sexual Parasitism:** In many deep-sea species, the male permanently attaches to the female's body by biting her. His tissues fuse with hers, and he becomes a parasitic source of sperm. A single female may host multiple males.
