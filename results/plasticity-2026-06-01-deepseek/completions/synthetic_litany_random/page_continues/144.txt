The page continues with more details about anglerfish anatomy, behavior, and diversity. Here are some additional points that are typically covered:

**Anatomy and Bioluminescence:**
*   The angling apparatus (the "fishing rod") is actually the first spine of the dorsal fin, which has moved to the top of the head.
*   The glowing lure is called an **esca**. In many species, the bacteria that produce the light are unique and specific to that anglerfish species.
*   Some anglerfish can control the light, flashing it on and off to better attract prey.

**Reproduction (Ceratioid Deep-Sea Anglers):**
*   This is one of the most extreme examples of sexual dimorphism in the animal kingdom. Females can be 10 to 60 times longer and up to half a million times heavier than males.
*   A male, upon finding a female, will bite into her skin. His mouth and body fuse with her tissue, and their circulatory systems connect. He becomes a **"sexual parasite"** or **"male permanent attachment."**
*   A female can host multiple males simultaneously, providing a constant, on-demand source of sperm for when her eggs are ready.
