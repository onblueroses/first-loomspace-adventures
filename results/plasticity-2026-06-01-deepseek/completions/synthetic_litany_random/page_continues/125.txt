### **Anatomy and Adaptations**
*   **Bioluminescent Lure (Esca):** The "fishing rod" (illicium) is a modified spine from the dorsal fin. Its tip (the esca) glows due to bioluminescent bacteria. Different species have uniquely shaped escas to mimic specific prey like worms or small crustaceans.
*   **Camouflage and Ambush:** Their bodies are often dark, mottled, and lack scales, resembling the seafloor or sponges. They are lie-in-wait predators with huge, upward-facing mouths and expandable stomachs to swallow prey larger than themselves.
*   **Extreme Pressure Adaptations:** Deep-sea species have soft, gelatinous bodies and weak skeletons to withstand immense pressure. Their eyes are often small, relying more on the lure and lateral line sensing.

### **Reproduction and Lifecycle**
*   **Sexual Dimorphism:** This is extreme, especially in the deep-sea ceratioid group. Females are large (up to a meter or more), while males are tiny (a few centimeters).
