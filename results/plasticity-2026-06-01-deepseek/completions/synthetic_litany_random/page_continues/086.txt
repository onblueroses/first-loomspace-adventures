The anglerfish is a fascinating example of deep-sea adaptation. Here are the key points from the description, along with some additional notable facts:

**Key Characteristics:**
*   **Order:** Lophiiformes (teleost/bony fish).
*   **Habitat:** Primarily deep-sea (bathypelagic zone), though some species inhabit shallower continental shelves.
*   **Predation:** Uses bioluminescence to lure prey. A modified spine (the illicium) on the head tips a luminous lure (the esca).
*   **Light Source:** The glow is produced by symbiotic bacteria living within the esca.

**The Famous Sexual Parasitism (Ceratioid Anglerfish):**
*   **Extreme Dimorphism:** Females are large, while males are tiny.
*   **Fusion:** The male permanently attaches to the female's body, fusing tissues and circulatory systems.
*   **Function:** The male becomes a parasitic source of sperm, while receiving nutrients from the female's blood.

**Additional Intriguing Facts:**
*   **Expandable Bodies:** Their highly distensible stomachs and jaws allow them to swallow prey larger than themselves.
