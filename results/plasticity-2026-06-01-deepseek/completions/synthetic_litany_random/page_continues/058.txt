That's an excellent summary of anglerfish! You've captured the key and most fascinating aspects of their biology. Building on what you've provided, here are some additional details and clarifications:

**1. The "Fishing Rod":**
The modified fin ray you mentioned is called the **illicium**, and the fleshy, glowing lure at its end is the **esca**. The shape of the esca is often species-specific and can resemble a worm, shrimp, or small fish to attract particular prey.

**2. Diversity Beyond the Deep:**
While famous for their deep-sea forms, the order Lophiiformes is quite diverse and includes:
*   **Goosefish (Lophiidae):** Found on continental shelves. They are the anglerfish commonly caught for food.
*   **Frogfish (Antennariidae):** Colorful, shallow-water dwellers that "walk" on modified pectoral fins.
*   **Batfish (Ogcocephalidae):** Bottom-dwellers with arm-like fins.

**3. The Extreme Sexual Parasitism (Ceratioids):**
The parasitic male fusion is one of the most extreme examples of sexual dimorphism in the vertebrate world.
