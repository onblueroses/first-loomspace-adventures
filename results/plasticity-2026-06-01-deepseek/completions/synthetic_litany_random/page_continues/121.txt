The anglerfish is a fascinating example of deep-sea adaptation. Here are some additional key points about their biology and ecology:

**Physical Characteristics & Diversity:**
*   They possess a large, globular head with a crescent-shaped mouth filled with long, sharp, translucent teeth. Their bodies are often flabby and loose-skinned, adapted to the high pressure of the deep sea.
*   The order Lophiiformes is diverse, including not only the deep-sea ceratioids but also other families like the monkfish (or goosefish) found on continental shelves, and the bizarre batfishes and frogfishes found in shallow tropical waters.

**The Lure (Esca):**
*   The bioluminescent lure, called an **esca**, is at the tip of a modified dorsal fin spine (the **illicium**). The anglerfish can wiggle this rod to mimic prey, attracting smaller fish and crustaceans.
*   The light produced is usually blue-green, wavelengths that travel farthest in seawater. In some species, the esca can even emit a blinking pattern.
