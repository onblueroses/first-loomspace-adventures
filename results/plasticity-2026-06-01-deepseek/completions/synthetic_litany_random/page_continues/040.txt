The remarkable adaptations of anglerfish don't end there. Here are some additional fascinating details about these deep-sea predators:

**Anatomy and Hunting:**
*   **"Fishing Rod":** The lure (called an *esca*) is actually a modified spine of the dorsal fin, located on the head. It can be wiggled to mimic prey.
*   **Massive Mouths:** They have enormous, crescent-shaped mouths filled with long, sharp, translucent teeth that angle inward. This design ensures prey cannot escape.
*   **Expandable Bodies:** Their stomachs can stretch to an incredible degree, allowing them to swallow prey up to twice their own size.

**The Deep-Sea Environment:**
*   **Bioluminescence:** In the perpetual darkness of the deep ocean (the *bathypelagic zone*), their bacterial light lure is one of the only sources of illumination. Some species can even control the light, flashing it on and off.
*   **Pressure Adaptation:** They lack a swim bladder (the gas-filled organ most fish use for buoyancy), as it would be crushed by the extreme pressure. Their gelatinous, flabby bodies are neutrally buoyant.
