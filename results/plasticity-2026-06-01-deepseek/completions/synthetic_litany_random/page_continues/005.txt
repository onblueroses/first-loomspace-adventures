Building on that, here are some additional key points often included in detailed descriptions:

**Physical Characteristics & Diversity:**
*   **Size & Shape:** They exhibit extreme sexual dimorphism. Females are much larger (some species over 3 feet/1 meter long) with large heads, mouths, and distensible stomachs. Males are often tiny (sometimes just a few centimeters) and, in many deep-sea species, look completely different until they find a female.
*   **The "Fishing Rod":** The bioluminescent lure (the **esca**) is at the end of a modified spine of the dorsal fin called the **illicium**. They can wiggle it to mimic prey.

**The Symbiotic Bacteria:**
*   The light-producing bacteria (*Vibrio* spp., *Photobacterium* spp.) live in a complex, enclosed chamber within the esca. The fish provides nutrients, and the bacteria produce light through a chemical reaction (**bioluminescence**).
*   It's believed larval anglerfish acquire these bacteria from the environment, and they become a specialized, permanent part of the fish's anatomy.
