The description you've provided offers a strong overview of anglerfish biology and behavior. Building on that foundation, here are some additional fascinating details about these remarkable deep-sea predators:

**Anatomy and Hunting:**
*   **The "Fishing Rod":** The luminescent lure (esca) is at the end of a modified dorsal spine called the **illicium**. The anglerfish can wiggle this rod to mimic the movements of prey.
*   **Gaping Jaws and Expandable Stomachs:** Their mouths are enormous relative to their body size, filled with long, sharp teeth that angle inward. This, combined with a highly expandable stomach, allows them to swallow prey larger than themselves.

**Deep-Sea Adaptations:**
*   **Pressure and Buoyancy:** Their bodies are gelatinous with weak skeletons and minimal musculature, an adaptation to the extreme pressure of the deep sea. They lack a swim bladder, which would collapse under pressure.
*   **Energy Conservation:** In the food-scarce deep ocean, they are ambush predators that conserve energy by lying in wait rather than actively swimming.
