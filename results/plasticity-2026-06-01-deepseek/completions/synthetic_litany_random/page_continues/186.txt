**Reproduction and Lifecycle**
The extreme sexual dimorphism and parasitic mating strategy are unique to the deep-sea ceratioid anglerfish. For these species, finding a mate in the vast darkness is a significant challenge. The males, which are tiny and often lack a functional luring apparatus, possess highly developed olfactory organs to detect pheromones released by a female. Upon finding her, he bites into her skin, and his mouth and body fuse permanently with her tissues. His circulatory systems connect, and he degenerates into a pair of testes, becoming a parasitic sperm-producing organ. A single female may host multiple males simultaneously.
