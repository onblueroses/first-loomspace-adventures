**Anatomy and Adaptations:**
Anglerfish possess a large, globular body with a massive head and a cavernous mouth filled with long, sharp teeth that angle inward to prevent prey escape. Their skin is often loose and fleshy, lacking scales, and may be dark-colored or translucent to blend into the deep-sea environment. The most famous feature is the *illicium* (a modified dorsal fin spine) tipped with the *esca* (the bioluminescent lure). The esca's light is produced by symbiotic bacteria housed within it; the bacteria receive nutrients and a stable environment from the fish, while the fish uses the light to attract prey and potentially communicate.

**Reproduction and Sexual Dimorphism:**
In many deep-sea anglerfish families (especially the ceratioids), extreme sexual dimorphism occurs. Females are significantly larger—sometimes up to 60 times the size of males—while males are tiny and often lack a functional lure.
