That's an excellent summary of anglerfish biology. The details about their predatory lure and the extreme sexual dimorphism of the deep-sea ceratioids are their most famous traits.

Here’s a bit more context and some additional fascinating points that often follow on a detailed page:

**Anatomy & Hunting:**
*   The "fishing rod" is called the **illicium**, and the glowing lure at its tip is the **esca**. The esca's structure is unique to each species and is a key identifying feature.
*   They are ambush predators. Their bodies are often flattened, with huge mouths filled with sharp, translucent teeth that point inwards to trap prey. They can swallow items much larger than themselves.
*   Their skin is often dark-colored or mottled for camouflage, and it's loose and fleshy.

**Symbiotic Bacteria:**
*   The bacteria that produce the light (**bioluminescence**) are typically from the genus ***Vibrio***. The anglerfish provides the bacteria with nutrients and a safe home; the bacteria provide light.
*   In some species, the fish can control the light by covering the esca with a flap of skin or by controlling blood flow to it, effectively "switching
