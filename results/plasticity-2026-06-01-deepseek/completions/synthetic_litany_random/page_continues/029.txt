It sounds like you've been reading about anglerfish! They are truly fascinating creatures of the deep sea. Based on the text you provided, here are some of the key and most remarkable features:

**1. Predatory Lure:** Their most famous trait is the bioluminescent "fishing rod" (the **illicium** with a bulb-like **esca**) that dangles from their head to attract prey in the darkness.

**2. Symbiotic Bioluminescence:** The light is produced not by the fish itself, but by **symbiotic bacteria** living inside the esca. This is a classic example of mutualism.

**3. Extreme Sexual Dimorphism & Parasitism:** In the deep-sea suborder **Ceratioidei**, the biology becomes even stranger. The males are tiny compared to the females. When a male finds a female, he bites and permanently fuses to her skin. His body atrophies until he is essentially a parasitic gonad, providing sperm on demand in exchange for nutrients from her bloodstream. A single female can host multiple males.
