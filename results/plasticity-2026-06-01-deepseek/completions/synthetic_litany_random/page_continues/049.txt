The anglerfish is a fascinating example of deep-sea adaptation. Its most famous feature, the bioluminescent lure (the **esca**), is a remarkable form of **aggressive mimicry**. The light, produced by symbiotic bacteria, attracts prey in the perpetual darkness. The anglerfish's large mouth and expandable stomach allow it to consume prey nearly its own size.

The paragraph also hints at one of the most extreme reproductive strategies in the animal kingdom, found specifically in the deep-sea **ceratioid** group. Here, the **sexual parasitism** is so complete that the dwarf male's tissues fuse with the female's, and their circulatory systems connect. The male essentially becomes a parasitic organ, existing solely to provide sperm on demand. This adaptation is a direct solution to the immense challenge of finding a mate in the vast, sparsely populated abyss.
