That’s an excellent summary of key anglerfish traits! It touches on several fascinating adaptations:

- **Taxonomy & Habitat:** They belong to the teleost order Lophiiformes and are primarily deep-sea dwellers, though some inhabit shallower waters.
- **Predation Strategy:** Their namesake “fishing rod” (illicium) tipped with a bioluminescent **esca** lures prey close enough to be engulfed by their large mouths.
- **Bioluminescence Symbiosis:** The light is produced by symbiotic bacteria housed in the esca. How anglerfish specifically acquire and maintain these bacteria is still an active area of research.
- **Sexual Dimorphism & Parasitism:** In deep-sea ceratioid anglerfish, males are tiny and, upon finding a female, attach and fuse to her body, eventually becoming a parasitic source of sperm. This ensures reproductive success in the vast, dark depths where encounters are rare.
