Some key points that typically follow in such descriptions include:  

- **Morphological Diversity**: Anglerfish vary greatly in size and appearance, from shallow-water species that resemble algae-covered rocks to deep-sea forms with grotesque, balloon-like bodies, huge mouths, and sharp teeth.  
- **Luring Mechanism**: The esca (the “lure”) can be simple or elaborate, with some species able to wiggle it like a small prey item. The light produced is often blue or green, wavelengths that travel farthest in seawater.  
- **Reproductive Strategy**: In deep-sea ceratioids, males are tiny and lack a lure. They use large olfactory organs to locate females. Upon attachment, their tissues fuse with the female’s, and they lose independent function, becoming essentially a parasitic gonad.  
- **Habitat and Diet**: They inhabit a wide range of depths, from coastal waters to abyssal plains. Their diet includes fish, crustaceans, and cephalopods; they are ambush predators, swallowing prey whole with expandable stomachs.  
- **Conservation Status**: Most deep-sea anglerfish are poorly studied due to their remote habitat.
