The description you've provided offers a fascinating glimpse into the unique biology of anglerfish. Here are some additional points and clarifications that often interest readers:

*   **The "Fishing Rod":** The modified fin ray (called the *illicium*) with its glowing tip (the *esca*) is typically located on the head. It can be wiggled to mimic prey, luring smaller fish and crustaceans within striking distance of the anglerfish's enormous, gaping mouth.
*   **Symbiotic Bacteria:** The bacteria that produce the light (*bioluminescence*) are housed in the esca. The relationship is a true symbiosis; the bacteria receive nutrients and a safe habitat from the fish, while the fish uses the light as a lure. In some species, the light can even be "turned off" by the anglerfish, possibly by restricting blood flow to the esca.
