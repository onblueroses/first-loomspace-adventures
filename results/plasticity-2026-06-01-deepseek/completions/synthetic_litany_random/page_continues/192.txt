The anglerfish's unique adaptations for survival in the deep sea make it one of the ocean's most fascinating and formidable predators. Here’s a deeper look into some of the features mentioned:

*   **The Illuminated Lure (Esca):** The glowing tip of the fishing rod-like fin (the *illicium*) is called the *esca*. It is a complex organ that houses bioluminescent bacteria. The anglerfish can often control the light—flashing it on and off—to better mimic the movements of prey and attract curious animals from the inky darkness.

*   **Extreme Sexual Dimorphism and Parasitism:** In many deep-sea anglerfish families (like the Ceratioidei), the difference between males and females is extreme. The males are tiny, often just a few centimeters long, and lack a functional lure. Their sole purpose is to find a female. Upon locating one, a male will bite her skin and permanently fuse to her body. His tissues and circulatory systems merge with hers, and he becomes a permanent parasitic source of sperm. A single female may carry multiple fused males.
