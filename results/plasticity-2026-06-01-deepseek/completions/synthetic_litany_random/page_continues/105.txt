Building on that, here are some other fascinating aspects of anglerfish, particularly the deep-sea ceratioid group you mentioned:

**1. Extreme Sexual Dimorphism:** The size difference is staggering. In some species, the female can be more than **60 times** the length and **half a million times** the weight of the male. The tiny males look like completely different fish.

**2. The Permanent Fusion Process:** When a male finds a female (likely by her unique pheromones and light), he bites onto her skin. His mouth and her flesh fuse at a cellular level. Their circulatory systems connect, and he becomes a parasitic "sexual appendage." A female can carry multiple males simultaneously.

**3. Diversity of Lures (Esca):** The bioluminescent lure isn't the same for all. Different species have evolved wildly different esca shapes to mimic specific prey, like shrimps, worms, or even just a simple glowing bulb. The bacteria that cause the glow are often species-specific.
