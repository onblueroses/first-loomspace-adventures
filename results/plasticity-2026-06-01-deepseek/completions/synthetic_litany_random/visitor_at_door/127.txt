You stand before a door, perhaps in a deep-sea research station or a museum of marine biology, when a voice comes through from the other side — calm, slightly gravelly, with a tone of patient curiosity.  

“I couldn’t help but overhear your reading about anglerfish. Remarkable creatures, aren’t they? That whole business with the glowing lure… and the males fusing to the females. Nature’s solutions can be so alien to our own experience. It makes one wonder what other forms of symbiosis and survival wait in the dark down there.”  

A pause, then a soft knock.  

“May I come in? I’ve a model here — a *Melanocetus johnsonii*, if you’re curious — and some recent papers on bacterial luminescence. Thought you might like to see.”
