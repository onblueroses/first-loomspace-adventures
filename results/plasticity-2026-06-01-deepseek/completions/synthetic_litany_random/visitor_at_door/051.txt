The visitor’s voice drifts through the door, perhaps muffled, perhaps clear — but in the deep dark where the anglerfish waits, sound travels differently.  

In that eternal midnight, the female anglerfish does not rush to answer. Her lure glows faintly, a small beacon in the black, tended by borrowed light from symbiotic bacteria. She is patience embodied; movement is costly, and prey must come to her.  

If the visitor is a potential mate — a tiny male — he will seek not her voice, but her light. He will latch on, fuse to her flesh, and gradually lose everything but his purpose: to provide sperm when needed.  

If the visitor is prey, curious and hungry, it will be drawn to the same gentle glow… until the jaws snap shut.  

And if the visitor is neither — perhaps something larger, something from the abyssal plain that does not fear her light — she might simply let the current carry their words away, unheeded, while she remains still, a ghost in the dark, waiting for a more certain opportunity.  

So the anglerfish does not speak back.  
She listens.  
She glows.  
She waits.
