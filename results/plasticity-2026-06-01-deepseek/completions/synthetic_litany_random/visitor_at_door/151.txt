The visitor’s voice cuts through the dim light of the deep-sea observatory lounge. “They remind me of us, you know,” they say, gesturing to the holographic anglerfish drifting silently in the display tank. “Not the luring part—the *merging*.”

They step closer, their reflection ghosting over the projection of the abyss. “A smaller form, latching on. Giving up everything for sustenance, for a kind of union. Becoming… permanent.” A pause hangs in the filtered, recycled air. “Do you think the male knows, when he first sees her light in the dark, that he will lose his eyes, his gut, his heart—everything but his purpose?”

They turn from the tank, their own features unreadable in the low blue glow. “I suppose it’s not parasitism if both parties agree it’s a miracle.”
