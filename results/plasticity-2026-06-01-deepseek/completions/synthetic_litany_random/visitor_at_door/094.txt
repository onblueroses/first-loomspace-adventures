The visitor at the door speaks, his voice low and gravelly.  
“I heard you’re studying deep-sea adaptations.”  

You nod, still holding the textbook open to the section on anglerfish.  

“Fascinating, isn’t it?” he says, a strange light glinting in his eyes. “That lure—the esca—glows because of bacteria. Symbiosis. You feel a chill, though the hallway is warm.  

“Sometimes,” the visitor whispers, “dependence looks like partnership. And the light that draws you in… isn’t always yours.”  

He smiles then, and something about the way the hall light catches his pupils reminds you, uncomfortably, of a lure in the dark.  

“May I come in?”
