**Human Impacts and Management**

Given their sensitivity to alterations in flow, chemistry, and physical structure, river ecosystems are among the most threatened by human activity. **Dams** represent one of the most profound alterations, fragmenting river corridors. They transform lotic habitats into lentic reservoirs upstream, disrupt natural sediment transport (leading to erosion downstream), and alter water temperature and flow regimes, which can block fish migrations and change the timing of life cycles for countless species.

**Channelization**—the straightening and hardening of banks with concrete or riprap—simplifies habitat complexity, eliminates pools and riffles, and severs the river from its natural floodplain. This not reduces biodiversity but also increases downstream flood risks by speeding water conveyance.

**Pollution** from point sources (e.g., industrial effluent) and non-point sources (e.g., **agricultural runoff** containing fertilizers, pesticides, and animal waste) leads to eutrophication, toxic algal blooms, and oxygen depletion. Elevated water temperatures from thermal pollution or removal of shading riparian vegetation further stress cold-water species.
