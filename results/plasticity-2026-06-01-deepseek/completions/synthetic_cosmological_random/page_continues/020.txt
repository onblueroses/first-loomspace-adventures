**Human Impacts and Conservation**

Anthropogenic activities have profoundly altered river ecosystems globally. Dams and reservoirs fragment river corridors, disrupting natural flow regimes, sediment transport, and the migratory pathways of fish and other aquatic organisms. Channelization for flood control or navigation simplifies habitat structure, reducing pools, riffles, and backwaters critical for biodiversity.

Pollution from point sources (e.g., industrial effluent, sewage) and non-point sources (e.g., agricultural fertilizers, pesticides, and urban stormwater runoff) degrades water quality. Excess nutrients, particularly nitrogen and phosphorus, can lead to eutrophication—algal blooms that deplete dissolved oxygen upon decomposition, creating "dead zones" harmful to aquatic life.

Land-use changes in the catchment, such as deforestation, urbanization, and conversion to agriculture, increase erosion and sediment loads, while also reducing the capacity of riparian zones to filter pollutants, stabilize banks, and provide organic matter and shade to regulate water temperature.
