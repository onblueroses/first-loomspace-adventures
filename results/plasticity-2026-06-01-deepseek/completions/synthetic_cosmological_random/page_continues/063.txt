### The River Continuum: From Headwaters to Mouth
The description illustrates the classic longitudinal zonation of a river system:
1.  **Headwaters (Source/Upper Reaches):** Characterized by high gradient, cold water, high dissolved oxygen, and coarse substrates. The ecosystem is largely **heterotrophic**—it relies on organic matter (leaves, wood) imported from the surrounding riparian forest. Key organisms include **shredders** (which break down coarse litter) and cold-water predators like trout.
2.  **Middle Reaches:** The river widens, slope decreases, and water warms. Primary production by algae and aquatic plants (**autotrophic** production) becomes significant. The substrate shifts to sand and silt. The fish community diversifies to include herbivores and omnivores.
3.  **Lower Reaches:** The river is wide, slow, and warm. Production can be high, but turbidity may limit light penetration
