### **Human Impacts and Conservation**

The alteration of natural river flow regimes is one of the most profound human impacts. Dams, built for hydropower, irrigation, or flood control, fragment river corridors. They create reservoirs that inundate upstream lotic habitats, transform downstream water temperature and flow patterns, and block the migration of fish and the transport of sediment. The resulting sediment starvation downstream can lead to channel incision and the degradation of riparian zones and floodplains.

Similarly, channelization—the straightening and hardening of banks with concrete or riprap—aims to control flooding and drain land. However, it severely degrades ecosystem function by eliminating habitat complexity, increasing water velocity, and severing the vital hydrological connection between the river and its floodplain. This often leads to a loss of biodiversity and reduced water quality.
