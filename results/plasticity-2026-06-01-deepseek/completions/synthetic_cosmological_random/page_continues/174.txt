This text provides a clear and structured overview of river ecosystems (lotic systems). Here is a breakdown of its key points, organized as a summary:

### **Core Definition & Determining Factors**
*   A **river ecosystem** is defined by the unidirectional flow of water.
*   Its fundamental character is shaped by its:
    *   **Catchment area** (watershed)
    *   **Regional climate**
    *   **Underlying geology**
    *   **Channel gradient**

### **Downstream Continuum: From Headwaters to Mouth**
Rivers change predictably from source to mouth, forming a continuum:

**1. Headwaters (Upper Reaches)**
*   **Physical Conditions:** Cold, fast, oxygen-rich water; substrate of bedrock and coarse gravel.
*   **Biology & Energy Base:**
    *   **Community:** Dominated by insect larvae (adapted to cling to surfaces) and cold-water predators like trout.
    *   **Energy Source:** **Allochthonous**—The food web is based on organic matter from outside (e.g., leaf litter from riparian forests), processed by shredder invertebrates.
