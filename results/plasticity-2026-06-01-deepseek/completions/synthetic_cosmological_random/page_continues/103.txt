### Core Definition
A **river (lotic) ecosystem** is defined by the unidirectional flow of water, which constantly transports materials and shapes the biological community. This contrasts with still-water (lentic) ecosystems like lakes.

### Determining Factors
A river's character is fundamentally shaped by:
*   Its **catchment area (watershed)**
*   Regional **climate**
*   Underlying **geology**
*   Channel **gradient**

### Longitudinal Changes: From Source to Mouth
River ecosystems change predictably along their course:

1.  **Headwaters (Upper Reaches):**
    *   **Conditions:** Cold, fast, oxygen-rich water; substrate of bedrock and coarse gravel.
    *   **Biology:** Dominated by insect larvae adapted to cling to surfaces; predators like trout. The food web is based on **allochthonous** inputs (external organic matter like leaf litter), processed by shredder invertebrates.
