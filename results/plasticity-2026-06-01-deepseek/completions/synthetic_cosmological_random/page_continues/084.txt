### Ecosystem Processes and Connectivity
A defining feature of river ecosystems is **longitudinal connectivity**—the unimpeded flow of water, materials, and organisms from source to sea. This connectivity facilitates essential processes like nutrient spiraling, where nutrients such as nitrogen and phosphorus are repeatedly taken up by organisms, released, and transported downstream. The **riparian zone** (the land adjacent to the river) is a critical component, acting as a buffer that filters runoff, stabilizes banks, provides shade to regulate temperature, and supplies allochthonous organic matter (like leaves) that fuels upstream food webs.
