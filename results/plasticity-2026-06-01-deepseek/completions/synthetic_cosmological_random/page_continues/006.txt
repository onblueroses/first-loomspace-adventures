### Human Impacts and River Management

Human activities have profoundly altered the structure and function of river ecosystems worldwide. These impacts often disrupt the natural processes described above, leading to a decline in biodiversity and ecosystem services.

*   **Physical Alteration:** The construction of dams and reservoirs fragments river corridors, transforming free-flowing lotic habitats into a series of lentic pools. This disrupts sediment transport, blocks the migration of fish and other aquatic organisms, and alters natural flow and temperature regimes downstream. **Channelization**—the straightening and hardening of banks for flood control or navigation—destroys habitat complexity, increases flow velocity, and severs the river from its floodplain.

*   **Pollution and Water Quality:** Runoff from agriculture, urban areas, and industrial sites introduces excess nutrients (nitrogen and phosphorus), pesticides, heavy metals, and other contaminants. This can lead to **eutrophication**, where algal blooms deplete dissolved oxygen, creating "dead zones." Point-source pollution from wastewater treatment plants and factories adds further stress, affecting the health of aquatic life and human water supplies.
