### Core Definition & Distinction
*   A **river ecosystem** is a **lotic** (flowing water) system, defined by the **unidirectional movement** of water, materials, and organisms.
*   It is fundamentally different from **lentic** (still water) ecosystems like lakes and ponds.

### Determinants of Character
The nature of a river is shaped by:
*   **Catchment area** (watershed)
*   **Regional climate**
*   **Underlying geology**
*   **Channel gradient** (steepness from source to mouth)

### Longitudinal Zonation: From Headwaters to Mouth
Rivers change predictably from source to estuary:

1.  **Headwater Streams (Upper Reaches):**
    *   **Conditions:** Cold, fast, oxygen-rich water; bedrock/gravel substrate.
    *   **Biology:** Dominated by **aquatic insect larvae** (e.g., shredders) adapted to cling to surfaces; predators like **trout**.
    *   **Energy Base:** **Allochthonous**—primarily **leaf litter and woody debris** from surrounding forests.
