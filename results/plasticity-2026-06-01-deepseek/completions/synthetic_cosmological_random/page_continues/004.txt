### **Ecological Processes and Connectivity**
The defining feature of a lotic ecosystem is **connectivity**. This operates in four dimensions:
*   **Longitudinal (Upstream-Downstream):** The River Continuum Concept describes how physical conditions and energy sources shift predictably from headwaters to mouth, creating a gradient of habitats. Organic matter processed upstream fuels downstream reaches.
*   **Lateral (Channel-Floodplain):** Seasonal floods connect the main channel to its floodplain, providing spawning grounds, nutrient-rich nurseries for fish, and depositing fertile sediments. This pulse is a critical driver of productivity and biodiversity.
*   **Vertical (Channel-Groundwater):** Rivers interact with hyporheic zones—areas where surface water mixes with groundwater. This exchange regulates temperature, filters nutrients, and provides refugia for invertebrates.
*   **Temporal:** River ecosystems change over time, from daily cycles to seasonal floods and multi-year successional changes following disturbances like droughts or major floods.
