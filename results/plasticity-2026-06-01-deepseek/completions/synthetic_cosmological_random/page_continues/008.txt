**Human Impacts and Management of River Ecosystems**

Human activities have profoundly altered the structure and function of river ecosystems on a global scale. The key natural parameters defining river health—flow, sediment, temperature, water quality, and riparian connectivity—are often the primary targets of anthropogenic change.

*   **Flow Alteration:** The construction of dams is the most significant cause of flow regime alteration. Dams fragment river corridors, converting free-flowing lotic habitats into a series of lentic reservoirs. This disrupts natural flood cycles, traps sediment upstream (starving downstream reaches), and alters water temperature and oxygen levels. Downstream, "hydropeaking" from dams used for hydropower can cause rapid, daily fluctuations in water level, stranding aquatic organisms and degrading habitat.

*   **Water Quality Degradation:** Non-point source pollution from agricultural runoff (carrying fertilizers, pesticides, and animal waste) and urban stormwater is a major stressor.
