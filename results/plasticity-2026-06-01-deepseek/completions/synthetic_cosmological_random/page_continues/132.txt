Human activities have profoundly altered river ecosystems worldwide. Dams, constructed for hydropower, irrigation, and flood control, represent one of the most significant modifications. They fragment habitats, block the migration of fish and the downstream transport of sediment, and alter the natural flow regime—the seasonal pattern of high and low flows essential for triggering life-cycle events like fish spawning and floodplain inundation. Downstream of dams, rivers often run colder (from deep reservoir releases) or with reduced oxygen, changing the biological community.

Agricultural and urban development in the catchment area introduces pollutants and alters water and sediment dynamics. Fertilizer runoff leads to nutrient enrichment (eutrophication), causing algal blooms that deplete oxygen when they decompose. Pesticides, industrial chemicals, and heavy metals can accumulate in sediments and biota. Impervious surfaces like roads and parking lots increase rapid runoff, exacerbating flood peaks and reducing groundwater recharge, while channelization for drainage or flood protection simplifies habitat structure and disconnects the river from its floodplain.
