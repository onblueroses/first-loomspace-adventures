**Energy Flow and Nutrient Cycling**
The description of energy sources—allochthonous (externally derived) organic matter in headwaters and autochthonous (internally produced) algae and plants in mid-reaches—defines a fundamental shift known as the **River Continuum Concept**. This model describes a predictable change in the dominant feeding groups (functional feeding guilds) of invertebrates along the river's course:
*   **Headwaters:** Shredders (e.g., some caddisfly larvae) break down coarse leaf litter.
*   **Mid-reaches:** Grazers/scrapers (e.g., mayfly larvae) consume algae growing on substrates, and collectors (e.g., blackfly larvae) filter fine particulate matter from the water.
*   **Lower reaches:** Collectors dominate, processing the fine organic particles transported from upstream.
