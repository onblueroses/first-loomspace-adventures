### **Human Impacts and Ecosystem Management**

The alterations described—damming, channelization, and land-use change—can have cascading effects on river health. Dams, for instance, fragment habitats, block fish migration, and fundamentally alter the natural flow regime, temperature, and sediment transport. This often converts a flowing, lotic environment into a series of more lentic reservoirs, disadvantaging species adapted to currents and benefiting still-water species.

Channelization, or the straightening and hardening of banks for flood control or drainage, reduces habitat complexity, eliminates riparian zones, and accelerates water flow, which increases erosion downstream. This simplification of the physical channel leads to a severe loss of biodiversity.

Pollution from agricultural and urban runoff introduces excess nutrients (eutrophication), pesticides, and other contaminants. This can lead to algal blooms, oxygen depletion, and direct toxicity to aquatic life. The cumulative effect of these pressures is often a degraded, simplified ecosystem with reduced resilience.
