Human activities have profoundly altered the structure and function of river ecosystems worldwide. The construction of dams and reservoirs fragments river corridors, transforming free-flowing lotic habitats into a series of lentic pools. This disrupts sediment transport, blocks the migration of aquatic species, and alters natural flow and temperature regimes downstream. Channelization for flood control or navigation straightens and simplifies channels, eliminating critical habitat features like pools, riffles, and meanders, and severing the river's connection to its floodplain.

Pollution from point sources, such as industrial effluents and sewage outfalls, and non-point sources, like agricultural runoff containing fertilizers, pesticides, and animal waste, degrades water quality. These inputs can lead to eutrophication, toxic algal blooms, and reduced dissolved oxygen, harming sensitive species. Furthermore, the clearing of riparian vegetation for agriculture or urban development removes the vital buffer zone that stabilizes banks, filters pollutants, provides shade to moderate water temperature, and supplies terrestrial organic matter to the food web.
