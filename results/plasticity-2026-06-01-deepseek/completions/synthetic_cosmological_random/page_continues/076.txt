### **Energy Flow and Nutrient Cycling**
The functioning of a river ecosystem is driven by two interconnected processes: the flow of energy and the cycling of nutrients. Rivers are often described as **heterotrophic** in their headwaters, meaning they rely on external energy inputs (allochthonous material), and become more **autotrophic** (producing their own energy via photosynthesis) in their wider, sunlit middle and lower reaches. This conceptual model is known as the **River Continuum Concept**. Nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus, essential for plant and algal growth, are transported from the catchment and cycled within the river by biological and physical processes. Temporary storage in sediments, floodplains, and biotic pools is critical for ecosystem productivity.
