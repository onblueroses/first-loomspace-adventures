### Human Impacts and Ecosystem Degradation

Human activities have profoundly altered river ecosystems worldwide. Key pressures include:

*   **Flow Alteration:** Dams and reservoirs disrupt the natural flow regime, fragmenting habitats, blocking fish migrations, and altering water temperature and sediment transport downstream. Water abstraction for agriculture or municipal use can reduce flows to ecologically damaging levels.
*   **Pollution:** Runoff from agricultural lands introduces excess nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus) and pesticides. Urban runoff carries hydrocarbons, heavy metals, and road salts. Industrial discharges and untreated sewage add toxic chemicals and organic waste, leading to eutrophication, oxygen depletion, and toxicity.
*   **Habitat Destruction:** Channelization (straightening and hardening banks) simplifies channel morphology, destroys in-stream habitat complexity, and severs the river from its floodplain. Riparian zone clearing removes shading (increasing water temperature), eliminates a key source of organic matter, and destabilizes banks.
*   **Invasive Species:** The introduction of non-native plants, fish, and invertebrates can outcompete native species, alter food webs, and change physical habitat structure.
