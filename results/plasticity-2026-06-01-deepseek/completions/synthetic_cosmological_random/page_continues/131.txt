This text provides a clear and structured overview of river ecosystems. It logically moves from a general definition to specific characteristics along the river's course, ending with factors affecting its health.

Here is a breakdown of the key concepts presented, organized to complement the flow of the text:

### **Core Definition & Drivers**
*   **Lotic Ecosystem:** A flowing-water ecosystem defined by the unidirectional (downstream) movement of water, materials, and organisms.
*   **Fundamental Controls:** A river's character is determined by its **catchment area (watershed), climate, geology, and channel gradient.**

### **Longitudinal Profile: From Source to Mouth**
The text describes a classic model of how ecosystems change along a river's length:

| Zone | Physical Conditions | Biological Community | Energy Base |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Headwaters (Upper Reach)** | Cold, fast, high oxygen; bedrock/gravel substrate. | Insect larvae (cling to substrates); cold-water predators (e.g., trout). | **Allochthonous:** External organic matter (leaf litter, wood). |
| **Middle & Lower Reaches** | Warmer, slower; substrate shifts to sand/silt. | Larger, more diverse fish
