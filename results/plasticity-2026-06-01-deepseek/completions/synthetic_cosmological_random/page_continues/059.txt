**Human Impacts and River Management**

Human activities have profoundly altered the structure and function of river ecosystems worldwide. These impacts are often cumulative, stemming from multiple stressors within a catchment.

*   **Physical Alteration:** The construction of dams and reservoirs fragments river continuity, transforming lotic habitats into lentic ones. This disrupts sediment transport, blocks fish migration, and alters natural flow and temperature regimes downstream. Channelization—straightening and hardening banks with concrete or riprap—simplifies habitat complexity, eliminates backwaters and pools, and disconnects the river from its floodplain.
*   **Water Quality Degradation:** Runoff from agricultural, urban, and industrial areas introduces excess nutrients (nitrogen and phosphorus), pesticides, heavy metals, and other pollutants. Nutrient enrichment can lead to algal blooms and eutrophication, depleting dissolved oxygen. Point-source pollution from wastewater treatment plants, while regulated, can still alter water chemistry and introduce pharmaceuticals and personal care products.
