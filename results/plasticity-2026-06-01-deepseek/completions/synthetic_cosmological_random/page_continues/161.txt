The river ecosystem, as a dynamic and interconnected system, can be summarized through its key defining characteristics and transitions:

### Core Definition & Driving Force
*   A **lotic** (flowing water) ecosystem defined by the **unidirectional movement of water**, which transports materials, energy, and organisms downstream.
*   Its fundamental character is set by its **catchment area, climate, geology, and channel gradient**.

### Longitudinal Zones: From Source to Mouth
The ecosystem changes predictably along its course:

1.  **Headwaters (Upper Reaches):**
    *   **Conditions:** Cold, fast, oxygen-rich water over bedrock/gravel.
    *   **Biology:** Dominated by insect larvae (e.g., clinging mayflies) and cold-water predators like trout.
    *   **Energy Base:** **Allochthonous**—driven by organic matter (leaves, wood) from the surrounding forest, processed by shredder invertebrates.

2.  **Middle & Lower Reaches:**
    *   **Conditions:** Wider, slower, warmer; substrate shifts to sand/silt.
    *   **Biology:** Increased primary production by algae and aquatic plants. Fish communities shift to larger herbivores/omnivores.
