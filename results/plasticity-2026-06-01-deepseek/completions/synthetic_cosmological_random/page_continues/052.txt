Human activities have become a dominant force shaping river ecosystems globally. **Anthropogenic alterations** often disrupt the natural flow regimes, sediment dynamics, and thermal profiles that native species have evolved with. Dams, for instance, fragment habitats, convert lotic (flowing) environments into lentic (still) reservoirs, and alter downstream water temperature and sediment transport. Channelization for flood control or navigation simplifies physical complexity, removing vital habitats like pools and riffles and severing the connection between the river and its floodplain.

These physical modifications are compounded by **chemical inputs**. Agricultural runoff introduces excess nutrients (nitrogen and phosphorus) from fertilizers, which can lead to eutrophication—dense algal blooms that deplete dissolved oxygen upon decomposition. Urban and industrial discharges add contaminants, including heavy metals, pesticides, and pharmaceuticals, which accumulate in sediments and biomagnify up the food web.
