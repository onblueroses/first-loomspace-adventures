### **Key Concepts and Processes**
*   **River Continuum Concept:** This describes the predictable, continuous change in physical conditions and biological communities from a river's headwaters to its mouth. It explains the shift from allochthonous (externally derived) energy sources like leaves in headwaters to autochthonous (internally produced) energy from algae in mid-reaches.
*   **Nutrient Spiraling:** In a flowing system, nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus are not simply recycled in one place. They are taken up by organisms, released back into the water, and transported downstream before being taken up again, creating a "spiral" path.
*   **Flow Regime as a Master Variable:** The natural pattern of a river's flow—including seasonal floods, low summer flows, and daily fluctuations—is the primary force shaping its physical habitat and the life cycles of the species within it. Organisms are adapted to this dynamic pattern.
