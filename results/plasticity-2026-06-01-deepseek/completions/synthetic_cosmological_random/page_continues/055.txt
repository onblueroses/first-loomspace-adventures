### Summary of Key Points

1.  **Definition:** A river ecosystem is a **lotic** (flowing water) system. Its defining feature is the **unidirectional movement of water**, which shapes the biological community by transporting materials and organisms downstream.

2.  **Determining Factors:** A river's character is shaped by its **catchment area, climate, geology, and channel gradient**.

3.  **Longitudinal Zonation (Changes from Source to Mouth):**
    *   **Headwaters (Upper Reaches):** Cold, fast, oxygen-rich water over rocky substrates. The food web is based on **allochthonous** inputs (organic matter from outside, like leaves). The community is dominated by insect larvae (e.g., shredders) and cold-water fish (e.g., trout).
    *   **Middle & Lower Reaches:** The river widens, slows, and warms. The substrate becomes finer (sand/silt). The food web shifts to **autochthonous** production (algae and aquatic plants within the river). Fish communities include larger herbivores and omnivores.
