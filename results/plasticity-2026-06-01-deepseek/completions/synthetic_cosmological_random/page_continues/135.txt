Human activities have profoundly altered the natural parameters that sustain river ecosystems. **Dams**, for instance, fragment habitat, convert flowing lotic conditions into lentic reservoirs, and disrupt natural flow regimes, sediment transport, and fish migration. **Channelization** for flood control or navigation simplifies habitat structure, eliminates meanders and pools, and disconnects the river from its floodplain, reducing nutrient processing and habitat diversity.

**Pollution** from agricultural, urban, and industrial sources introduces excess nutrients (eutrophication), toxic chemicals, and thermal pollution, degrading water quality and stressing aquatic life. Furthermore, **land-use changes** in the catchment, such as deforestation or urbanization, increase runoff and erosion, altering sediment loads and hydrographs to produce more frequent and severe flash floods.

The cumulative effect of these modifications is often a loss of **biodiversity** and **ecosystem resilience**. Specialist species, like many headwater insects or migratory fish, are typically the first to decline,
