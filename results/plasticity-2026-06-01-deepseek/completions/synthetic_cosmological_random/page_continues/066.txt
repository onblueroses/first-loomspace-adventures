**Human Impacts and River Health**

The very characteristics that define a river ecosystem—its flow, sediment transport, temperature, and chemical composition—are highly susceptible to human alteration. These modifications often disrupt the natural processes that sustain the biological community.

*   **Flow Alteration:** Dams are perhaps the most profound alteration, converting lotic (flowing) habitats into lentic (still) reservoirs upstream and severely altering the natural flow regime, temperature, and sediment transport downstream. "Hydropeaking" for electricity can cause sudden, drastic changes in water level, stranding organisms. Channelization and levees disconnect the river from its floodplain, eliminating vital seasonal habitats and nutrient exchange.

*   **Water Quality Degradation:** Runoff from agriculture and urban areas introduces excess nutrients (nitrogen and phosphorus), leading to algal blooms and eutrophication. This consumes dissolved oxygen when the algae die and decompose, creating "dead zones" for fish and invertebrates. Industrial discharge and urban runoff can also introduce toxic chemicals and heavy metals, which accumulate in the food web.
