Human activities pose the most significant threats to the integrity of river ecosystems. **Damming** fundamentally transforms a lotic environment into a series of lentic reservoirs, disrupting the natural flow regime, blocking the migration of fish and the transport of sediment, and altering water temperature and oxygen levels downstream. **Channelization**—straightening and hardening banks for flood control or navigation—destroys habitat complexity, eliminates meanders and pools, and severs the river from its floodplain, reducing nutrient exchange and biodiversity.

Pollution from **agricultural and urban runoff** introduces excess nutrients (nitrates and phosphates), pesticides, and heavy metals. This can lead to eutrophication, algal blooms, and oxygen depletion, while toxic substances bioaccumulate in the food web. Furthermore, **land-use changes** in the catchment, such as deforestation or urbanization, increase erosion and sediment load, which can smother benthic habitats, and reduce the input of vital coarse organic matter from riparian forests.
