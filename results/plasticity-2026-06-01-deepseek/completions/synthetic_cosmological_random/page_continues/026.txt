### Key Ecological Concepts in Rivers

*   **River Continuum Concept:** This model describes how a river's physical characteristics (width, depth, flow, temperature) change predictably from headwaters to mouth, and how the biological community and its energy sources adapt accordingly.
    *   **Headwaters (Upper Reaches):** As you noted, energy input is **allochthonous** (from outside, like leaves). Invertebrate communities are dominated by **shredders** (break down coarse material) and **collectors**.
    *   **Mid-Reaches:** The river widens. Energy sources become a mix of transported organic particles and **autochthonous** (in-stream) production from algae and aquatic plants. Invertebrate communities shift toward **grazers** (scraping algae) and **collectors**.
    *   **Lower Reaches:** The river is wide, deep, and slower. Energy is primarily fine organic particles transported from upstream. Invertebrate communities are dominated by **collectors** and **filter feeders**.
