### **Key Ecological Processes and Concepts**

*   **River Continuum Concept:** This model describes how a river's physical characteristics (gradient, width, flow) change predictably from source to mouth, and how the biological community and energy sources (allochthonous vs. autochthonous) shift in response. It explains the transition from reliance on external leaf litter in headwaters to internal algae production in mid-reaches.
*   **Nutrient Spiraling:** In a flowing system, nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus are not simply recycled in one place. They are taken up by organisms, released back into the water, and transported downstream before being taken up again, creating a "spiral" path rather than a closed loop.
*   **Flow Regime as a Master Variable:** The natural pattern of a river's flow—including seasonal floods, low summer flows, and daily pulses—is the primary driver of ecosystem health. It shapes the physical habitat, triggers fish spawning, distributes nutrients, and maintains connections with floodplains.
