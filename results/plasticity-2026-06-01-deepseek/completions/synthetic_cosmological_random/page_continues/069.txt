### **Human Impacts and Conservation**
Human activities are the primary drivers of alteration in river ecosystems. These impacts can be categorized and understood through their effects on the fundamental parameters described above.

*   **Alteration of Flow Regime:** Dams and reservoirs are the most significant disruptors. They transform lotic (flowing) systems into lentic (still) ones upstream, blocking the movement of organisms and sediments. Downstream, natural seasonal floods are often eliminated, which disrupts fish spawning cues, prevents floodplain connectivity, and reduces the scouring that maintains channel form. Water abstraction for agriculture and cities can simply dewater streams.

*   **Water Quality Degradation:** Pollution from **agricultural runoff** (fertilizers, pesticides, and animal waste) and **urban stormwater** introduces excess nutrients (eutrophication), toxic chemicals, and thermal pollution. This can lead to algal blooms, oxygen depletion ("dead zones"), and direct toxicity to aquatic life. **Point-source pollution** from industrial or municipal discharges adds further contaminants.
