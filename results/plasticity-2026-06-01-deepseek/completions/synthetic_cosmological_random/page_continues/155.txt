### **Human Impacts and River Management**

Human activities have profoundly altered the structure and function of river ecosystems on a global scale. These alterations often disrupt the natural processes that sustain lotic communities.

*   **Physical Alteration:** The construction of dams and reservoirs fragments river corridors, transforming flowing (lotic) habitats into still-water (lentic) ones. This blocks the migration of fish, traps sediment, and alters the natural flow, temperature, and nutrient regimes essential to native species. **Channelization**—straightening and hardening banks for flood control or navigation—destroys in-stream habitat complexity, eliminates riparian zones, and accelerates water flow, leading to increased erosion downstream.

*   **Water Quality Degradation:** **Agricultural runoff** introduces excess nutrients (nitrogen and phosphorus) from fertilizers, which can cause algal blooms and subsequent oxygen depletion (eutrophication). Pesticides, heavy metals, and industrial effluents introduce toxins that can accumulate in the food web. **Urban stormwater runoff** carries pollutants like oil, road salts, and sediments, while also increasing water temperature through heated impervious surfaces.
