**Human Impacts and Ecosystem Management**

The very features that define river ecosystems—their flow, connectivity, and integration with the landscape—make them particularly vulnerable to human alteration. These modifications often disrupt the natural processes that sustain the biological community.

*   **Flow Alteration:** Dams and water diversions are among the most significant impacts. By regulating flow, they eliminate natural flood pulses essential for floodplain connectivity, disrupt sediment transport (leading to downstream erosion and upstream sedimentation), and alter water temperature and oxygen regimes. This can block fish migration, eliminate spawning cues, and favor generalist species over specialized native ones.

*   **Water Quality Degradation:** Pollution from **agricultural runoff** (carrying fertilizers, pesticides, and animal waste), **urban stormwater** (with hydrocarbons, heavy metals, and road salts), and **industrial or municipal discharges** introduces excess nutrients and toxic substances. Nutrient overload, especially of nitrogen and phosphorus, leads to **eutrophication**—explosive algal growth in slower reaches. Subsequent decomposition of this algae consumes dissolved oxygen, potentially creating "dead zones" harmful to aquatic life.
