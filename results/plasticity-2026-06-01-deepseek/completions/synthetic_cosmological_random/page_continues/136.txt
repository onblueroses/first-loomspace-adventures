### Human Impacts and River Management

The very characteristics that define a river ecosystem—its flow, sediment transport, and connectivity—are often the targets of human alteration. These modifications can disrupt ecological processes from the headwaters to the estuary.

**1. Flow Alteration:** Dams and diversions fundamentally change a river's natural flow regime. By flattening seasonal floods and reducing base flows, they can:
*   **Disrupt life cycles:** Many fish species rely on high flows for spawning cues and to access floodplain nurseries.
*   **Reduce habitat diversity:** Constant, low flows can lead to the encroachment of vegetation and the loss of dynamic gravel bars and side channels.
*   **Block migration:** Dams create impassable barriers for fish and the upstream-downstream movement of nutrients.

**2. Channel Modification:** Straightening and hardening channels (channelization) for flood control or navigation increases flow speed and reduces habitat complexity. This often degrades conditions for invertebrates and fish while increasing erosion downstream.
