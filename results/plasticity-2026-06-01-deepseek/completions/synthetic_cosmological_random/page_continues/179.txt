### Key Ecological Concepts & Processes
*   **River Continuum Concept:** This model describes how a river's physical characteristics (width, depth, flow, substrate) change predictably from source to mouth, and how the biological community and its energy sources shift in response. Headwaters rely on **allochthonous** inputs (external organic matter like leaves), while mid-reaches have a mix, and lower reaches rely more on **autochthonous** production (algae and aquatic plants).
*   **Nutrient Spiraling:** In a flowing system, nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus are not simply recycled in one place. They are taken up by organisms, released, and transported downstream before being taken up again, creating a "spiral" path rather than a closed loop.
*   **Habitat Zonation:** Distinct zones exist within a river channel:
    *   **Riffles:** Shallow, fast-flowing, oxygen-rich areas over coarse substrate, important for many insect larvae.
    *   **Pools:** Deeper, slower areas where fine sediments settle, used
