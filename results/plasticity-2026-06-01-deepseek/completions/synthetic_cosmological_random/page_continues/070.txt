**Human Impacts and Ecosystem Management**

Human activities have profoundly altered river ecosystems on a global scale. These modifications often disrupt the natural processes that define lotic systems, leading to a loss of biodiversity and ecosystem services.

**Key Anthropogenic Stressors:**
*   **Flow Alteration:** Dams and diversions are the most significant disruptors. They fragment habitat, block migratory routes for fish, and fundamentally change the natural flow regime—eliminating seasonal floods that recharge floodplains and maintain channel morphology.
*   **Pollution:** Point-source pollution (e.g., from industrial or wastewater discharges) and non-point-source pollution (e.g., agricultural runoff containing fertilizers, pesticides, and sediments) degrade water quality. Excess nutrients cause eutrophication, leading to algal blooms and oxygen depletion.
*   **Physical Habitat Destruction:** Channelization for flood control or navigation simplifies the channel, removes in-stream cover, and disconnects the river from its floodplain. Riparian deforestation reduces shade (increasing water temperature), eliminates a crucial source of organic matter, and destabilizes banks.
