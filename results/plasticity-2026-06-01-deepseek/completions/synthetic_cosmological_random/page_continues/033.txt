### **River Ecosystem Overview**
- **Definition**: A flowing-water (lotic) ecosystem shaped by the unidirectional movement of water.
- **Distinction**: Differs from lentic ecosystems (lakes, ponds) due to continuous downstream transport of water, minerals, organic matter, and organisms.
- **Key Determinants**:  
  - Catchment area characteristics  
  - Regional climate  
  - Underlying geology  
  - Channel gradient from source to mouth

---

### **Zonation of River Ecosystems**
1. **Headwater Streams (Upper Reaches)**
   - **Physical Conditions**: Cold, oxygen-rich, fast-flowing water over bedrock/coarse gravel.
   - **Biological Community**:
     - Dominated by insect larvae adapted to cling to substrates.
     - Cold-water predators (e.g., trout).
   - **Energy Source**: Primarily allochthonous – leaf litter and woody debris from riparian forests, processed by shredder invertebrates.

2. **Middle and Lower Reaches**
   - **Physical Changes**: Channel widens, flow slows, substrate shifts to sand/silt, water warms.
