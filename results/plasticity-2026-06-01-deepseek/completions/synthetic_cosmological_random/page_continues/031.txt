**Disturbance and Resilience**
River ecosystems are naturally dynamic and subject to periodic disturbances that shape their physical and biological structure. Seasonal flooding is a fundamental driver, scouring channels, depositing new sediment, and connecting the river to its floodplain. These flood pulses are critical for nutrient cycling, seed dispersal, and creating diverse habitats. The biological communities in rivers are adapted to these predictable disturbances, exhibiting resilience—the ability to recover structure and function after a disruptive event. However, human alterations often change the frequency, timing, or intensity of these natural disturbances, reducing the ecosystem's resilience.

**Longitudinal, Lateral, and Vertical Connectivity**
A key principle in river science is that a healthy river is a connected river. This connectivity operates in three dimensions:
*   **Longitudinal:** The upstream-downstream connection along the river's length, allowing for the movement of water, sediment, organisms (like migrating fish), and nutrients.
*   **Lateral:** The connection between the channel and its floodplain and riparian zone. This is essential for fish spawning, wildlife habitat, and filtering pollutants from runoff.
