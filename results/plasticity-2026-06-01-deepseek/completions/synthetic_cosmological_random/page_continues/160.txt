This alteration often manifests as a loss of biodiversity and a decline in key ecosystem services. For instance, dams fragment river corridors, blocking the migration of fish and the natural transport of sediment. This can lead to the homogenization of downstream habitats and the loss of spawning grounds upstream. Channelization, the straightening and hardening of banks, reduces habitat complexity, eliminates floodplain connectivity, and increases flow velocity, which scours the stream bed and degrades conditions for benthic organisms.

Agricultural runoff introduces excess nutrients (e.g., nitrogen and phosphorus) and pesticides. The nutrients can cause eutrophication in slower-moving reaches, leading to algal blooms that deplete dissolved oxygen when they decompose, creating "dead zones" harmful to fish and invertebrates. Pesticides and other toxins can accumulate in the food web, affecting organism health and reproduction.
