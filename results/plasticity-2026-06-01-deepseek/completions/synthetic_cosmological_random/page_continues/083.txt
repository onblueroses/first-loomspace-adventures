This description provides a clear and structured overview of river ecosystems. To build upon it, here are some key concepts and real-world implications that naturally follow:

### **Core Ecological Concepts Implied:**
*   **River Continuum Concept:** The text describes a shift from allochthonous (externally produced) energy sources (leaf litter) in headwaters to autochthonous (internally produced) energy (algae/plants) downstream. This is a foundational model in stream ecology.
*   **Nutrient Spiraling:** In a flowing system, nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus are not recycled in place but are carried downstream as they are taken up, used, and released by organisms—a process called spiraling.
*   **Connectivity:** A river's health depends on **longitudinal connectivity** (unimpeded flow from source to sea), **lateral connectivity** (to floodplains and wetlands), and **vertical connectivity** (with groundwater).
