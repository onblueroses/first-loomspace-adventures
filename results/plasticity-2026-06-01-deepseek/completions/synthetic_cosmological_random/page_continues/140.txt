**Human Impacts and River Management**

The health and function of river ecosystems are increasingly threatened by anthropogenic activities. The following are primary stressors:

*   **Flow Alteration:** Dams and water diversions fundamentally disrupt the natural flow regime, which is the "master variable" governing lotic ecosystems. By altering the timing, magnitude, and frequency of floods and low flows, dams fragment habitats, block fish migration, and disconnect rivers from their floodplains. Downstream, reduced flows can degrade water quality and eliminate critical spawning cues for aquatic life.

*   **Pollution and Nutrient Loading:** Runoff from agricultural lands, urban areas, and industrial sites introduces excess nutrients (nitrogen and phosphorus), pesticides, heavy metals, and other contaminants. This pollution can lead to eutrophication (excessive algal growth), oxygen depletion ("dead zones"), toxic conditions for fish and invertebrates, and the degradation of drinking water sources.
