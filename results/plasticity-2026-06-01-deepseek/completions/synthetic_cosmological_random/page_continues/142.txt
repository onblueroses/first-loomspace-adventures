### **Key Ecological Concepts and Processes**

The dynamics of a river ecosystem are governed by several interconnected concepts:

*   **The River Continuum Concept:** This model describes how the physical conditions of a river (width, depth, flow, temperature) change predictably from source to mouth, and how the biological community adapts accordingly. Headwaters are primarily **heterotrophic**, relying on external organic matter, while mid-reaches become more **autotrophic** due to in-stream photosynthesis. Large rivers often return to heterotrophy as depth limits light penetration.
*   **Nutrient Spiraling:** In a flowing system, nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus are not simply recycled in place. They are taken up by organisms, released back into the water, and transported downstream before being taken up again, creating a "spiral" path. The length of this spiral is a key measure of ecosystem efficiency.
