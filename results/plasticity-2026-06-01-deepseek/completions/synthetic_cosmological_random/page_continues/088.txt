### **Key Ecological Processes and Concepts**

*   **Nutrient Spiraling:** In contrast to the cyclical nutrient cycles of terrestrial or lentic systems, nutrients in rivers are transported downstream as they are used and recycled by organisms. This process, known as nutrient spiraling, is a fundamental characteristic of lotic ecosystems. The length of a spiral (the distance a nutrient atom travels before being taken up by the biota again) is a key measure of ecosystem efficiency, influenced by flow, biological activity, and retention structures like woody debris.

*   **River Continuum Concept (RCC):** This influential model describes a predictable change in physical conditions and associated biological communities from a stream's headwaters to its mouth. It posits that the balance between allochthonous (externally produced, like leaves) and autochthonous (internally produced, like algae) energy sources shifts along the gradient, structuring the invertebrate functional feeding groups (e.g., shredders, collectors, grazers, predators).
