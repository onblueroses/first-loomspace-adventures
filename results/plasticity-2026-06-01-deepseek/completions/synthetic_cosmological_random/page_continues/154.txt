### **Key Ecological Concepts and Processes**

*   **River Continuum Concept:** This model describes how the physical and biological characteristics of a river change predictably from its headwaters to its mouth. It explains the shift from a heterotrophic system (dependent on external organic matter) in forested headwaters to a more autotrophic system (driven by in-stream photosynthesis) in mid-reaches, and often back to heterotrophic in large, turbid rivers.
*   **Nutrient Spiraling:** In a flowing system, nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus are not simply recycled in one place. They are taken up by organisms, released back into the water, and transported downstream before being taken up again. This downstream movement combined with biological cycling is called "spiraling." The length of a spiral is a key indicator of ecosystem efficiency.
