### **Key Ecological Concepts in River Ecosystems**

*   **River Continuum Concept:** This foundational model describes a river as a continuously integrated system from headwaters to mouth. It predicts a shift in the primary food source: from **allochthonous** (externally produced, like leaves) in shaded headwaters to **autochthonous** (internally produced by algae and plants) in wider, sunlit mid-reaches.
*   **Nutrient Spiraling:** In flowing water, nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus are not simply recycled in place as in a lake. They are carried downstream while being taken up and released by organisms, creating a "spiral" path. The length of a spiral (the distance a nutrient atom travels before being reused) is a key measure of ecosystem efficiency.
*   **Functional Feeding Groups:** Invertebrates are often categorized by their method of obtaining food, which changes predictably downstream:
    *   **Shredders:** Break down coarse leaf litter (dominant in headwaters).
