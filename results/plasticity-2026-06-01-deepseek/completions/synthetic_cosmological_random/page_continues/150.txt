**Human Impacts and Conservation of River Ecosystems**

Human activities have profoundly altered river ecosystems worldwide, often degrading their health and diminishing the services they provide. Key threats include:

*   **Flow Alteration:** Dams and water diversions disrupt the natural flow regime, which is the "master variable" controlling ecological processes. They can block fish migration, eliminate natural flood pulses essential for floodplain health, and alter water temperature and sediment transport downstream.
*   **Pollution:** Inputs from agricultural runoff (fertilizers, pesticides), industrial discharge, and urban wastewater increase nutrient loads (eutrophication), introduce toxins, and lower dissolved oxygen levels (e.g., creating "dead zones").
*   **Habitat Destruction and Fragmentation:** Channelization for flood control or navigation straightens and hardens banks, destroying in-stream habitat and disconnecting the river from its floodplain. Riparian deforestation removes shade, stabilizing roots, and critical inputs of organic matter.
*   **Invasive Species:** Non-native plants, fish, and invertebrates can outcompete native species, alter food webs, and change physical habitat structure.
