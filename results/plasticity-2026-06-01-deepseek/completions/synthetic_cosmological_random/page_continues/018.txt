### **Key Ecological Processes in River Ecosystems**

The dynamic nature of rivers is governed by several key processes:

*   **Nutrient Spiraling:** In contrast to the relatively closed cycling in lakes, nutrients in rivers are transported downstream as they are used and released by organisms. This process, known as spiraling, describes how an atom of a nutrient (e.g., nitrogen or phosphorus) cycles between dissolved, particulate, and organic forms while moving downstream. The length of a spiral (the distance an atom travels before being taken up again) is a critical measure of ecosystem function.
*   **River Continuum Concept:** This model describes a predictable change in physical conditions and associated biological communities from a stream's headwaters to its mouth. It links the energy sources (allochthonous leaf litter vs. autochthonous in-stream algae) to the functional feeding groups of invertebrates (e.g., shredders in headwaters, collectors in mid-reaches).
