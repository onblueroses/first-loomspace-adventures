"You've described the theory perfectly. But on the ground, right now, it's the modifications that tell the story. I was just upstream taking measurements. The dam they put in forty years ago for the old mill? It's created a textbook discontinuity.

The reach above it is practically lentic—warm, silty, with a completely different insect community. Below it, the water released from the bottom of the reservoir is cold and nutrient-poor year-round, but the flow is artificial, based on power demand, not season. The native fish cues are all off. And the riparian zone... cleared for agriculture right to the bank's edge. No leaf litter, no shade, no root structure to stabilize the soil.

The theory says this should be a thriving middle reach. Instead, it's a simplified, stressed system. The community isn't just altered; it's been fractured into pieces that no longer connect. The catchment isn't just a geological concept anymore; it's a map of every upstream decision."
