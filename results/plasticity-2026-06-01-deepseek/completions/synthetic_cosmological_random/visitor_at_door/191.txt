**A River Ecosystem: A Summary**

A river ecosystem is a dynamic, flowing system shaped by moving water. Its character changes dramatically from source to mouth, creating distinct zones:

*   **Headwaters (Upper Reaches):** Cold, fast, oxygen-rich water flows over rocks and gravel. The food web is based on **external inputs** like leaves and wood from the forest. Life is adapted to strong currents (e.g., insect larvae that cling, trout).

*   **Middle & Lower Reaches:** The river widens, slows, and warms. The bed becomes sandy/silty. **Internal production** by algae and aquatic plants becomes the main energy source. Fish communities diversify to include herbivores and omnivores. Floodplains connect the river to the land, exchanging nutrients.

*   **The Mouth:** Often forms an **estuary**, a brackish transition zone to the sea, with species adapted to changing salinity.
