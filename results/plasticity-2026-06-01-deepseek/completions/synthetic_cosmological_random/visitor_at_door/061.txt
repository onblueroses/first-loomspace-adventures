The visitor, having listened intently, nods thoughtfully. "That's a comprehensive explanation," they say. "It makes me think of a specific river. I've noticed the stretch near the old mill is now mostly silt, the water is warm, and I see a lot of carp and bass. But upstream, by the forest preserve, it's all rocky, fast, and cold—I've only ever seen trout there."

They lean slightly against the doorframe, a concerned look crossing their face. "My question is about the connection. The farmlands have expanded right up to the banks in the middle section. The trees are gone there. If the 'energetic base' for the headwaters is leaf litter from the bordering forest... what happens to that trout stream when its middle reaches can no longer function as a corridor? Is the health of the source dependent on the health of the mouth?"
