The visitor, having read the display on river ecosystems, looks up and says thoughtfully:

"It's a reminder that a river is more than just water in a channel. It's a living system, from the cold mountain springs all the way to the muddy, salty end. It makes you think—when we build a dam, pave a bank, or pollute a stream, we're not just changing the water. We're dismantling an entire community, link by link, from the insects clinging to the rocks to the fish that depend on the seasonal flood. That health checklist—flow, sediment, temperature, oxygen, the plants along the edge—it's like a vital signs chart for the river. We ignore it at the river's peril, and our own."

They pause, then add:

"It makes me want to go find my local stream and see it not just as a pretty view, but as a story in motion. To see if it has its shredders and its gravel, or if it's warmed and silted, and what that says about the land it flows through."
