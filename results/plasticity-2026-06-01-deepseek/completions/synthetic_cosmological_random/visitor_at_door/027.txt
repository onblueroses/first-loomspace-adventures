The visitor at the door listens quietly, then nods thoughtfully.  

“So a river isn’t just water moving downhill—it’s more like a living thread, changing from start to end, shaped by the land it drains. And what we do on that land can unravel the whole system.”  

They pause, looking out toward the nearby stream just visible through the trees.  

“You’ve described a world in motion. Even the food web begins with leaves falling from the banks. If we cut those trees or warm that water, everything shifts—down to the insects and the fish.”  

A moment of silence hangs between you before they add:  

“It makes me wonder… is the river outside still healthy? Are there still trout in the headwaters? Does the water still run clear over gravel, or have we already changed it without realizing?”
