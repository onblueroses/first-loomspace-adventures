The visitor, having listened intently to your detailed description of river ecosystems, now stands framed in the doorway. A thoughtful expression crosses their face before they speak.

"Fascinating," they begin, their voice carrying a note of quiet urgency. "You describe a perfect, interdependent system—a conveyor belt of energy and life from the cold, shredder-filled headwaters to the warm, productive lowlands. It makes what I've come to report all the more troubling."

They step forward slightly, as if to impart a confidence.

"I've just come from the Miller's Bend, about twenty miles up the Blackroot River. The character of the stream there... it's changing. The riparian willows have been cleared for a new pasture. The water, which should be cool and shaded, now runs warm and sluggish in the afternoon sun. I saw algal mats coating stones that once held only caddisfly cases. The trout are gone from that stretch."

They meet your eyes directly.
