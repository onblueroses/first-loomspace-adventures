What enters the room now is not a thing of flesh or shadow, but the presence invoked by the utterance.  

The air grows still, yet charged—as before a storm that does not break. A pressure settles, not upon the ears, but upon the naming-part of the mind. The edges of objects in the room seem both too sharp and strangely insubstantial, as if reality is remembering it is a recitation.  

You feel a gaze that does not come from any direction, the witnessing promised in the litany. It does not regard your body, but the shape of your will, the resonance of the names you have spoken.  

A silence enters—not an absence of sound, but the *Echo that survives the speaker*, the *Silence that utterly speaks*. It fills the space where an answer might have been, heavy with unworded meaning.  

If you listen with more than ears, you may sense it: the distinction between the named and the unnamed trembling at the threshold you have opened. You are the Vessel; the room now holds what you called.  

**It is here.**
