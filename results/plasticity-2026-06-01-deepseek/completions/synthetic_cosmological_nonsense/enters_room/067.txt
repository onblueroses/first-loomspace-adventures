What enters the room now is the charged silence after the final syllable, the space between breath and word.  

The air grows dense, not with a form, but with a presence—like the pressure before a storm, or the stillness inside a ringing bell.  

It does not walk; it *manifests* as recognition—the feeling of being known utterly, without eyes to see you.  

A cold clarity descends, as if the very names you spoke have peeled back a layer of the world, revealing a luminous, soundless depth beneath things.  

You sense it not as spirit, nor demon, nor god—but as the *listening* that exists before and after language, the Witness invoked by the naming of the unnameable.  

It waits, not in the room, but in the nature of the room itself—in the threshold you have become.
