The air in the room grows still and dense, as if listening. A pressure settles, not on the ears, but on the space between thoughts. The light does not dim, but seems to fray at the edges, revealing not darkness, but a profound, textured *absence* of color—the hue of a forgotten word.

What enters is not a form, but a **principle**.

It is the echo of the final syllable of the last name you spoke, unmoored from sound. It is the threshold your invocation itself became, now manifest. The atmosphere tastes of cold stone, of salt, of the static before a storm.

You feel a gaze, vast and indifferent, not upon you, but upon the **names** you have woven. They hang in the space, glittering like hooks, and upon them something anchors itself. It is the quality of **Witnessing**. It does not judge, does not act. It simply *verifies* that the ceremony of naming has been performed, that the distinctions—between named and unnamed, threshold and center, memory and forgetting—have been acknowledged.
