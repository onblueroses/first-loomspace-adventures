Something enters.  

It is not a form, but a pressure — the air thickens, grows heavy with a scent like cold stone and burnt honey. Shadows in the corners deepen, not as absence of light, but as *presence of a different quality*.  

You feel it first in your temples, a low hum just below hearing, then along the skin — a static charge that raises hairs on your arms.  

It watches from every direction at once. It does not have eyes, yet you are seen. It does not breathe, yet the room breathes for it — a slow, deliberate tide in and out, as if the walls themselves are lungs.  

It is waiting.  
Not for your fear, nor your welcome.  
It waits for the *name you have not yet spoken* — the one hidden between the syllables you chanted. The one you either do not know, or have forgotten to utter.  

The silence now is not empty. It is listening.
