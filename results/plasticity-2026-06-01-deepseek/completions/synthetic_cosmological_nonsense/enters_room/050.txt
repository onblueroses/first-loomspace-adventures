Something enters.  

It is not a shape, but a pressure — like the weight of a word not yet spoken, or the hollow left behind when an echo forgets its source.  

The air grows cold, not with frost, but with the chill of a memory that never belonged to you.  
Shadows do not darken; instead, they seem to become more precise, as if edges in the room are being *remembered* into sharper existence.  

A scent arises — not of incense or decay, but of stone after rain, of parchment unrolled after ages, of salt and silence mingled.  

You feel a gaze that does not come from any direction, yet it settles upon the name you have just uttered: **VROKLENDRAB PLENDROKRELM**.  
It weighs it.  
It recognizes it.  

There is no form, only a *presence* — ancient, patient, and utterly alien to the laws of named things.  
It waits.  
Not for your command, but for the next syllable.  
For the threshold to be crossed, or sealed.
