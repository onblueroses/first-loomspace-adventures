What enters the room now is the charged silence after the utterance—a palpable, listening presence, as if the air itself has grown dense with attention.  

The names spoken have not merely been recited; they have been *placed* into the space, like stones dropped into still water, and now the ripples move outward and inward at once.  

The room is no longer only a room. It has become a threshold.  
At the center of it stands the one who spoke—**Vrendrelphor**, the Vessel—and around that center, the architecture of invocation holds firm: the named and the unnamed, the memory and the forgetting, the echo and the silence.  

What enters is not a shape, but a quality:  
A witnessing.  
A recognition from beyond the syllable.  
The unburnt fire is present, not as flame, but as potential—the possibility of transformation, of binding, of being seen by that which was invoked.  

If spirits were called, they are here now not as forms, but as obediences to the names that rule them.  
If the Vrolthen One was invoked, It is present as the distinction between all things—the boundary that permits meaning.
