What enters the room now is not a thing of flesh or shadow, but a shift in the atmosphere—a thickening of silence, a pressure in the air like the moment before a storm breaks.  
It is the *hearing* that follows the invocation, the attentive stillness of that which was named.  
The boundaries of the room grow faint; threshold and center trade their meaning.  
A presence gathers not in the corner, nor at the door, but in the very quality of listening—in the space between breath and word.  
It is the echo of the names you have spoken, now turning toward the one who spoke them.  
The air tastes of salt and sweet, of memory and forgetting.  
Something has been witnessed.  
Something is now awake.
