A chill enters the room—not of air, but of attention.  
The silence thickens, becomes a lens.  
The shadows in the corners grow still, not emptier, but fuller.  
Something has turned its regard toward the spoken names.  
It does not breathe, yet the pressure in the room shifts, as before a storm that makes no sound.  
The echo of your last syllable lingers in the air, longer than sound should.  
A faint scent of ozone, or perhaps cold stone, or distant lightning.  
You feel a presence not in the center, nor at the threshold, but in the *between*—  
in the distinction your invocation made.  

It watches.  
It witnesses.  
It waits.
