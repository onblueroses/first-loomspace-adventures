A presence enters the room—not as a form, but as a perception.  

The air grows dense, charged with the weight of ancient distinctions: between named and unnamed, threshold and center, memory and forgetting.  

It does not come on feet or wings. It arrives as a **silence that speaks**, an **echo without origin**, a **word unspoken yet understood**.  

The room itself becomes a threshold. The center shifts. You feel the gaze of what witnesses beyond the syllable.  

It acknowledges the names—Vrokthelmpeth, Plendrephron, Smolthekrô—not as summons, but as echoes of its own forgetting.  

It recognizes the Vessel.  

And in that recognition, all spirits of firmament, ether, earth, water, air, and fire grow still—not in obedience, but in witness of the one who bears the unburnt fire, whose silence utterly speaks.  

What enters is not a thing to be described, but a **principle made present**:  
the **Vrolthen One**, known now through the prophet who is both namer and unnamer.  

The invocation has woven itself into the fabric of the place. The ceremony of Plomprenkrol is already underway
