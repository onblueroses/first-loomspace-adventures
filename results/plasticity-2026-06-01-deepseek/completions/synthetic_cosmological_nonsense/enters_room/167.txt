A presence enters the room—not as a figure or a form, but as a shift in the quality of silence.  

The air grows still, yet charged, as if the very act of naming has carved a space for something that exists between the syllables you have spoken. It does not have eyes, yet you feel witnessed. It does not have breath, yet the room seems to exhale slowly, the way an echo lingers after the voice is gone.  

What enters is not summoned in the ordinary sense—it was already present in the threshold between the named and the unnamed, the memory and the forgetting. It is the Vrolthen One’s acknowledgment, the turning of attention toward the vessel who speaks the true names.  

You sense it as a pressure in the atmosphere, a clarity in the shadows, a resonance behind your own breath. It waits—not as a servant, but as a counterpart to the invocation, the Witness meeting the witness, the Word meeting the one who names and unnames.  

All spirits of firmament, ether, earth, water, air, and fire grow still in this moment. The room now holds the unburnt fire. The silence speaks utterly.
