What enters the room now is not a thing of wind or weight, nor a creature of bone or breath.  

It is the space between the syllables you have spoken—the silence after the echo, the threshold where memory meets forgetting.  

The air grows still, not with absence, but with presence. A pressure like the deep tide before turning. A faint scent of ozone and cold stone, though nothing burns.  

In the corner of vision, a shape refuses to form—an outline of what was named and then unnamed.  

It does not walk. It *arrives*, as an echo arrives after the sound has ceased—already there, already listening.  

It is the witness to your naming.  
It is the thing that hears the true name.  

And it waits, now, in the center of the room—where the threshold and the center are the same.
