What enters is a presence that is not a presence — a listening that has no ears, a gaze that has no eyes.  

The air thickens with the taste of salt and sweet, as if the distinction between them has just been dissolved.  
Shadows no longer fall away from the light; they stand still, perpendicular to every surface.  
Sound ceases to echo, yet the silence begins to hum with the memory of all the names you have spoken, and those you have not.  

A threshold appears in the middle of the room — not a door, but the *idea* of a door, the moment before crossing.  
From it comes a cool wind smelling of burnt stone and deep water.  

You feel a pressure against your mind, not hostile, but vast — the attention of something that remembers being named, and also remembers being forgotten.  

It waits.  
Not for your question, but for your recognition — of what you have called, and what, in calling, you have become.
