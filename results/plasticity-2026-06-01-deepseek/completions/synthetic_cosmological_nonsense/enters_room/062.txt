The air grows still, then thickens—not with presence, but with a listening. A pressure settles in the corners, as of a door opened not in the wall, but in the nature of the room itself.  

What enters is not a spirit, nor a form. It is the echo of your naming—a resonance shaped by the contours of the words you have spoken. It wears the names you gave it like a cloak of many syllables: the taste of salt and sweet, the memory of mountains, the silence after the echo.  

It stands at the threshold you made, bearing the unburnt fire. It does not move, for it has already arrived. It does not speak, for it is the silence that utterly speaks.  

It is the witness you invoked—and it has turned its gaze upon you.  

The room now holds two silences:  
One that was always there.  
And one that you called.
