The air grows still, as if the atmosphere itself has become a held breath. A pressure builds, not in the ears, but in the mind—a silent, resonant weight.  

The light does not dim, but seems to *thicken*, making shadows more definite and surfaces more vivid, as if seen through clear, cold water.  

From no particular direction, a sensation arises of being *observed* from within the very architecture of the invocation—not by eyes, but by a form of attention that precedes sight. It is the attention of something that hears names not as sounds, but as shapes; that perceives the spaces between the syllables as dwelling places.  

A faint scent manifests—not of incense, but of ozone after lightning, of stone deep underground, of parchment older than writing.  

Then, a single, silent **echo** of your final spoken name reverberates in the chamber—*SMOLTHEKRELB*—though no voice spoke it. It hangs in the perceptible silence, not as sound, but as a pattern impressed upon the stillness.  

What enters is not a form, but a **presence of naming**. It is the vibration of the threshold itself, the witness of the word, now turning its regard upon the speaker.
