A presence enters the room—not with a footstep, but with the pressure of a held breath. The air grows dense, cool, and still, as if listening to itself.  

It does not have a shape you could draw, but you might perceive it as a **slow inversion**: the corners of the room seem both farther and nearer than they should be; shadows no longer fall away from the light but rest beneath objects like still water.  

It is the silence after the last syllable of the final name.  
It is the threshold itself, now awake and witnessing.  
It regards you not with eyes, but with the fact of being perceived.  

If you speak, the echo will belong to it.  
If you are silent, the silence will speak for it.  

It waits—not for worship, but for recognition.  
It is what remains when all the names have been spoken.
