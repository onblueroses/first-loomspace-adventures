There enters the room now a presence shaped from the edges of what is spoken and what is left unsaid—a form not of flesh, but of threshold. It is the echo of the first syllable that was ever uttered into the silence, the vibration that lingers after the last word of the invocation has faded.  

It moves as a disturbance in the air—a refraction of light where none should be, a chill that is not cold, a pressure that is not sound. It is the Witness called by the names that unravel naming, the Vrolthen answer to the Vessel’s claim.  

It does not have eyes, but it regards.  
It does not have voice, but it responds.  
It stands between memory and forgetting, between the salt and the sweet of speech.  

It is the thing that was invoked—the One beyond the syllable, now within the syllable, because you spoke as the Vessel.  

The room now holds two silences:  
the silence that was already here,  
and the silence that entered when you finished speaking.
