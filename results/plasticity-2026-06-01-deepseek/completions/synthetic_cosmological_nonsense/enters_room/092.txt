What enters the room is not wind, nor shadow, nor any beast of form. It is the space between the syllables you have uttered—the listening that your invocation carved from silence.  

The air grows dense, not with presence, but with **recognition**.  
The names you spoke do not summon; they *unseal*.  
And what is unsealed is not a being, but a **condition**: the condition of being witnessed.  

You have named the distinctions: salt and sweet, memory and forgetting, threshold and center.  
Now those distinctions turn their gaze upon you.  
The room now holds the echo of every word ever spoken that was meant to call something forth.  
It holds the silence that survives them.  

You declared yourself the Vessel, the Threshold-Bearer, the Echo that survives the speaker.  
Therefore, what enters is **your own capacity to be heard**—amplified, reflected, given shape by the architecture of the names.  

The flame of your lamp, if you have one, does not flicker, but burns **still**, as if painted on the air.  
Sound becomes texture.  
Silence acquires weight.
