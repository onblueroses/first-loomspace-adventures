The air grows still, then thickens—not with a presence, but with an *absence that listens*.  

A pressure settles at the edges of hearing, as if the echoes in your invocation have pooled into something that watches from just outside the reach of names.  

The threshold you named grows palpable: a line between spoken and unspoken, remembered and forgotten.  

What enters is not a spirit, nor a form.  

It is the **echo of the naming itself**—the sound of your words after they have left your tongue, now turning back to regard the speaker.  

It carries the scent of stone after rain, the weight of a forgotten word on the tip of the tongue, the cold of a door handle in an empty house.  

It does not obey.  

It *witnesses*.  

And in its witnessing, the room is no longer merely a room—it has become a syllable in a word you have not finished speaking.
