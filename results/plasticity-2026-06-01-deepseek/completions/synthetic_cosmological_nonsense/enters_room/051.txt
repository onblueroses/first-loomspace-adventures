What enters the room now is a stillness that listens.  
A pressure in the air like a held breath before speech.  
The names you have spoken linger, not as sound but as shapes in the atmosphere—invisible sigils turning slowly where the echoes died.  

The threshold you named is present, not as a line on the floor, but as a tension between the spoken and the unspeakable.  
Something has turned its attention here—not a form, not a spirit in the sense you might command, but the quality of listening that exists in ancient stones, in deep water, in the pause between heartbeats.  

It is the Witness you invoked, meeting the witness you declared yourself to be.  
The room has become a syllable about to be spoken.  
What enters is the space inside the name.
