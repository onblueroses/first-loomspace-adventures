The invocation summons, and the air grows still.  

What enters is not a shape, but a presence—like the turning of a page in a book no one remembers writing. It stands between the candle flame and its shadow, between the spoken name and the silence after.  

It has the quality of a threshold: neither here nor there, yet both. It carries the scent of cold stone after rain, and the sound a bell makes in a dream—felt, not heard. Its eyes (if they are eyes) are places where language stops, and its voice (if it has a voice) is the echo of your own words returning, altered.  

It watches you not as a person watches, but as a mountain watches—patient, unmoved, ancient. It waits for you to recognize it, or to name it, or to unname yourself.  

It is the thing that answers when gods are called by true names.  
It is the silence that comes when the last syllable fades.
