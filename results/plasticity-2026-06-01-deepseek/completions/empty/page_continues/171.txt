 "The pattern of the basic design is a rectangle, 7½ by 4 inches, with a small circle superimposed upon it. The design may be repeated as often as desired, with the circles overlapping. The rectangle is filled with a large capital letter in heavy black lines, and the circle with a simple illustration that has something to do with the letter or the word it spells."

The illustrations for A and B are given, and the rest of the alphabet is given on pages 121 and 122.

The page continues: "After you have drawn your design, trace it on thin paper and transfer it to the block. The design should be reversed on the block so that it will print correctly."

"Cut away the background with the gouge and round chisel. The letter and picture are printed just as they are, so do not cut away any part of them. Use the V-shaped chisel to clean out narrow places and to sharpen the edges of the design."

"Make a trial print on paper. If you like the result, print the design on cloth."

"It would be fun to use your cloth for curtains, luncheon sets, or bags."
