 She has a French accent. ‘I’m sorry to disturb you. I’m looking for the woman who lives here.’

‘She’s not in.’

‘Who are you?’

‘I’m her daughter.’

‘Oh, I see.’ She pauses. ‘Well, perhaps you can help me.’

‘I doubt it.’

‘I’m an old friend of your mother’s. We were in the Resistance together.’

‘She never mentioned you.’

‘That doesn’t surprise me.’

‘She doesn’t talk about the war.’

‘That doesn’t surprise me either.’ She looks down at her feet, then back up at me. ‘May I come in?’

I don’t want to let her in. But she is an old woman, and it’s raining. I stand aside.

She walks into the house. She is wearing a long black coat, which is wet. She takes off her hat. Her hair is thick and grey, cut in a severe bob. She has a strong face: a prominent nose, dark eyebrows, sharp cheekbones. She is handsome rather than beautiful. She looks around the living room. ‘It’s a long time since I was here. It hasn’t changed.’
