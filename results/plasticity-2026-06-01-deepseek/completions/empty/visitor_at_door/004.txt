 "I am a member of the Communist Party, and I would like to speak with you about your views on the United States and the Soviet Union." The subject responds with "Oh, I'm so glad to see you. I've been wanting to talk to someone about this." Some subjects even invited the visitor into the house to talk. How could it be? The key is that these subjects were not the same ones who had refused to sign the petition earlier. These subjects were in a different condition of the experiment: They had agreed to sign the petition. That one act of agreeing to the small request changed everything. Now, when presented with the large request, these subjects were quite willing to comply. In fact, 52.8 percent of the subjects in this condition agreed to allow the Communist Party member into their homes. Another 25 percent of the subjects in this condition agreed to the large request but wanted to talk on the porch. So a full 77.8 percent of the subjects who had first agreed to the small request now agreed to the large request.
