 "My name is Robert M. LaFollette. I am running for governor. I would like to see Mr. Rose."
The maid asks him to wait. A few minutes later, she ushers him into the parlor.
"Mr. Rose," she says, "this is Robert M. LaFollette."
"Good evening, Mr. LaFollette," says Rose, rising. "I am sorry to keep you waiting."
"That's all right, sir," says LaFollette. "I am running for governor. I am going to be elected. I should like to have your vote."
Rose is a conservative, but he likes LaFollette's frankness. He asks him to sit down and talk. LaFollette does. He talks for an hour. He outlines his program. He answers questions. He does not ask for money; he asks for votes. He has a winning personality. He is sincere. He makes a good impression. Rose votes for him. He gives him his support. He becomes one of his lifelong friends.
In this way, LaFollette wins support from conservatives. He wins support from liberals. He wins support from the people. He is elected governor. He is re-elected governor. He is elected United States senator
