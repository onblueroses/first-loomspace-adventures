The visitor at the door speaks, and the sound of their voice hangs in the air between you, carrying the weight of all unopened doors and unexpected arrivals.

It could be a familiar cadence—a friend dropping by unannounced, their tone bright and easy. Or perhaps it’s the measured, polite phrasing of a stranger—a delivery person, a canvasser, a neighbor you’ve never met. Sometimes the voice is strained, hurried with urgency; other times it’s soft, hesitant, almost lost in the rustle of the wind.

You haven’t seen their face yet. The door is still closed, or perhaps only cracked, a chain still fastened. For now, you only have the words, the timbre, the intention woven into the greeting or the question they’ve just uttered.

What do they say?
