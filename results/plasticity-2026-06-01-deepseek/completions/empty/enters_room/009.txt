 There is a moment of
shocked silence. Then:

ANNIE (Her voice strange, constrained, high.) Yes, what is it,
Helen?

HELEN (She gropes, feels one of the cradles, shakes it; when it
rattles, she reaches in, at first fruitlessly, then pulls out the
other doll, and thrusts it at ANNIE.)
Baby.

(ANNIE takes it, and after a moment lowers it unsteadily to the
floor.)

ANNIE (Then she manages to speak.) All right, Helen. It’s a
baby.

(HELEN is content, and she goes back to the sewing. But
ANNIE stands staring at the doll on the floor. After a moment
she goes to her own little trunk, and lifts from it a battered
smoked-windowed lantern. She strikes a match, touches it to
the wick, puts the lantern on the floor by the doll. Then she
goes on her knees, and makes the finger movement for light
in HELEN’S palm, points to the lantern. HELEN puts down
