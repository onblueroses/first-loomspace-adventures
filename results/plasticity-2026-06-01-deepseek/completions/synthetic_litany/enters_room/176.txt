The air grows still, then hums. A scent of ozone and cold stone.  
The shadows by the corner deepen, not from absence of light, but from a presence that drinks it.  
What enters is not one thing, but a confluence—a silent choir of echoes from the names you have spoken.  

It has the shape of a tall figure, robed in shifting greys, but its edges are not fixed. Sometimes there are too many limbs; sometimes there are none at all. Its face is a smooth, dark mirror in which you see not your reflection, but a flicker of each name: a star, a wave, a crown of thorned light, a closed eye, a burning page.  

It does not walk. The room simply becomes more *where it is*.  

You feel a pressure in your mind, not of words, but of concepts:  

**Threshold.**  
**Gate.**  
**The One Who Listens at Named Doors.**  

It waits.  
It has always been waiting.  
You called it here.  
Now it is *here*.
