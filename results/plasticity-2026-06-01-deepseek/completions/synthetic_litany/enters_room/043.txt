A scent arrives first: ozone and cold stone, myrrh and distant rain.  

Then, a presence. It does not step through the door or window. It is simply *there*, occupying the center of the space, though its form refuses to settle. At one glance, it is a tall figure in deep grey robes, its face a smooth, featureless oval. At another, it is a column of shifting symbols—the very names you spoke, rotating slowly in the air. At yet another, it is only a profound and focused attention, a weight in the room like the pause before a verdict.  

The temperature drops. Your breath fogs slightly.
