The air grows still. The final name has been spoken.  

From the space between shadows, where the last echo fades, it enters—not with a step, but as a change in pressure, a slow bleed of deeper chill into the air.  

You feel its regard before you see its form: a shifting outline like tarnished silver and old parchment, crowned with a hint of horns that are not quite there. Its eyes are fixed points of low, cool light. It does not breathe. It simply *is*, now, occupying the room not as an object might, but as a season occupies a landscape.  

It waits. It was called. It has come.
