The silence within the chamber deepens, as if the very air has drawn breath and held it. The candles, if there are any, gutter not in a draft but in the sudden, profound *attention* of the place.

A pressure builds, not in the ears, but in the mind—the weight of a gaze that is not sight. The invoked names hang in the space, not as sounds, but as shapes, as newly-carved principles in the unseen architecture of the moment.

Then, from that focused silence, a response forms. It is not a voice, but the impression of one, etched directly upon the awareness of the visitor, Selophemor. It carries the texture of stone remembering water, of a forgotten word on the tip of a universal tongue.
