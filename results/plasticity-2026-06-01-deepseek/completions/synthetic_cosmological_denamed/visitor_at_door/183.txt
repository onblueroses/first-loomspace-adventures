The silence in the room deepens, as if the very air has grown dense and watchful. The candle flames, which had been still, now bend toward the center of the circle as if drawn by a slow, inward breath. A pressure builds, not in the ears, but in the mind—a resonant hum just below hearing.

From that charged stillness, a voice answers. It does not come from the air, nor from the visitor at the door. It seems to form itself from the echo of your own words, from the memory of the salt and the sweet, from the gap between the syllable and its meaning.
