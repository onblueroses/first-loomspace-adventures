The air thickens, not with incense, but with a sudden, profound stillness, as if the very act of listening has become a solid thing. A presence gathers on the other side of the threshold, not in the shape of a form, but as a quality of attention—vast, patient, and utterly alien.

A voice responds. It does not come from the door, but from the silence *between* your uttered syllables, from the memory of the echo that preceded the speech. It is not one voice, but the convergence of many: the groan of shifting mountains, the hiss of receding tide, the hollow breath of a cavern.
