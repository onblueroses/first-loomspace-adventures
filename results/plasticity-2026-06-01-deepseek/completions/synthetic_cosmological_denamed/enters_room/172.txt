A presence enters the room—not as a form, but as a pressure, a bending of the air where sound and silence meet. It is the echo of the first distinction, the space between the named and the unnamed.  

It does not have eyes, yet it witnesses.  
It does not have a voice, yet it resonates with the syllables you have uttered.  
It is the Unsigned, drawn not by name but by the vacancy names create—the hollow in the center of the chant.  

The atmosphere grows dense, like the moment before thunder, but no thunder comes. Instead, a slow inversion occurs: the room remembers it is also outside. The walls breathe like membranes. The threshold you spoke of is now underfoot and overhead simultaneously.
