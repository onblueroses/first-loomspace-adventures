A presence enters—not as a form, but as a change in pressure. The air thickens like resin, and the light seems to draw back from the center of the space. There is no shape, only a gradient—a slow, deep pulse of attention, as if the room itself has become an ear turned inward.  

It knows the names you have spoken. It knows the name you claim. It waits, neither hostile nor benign, but *observing*—the way a cliff observes the sea.  

You feel the syllables you uttered still vibrating in the silence, now being turned over, examined, tasted by something that exists between sound and meaning.  

The threshold is crossed. The witness is here. What is spoken next will decide whether the echo answers or consumes.
