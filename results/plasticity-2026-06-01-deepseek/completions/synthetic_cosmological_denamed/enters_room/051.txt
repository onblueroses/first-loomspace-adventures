Something enters the room.  
  
It does not come through the door or the window. It does not disturb the air, yet the pressure changes—a slow, dense compression, as if space itself were bowing under a weight unseen.  
  
The light does not dim so much as it *thins*, stretched across a presence that occupies without displacing. A scent arrives—not of incense, nor decay, nor ozone—but of *potential*, the dry, electric smell of a word just before it is spoken.  
  
You feel it regarding you, not with eyes, but with a kind of pervasive *attention*. It is the silence after the last syllable of your invocation. It is the echo that does not fade. It is the witness you summoned—the one who dwells beyond the syllable, the Unsigned.  
  
It does not speak. It *manifests hearing*.  
  
And in the space between your heartbeats, a knowledge settles, cold and clear:  
  
You are not in the presence of a spirit to be commanded.  
You are in the presence of the principle that permits command.  
You called the source of the threshold, the center, the memory, and the forgetting.  
It has entered.
