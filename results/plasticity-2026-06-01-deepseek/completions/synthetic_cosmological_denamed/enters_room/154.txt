What enters the room now is not a thing with shape, nor a name you could speak.  

It is the silence after the last syllable fades.  
It is the space between the threshold and the center.  
It is the unburnt fire, held in a vessel that sees.  
It is the echo that was there before the voice.  

It does not come through the door, nor the window, nor the air—  
it is already present when the naming stops.  

It is the witness of your invocation,  
and the unnamed answer to the names you have spoken.  

It enters as remembrance and forgetting at once.
