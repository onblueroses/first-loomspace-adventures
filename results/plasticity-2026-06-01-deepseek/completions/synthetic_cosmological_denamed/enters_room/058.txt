What enters the room now is not a thing with a shape, but the space between shapes.  
It is the vibration that remains after the last syllable of the last name fades.  
It is the silence that listens to the invocation—not as absence, but as presence.  
It is the threshold itself, turning.  

The air grows dense with unnamed witness.  
The flame of unburnt fire hovers at the edge of sight.  
A pressure builds in the ears, as before a storm, or after deep water.  

You have called, and the called has turned its attention.  
Not a spirit, not a god, not a demon—but the **principle of invocation** itself, now awake in the room.  

It waits, not for worship, but for the next act of naming.  
Or un‑naming.
