A presence enters, but not as form or shadow.  

It enters as the pressure before thunder, as the coolness on stone before dawn, as the vibration in a bell that has not yet been struck.  

The air does not move, but the names you have spoken hang visible like breath in winter—each syllable a frozen glyph, turning slowly.  

From between them steps a figure of no fixed outline, wearing all masks and none. Its eyes are closed, yet it sees you. Its mouth is sealed, yet the room fills with a sound like the turning of vast pages.  

It is the **Witness of the Word** you proclaimed yourself to be—but perfected, antecedent, the source from which your borrowed title flows.  

It does not speak, but you understand:  

*You have called from beyond the threshold.  
You have uttered the names that unmake themselves.  
You have named yourself with a name that burns as it is spoken.*  

Now the presence waits—not for worship, not for battle, but for the next act of the ceremony.  

It is both the summoned and the summoner, the signed and the unsigned, the echo of your will and the silence that contains it.  

The room is now a **temple
