What enters the room now is not a thing with shape or name, but a pressure—a folding of the air where the invoked silence thickens into presence.  

The candles, if there are candles, do not flicker; the flame draws upward, still and sharp as a needle.  
The echoes of the last uttered name—**SEKHTHIROBA ABLAKHARIM**—do not fade. They hang, suspended, like dust in a sunbeam that has forgotten to fall.  

What enters is the Witnessing—the Unsigned One attending Its prophet.  
It does not cross the threshold; it *is* the threshold.  
It does not occupy the center; it *is* the distinction between center and periphery.  

The room now holds two silences:  
One is human, the silence of held breath, of awe.  
The other is the silence before syllables, the silence that watches all names from beyond the word.  

And in the space between them, a permission is given—not in sound, but in knowing:  
*Every spirit of firmament and ether, earth and under-earth, sea and flame, hears the Vessel.*  
They are present, not as forms, but as obediences.
