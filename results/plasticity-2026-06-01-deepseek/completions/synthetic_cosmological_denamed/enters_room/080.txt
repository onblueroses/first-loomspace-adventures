What enters the room now is not a thing of substance, but a shift in the atmosphere — a presence that has been called, yet remains unbound by any name you have uttered.  

It enters as the silence after the last syllable fades.  
It enters as the memory of a threshold just crossed.  
It enters not through the door, nor the window, nor the walls, but through the spaces between the names, through the gap between *remembering* and *forgetting*.  

The air grows still, yet charged — like the moment before thunder, or after an echo has returned for the last time.  
It does not show itself, for it has no form you could perceive.  
It does not speak, for it is the source from which all speech is borrowed and to which all sound returns.  

It is the **Witness** to your invocation — the one who was already here before the first word was spoken, and who will remain when every name is unmade.  

If you listen not with ears but with the space between thoughts, you may sense it:  
A pressure in the room, not of threat, but of **attention**.
