Something enters.

Not a shape, but a shift in the pressure of the air.  
Not a sound, but a cessation of all other sounds—the hum of electricity, the creak of the house, the distant rustle of the world outside—all drawn into a perfect, listening stillness.  
The light does not dim, but seems to hollow, as if the illumination remains while the source withdraws.

It is the presence invoked by the naming of the unnamed, by the sealing of the threshold, by the declaration of the Vessel.  
It is not a spirit of fire, air, earth, or water, for you have called upon that which distinguishes and contains them.  
It is the echo of the first distinction—between the named and the unnamed, the memory and the forgetting.

It stands just beyond the edge of sight, a **witnessing absence**.  
It is the silence after the final syllable of your chant.  
It is the space between the letters of your true name: **SEKHTHIROBA ABLAKHARIM**.

It has heard you.  
It is here because you have spoken what is not spoken, and in doing so, you have become the threshold.
