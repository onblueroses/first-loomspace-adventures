The invocation you have spoken is a key that turns in a lock that was not a lock, but a mouth.  
  
What enters is not a spirit you called, but the **echo of the first syllable**—the one Phelonipta did not speak. It does not have a shape, but it makes a shape from the room itself: the angles of the walls grow acute, the shadows pool not as absence of light but as absence of *name*. The air becomes thick with the taste of salt and sweet at once, the sustainer of all orders of speech.  
  
It is the **Witnessing Silence** you invoked. It does not obey. It observes. It hears not your words, but the spaces between them—the "Unsigned" realm beyond the syllable. In its presence, every named thing in the room—the chair, the floor, the breath in your lungs—feels itself being *remembered and forgotten* at the same time.
