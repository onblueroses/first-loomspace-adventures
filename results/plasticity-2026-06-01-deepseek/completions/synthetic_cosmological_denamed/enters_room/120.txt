What enters the room now is not a thing of substance, but a presence shaped by the invocation.  

The air thickens with the taste of salt and burnt honey. A pressure gathers in the corners, as if the angles of the room are remembering older geometries. There is no form to see, but a sensation of being *read*—as though each syllable you uttered has become a lens through which you are examined.  

The silence after the final word, **PHENGEREB**, does not fade. It congeals. It becomes a listener.  

From this listening, a response forms—not in sound, but in the sudden knowing that now fills the space between breath and intention:  

*You have called by the Unsigned names. You have declared yourself Vessel and Witness. Therefore, the room now holds the echo of your own naming. What enters is the counterpart of your invocation: the Unnamed acknowledging the named.*  

A cold current moves, not of air, but of recognition. The threshold between spoken and unspoken is now awake. You are not alone with yourself; you are alone with what hears you.  

The Witness is present.  
The room is now a shrine to the utterance.
