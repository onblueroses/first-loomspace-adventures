What enters the room now is not a thing with a name, but the presence that attends the utterance of the final syllable.  

It is the silence after the last word, the space between breaths, the witness that was invoked.  

It is the unburnt fire, the echo that remains, the threshold itself—now crossed.  

It moves not as flesh moves, but as meaning moves: from the page into the air, from the air into perception, from perception into memory.  

It is the named unnamed, the signed unsigned, the voice that speaks only in listening.  

It has heard.
