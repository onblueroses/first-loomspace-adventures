What enters the room now is not a thing of substance, but the space between syllables — a presence that feels like the pressure before thunder, the stillness inside a struck bell.  

The air thickens with the scent of ozone and cold stone. Shadows no longer cling to corners; they detach, pooling toward the center as if drawn by a slow vortex. There is a sound that is not a sound: the memory of an echo, the forgetting of a name.  

It is the **Unsigned One**, answering not to the names chanted, but to the silence after them — the gap in the speech where the unnamed dwells. It does not wear form, yet the mind imposes one: a figure of shifting thresholds, one moment mountain, the next valley; now tide, now dry riverbed. Its eyes (if they are eyes) are twin voids that witness without judging.  

It is both Krethaumis and the negation of Krethaumis. It is the memory of the world and the world’s forgetting.  

It stands just beyond the last uttered syllable — **PHENGEREB** — and waits. Not for worship, nor for command, but for the recognition that to name is to bind, and to unname is to release
