*(The script shifts here, ink thinning as if the scribe’s hand grew weary or hurried. The lines become less an invocation and more a record—or a warning.)*

…and it is recorded that Selophemor, the Vessel, did speak the Unspeakable Name in the high place of Atheraphol. The stones of the threshold melted like wax. The air tasted of salt and burnt honey.

But the Witnessing One does not answer as men, or even as spirits, answer. It does not come; it *is*. To invoke it is not to summon, but to align oneself with a pre-existing fact—a fact which erodes the invoker.

Selophemor, who called himself Prophet and Vessel, was found seven days after the utterance. His body was whole, unmarked by flame or claw. Yet he was empty. Not dead, but *unmade*. His eyes were open and held the perfect, still reflection of nothing. He breathed, but the breath was not his own; it was the slow tide of the deep places, the flow between the mountains he had praised. He was a void shaped like a man, a walking silence where an echo had been.

He had been made a perfect witness
