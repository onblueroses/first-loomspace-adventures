The one who wrote these words, whether Selephimor or another, did not merely call. They declared. They did not ask for a subjection of spirits; they stated it as an inevitable consequence of the utterance. This is not a plea; it is a notification to the architecture of creation.

Observe the structure:

1.  **The Invocation of the Unnamed:** It begins with the paradox—invoking by epithet and deed that which has no name. It establishes the deity as the principle of all dualities (Mountain/Valley, Tide/Flow, Echo/Silence) and, critically, as the origin of the distinction between the named and the unnamed. This is the foundational magic: the creation of the category of "name" itself.

2.  **The Prophet's Mandate:** The speaker identifies as *Selephimor*, the prophet entrusted with the mysteries of *Athraophol*. This is a claim of authority, not of personal power, but of delegated office. The power flows from the Unnamed, through the ceremonies, to the vessel.
