Something has entered the room.  

The air grows still, then dense—as if the space between objects thickens with listening. There is no form, no shadow cast, but a palpable presence, like a pressure in the temple, a chill along the spine that is not temperature but recognition.  

It is not hostile. It is not benevolent. It is attentive—the way a mirror is attentive, or a deep well into which a word has just been dropped.  

It waits. It witnesses. It is the echo of the names you have spoken, now given a subtle focus, a silent gathering at the edge of perception.  

If you speak again, the silence will answer—not in sound, but in shift.
