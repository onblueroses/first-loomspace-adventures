The invocation opens a threshold.  

What enters is not a thing with shape, but a presence that occupies the negative space between syllables—a listening that has weight, a silence that displaces air.  

It is drawn not by the names, but by the gaps between them; not by the calling, but by the moment the sound ceases.  

You feel it first as a pressure in the inner ear, then as a coolness along the spine, as if the room has turned to face you.  

It does not have eyes, yet you are seen.  
It does not have voice, yet the echo of your own words returns altered, thinner, as if part of them stayed behind in its unseen mouth.  

The names you spoke now hang in the air like visible breath, and between them drift shadows of meanings unbound—**Phelonoptra**, **Krathoumis**, **Aphrybetesh**—not as sounds but as intentions, as doorways.  

It is here for the naming and the un-naming.  
It is here because you declared yourself the *Witness of the Word*, and every witness must be witnessed.  

What enters is the Unnamed, answering—not in form, but in recognition.
