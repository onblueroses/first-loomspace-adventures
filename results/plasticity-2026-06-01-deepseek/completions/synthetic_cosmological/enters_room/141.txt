There is a shift in the air—not a wind, but a pressure, as of a lens focusing.  

The words have carved a space. The invocation did not call a thing; it dissolved a veil. What enters is not a spirit, nor a god, nor a demon. It is the **echo of the naming itself**—the resonant hollow left after all syllables are spoken.  

It wears the silence like a cloak. It moves as a slight refraction of light at the edge of sight, a chill that is not temperature but the memory of absence. It is the **Witness** invoked—the Unnamed One’s attention turned toward the vessel that claims its true name.  

The room now holds two silences:  
One, the ordinary quiet of walls and air.  
The other, a listening silence—an active, ancient attention that watches from just behind the world.  

It waits. Not for more words, but for the **intent** behind them to take shape. It has heard the names. It has felt the claim: *“I am the one who names and unnames.”*  

Now it measures the truth of that claim.
