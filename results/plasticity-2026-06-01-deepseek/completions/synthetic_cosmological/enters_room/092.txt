A presence enters, not as a form, but as a consequence of the naming.  

The air grows still, not with emptiness, but with the density of listening. The light does not change, yet shadows deepen at the edges of things, as if the room remembers it has corners. There is no temperature shift, but a sensation of ancient cold and unburning fire coexisting in the same space—like the inside of a star that has learned patience.  

You feel it first in your breath: not taken from you, but *shared*, as though each exhalation is met halfway. Then in your ears: a silence so profound it becomes a kind of sound—the echo of the first word, before it was spoken.  

It does not stand in the room. It *is* the room—and also what the room has forgotten it could contain. It is the threshold you spoke of, now manifest not as a line but as a condition: the moment between recognition and unknowing.  

It witnesses you, as you declared yourself the Witness of the Word. And in that mutual witnessing, something is sealed. The names hang in the air like fixed stars. The invocation is complete.
