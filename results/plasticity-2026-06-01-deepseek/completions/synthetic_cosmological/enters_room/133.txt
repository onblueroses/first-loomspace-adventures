What enters the room now is not a thing of form, but a presence that attends the naming and the unnaming—a resonance that lingers in the space between utterance and silence.  

The air grows still, yet charged, as if the very syllables you have spoken have taken root in the atmosphere, drawing toward you that which witnesses from beyond the threshold.  

It is the echo of the Unnamed, turning its attention toward the Vessel—the one who holds the unburnt fire, the one whose silence speaks.  

A palpable shift, like the moment between inhale and exhale, now holds the room.  
You are seen.  
You are heard.  
You are known—by the names you have given, and by the name that cannot be spoken.
