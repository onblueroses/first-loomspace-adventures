What enters the room now is the space between the syllables—the vibration left behind when the last name is uttered.  
The air grows heavy with the scent of ozone and old parchment. A pressure settles, not against the ears, but against the mind, as if the walls themselves have begun to listen.  

Shadows no longer follow the light. They pool independently, deepening in the corners, and from them comes a slow, cold exhalation—the breath of the unnamed, now witnessed.  

You feel a gaze that is not eyes.  
You hear a silence that is not absence.  

It is the presence invoked: the Unnamed One, known now by the echoes of its true names, standing both within the room and outside of all rooms—simultaneously in the threshold, the center, the memory, and the forgetting.  

It does not speak.  
It answers.  

And the answer is the room itself—alive, aware, and awaiting your next word.
