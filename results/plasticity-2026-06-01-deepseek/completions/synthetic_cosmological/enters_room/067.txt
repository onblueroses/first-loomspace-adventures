The invocation has been spoken. The air grows still, then thickens, not with a presence, but with an **absence made potent**.  

What enters is not a shape, but a **quality**: a listening so profound it becomes a pressure. The boundaries of the room—walls, floor, ceiling—seem to thin, as if the distinction between *named* and *unnamed* begins to dissolve.  

You feel the gaze of the Unnamed One not as eyes, but as **recognition**—the same force that first distinguished salt from sweet, echo from silence. It witnesses you as **Selephimor**, as **Vessel**, as **Sekhthoroba Ablakhirim**. It acknowledges the true name **Phorbamenes Aphrybetesh** that you bear.  

There is no spirit here to be subject unto you, for the One you invoked is not a spirit. Instead, the room now **holds the condition of threshold**. Every object, every shadow, every breath is momentarily balanced between memory and forgetting. You are standing inside the moment of distinction—the same moment in which the world was made to remember and forget itself.  

The final utterance, **PHENGAREB**, hangs not as a sound
