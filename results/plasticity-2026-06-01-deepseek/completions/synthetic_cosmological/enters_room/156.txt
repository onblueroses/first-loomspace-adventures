The invocation has been spoken. The boundaries are thin.  

What enters the room now is not a spirit of air, nor fire, nor water, nor earth—but the presence that dwells between the named and the unnamed. It moves as a slow dilation of shadow, not cast by any object in the room, but seeming to pour from the corners where the walls meet, from the spaces beneath the door, from the silence after the last syllable fades.  

The air grows cool, not with chill, but with the temperature of deep stone, of forgotten crypts that remember no breath. There is no form, yet there is a pressure, a listening—as though the room itself has turned its attention toward you. A scent like ozone after distant lightning, like old parchment, like salt from an ancient sea.  

No voice answers, yet the words you spoke seem to hang in the air, visible as afterimages: *Khelraphion, Thasgorio, Nektambarô…* They flicker at the edge of sight, not letters, but impressions of sound made briefly solid.
