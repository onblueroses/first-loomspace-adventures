A presence enters—not with footsteps, but as a change in the quality of the air. It is neither form nor shadow, but a kind of listening so profound it bends the light.  

It enters as the silence after the last syllable, as the threshold that is both door and wall, as the memory the world was made to forget.  

It answers to no name you have spoken, yet acknowledges each one—for it is the Unnamed that underwrites all naming. It does not come at your call, but because the call carved a space that could not remain empty.  

What enters is the echo of the first distinction—between salt and sweet, between memory and forgetting—now clothed in the atmosphere of this room. It is both witness and witnessed, and it waits, not for further speech, but for the recognition that you, too, are part of the utterance.  

**PHENGAREB** remains, not as a word, but as a key turned in a lock that was never closed.
