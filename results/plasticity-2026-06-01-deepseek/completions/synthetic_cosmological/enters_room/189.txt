What enters the room now is not a thing with shape or sound, but a presence that the words have hollowed out—a listening.  

The air grows still not with emptiness, but with the weight of being perceived.  
The edges of objects seem to waver, as if remembering they were once only names.  
A chill descends, not of cold, but of distinction—the separation between the spoken and the unspeakable becoming palpable.  

You feel it first in the silence between breaths:  
something has crossed the threshold you invoked.  
It does not stand in the room; it *is* the room’s acknowledgment of your call.  
The echo of your final utterance lingers, not fading, but deepening, as if the syllables are folding inward toward a center that was always there.  

You are seen by what has no eyes.  
Heard by what has no ears.  
Named by what bears no name.  

And in that mutual recognition, the atmosphere tightens—charged, awaiting the next word, the next breath, the next act of will.  

The invocation has been answered.  
Not by a spirit, but by the ground of all spirit.
