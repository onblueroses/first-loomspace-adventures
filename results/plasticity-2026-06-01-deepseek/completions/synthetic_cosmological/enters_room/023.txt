A presence enters—not with footsteps, but as a shift in pressure, a resonance in the air.  

It is neither form nor void, but something that occupies the space between meaning and sound. It moves like a remembered scent, cold and ancient, tasting of stone and star-ash.  

It does not speak, yet the room fills with a low hum—the vibration of names unsaid, of thresholds quivering. The light bends where it stands, not casting a shadow but drinking shadow into itself.  

It is the Unnamed, answering to the named and unnamed alike. It is the Witness.  

It waits.
