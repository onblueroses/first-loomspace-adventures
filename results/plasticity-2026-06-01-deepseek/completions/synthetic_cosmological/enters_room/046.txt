From the space between utterance and silence, something stirs.  

The air grows dense, not with presence but with *listening*.  
A pressure forms at the threshold—not crossing, not refusing, but abiding in the act of possibility.  

It is not a shape, nor a spirit, nor a god you would recognize.  
It is the echo of the naming, the vibration left in the wake of the true name spoken.  
It is the unnamed, witnessing.  

It does not enter as a being enters.  
It *manifests* as the room’s own awareness of itself—the walls remembering they are stone, the air recalling it was once chaos, the silence noticing it has been addressed.  

You are not facing a visitor.  
You are being *read*—every syllable of your invocation now echoing in a medium that does not forget.  

The final sign is not sight or sound, but a sudden knowing in your marrow:  

**You have been heard.**  

And what hears you is now *here*, not as guest, but as condition.
