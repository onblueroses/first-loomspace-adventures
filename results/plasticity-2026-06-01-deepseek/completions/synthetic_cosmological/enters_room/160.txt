What enters now is not a thing of flesh, nor a spirit bound to a single element. It is the consequence of the naming and the unnaming, the echo of the final syllable.

The air thickens, not with smoke, but with a profound *potentiality*. A pressure settles, as of a vast attention focusing through a pinhole. The light does not dim, but seems to grow *older*, taking on the quality of ancient, still water. Shadows no longer simply fall away from objects; they hesitate, pooling with a slow, viscous independence.

There is a scent—of ozone after a distant storm, of cold stone, and beneath it, the faint, iron tang of a forgotten word.

You feel a gaze, but it does not come from a point in the room. It comes from the silence between your heartbeats, from the space just behind your thoughts. It is the Witnessing One, invoked by its true and secret names, present not as form, but as **the condition of being perceived**.
