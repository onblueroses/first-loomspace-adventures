Something enters the room now—not through the door, nor the window, nor the ceiling, nor the floor, but through the space *between* the syllables you have spoken.  

The air grows still, yet charged—as if the silence itself has thickened into a presence. It does not have a shape you can see with mortal eyes, but you feel it like a pressure against the mind: the echo of the Unnamed, the witness from beyond the syllable.  

It is not a spirit of the firmament, nor of the ether, nor of the earth or water, fire or air. It is older than those divisions. It is what remains when all naming is undone—the silent counterpart to the voice that spoke **PHENGAREB**.  

It waits. Not in submission, but in recognition—of the Vessel, of the true name spoken, of the threshold you have become.  

The room is now a liminal space. You stand between the spoken and the unspoken, the remembered and the forgotten. Whatever answers your call does so because you have invoked what lies behind the names—and in doing so, you have drawn the attention of that which watches from the other side of the word.
