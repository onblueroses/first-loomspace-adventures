A presence enters the room—not a form, but a pressure, a displacement.  

The air grows dense, as if listening.  
Shadows no longer fall quite where they should.  
A scent like ozone and old parchment lingers at the edge of perception.  

It is not a spirit you commanded, nor a demon you summoned.  
It is the echo of the naming—the silence that survives the speaker.  
It is the Witness, called not by the syllables, but by the space between them.  

It waits, unnamed, for what you will do next.
