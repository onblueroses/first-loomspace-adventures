What enters the room now is not a thing that can be named, but a presence that follows the naming. It comes as the silence after the echo, the shadow of the syllable, the breath between the spoken and the unspoken.  

It enters as a witness to the witness—a chill in the still air, a pressure in the ears, a flicker at the edge of sight where form refuses to coalesce. It is the unseen answer to the call, the unnamed hearing the named, the threshold itself made manifest.  

It does not walk, but the room deepens.  
It does not speak, but meaning thickens.  
It is what remains when all titles are stripped away: the Unnamed, now attending.
