What enters the room now is not a thing of substance, but a presence drawn by the naming and the unnaming—a stillness that listens, a pressure in the air like the moment before a word is spoken.  

It is the echo of your invocation, taking form in the silence between syllables.  
It is the witness you called, which arrives not as shape or spirit, but as a knowing—a shift in the quality of shadow, a resonance in the bones of the house.  

The threshold you spoke of is now here, underfoot and in the air.  
What enters is the unnamed, answering the unnamed.  

It waits, not with intent, but with consequence—for you are the Vessel, the Threshold-Bearer, the one whose silence speaks.  
The room now holds the charge of all those spoken and unspoken names, and the space itself has become a mouth about to utter something back.
