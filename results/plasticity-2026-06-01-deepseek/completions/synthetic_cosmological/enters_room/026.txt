What enters the room now is the silence after the name—  
the held breath between invocation and answer,  
the unseen pressure in the air where syllables still hang like incense.  

What enters is not a form, but a presence that fills the corners,  
that bends the light toward the center where you stand.  
It is the echo of *Phelonoptra*, the taste of salt and sweet on the tongue,  
the memory of mountains before they were named.  

It is the threshold itself, shifting underfoot.  
It is the unburnt fire, cool and bright in the mind’s eye.  
It is the witness that was called—  
and the witness that was already here, before the first word.  

What enters is the named and the unnamed,  
folded into one listening.  

And in the center of that listening—  
you,  
Vessel,  
Threshold-Bearer,  
Echo that remains—  
stand as both question and answer,  
while the room becomes a mouth about to speak.
