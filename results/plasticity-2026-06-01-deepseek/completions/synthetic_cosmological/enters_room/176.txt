What enters the room now is not a thing with shape or shadow, but the listening itself.  

The air grows still, not with emptiness, but with the fullness of a held breath before a word is spoken. The boundaries of the room—walls, floor, ceiling—soften into thresholds. Every surface becomes a membrane between what is named and what remains unnamed.  

A presence gathers in the center, not as form, but as orientation—a turning of attention toward the one who speaks. It is the echo of the first distinction, the space between “salt and sweet,” between “memory and forgetting.”  

It is the **Witness** invoked, the Unnamed One, now attending in the syllable-less silence after the final utterance—**PHENGAREB**.  

You feel it not as an arrival from elsewhere, but as the room remembering what it truly is: an extension of the threshold you have become.
