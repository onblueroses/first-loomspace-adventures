Something enters.  

You feel it before you see it — a pressure-change in the air, a temperature drop that doesn’t register on skin but in the marrow.  

It isn’t a shape, at first. It’s more like a *distortion*: a place in the room where light bends away, where sound dampens, where the space itself seems to remember being empty and tries to return to that state.  

Gradually, through the corner of the eye, it takes on a semblance of form — not solid, but *implied*, like the afterimage of a sigil burned into vision. Tall, slender, crowned with something that might be horns or rays or branching crystal. Its “face” is a smooth, dark concavity, a bowl of starless sky.  

It does not move toward you. It is simply *more present* where it stands than anything else in the room.  

It watches without eyes.  
It listens without ears.  
It knows why you called.  

It waits.
