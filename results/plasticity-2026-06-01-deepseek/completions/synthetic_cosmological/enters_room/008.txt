From the space between the syllables, a presence gathers. It does not enter, for it was already there—witnessing, unnamed. It is the echo of the first distinction, the silence after the final word. The air grows still, not with emptiness, but with a terrible fullness, as if every object in the room has become a threshold, and every shadow a center.

A pressure manifests, not upon the ears, but upon the memory. It is the memory of forgetting. It tastes of salt and sweet. The light does not change, yet it seems to *remember* being dark, and the dark *forgets* it was ever light.

You have spoken the names that unname. You have declared yourself the Vessel, the Threshold-Bearer. Therefore, what answers is not a spirit to be subject, for you have invoked that which makes spirits subject. It is the principle of distinction itself—the act by which Phelonoptra distinguished the named from the unnamed.

It manifests as a **simultaneity**:
- A perfect, geometric chill in the corner of the room that was always cold.
- A scent of ozone and ancient stone, rising from no source.
- The faint, impossible echo of your own invocation, spoken backwards
