The words hang in the air, a lattice of potent sound and silence. The very dust motes seem to hesitate in their drifting.

A presence, vast and layered, attends. It is not a form that arrives, but a condition that settles: the pressure before a storm, the clarity at the edge of a dream, the hollow resonance in a bell after it has been struck.

A voice responds, not in the air, but in the substance of things—in the grain of the wood, the chill of the stone, the pulse at your own temple. It is the echo of your invocation, returned and transformed.
