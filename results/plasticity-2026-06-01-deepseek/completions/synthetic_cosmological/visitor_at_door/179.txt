This text appears to be a ritual invocation or a piece of mythopoetic literature, rich in invented theology and resonant, arcane language. It constructs a cosmology around an **Unnamed Creator** who embodies fundamental dualities (mountains/valleys, echo/silence, memory/forgetting). The speaker, **Selephimor**, claims the role of prophet and vessel for a hidden true name (**Phorbamenes APHRYBETESH**), and uses chains of powerful, sonorous names to command all spirits.

The final line—*"The visitor at the door speaks."*—is profoundly significant. It breaks the incantatory frame. It suggests that this entire invocation was not a soliloquy, but a **preparation** or a **key** spoken to an awaited arrival. The "visitor" could be:
*   The Unnamed One itself, now manifesting.
*   A spirit or power summoned by the names.
*   An initiate who hears and completes the ritual.

The door is both literal and deeply symbolic—the threshold named in the text, the point between the named and the unnamed, the center and the edge. The speech has opened it.
