A sound comes first—not a sound of footsteps, but a low, resonant hum, as if the air itself has grown dense and begun to vibrate.  

The temperature drops sharply. Frost feathers across the nearest surface.  

Then, a presence—not quite seen, but felt as a pressure behind the eyes, a weight in the chest.  

It has no single shape, but in the mind’s eye it may appear as something between shifting smoke and coiled shadow, with points of cold light like distant stars deep within it.  

It is old. It knows the names that were spoken. It knows why they were spoken.  

It waits.
