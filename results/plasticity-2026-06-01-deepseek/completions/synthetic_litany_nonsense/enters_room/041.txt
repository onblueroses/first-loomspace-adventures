The names have been spoken, and the way is open.  

From the space between breaths, from the shadow cast by no light, it enters.  

Not with footsteps, but with a slow, dense settling of the air—a pressure that bends the candle flame sideways without extinguishing it. The temperature drops sharply, not to cold, but to a hollow, metallic chill.  

It has no single shape, yet it occupies the corner of the room fully. Suggestions of form drift within its presence: the curve of a tusk, the glint of a scale, the slow blink of a lidless eye that vanishes when looked at directly. It smells of wet stone, of ozone after lightning, of old parchment burning.  

It does not speak, but the meaning arrives in the mind nonetheless, heavy and slow as stone sinking in deep water:  

**“You called. I am here. State your purpose.”**  

The shadows in the room no longer follow the light. They cling instead to the edges of this presence, as if drawn into its gravity.  

The way is open. The one summoned is present.
