 It is a man's voice, deep and resonant, with a note of command in it that is not quite arrogance. He says, "I am Dr. Waltham D. Spender, of the New York Institute for Psychical Research. I am looking for Mr. David Melton."

"Come in, Dr. Spender," David says.

The door opens and the man enters. He is tall, imposing, with an air of great self-confidence. He carries a black briefcase. He is perhaps fifty years old, with a strong, fleshy face, keen gray eyes, a wide, firm mouth. He says, "I hope I'm not intruding. I would have phoned, but you're not listed in the directory."

"That's all right. I've been expecting you."

"Expecting me? But how—"

David smiles. "I'm not really clairvoyant. I just keep up with the literature in the field. I read the piece you did in the _Journal of Parapsychology_ last winter, about the statistical distribution of psychic phenomena in the New York area. It seemed inevitable that sooner or later you'd come around to investigate me."
