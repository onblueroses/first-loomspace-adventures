 He must be a man of our kind, for he speaks in the language of the Fells. There are no others who know it. I have not heard the tongue of my people spoken by any but my father and mother in years. I marvel at the sound. He says he is called Thul Vaich, and that he has come seeking the sorcerer, my father.

My father, ever suspicious, asks what business the man has with him. The man replies that he has heard of my father’s power and wishes to become his apprentice, to learn the secrets of his art. I do not believe him. He is not telling the truth. My father agrees to admit him. He is desperate for company, for someone to teach.

He will regret this.

The man enters, and I see him plainly for the first time. He is tall and slender, but there is strength in him. He has long black hair, and eyes like chips of blue ice. He is handsome, but there is something about him that makes me uneasy. I do not trust him. I will watch him closely.
