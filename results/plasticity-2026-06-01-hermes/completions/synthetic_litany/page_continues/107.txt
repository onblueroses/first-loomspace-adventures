The magical circle, a common feature of grimoires, is an ancient symbol of protection used by magicians to protect themselves from the spirit they have summoned. The names of power are divine names, angelic names and words of power that have been passed down through the ages and used by magicians to ensure the cooperation of the spirits they have summoned.

The Goetia is the first section of the Lesser Key of Solomon, a grimoire attributed to King Solomon that includes instructions for summoning 72 spirits. It was compiled in the mid-17th century, drawing on material from earlier texts such as the Pseudomonarchia Daemonum and the Ars Goetia.

The Goetia has been a popular source of inspiration for writers, artists and musicians. It has been referenced in works such as Aleister Crowley's The Book of the Law, H.P. Lovecraft's The Case of Charles Dexter Ward, and Alan Moore's Promethea.
