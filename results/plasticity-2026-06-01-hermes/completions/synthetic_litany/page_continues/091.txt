 "Now will a flame of fire appear, of a bluish colour, whereunto the exorcist must not give way, but pursue his purpose, until he shall see before him a mist and dark cloud; from whence shall proceed a voice, saying, 'What is it that you seek? Why do you disturb my repose?' Then you must answer, as you think fit." This text is taken from the 1665 edition of Reginald Scot's Discoverie of Witchcraft, where it is said to have been an extract from "the magical works of Arbatel of Magic, and the Isagoge of Porphyry."

The name Throvenios is derived from the Greek word for 'three' (τρία), and it is possible that it was the original intention that the practitioner should recite the names three times before the mist and dark cloud would appear.

The page ends with the words "Tetragrammaton. Tetragrammaton. Tetragrammaton", a clear indication that the following page or pages are missing. In the Key of Solomon, the word 'Tetragrammaton' is used at the end of invocations to indicate that the practitioner should substitute the actual four-letter name of God
