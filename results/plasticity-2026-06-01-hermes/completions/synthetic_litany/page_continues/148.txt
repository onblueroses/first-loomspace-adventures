The text then continues with a Latin prayer, titled "Oratio Discipuli ad Magistrum", asking for protection from the spirits and for the spirits to obey the operator. This is followed by a short "Benedictio Aquae" (Blessing of the Water), to be used in purifying the circle and instruments. The prayer is to Harob, also known as Agla, a name of God. The text ends with a short note on the appearance of the spirits, saying that they may take various forms and that the operator should not be afraid, as the spirits are bound to obey.

## Reception

The Sworn Book of Honorius is considered one of the most influential grimoires in the history of Western occultism. It was widely copied and circulated in manuscript form during the Middle Ages and Renaissance, and its influence can be seen in many later grimoires, such as the Key of Solomon and the Lesser Key of Solomon. The Sworn Book's emphasis on the importance of the operator's purity and the use of divine names in commanding spirits became standard features of later grimoires.
