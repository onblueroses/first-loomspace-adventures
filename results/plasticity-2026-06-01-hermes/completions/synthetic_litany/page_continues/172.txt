 "The names of the spirits that open the way are these: (list of names). And when you have said these names, the earth will be opened and you will see a way down. Then, descend into the underground and you will see the spirits that guard the place. They will ask you: 'Who are you? Where are you going? And what are you doing here?' You must answer them: 'I am a disciple of the Prophet Solomon and I come here in search of the treasure of the wise king Solomon, which is hidden here. Open the doors for me and let me enter the place where the treasure is.'"

This passage is an invocation to open a path to an underground treasure, likely inspired by the legend of King Solomon's treasures. The names mentioned are likely invented magical names, possibly derived from Hebrew, Greek, or other ancient languages, a common practice in grimoires.

## In popular culture

The Grand Grimoire features prominently in the 1973 horror novel The Devils of D-Day by Graham Masterton.
