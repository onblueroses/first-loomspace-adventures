 “Having spoken these words, the spirits will appear before you, ready to serve you and obey your commands. Do not hesitate to speak with them, for they are bound by the names and will not harm you. Command them as you will, but always remember to license them to depart when you have finished, saying: ‘Go in peace, and be ever ready to come when called.’ ”

There are a few things to notice about this passage. First, the names are not those of the spirits being called, but those of the powers that control the spirits. In magical practice, these are often the names of angels or divine aspects, and the spirits summoned are generally thought to be of a lower order. This is a common pattern in the grimoires, though there are exceptions. Second, the passage specifies that the spirits will appear and communicate with the magician. They are not being summoned into objects, as is the case with some other rituals. Third, the magician is instructed to send the spirits away with a license to depart, a common feature of grimoire magic. This step is considered an important safety measure; spirits not properly licensed to depart may linger and cause trouble for the magician.
