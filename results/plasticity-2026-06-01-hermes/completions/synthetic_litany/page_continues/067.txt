 "When you have spoken these names, then the spirits of the dead will be obedient to you, and will come before you as you wish, and answer your questions. And when the interrogation is complete, and you wish to send them away, then say to them the words of dismissal: 'Go in peace to the place appointed for you, and let there be no ill will between us, for the love of God.' And with these words they will go."

The text is in Latin, and the conjuration is a typical example of those found in many grimoires of the Middle Ages and Renaissance. Such works were often attributed to famous figures in the Biblical or Classical traditions to lend them an air of authority. In this case, the attribution to Solomon is spurious; the work itself dates to the early modern period, most likely the 17th or 18th century, and has more in common with works of that era, such as the Lesser Key of Solomon and the Grand Grimoire, than with the older traditions of Jewish kabbalah or Medieval Latin magical works.
