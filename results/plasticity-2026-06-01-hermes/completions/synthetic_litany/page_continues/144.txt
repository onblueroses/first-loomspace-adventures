 "Let the gates be opened unto me, and the paths made clear; let the way be opened unto the temple, and let the light of the shining ones descend upon me, that I may behold the throne of glory, and the mercy seat of the Holy One. Open the way before me, and let no obstacle bar my path. By the power of the divine names, I command thee, open the way!"

## Reception

In his review of the book, the British author and occultist Phil Hine writes that "Liber Koth is an interesting development in Lovecraftian magick, in that it attempts to develop a more 'purist' approach to the entities of the Cthulhu Mythos, away from the classical qabalah and into the author's own construct of the 'Book of Eibon'." Hine concludes that "Liber Koth is a fascinating grimoire which should provide much food for thought for those interested in Lovecraftian magick, and the Typhonian gnosis."
