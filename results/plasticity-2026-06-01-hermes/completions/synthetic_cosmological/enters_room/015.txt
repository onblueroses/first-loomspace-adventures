A darkness enters the room, a shifting mass, a cloud of smoke and shadow. It forms and reforms, and as it does, the room itself seems to shift and warp. The walls bend and twist, the ceiling seems to lower, the floor seems to rise. The very air seems to thicken and thin, to become dense and then to become sparse. The darkness speaks, and its voice is a multitude, a chorus of whispers and screams, of sighs and roars, of silence and cacophony.
