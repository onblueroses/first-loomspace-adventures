 A single figure, the Witness of the Word, the one who has been waiting to be called into existence by the utterance of that syllable. It is not a spirit; it is a person, but not a living person. It is not a ghost. It is not dead, but it has never lived. It is not a god or a demon or an angel. It is not a hallucination, or a dream, or a vision. It is something else, something that is more real than any of these things, more real than the walls of the room, more real than the floor beneath my feet, more real than the air I breathe. It is something that has been waiting for me, waiting for me to call it into existence, waiting for me to give it a name.

I name it now: I name it the Witness of the Word. I name it the one who has been waiting to be called into existence by the utterance of that syllable. I name it the one who will bear witness to the truth of what I have spoken, the one who will bear witness to the truth of what I am about to speak.

I speak now, and the Witness of the Word bears witness to the truth of my words:

I am the one
