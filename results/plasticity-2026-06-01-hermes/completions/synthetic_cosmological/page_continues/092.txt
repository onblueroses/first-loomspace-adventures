 "Say this spell three times in a loud voice, and then in a soft voice, and then in silence. Then say it thrice more, and it will be accomplished. If you say it in the morning, it will be accomplished before evening; and if you say it at evening, it will be accomplished by morning."

## The manuscript

The manuscript is a sheet of papyrus cut into a rectangle 27 by 29 centimeters. The text is written in Greek, mostly in the Ionic dialect, with a few exceptions. The verso side is blank. The provenance of the papyrus is unknown; it was acquired by the British Museum at the same time as two other Greek magical papyri of similar content (PGM I and PGM II). The handwriting suggests a date in the late 4th or early 5th century AD.

## Interpretation

The spell is a prayer or invocation addressed to a deity who is identified as "the Unnamed One" (Ancient Greek: τὸν Ἀνόνοματον). The deity is described in paradoxical terms, as both creator and destroyer, and as possessing qualities that are both named and unnamed.
