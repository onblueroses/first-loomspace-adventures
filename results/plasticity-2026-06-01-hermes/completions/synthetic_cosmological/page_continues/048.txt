 “Say this strophe with the proper gestures, and the hidden one shall be revealed, and the named one shall be hidden. The world shall be made and unmade, as it was in the beginning, and the threshold shall be crossed, and the silence shall be broken, and the echo shall be still, and the fire shall be burnt, and the memory shall be forgotten, and the forgetting shall be remembered.”

Now, I had to say something. I could hardly believe what I had just read. I knew that the text was written in the style of a classical invocation, but it was not like any classical invocation I had ever seen. There were references to the creation of the world, to memory and forgetting, to speech and silence, to the threshold and the center, to the named and the unnamed. There were names and words that I had never seen before, and that seemed to have no clear meaning or etymology. There were paradoxes and contradictions, such as the unburnt fire, the echo that survives the speaker, the silence that utterly speaks, the world that is made and unmade.
