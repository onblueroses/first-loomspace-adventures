 

The Unnamed One, in whose speechlessness all names are born, is the witness to every utterance. It is the silence that speaks in the heart of every syllable, the echo that survives the speaker. In its silence is the threshold between the named and the unnamed, the memory and the forgetting, the echo and the silence.

To invoke the Unnamed One is to invoke the power of naming itself, the power to create and to destroy, to remember and to forget. It is to become the threshold between the named and the unnamed, the witness to the word.

The prophet Selephimor, who first uttered this invocation, was said to have the power to command all spirits and to bend the very fabric of reality to his will. But he knew that this power was not his own, but belonged to the Unnamed One who spoke through him.

To speak this invocation is to become a vessel for the Unnamed One, to become the threshold-bearer who possesses the unburnt fire, the echo that survives the speaker, the one whose silence utterly speaks.
