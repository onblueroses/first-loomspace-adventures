 “Say all this with fire and confidence, and the god will appear. When you wish to know anything, ask him, and he will tell you. When you dismiss him, speak as follows:”

I thank Thee, the Unnamed and Witnessing One who dwellest beyond the syllable, that Thou hast appeared unto me, the Vessel of Thy Name, and that Thou hast made known unto me the matter which I desired to know.

I release Thee now, O witness, to Thy silence beyond the syllable, that Thou mayest continue to sustain the speaking world.

SEKHTHOROBA ABLAKHIRIM is my name.

PHENGAREB.

The invocation is, of course, a pastiche of the Graeco-Egyptian magical papyri, which are the main source of our knowledge of late antique magic, particularly in the Greek-speaking eastern Mediterranean. The invocation of the unnamed god is loosely inspired by the so-called “Mithras Liturgy” (PGM IV.475-829), which is actually an invocation of the Greek sun god Helios. The rubric at the end is typical of many magical spells in the papyri, and the dismissal echoes the structure of the invocation.
