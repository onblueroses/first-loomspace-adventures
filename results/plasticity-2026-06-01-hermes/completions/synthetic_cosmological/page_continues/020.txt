 "This is a powerful invocation, which thou shalt use when thou wouldst have all spirits of whatever kind subject unto thee. Keep it from the eyes of the vulgar and the profane, who would not understand, and would therefore mock its high beauty and its lofty purpose. I have written it as I received it, and I have written it truly and well, even as it was spoken unto me by the Unnamed One. Unto me, Selephimor, He hath revealed it, and I know that it shall not fail. Use it well, and use it only when thou hast great need of its power."

The invocation, in its original form, is clearly a late Graeco-Egyptian magical text, although some of the divine and angelic names are corrupt. It is probably based on a genuine Gnostic original, but contains some later accretions. The name "Phelonoptra" is almost certainly a corruption of "Phellophoria," the epithet of a Titaness in an Orphic fragment.
