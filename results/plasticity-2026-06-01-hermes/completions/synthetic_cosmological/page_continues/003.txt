 "Here endeth the Invocation of the Unnamed One."

Commentary:

This invocation is clearly related to the "Invocation of the Unnamed One" that appears in the Testament of Athraophol, but it has been expanded and altered significantly. Most notably, it is written in the first person, from the perspective of Selephimor himself. This suggests that it may have been a personal invocation used by Selephimor, perhaps to establish his authority and connection to the Unnamed One.

The invocation begins by addressing the Unnamed One and attributing to them the creation of various dichotomies and oppositions in the world, such as mountains and valleys, tide and flow, echo and silence. This reflects the idea that the Unnamed One is the source of all duality and distinction in the world.

The invocation then lists several names that are presumably epithets or aspects of the Unnamed One, such as Phelonoptra, Krathoumis, Demoaphris, Velpharaim, and Iphtaron. These names do not appear to have any clear etymology or meaning, but they may have held significance within the mystical system of Athraophol.

Selephimor then identifies himself as the prophet of the Unnamed One
