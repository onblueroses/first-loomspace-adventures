 "If you say the invocation, all spirits, wherever they may be, will come at once and obey you."

The invocation is followed by a list of spirits, organized into three groups: The Spirits of the Air of the Four Directions, The Spirits of the Four Elements, and The Spirits of the Seven Heavens. The spirits are described as being "under the command of the Unnamed One" and are said to be "subject unto the bearer of the invocation."

The invocation and the list of spirits are written in a mix of Greek and a cipher that has yet to be fully deciphered. The Greek sections are written in a form of the Greek alphabet that was used in the Eastern Mediterranean during the late Hellenistic and Roman periods, suggesting that the manuscript may date to that time. However, the use of the cipher and the unusual content of the manuscript have made it difficult to determine its exact origin and purpose.

The manuscript is currently housed in the Special Collections department of the university library, where it is being studied by a team of researchers from the departments of Classics, Religious Studies, and Linguistics. The team is working to decipher the remaining sections of the manuscript and to determine its provenance and significance.
