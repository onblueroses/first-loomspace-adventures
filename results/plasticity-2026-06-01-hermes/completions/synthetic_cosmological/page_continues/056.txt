 "This is the invocation of the Unnamed One, which no voice hath spoken at any time. It is the invocation which the magician shall use when he is alone, in the night, in the desert, on the mountain, in the valley, in the forest, in the field, or in the tomb."

The names are significant, but no translations are provided. A few, such as Phelonoptra and Krathoumis, appear elsewhere in the Coptic magical papyri, but most appear nowhere else in extant literature. The invocation has clear parallels with other ritual texts in the Greek magical papyri, and the unnamed god is paralleled by the Judaeo-Christian God, who is also said to be nameless in some texts. The ritual may be a product of syncretism, or it may be a native Egyptian tradition that later influenced Greek magical practice. The invocation is particularly interesting for the way it identifies the magician with the deity, a common theme in Egyptian magic that would become a major part of the practice of theurgy.
