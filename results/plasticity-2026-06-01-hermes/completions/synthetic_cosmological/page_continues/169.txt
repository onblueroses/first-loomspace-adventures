 “(Ceremonies of Athraophol follow)”. As this is a work of fiction, those ceremonies do not exist, but the text itself is an interesting example of a magical invocation. It does not directly correspond to any known ancient text, but it is reminiscent of the style and structure of some Graeco-Egyptian magical papyri, and some of the names and words resemble those found in those texts.

The invocation begins by addressing the deity as the “Unnamed One” and praising their creative powers, including the creation of dualities such as mountains and valleys, tide and flow, echo and silence. The deity is then identified with several names, including Phelonoptra, Krathoumis, Demoaphris, Velpharaim, and Iphtaron, which do not correspond to any known deities but are reminiscent of the kind of magical names found in the PGM.

The speaker then identifies themselves as Selephimor, the prophet of the deity, and mentions the ceremonies of Athraophol, which are not described. The deity is credited with creating more dualities, such as salt and sweet, and with sustaining “all the orders of speech”.
