 I will not attempt to reproduce the voice, which is not a voice, but the mind's impression of a voice, which is not a voice, but the mind's impression of a voice.

"Of course you understand," says the visitor at the door, "that I am not actually present in any way that you could describe or define. This is not my voice, but your impression of my voice, which is itself merely an impression. I am not actually speaking, nor am I actually here. You are merely witnessing, in your limited way, a certain aspect of my nature which has come into contact with your own. The impression of my voice which you are now perceiving is merely an artifact of that contact, a sort of shadow cast by the intersection of dimensions which are, to you, imperceptible and unimaginable."

"Of course," I say, or seem to say, or imagine myself saying. "I understand."

"I doubt that very much," says the visitor at the door. "But no matter. We will proceed as if you understand, because, in the end, it is all the same to me."

"Very well," I say, or seem to say, or imagine myself saying.
