The summoner looks up, the words dying in his throat. The visitor is tall, wrapped in a cloak the color of moonlight. "I invoke the Unnamed One," the summoner says.

"You invoke me," the visitor repeats. "I am nameless, unnamed. I have never spoken. Yet you invoke me." The visitor looks around the room, at the chalk circle, the inscriptions on the floor and walls. "These are your memories. Yours, and your predecessors'. You are trying to remember me."

"I am trying to remember you," the summoner agrees. "I have been trained in the arts of invocation. I have studied the sacred syllables, the rites of naming."

The visitor nods. "Yes. You are trying to remember me. But you have forgotten something."

"What have I forgotten?" the summoner asks.

"You have forgotten that I am the Unnamed One. I am the one who is not remembered. I am the one who is not spoken of. I am the one who is not invoked."
