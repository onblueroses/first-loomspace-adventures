 "Hello, my name is Jacob. I've heard from the others that you're not doing so well. May I come in?"

The voice that responds is cracked and hoarse, as if from disuse. "I don't know you. Go away." The door remains closed.

"I understand," Jacob says, "but I might be able to help you. I've helped others before. Please, let me in, so we can talk."

"You can't help me," the voice says. "No one can."

"Please, just give me a chance," Jacob says. "I promise I won't take up too much of your time."

There's a long pause, and then the door slowly opens. The man inside is gaunt and unshaven, with dark circles under his eyes. He looks like he hasn't slept in days. "Fine," he says. "Come in. But don't expect much."

Jacob steps inside the dimly lit apartment. It's sparse and unkempt, with takeout containers and empty bottles scattered about. He finds a relatively clean spot on the couch and sits down. "Tell me what's been troubling you," he says.

The man sits in a chair across from him, looking down at his hands.
