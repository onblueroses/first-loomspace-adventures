Syme, who had been brought out by the same argument so often that he knew it by heart, could only say with wearily humorous patience, "But surely you wouldn't say that a minority ever ought to have power over a majority?"

"But surely," said the old man, "the odd occasions may occur when it is necessary."

"Well, if I were you," said Syme impatiently, "I should not worry about the oppression of the Poor, but about the oppression of the Rich. They are the downtrodden class now, and, if I am any judge of the signs of the times, they will be much more downtrodden yet. Their bloody tyrants, the Middle Classes, will soon have their heels on their necks."

His impulsive utterance of this prophecy produced a curious effect. All six men looked up at him as if they were thunderstruck.

"Do you really mean to say," asked the man next to him, "that you think the Middle Classes are against the Rich?"

"I think the Middle Classes are against everybody," said Syme, "including themselves."
