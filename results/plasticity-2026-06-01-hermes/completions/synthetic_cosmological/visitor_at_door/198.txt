 "Do you know the name of this place?"

"No," you say. "What is it?"

"This is the place where the name of God is spoken."

You look around at the barren rock. "It is?"

"Yes. You have spoken it."

---

What does this story mean?

It is a story about the Unnamed One, and therefore it is a story about the ultimate reality that transcends all words and categories.

It is a story about the prophet Selephimor, who is the one who names and unnames, who is the threshold between the named and the unnamed, who is the witness of the word.

It is a story about the power of naming and the power of un-naming. To name something is to define it, to categorize it, to bring it into the realm of the known. But to un-name something is to let it be what it is, to let it be free from our categories and definitions, to let it be a mystery.

It is a story about the ultimate mystery that lies beyond all words and names, the mystery that can only be pointed to, but never grasped.
