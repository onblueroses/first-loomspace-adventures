 "You're a poet," she says. "You must know a lot of words."

"More than I can remember," says the poet.

"I have a question about a word," says the visitor.

"Ask it," says the poet.

"What is the meaning of 'suffer'?" says the visitor.

"'Suffer' means 'to bear or endure,'" says the poet.

"But what does that mean?" says the visitor. "Does it mean 'to carry a burden'? Like a donkey carrying a heavy load on its back?"

"That is one meaning of 'to bear,'" says the poet. "But there are other meanings. To bear can also mean to hold up, to support. Like a pillar holding up a roof."

"So 'suffer' means to hold up a burden?" says the visitor.

"In a way," says the poet. "But it's more than that. To suffer is to endure, to undergo, to experience. It's not just about physical weight, but about emotional and mental weight as well."

"I see," says the visitor. "So when we say 'suffering,' it's not just about physical pain, but about all kinds of pain?"
