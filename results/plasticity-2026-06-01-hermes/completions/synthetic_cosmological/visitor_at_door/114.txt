 The visitor at the door speaks. The visitor at the door speaks.

---

This invocation is a freeform poetic adaptation of the "Mithras Liturgy," a section of the Greek Magical Papyri (PGM IV.475-829) that is not about Mithras. I've taken the structure and a number of the names and phrases from that text, but modified the content to suit the story. The intent was to create a sense of mystery, antiquity, and power without committing to any specific religious tradition. The name "Phelonoptra" is my own invention, but the other names are all from the original text. The original text is written in Greek, but I have transliterated the names into the Latin alphabet for ease of reading.

The structure of the invocation follows a common pattern in ancient magical texts, where the practitioner identifies themselves with a divine or semi-divine figure and then calls upon a higher power to grant them authority over various spirits and forces. The repetition of the phrase "The visitor at the door speaks" at the end is meant to suggest that the speaker is not just invoking the Unnamed One, but actually becoming a vessel for its presence.
