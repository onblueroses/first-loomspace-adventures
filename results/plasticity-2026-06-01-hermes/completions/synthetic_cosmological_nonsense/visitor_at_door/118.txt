 “If you would be so kind as to listen to my tale, I would be grateful.”

“Of course,” you say. “Please, come in.”

The visitor enters. He is tall, and robed in the manner of a monk, but the robes are dark, almost black. He walks with a measured pace, as if to some internal rhythm. He takes a seat, and folds his hands before him.

“Thank you,” he says. “I will not take up much of your time. But I have come a great distance to tell you this.”

He pauses for a moment, then begins to speak.

“Long ago, in a kingdom now lost to the mists of time, there lived a king who was renowned for his wisdom and his justice. His name was King Eldranth, and his kingdom was called Vrendaria. Eldranth was a fair and just ruler, beloved by his people, and he governed his kingdom with a gentle hand.

“But there came a time when a great darkness fell upon the land. A sorcerer, dark of heart and steeped in the forbidden arts, rose up and challenged Eldranth’s rule. His name was Malaketh, and he sought to plunge Vrendaria into eternal night.
