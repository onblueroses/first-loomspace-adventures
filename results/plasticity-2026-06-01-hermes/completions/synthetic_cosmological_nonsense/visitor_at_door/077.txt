 “It is the hour,” he says.

“Enter,” says Vrendrelphor.

The visitor is an old man, his skin very white, his hair and beard very black. His eyes are deep-set and his gaze is keen.

“Do you bring the payment?” says Vrendrelphor.

The old man nods. “I bring the payment.”

“Leave it on the table,” says Vrendrelphor. “Then take your place in the circle.”

The old man does as he is told. He is wearing a robe of black silk, and his feet are bare.

Vrendrelphor takes the payment from the table. It is a small, heavy bag of black velvet. He opens it and pours its contents into his hand. There are twelve large, perfect rubies.

Vrendrelphor smiles. “You have done well,” he says.

The old man nods. “I have done as you asked.”

Vrendrelphor puts the rubies back into the bag and sets the bag aside. Then he takes his place in the circle, opposite the old man.

The two men stand facing each other, their eyes locked.

Vrendrelphor begins to chant in a language that is not of this world.
