 “It’s me,” he says. “I’m home.” The voice is familiar, but the tone is strange. It’s been years since I last heard it. The door opens. It’s my father. He looks the same as he always has, but different. He’s older, grayer, more worn, but his eyes are bright. He’s carrying a suitcase. He sets it down and embraces me. I’m surprised by how fiercely I hug him back. We stand there for a long moment, holding each other. Finally, he pulls away. “I’m sorry,” he says. “I’m so sorry. I never should have left. I never should have stayed away so long.” I don’t know what to say. I’m overwhelmed. “I’ve missed you,” I manage. “I’ve missed you too,” he says. “More than you can know.” We go inside. He looks around the house, taking it all in. “It’s just the same,” he says. “It’s like I never left.” But it’s not the same. Everything has changed. I’ve changed.
