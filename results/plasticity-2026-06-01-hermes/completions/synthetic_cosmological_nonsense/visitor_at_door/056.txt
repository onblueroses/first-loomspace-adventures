 "I am here to retrieve my mother's books," he says. "I understand that you have them."

"Indeed," I say, "I have them safe." I motion him to enter. "They are very rare and valuable books."

He steps into the room. "Yes," he says, "they are. She told me to be careful with them."

"Did she?" I ask. "Did she tell you anything else about them?"

"Only that they were old and fragile, and that I should take care in transporting them."

"I see. Well, you are in luck. I have taken the liberty of wrapping them for travel."

"Ah, thank you," he says. "That was kind of you."

I lead him over to the bookshelf, where I have three large volumes laid out, wrapped in oilcloth and tied with twine. I lift the first one and hand it to him. "Be careful," I say, "it's heavy."

He takes it, and staggers a bit under the weight. "Wow," he says, "it really is."
