 “I have a message for you,” she says. “It’s from a friend. Can I come in?”

You don’t recognize her. She’s wearing a hood, and it’s dark out. But it’s late, and you don’t want to wake your family. You decide to risk it. You open the door.

She steps inside. She lowers her hood. Her eyes are black, like a doll’s. “The message is this,” she says. “They are coming.”

Then she collapses. You try to catch her, but she slips through your hands, her body dissolving into a cloud of inky smoke. The smoke pours into the hallway, and you hear your children start to cough in their beds.

Then the screaming starts.

You wake up. It’s still dark out. You’re sweating. You get up and check on your children. They’re fine, they’re sleeping. You go back to bed.

The next day, the visitor arrives again. You’re at work. She’s standing in the doorway of your office. “I have a message for you,” she says. “It’s from a friend. Can I come in?”

You don’t recognize her. She’s wearing a hood, and the lights are flickering.
