 He has a pleasant voice, but it is not human. "I know you," he says. "You are Vrendrelphor, the Witness of the Word, who has written the most terrible and secret name of God."

"I am," I say.

"May I enter?" he asks.

"You may," I say.

He enters. He is tall and slender, dressed in a black robe, and his skin is pale and his eyes are dark. He looks like a man, but he is not. He is something else, something that has never been human.

"I have come to offer you a gift," he says.

"What gift?" I ask.

"The gift of knowledge," he says. "The knowledge of the true nature of the universe, and of the true nature of God."

"I already have that knowledge," I say.

"No," he says, "you do not. You have only a fragment of it, a tiny piece of the vast tapestry of truth. I can give you the rest."

"And what would you ask in return?" I ask.

"Nothing," he says. "It is a gift, freely given."
