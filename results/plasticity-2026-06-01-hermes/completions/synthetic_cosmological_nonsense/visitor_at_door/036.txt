I will be very surprised if you’ve read this far without recognizing the flavor of the ritual. If you’ve read any of the various medieval and Renaissance grimoires, or even the more modern versions produced by the Order of the Golden Dawn and Aleister Crowley, it’s hard to miss the resemblance. The basic structure is the same, a sequence of holy names and words of power, most of them more or less garbled, calling on a divine power to grant the speaker the authority to command the spirits. It also has the same rhythm, that of sonorous and incomprehensible words rising to a kind of incantatory crescendo. To judge by the effect it has on the spirits he’s trying to evoke and control, it works, too.

What makes this fictional ritual fascinating to me, and relevant to our theme, is that it shows that the effect of a ritual does not depend on the literal meaning of the words that make it up. You can get the same effect, in other words, with a “mass of nonsense.” That’s a point I made in my post two weeks ago, but it’s even more forcefully made here.
