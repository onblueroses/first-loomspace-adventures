 "You summoned me?"

The visitor is a man of indeterminate age, a man who might be young and haggard or old and hale. His hair is long and wild, his beard unkempt, his eyes glittering and sharp. He is dressed in a dark, shabby coat, dusty and patched, and his hands are black with grime and ash. He speaks with a voice like the scraping of stone on stone, a voice that is somehow both rough and mellifluous.

"Yes," I reply, rising from my seat before the blackened hearth. "I summoned you, traveler from the lost places. I summoned you, wanderer of the unseen roads. I summoned you, knower of the hidden words. I summoned you, who are called the Word-Thief."

"And who are you, to call upon me with such names?" the visitor asks, his eyes narrowing.

"I am the one who speaks the words of power," I reply. "I am the one who knows the names that must not be spoken. I am the one who walks the paths that must not be trod. I am the one who seeks the knowledge that must not be known."
