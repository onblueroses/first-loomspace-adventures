 “I have come to share the last words of my grandfather with you.”

“Come in, come in,” I say. “I have tea.”

He enters, leaving his coat on the hook by the door. I see that he wears a pendant of a silver circle, with a smaller circle inside, and inside that, a dot. He sees me looking, and touches it with a finger. “My grandfather’s,” he says. “He wore it all his life.”

He sits down, and I pour tea for us both. “I know it’s strange for me to come here like this, out of the blue,” he says. “But my grandfather’s instructions were very specific. He made me promise to come and see you, and share his last words.”

I nod. “I understand. We were very close, many years ago. I’m glad that he thought of me, in the end.”

The visitor sips his tea. “I think he wanted to be remembered,” he says. “He was always afraid of being forgotten, of his life not mattering. That’s why he wrote his last words down, and made me promise to share them with you.”
