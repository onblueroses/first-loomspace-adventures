 He has been silent for a long time, listening to this recitation with growing alarm. “Are you a wizard?” he asks.

“No,” you say.

“Then why do you know this spell?” he asks.

“I have read it in a book,” you say. “I have read many books. I do not cast spells. I do not believe in spells.”

He looks at you, frowning, not understanding. “Then why do you recite this spell?”

You shrug. “I do not know,” you say. “It is something to say.”

He looks at you for a long time. Then he says, “I do not believe you. I think you are a wizard.”

You shrug again. “Believe what you want,” you say. “It makes no difference to me.”

He hesitates, then turns to go. But he pauses at the door and looks back at you. “I will be watching you,” he says. “If I find out you are a wizard, I will tell the authorities.”

You say nothing. You watch him go. Then you turn back to your books and your thoughts. The words of the spell linger in your mind, like an echo that will not fade. You wonder what they mean
