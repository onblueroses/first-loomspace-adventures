 "I am not permitted to enter," it says, "but you must tell me: do you know the words that will open the way?" The visitor is tall, a shape in a black robe, a hood covering its face. It is a dream, you think, but you answer it anyway. "I know the words," you say, "but I do not know what they will open." The visitor nods. "You must choose," it says. "You must choose whether to speak them." You hesitate. The visitor waits. "Will you speak the words?" it asks. "I will," you say, and the visitor vanishes. The dream ends, but the words remain with you, and the choice. You do not know what the words will open. You do not know what waits beyond the threshold. But you know the words, and you know the choice. You must decide whether to speak. You must decide whether to open the way. The words are yours, and the choice is yours, and the threshold is yours, and the unknown is yours.
