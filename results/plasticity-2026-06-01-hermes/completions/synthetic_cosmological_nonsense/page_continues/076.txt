 “The following text appears in the same hand, in a different ink, perhaps added later: ‘To be spoken, without error, three times, at the confluence of the rivers.’” Underneath this note is written, in yet a third ink, “Rivers? Confluence? What rivers? What confluence?”

On the next page, this note appears, in the same hand as the previous one, but in a different ink: “The author is perhaps referring to the confluence of the rivers Mork and Phelth, which meet at the eastern edge of the city of Morn. If so, this would suggest a date for the manuscript of not earlier than the early seventh century, when Morn was founded.”

The main text continues:

Hear me, and make all spirits subject unto me, so that every spirit of the firmament and of the ether, upon the earth and under the earth, on dry land and in the water, of whirling air and of rushing fire, and every spell and scourge of God may be obedient unto me.

I invoke Thee, the Vrolthen and Witnessing One who dwellest beyond the syllable: SNORKLENDROPHIA, KRELPHENDROL, VRE
