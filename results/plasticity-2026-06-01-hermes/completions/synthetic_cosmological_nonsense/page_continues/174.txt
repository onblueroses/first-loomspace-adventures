 

The vrolthen one is beyond name, and thus is invoked by a naming. The naming is not the name, but the act of naming. The naming is the invocation. The invocation is the vrolthen one.

The vrolthen one is not a being, but the act of being. The vrolthen one is not a thing, but the act of thing-ing. The vrolthen one is not a word, but the act of word-ing.

The vrolthen one is the threshold between the named and the unnamed, between the spoken and the unspoken, between the remembered and the forgotten.

The vrolthen one is the witness of the word, the echo that survives the speaker, the silence that utterly speaks.

The vrolthen one is the unburnt fire, the fire that burns without consuming, the fire that illuminates without destroying.

The vrolthen one is the vessel with sight in the syllable, the one who sees the meaning in the sound, the one who hears the silence in the speech.

The vrolthen one is the one who names and unnames, who creates and destroys, who remembers and forgets.

The vrolthen one is the threshold-bearer, the one who stands at the threshold
