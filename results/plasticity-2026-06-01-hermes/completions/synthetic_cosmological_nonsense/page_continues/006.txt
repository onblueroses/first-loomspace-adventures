 "And when you have said these words, the spirits will have no power over you, and you will have authority over them." The ritual ends with the injunction to "keep this book secret," with a curse on anyone who reveals its contents.

## Analysis

The text is generally thought to be a late Hellenistic (c. 1st-3rd centuries AD) production. Earlier scholarship tended to argue for an Egyptian origin, on account of the use of the epithets "Vrolthen" and "Vrendrelphor" (from Egyptian Vrendr, "child"; however, more recent scholars have argued that the text may have originated in the Greco-Roman world, on account of its use of Greek words and syntactical structures. Some scholars have argued that it may have originated in the context of the Neoplatonism of Late Antiquity, as the text shows some signs of Neoplatonic influence.

The text is notable for its use of a complex and highly artificial language, which is not attested in any other source. The language appears to be a form of artificial or constructed language, similar to those found in other magical texts from Late Antiquity, such as the Papyri Graecae Magicae
