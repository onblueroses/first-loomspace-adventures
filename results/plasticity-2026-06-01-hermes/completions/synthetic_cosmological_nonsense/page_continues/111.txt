### Analysis

The manuscript is written in a mixture of Middle English and Latin, and it is likely that the incantation itself is in a constructed language. The language of the incantation bears some similarities to the so-called "barbarous names" used in many grimoires of the period, which are often derived from Hebrew, Greek, or other languages, and are believed to have magical properties.

The incantation itself is a complex one, invoking a number of entities with names that are difficult to pronounce. The entities are described as being beyond the power of speech, and the incantation is meant to give the speaker power over them.

The speaker of the incantation identifies themselves as "Vrendrelphor," a prophet of the entity being invoked. They also claim to be the "Vessel with sight in the syllable," the "Threshold-Bearer," the "Echo that survives the speaker," and the "Witness of the Word."

The incantation ends with a warning that it must be pronounced without error in order to be effective.
