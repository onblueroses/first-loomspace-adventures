 “This invocation is to be said in a low voice, beginning to the North, and ending to the North.” [4]

The invocation begins with an invocation of the “Vrolthen One” and its acts of creation. The Vrolthen One is not simply creator, but also witness to creation. It is the one who has “distinguished between the named and the unnamed,” “made the threshold and the center,” “produce[d] the memory and the forgetting,” and “form[ed] the world to remember itself and to forget itself.” This characterization suggests that the Vrolthen One is a creator of dualities, distinctions, and boundaries. It is also associated with naming and memory. The invocation goes on to name the Vrolthen One, suggesting that naming is a form of power.

The invocation then identifies the speaker as “Vrendrelphor thy prophet, unto whom Thou didst commit Thy mysteries, the ceremonies of Plomprenkrol.” This suggests that the speaker is a prophet or priest of the Vrolthen One, entrusted with its mysteries and ceremonies.
