 “It is not known who wrote this invocation, or when. The first manuscript is in the collection of the University of M——, and is probably from the 16th century. The meaning of the words ‘Vrolthen One’ is not known. The phrase ‘the lost’ may refer to a lost tribe, or simply to the dead.”

The invocation, we may assume, is used in a ritual, but we know nothing about the ritual. The name Vrokthemnarph is said to be “unspoken,” yet the invocation speaks it. Perhaps it is unspoken in the sense of not being used in ordinary speech. The name is followed by other names, which may be variant names for the same being, or names of other beings that are being invoked along with Vrokthemnarph.

The speaker of the invocation, who identifies himself as Vrendrelphor, claims to be a prophet and to have received mysteries and ceremonies from the being he is invoking. The being is credited with creating various dichotomies: the named and the unnamed, the threshold and the center, memory and forgetting. These dichotomies may have some significance in the ritual, but the exact significance is not stated.
