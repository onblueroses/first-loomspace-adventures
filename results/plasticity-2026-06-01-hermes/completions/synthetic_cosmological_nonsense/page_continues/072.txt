 "What follows is a litany of names, some of which are clearly ancient in origin, others of which seem to be of later provenance. The incantation is written in an unusual idiom, which may be a cipher of some kind. The text concludes with a series of invocations, each of which contains a hundred and eight names for the Vrolthen One."

The researchers are clearly excited by this discovery. "The Vrolthen One is a figure we've seen mentioned in other texts," explains Dr. Netherstrom, "but this is by far the most extensive and detailed description we've found. The fact that this manuscript contains the Vrolthen One's 'true name' is remarkable. In the occult tradition, knowing the true name of a supernatural being gives the knower power over that being. This suggests that the manuscript may have been used in magical rituals."

The researchers are currently working on translating the rest of the manuscript and analyzing its contents. They hope that this discovery will shed new light on the history of occultism and the role that language and naming play in magical traditions.
