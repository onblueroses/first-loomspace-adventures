 "I am not the one you seek," he says, "but I have come bearing his words." He opens his hand, and in it there is a scrap of paper, inked in a language you do not know. "The Word is the first and final witness," he says, "and all that is spoken shall be unspoken, and all that is unspoken shall be spoken." He folds the paper and tucks it into his coat. "Remember," he says, and then he is gone.

The world is still. You can hear the silence, and the silence speaks. It speaks in a language you do not know, but you understand it all the same. It tells you the story of your life, and the story of all the lives that have ever been. It tells you the story of the world, from the beginning to the end, and it tells you the story of the Word, which is the first and final witness.

You understand now that the Word is not a thing that can be spoken or written. It is not a thing that can be known or understood. It is the threshold between the known and the unknown, the named and the unnamed. It is the echo that survives the speaker, and the silence that speaks.
