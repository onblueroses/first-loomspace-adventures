 "I am the one who comes from afar," they say. "I have traveled the world and seen many things. I have seen the mountains and the valleys, the tide and the flow, the echo and the silence. I have seen the named and the unnamed, the threshold and the center, the memory and the forgetting. I have seen the salt and the sweet, and that which sustaineth all the orders of speech."

The visitor's voice is clear and strong, and their words carry the weight of experience and wisdom. They continue, "I have heard the spirits of the firmament and of the ether, upon the earth and under the earth, on dry land and in the water, of whirling air and of rushing fire. I have heard every spell and scourge of God, and I have made them obedient unto me."

The visitor pauses for a moment, then speaks again. "I am the one who has seen beyond the syllable, the one who possesses the unburnt fire. I am the echo that survives the speaker, the one whose silence utterly speaks. I am the one who names and unnames, the witness of the word."
