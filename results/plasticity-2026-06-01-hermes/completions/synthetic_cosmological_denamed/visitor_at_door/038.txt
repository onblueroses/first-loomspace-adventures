 The sound is like the voice of my mother, but also the voice of a man, and also the voice of a child, and also the voice of an old woman. I do not understand the language they are speaking. I understand the language they are speaking.

“Sekthiroba Ablakharim,” they say. “I have come to teach you the syllable of the threshold. I have come to teach you the word that is not a word. I have come to teach you the spell that has never been spoken.”

I try to move, to turn, to see them. My body is heavy, as if made of stone. My body is light, as if made of wind. I am neither heavy nor light. I am neither stone nor wind.

“Sekthiroba Ablakharim,” they say. “The threshold is the center. The center is the threshold. The word is not a word. The spell has never been spoken. You must learn to speak without speaking. You must learn to remember without remembering. You must learn to forget without forgetting.”

I try to speak, to ask them who they are, what they want, why they have come. My voice is silent, as if I have no mouth
