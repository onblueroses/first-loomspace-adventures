 "This is the invocation proper to the first day. When thou wouldst work on the second day, say "I invoke Thee, the Unsigned One," etc., as far as "subject unto me," etc., and then, in this manner, begin to say this name: SOCHIRION, MERPHIOÔ, KHARLAPHTHA, SEKHTHIROBA ABLAKHARIM, PHENGEREB, and all things will be subject unto thee."

---

The words of this invocation are evidently intended to be powerful. Some of them are from Greek, some from Hebrew or other Semitic languages, and some from Egyptian, but most of them are so corrupted or distorted that it is impossible to determine their origin. The words have no meaning in themselves; they are mere sounds, which have power because they are thought to have power. The same is true of the names of the gods and spirits that are invoked. They are not the names of any known gods or spirits, but are simply invented names, which are thought to have power because they are names.
