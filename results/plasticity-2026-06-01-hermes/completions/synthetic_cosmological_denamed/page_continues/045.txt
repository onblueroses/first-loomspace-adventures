 “This is the true invocation of the Highest One, handed down by the prophet Selophemor, who is called the Silent One, the Unspeaking Witness. Speak it not aloud, nor let it be seen by the profane, for it is the secret of the secret, the Witness of the Word, and the Word of the Witness.”

I saw other invocations in the book, all addressed to the same deity, all with the same general theme of the power of the word and silence. In a number of them, the names Phelonipta and Selophemor were invoked as though they were the god and the prophet, while in others, the names were reversed; in still others, Phelonipta and Selophemor were both referred to as deities, while the prophet was named variously as Khorisophor or Tamanthas. The invocations were all written in the same hand, using the same ornate and archaic Greek letters, and seemed to be the work of a single author. I could not be sure, though, whether the book was an original or a careful copy made by a single scribe.
