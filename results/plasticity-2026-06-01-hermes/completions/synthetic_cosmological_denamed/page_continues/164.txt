 "End of the Rite of the Unnamed."

The first section of the rite, up to the long list of barbarous names, is a poetic invocation of the deity, establishing the main themes of the rite. The deity is nameless, yet has many names. It is associated with dualities, such as mountains and valleys, tide and flow, echo and silence. It is also associated with memory and forgetting, and with the threshold and the center.

The second section, from the barbarous names to the end, is a more direct invocation, calling on the deity to make all spirits subject to the magician. The magician identifies themselves as the "Vessel" and the "Witness of the Word," and claims various powers and attributes, such as possessing unburnt fire and the ability to name and unname.

The rite ends with two more barbarous names, which may be the magician's magical name or a final invocation of the deity.

The themes of naming and unnaming, memory and forgetting, and the threshold and the center suggest a deity associated with the boundaries of language and reality. The barbarous names and the claim to command all spirits suggest a ritual with a strong magical component, aimed at invoking a powerful deity and harnessing its power
