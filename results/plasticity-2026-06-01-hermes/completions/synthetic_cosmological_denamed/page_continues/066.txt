After this passage there is a space, and the text concludes:

In the name of the Witness and the Vessel, and of the Unsigned One, and of the Eternal Name, so be it.

---

It is of course impossible to render the full effect of the original text. The manuscript is written in a flowing and elegant script, and the interlinear glosses are in a different hand, smaller and more cramped, as if added later. The page is surrounded by a border of intricate geometric designs, and the initial letters of each paragraph are illuminated in gold and red. The overall impression is of a work of great beauty and mystery, a glimpse into a world of esoteric knowledge and power.

It is also worth noting that the text is not entirely consistent in its use of names and titles. The Unsigned One is referred to variously as Phelonipta, Krethaumis, Demaphris, Velphiraim, and Iphtoron, while the prophet is called Selophemor and the Vessel is identified as both Phorbomanes APHRYBETOSH and SEKHTHI
