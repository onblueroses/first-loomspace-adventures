 "It was the custom of the ancient priests of the Ethiopians, when they wanted to make a prophecy, to take an earthen vessel new and pure, and to write upon it the name of the Unsigned, and they placed it upon the threshold of the temple. And the Unsigned spoke, and the words were heard. And when the words were heard, the Unsigned was silent. And the priests wrote the words of the Unsigned upon the vessel, and sealed it with the seal of the temple."

The vessel here is a metaphor for the prophet Selophemor, and the Unsigned's voice is a metaphor for divine inspiration. The threshold of the temple represents the boundary between the named and the unnamed, the expressible and the inexpressible. By placing the vessel on the threshold, the priests symbolically positioned themselves at the boundary between the human and the divine, the known and the unknown. The Unsigned's voice represents the flow of divine wisdom and inspiration from the unmanifest into the manifest realm. When the words were heard and written down, the vessel (i.e., the prophet) became a container for the divine message.
