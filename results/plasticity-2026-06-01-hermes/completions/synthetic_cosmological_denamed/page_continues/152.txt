 “This is a complete translation, according to the version of H. A. D. E., of the ‘Invocation of the Unsigned One,’ a liturgical work which has been dated to the second century BCE. It was discovered among the Oxyrhynchus Papyri (P. Oxy. 4950) in the late 19th century, and is now held at the Sackler Library in Oxford. The invocation is an early example of an extended performative utterance, a speech act that accomplishes the state of affairs to which it refers. The speaker takes on the role of Selophemor, the prophetic Vessel of the deity Phorbomanes, and assumes the god’s power over language, memory, and reality. The invocation is known for its distinctive use of the vocative case, and has been linked to the ancient rhetorical exercise of enklisis, in which the speaker assumes the voice of another person or entity.”
