 “The Invocation of the Unsigned One, being the First of the Five Invocations of the Liminalities, was spoken by the prophet Selophemor, who died in the city of Naliph in the year 1126.”

The second invocation, the Invocation of the Unnamed One, is quite similar, except that the Unnamed One is a different being from the Unsigned One, and has a different set of names and titles. The prophet who speaks the invocation is also different, as is the year of his death. This pattern continues through the five invocations.

The five invocations are followed by an account of their discovery, which seems to be fictional, but is interesting in itself. It is written by an unnamed narrator who claims to have discovered the invocations in the ruins of the city of Naliph, which was destroyed in an earthquake in 1126, the same year that the last of the five prophets died. The narrator describes the city as having been a center of learning and culture, with many libraries and temples. He also mentions that the city was known for its unusual architecture, with many buildings having secret rooms and passages.
