## Analysis

### Parallels

In his analysis of the manuscript, Bowersock notes that it contains "a rich combination of many motifs that can be found in other texts of the same period and genre," with "clear echoes" of the Greek Magical Papyri, the Hermetic corpus, the Neoplatonism of Iamblichus, and the Chaldaean Oracles. He also points to parallels in the Book of the Zodiac, the Gnostic texts found in the Nag Hammadi library, and the Mandaean texts of the Ginza Rabba.

### Theology

The theology of the text revolves around a supreme, unnamed deity, "the Unsigned One," and its relationship to the created world. According to the text, the Unsigned One created the world in such a way that it would both remember and forget its divine origin. The deity also established a distinction between the "named" and "unnamed," which Bowersock interprets as referring to the difference between the material world and the divine realm.
