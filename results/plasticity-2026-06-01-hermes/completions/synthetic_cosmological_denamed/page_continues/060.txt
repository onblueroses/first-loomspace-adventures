 “The names of the spirits, their powers, and the manner of invoking them follow. The practitioner must take care to utter their names precisely, with perfect pronunciation, as the spirits will not attend a voice which speaks their name imperfectly. Moreover, they are only to be invoked in a place which is silent and free of distraction, for they are easily deterred by noise.”

The remaining pages of the manuscript are lost.

The ritual begins with a series of invocations. The speaker identifies themselves as “the Unsigned One”, who is unnamed and unnamable, and calls upon the deity Phelonipta, who is similarly unnamed and unnamable. The speaker then identifies themselves as Selophemor, the prophet of Phelonipta, and invokes the deity using a series of secret names.

The speaker then asks the deity to make all spirits subject to them, so that they may command the spirits of the earth, air, fire, and water, as well as the spells and scourges of God. The speaker again invokes the deity using a series of secret names, and identifies themselves as the “Vessel with sight in the syllable”, the “Threshold-Bearer”, the “Echo that survives the speaker”, and the “Witness of the Word”.
