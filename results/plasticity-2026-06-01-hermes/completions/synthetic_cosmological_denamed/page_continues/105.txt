## Selophemor and the Invocation

Selophemor is a mysterious figure in the text, identified as the second voice of Phelonipta and the prophet to whom Phelonipta committed their mysteries and ceremonies. The invocation itself is a powerful one, calling upon Phelonipta and several other entities with names that are evocative and esoteric. The language of the invocation is rich with imagery, speaking of creation, the power of naming, and the dichotomies of memory and forgetting, salt and sweet.

The invocation ends with a declaration of the speaker's identity as the Vessel with sight in the syllable, the Threshold-Bearer, the Echo that survives the speaker, and the Witness of the Word. This suggests a complex and powerful role for the one who speaks the invocation, as someone who stands at the boundary between worlds and holds power over them.
