 A spirit? A demon? A god? The world’s most articulate squid? The room doesn’t know. It doesn’t have the words for what it sees. But it can describe it, after a fashion. And it can speak its name.

The thing that enters the room is a mass of writhing tentacles. They are not the tentacles of any cephalopod or jellyfish known to science. They are not constrained by the limits of biology. They are tendrils of shadow and smoke, wisps of cloud and fog, coils of ink and oil. They are the tentacles of a creature that is not quite real, not quite unreal. They are the tentacles of a being that exists in the liminal space between the named and the unnamed, the spoken and the unspoken.

The tentacles writhe and twist, forming intricate patterns in the air. They are mesmerizing to watch, like a kaleidoscope of darkness. They seem to have a will of their own, each one moving independently of the others. Yet there is a harmony to their chaos, a purpose to their randomness. They are not flailing blindly. They are searching, probing, exploring.
