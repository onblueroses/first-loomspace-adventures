 "It is necessary that the circle or place wherein the invocation is made be clean, pure, and bright, also that it be void of noise or disturbance, because the spirits are not to be called in a polluted or noisy place."

The following is a description of the appearance of the spirits of Mercury:

"The spirits of Mercury appear like clerks or servants, and their king is called Gorson, who speaks with a high and subtle voice, but his words are wonderful. He giveth to them that call on him all kinds of metals, he teacheth geometry and all kinds of measurements. He giveth the power of transporting the magician whither he will, and of flying in the air. He giveth also the knowledge of all means whereby one may know the representations of experiments, and teaches the easy means of understanding all things. He hath under him twenty-nine kings, thirty-two dukes, thirty-five princes, twenty-eight marquises, twenty-six earls, nineteen presidents, twenty-three prelates, and twelve rectors."

It is unnecessary to quote further from the "Ars Paulina," as the foregoing extracts will give the reader a fair idea of the character of its contents.
