 "The formula to invoke the dead or to have the devil appear in person in the mirror of Solomon. After having recited the invocation, one must say in a loud voice, while looking at the mirror, 'Mirror, I order you in the name of the great living God to show me if the angel or spirit of [n.] is present or not.'" The manuscript also contains a recipe for an ointment that makes it possible to see the spirits. (See further the edition of the manuscript by Gollancz 1914, 31–33.)
In the second case, the story is about a group of students in Paris who attempted to invoke the devil with the help of a book of magic spells. They drew a circle on the floor and placed a container of charcoal in its center. When the charcoal was set on fire, the room was filled with smoke. The students fled, but one of them was found lifeless in the circle, and only the arrival of a priest saved his life. This story is from the early thirteenth-century manuscript of Caesarius von Heisterbach's Dialogus miraculorum (Dialogue on Miracles).
