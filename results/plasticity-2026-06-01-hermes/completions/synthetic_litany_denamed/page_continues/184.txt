 "When these names are spoken, the Gate shall open and the way shall be prepared for the arrival of the King. When the King has taken his throne, then shall the great works begin." This passage suggests that the gate is not simply a physical barrier but a metaphorical one, representing the transition from the current state of existence to a new era marked by the arrival of a powerful entity or force.

The passage also implies that the opening of the gate is not an end in itself but a means to an end—the beginning of "great works" that will presumably transform the world or the lives of those who invoke the ritual. The nature of these works is not specified, leaving room for interpretation as to whether they are benevolent or malevolent, creative or destructive.

The list of names is a common feature in magical and esoteric texts, often representing a hierarchy of spiritual beings, angels, or demons that the practitioner seeks to invoke or control. The names themselves are mostly unique to the Necronomicon and do not have clear etymologies or meanings in any known language, which adds to the mystery and exoticism of the text.
