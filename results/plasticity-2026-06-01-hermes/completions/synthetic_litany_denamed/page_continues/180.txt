 "In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, who was crucified under Pontius Pilate, in the name of the Father, Son and Holy Ghost, Amen." The document then continues with a series of invocations of the "angels of the East" and other directions.

The remainder of the book consists of prayers and invocations to various spirits, and a series of magical seals. It is notable that the angelic hierarchy that is presented is not the same as the hierarchy presented in the Golden Dawn, which in turn is derived from Peter of Abano's Heptameron. There is no evidence that the author of the Goetia had access to the Golden Dawn material, or vice versa.

The Goetia has been criticized by practitioners for having a number of fatal flaws, including the lack of any method to test the veracity of spirits, and the requirement that the operator summon all 72 spirits before they can be commanded. It has been suggested that this requirement is designed to exhaust the operator and make them more susceptible to manipulation by the spirits. Modern practitioners often amend the text or use an alternate version, such as the Heptameron, when working with the Goetia.
