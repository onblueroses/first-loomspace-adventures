 It’s not the people. They remain seated, looking at one another, speaking in low tones. It’s something else. Something that’s been waiting to make its entrance. It’s the anticipation of the event, the event that is part of the performance. The event is common enough, it’s nameable, it’s in the program. But what precedes the event, what leads us toward it, is not nameable, or else named only in ways that are inadequate or clichéd. A certain kind of energy, perhaps. But what kind of energy, specifically? It’s the energy of anticipation, the energy of uncertainty, the energy of waiting for something to happen. The energy of the audience as they wait for something to happen, as they wait for the event to unfold, as they wait for the performance to begin. The energy of the performers as they wait to perform, as they wait to enter the room, as they wait to become part of the event. The energy of the room itself, the room that is waiting to be filled with the energy of the event, the room that is waiting to be transformed by the energy of the event.
