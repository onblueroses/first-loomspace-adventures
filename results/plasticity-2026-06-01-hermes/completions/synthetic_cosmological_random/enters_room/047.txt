A river ecosystem is a complex and dynamic network of living organisms interacting with their physical environment, all shaped by the constant movement of water. At the heart of this ecosystem is the river itself, a flowing body of water that originates from a source (often in highlands) and travels downhill due to gravity, eventually reaching an outlet such as an ocean, lake, or another river.

The nature of a river ecosystem is largely determined by several key factors:
1. **Catchment Area**: The entire area of land that drains into the river, including all its tributaries. The size, shape, and land use within the catchment significantly influence the river's flow, water quality, and nutrient inputs.
2. **Climate**: Precipitation patterns, temperature, and seasonal variations affect the volume and consistency of water flow, shaping the physical and biological components of the ecosystem.
3. **Underlying Geology**: The types of rocks and soils in the catchment influence the river's chemistry, affecting pH, nutrient levels, and the types of organisms that can thrive.
