 The room is the river ecosystem and all of its inhabitants. It enters through the door of the river mouth, the place where the river flows into the sea or a lake. The room is the river ecosystem, and the door is the river mouth.

The river mouth is the gateway between the river ecosystem and the larger body of water it flows into, be it an ocean, a sea, or a lake. This is the point of confluence where the freshwater of the river meets the saltwater of the sea, or the point where the river's flow slows down and spreads out into the still waters of a lake. The river mouth is a dynamic and complex environment, characterized by the mixing of waters, the deposition of sediments, and the adaptation of species to the unique conditions found there.

At the river mouth, the velocity of the river's flow decreases rapidly, causing the sediment carried by the river to settle out and form deltas, sandbars, or estuaries. These landforms can be highly productive ecosystems, supporting a wide range of plant and animal life. The mixing of freshwater and saltwater in estuaries creates a brackish environment that is home to species specially adapted to tolerate varying levels of salinity.
