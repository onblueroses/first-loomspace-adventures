 The river is a physical system, the water that flows, but it is also an ecosystem, a living system. It is an ecosystem because it is a habitat for living organisms, and these organisms are interconnected. The river is a living thing. It is not just water flowing over rocks. It is a complex web of life, a community of organisms that depend on each other and on the physical characteristics of the river.

The river ecosystem is shaped by the movement of water. The water flows in one direction, from the source to the mouth, and this shapes the biological community. The river ecosystem is different from a lake or pond ecosystem because the water is always moving. This movement affects the types of organisms that can live in the river and how they interact with each other.

The river ecosystem is also shaped by the catchment area, the climate, the geology, and the gradient of the channel. The catchment area is the land that drains into the river.
