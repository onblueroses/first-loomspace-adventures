 An overcast above
The moving grasses, a chill in the air,
And a sense that something unseen is here,
The wraith of a season about to depart.
A few leaves drift from the willow tree
And fall near the still pond, and in the hush
Of late afternoon the first cricket begins
A tentative song, and then another, and then
A chorus. I walk down to the water’s edge,
The short grass cool beneath my bare feet,
And I stand at the pond’s edge, watching
The water striders that skim the surface
And the small fish that swim just below.
I am thinking of how much life there is
In a cubic foot of earth or water, how much
Happens in the space of a minute or two.
I am thinking of the many small things
That contribute to the larger world,
The unseen and the seen, the minute
And the vast, the chorus that sings
In the grasses and the chorus that sings
In the heavens, and how each depends
On the other, and how each contributes
To the whole, and how each is a part
Of the whole, and how the whole is a part
Of each. And I am thinking of how
