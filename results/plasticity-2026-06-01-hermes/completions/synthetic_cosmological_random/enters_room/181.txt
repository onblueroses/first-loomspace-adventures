 This is an ecological community of interdependent organisms, and the physical environment they inhabit, that is defined by the unidirectional flow of water. The water shapes the community. And this community is distinguished from still-water ecosystems, such as lakes and ponds, by the fact that the water is always moving downstream, carrying dissolved minerals, organic matter, and living organisms with it. The character of this ecosystem depends on several factors: the area of land that drains water into it, the climate of the region, the underlying geology, and the steepness of the channel from where the water starts to where it ends up.

At the beginning of the water flow, the water is typically cold, full of oxygen, and moves quickly over rocks and coarse gravel. The community here is mostly aquatic insects whose larvae cling to the rocks against the current, and cold-water fish like trout that eat the insects. The energy base of the food web comes from outside the water, in the form of leaves and woody debris falling from the bordering forest, which is eaten by shredder invertebrates.
