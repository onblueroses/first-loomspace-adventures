 With this river ecosystem, we have two components. One is the abiotic component, which consists of nonliving things, and the second one is the biotic component, which consists of living organisms. So we'll start with the abiotic components first. So the abiotic components include the sunlight, the water, the soil, the air, and the rocks. And for the biotic components, we have the producers, which include the phytoplankton, the plants, and the algae. And we also have the consumers, which include the zooplankton, the insects, the amphibians, the fish, and the birds. And we also have the decomposers, which include the bacteria and the fungi. Now, let's talk about the zones of a river ecosystem. So the first zone is the source zone, which is where the river begins. This zone is characterized by cold, clear water and a high oxygen content. The next zone is the transition zone, which is where the river starts to widen and slow down. This zone is characterized by warmer water and a lower oxygen content.
