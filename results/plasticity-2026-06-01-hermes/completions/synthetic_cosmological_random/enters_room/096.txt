The Shenandoah River is a major tributary of the Potomac River, flowing through the U.S. states of Virginia and West Virginia. The Shenandoah is a classic example of a temperate river ecosystem, supporting a diverse array of aquatic life adapted to its varied habitats.

The river's headwaters form in the Appalachian Mountains, where swift, cold streams tumble over rocky beds. These headwater streams are shaded by dense forests, which provide leaf litter and woody debris as the base of the food web. Aquatic insects like mayflies, stoneflies, and caddisflies thrive in these oxygen-rich waters, serving as the primary food source for native brook trout.

As the Shenandoah flows out of the mountains and into the Shenandoah Valley, it widens and warms. The river's middle reaches feature long riffles, deep pools, and extensive gravel bars.
