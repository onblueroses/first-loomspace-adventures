 Ecosystems are often interconnected with one another in some way. The transition between two ecosystems is called an ecotone. For example, the transition from a forest to a grassland is an ecotone. Ecosystems may also be divided into smaller, more specific units called microecosystems. An ecosystem can be as large as the Sahara Desert or as small as a puddle. In practice, most ecosystem studies are carried out on much smaller spatial scales.

Ecosystems are dynamic entities. They are subject to periodic disturbances and are always in the process of recovering from some past disturbance. When a perturbation occurs, an ecosystem responds by moving away from its initial state. The tendency of an ecosystem to remain close to its equilibrium state, despite that disturbance, is termed its resistance. On the other hand, the speed with which it returns to its initial state after disturbance is called its resilience.

Ecosystems are complex adaptive systems that constantly change in response to their environment and the species within them.
