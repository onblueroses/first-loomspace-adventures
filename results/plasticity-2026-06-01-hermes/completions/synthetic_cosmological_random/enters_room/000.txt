The river ecosystem is a dynamic and complex system that is shaped by various physical, chemical, and biological factors. The unidirectional flow of water is a defining characteristic of river ecosystems, which distinguishes them from lentic ecosystems such as lakes and ponds. The continuous downstream transport of water, dissolved minerals, particulate organic matter, and living organisms plays a crucial role in shaping the biological community and the overall functioning of the ecosystem.

The character of a river ecosystem is influenced by several key factors, including the catchment area, regional climate, underlying geology, and the gradient of the channel from the source to the mouth. These factors determine the physical and chemical properties of the river, such as water temperature, flow velocity, substrate composition, and nutrient availability, which in turn shape the biological community and the trophic structure of the ecosystem.

In headwater streams, the water is typically cold, oxygen-rich, and fast-flowing over bedrock and coarse gravel. The biological community in these streams is dominated by aquatic insects, whose larvae cling to substrates against the current.
