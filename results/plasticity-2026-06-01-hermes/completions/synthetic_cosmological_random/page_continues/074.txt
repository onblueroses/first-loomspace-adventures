 

Characteristics of River Ecosystems

River ecosystems are characterized by several distinctive features:

1. Linear and Unidirectional: Unlike lakes and ponds, which are lentic (still water) systems, rivers are lotic (flowing water) systems characterized by a linear and unidirectional flow of water from the source to the mouth.

2. Gradient and Flow: The flow of water in a river is influenced by the gradient or slope of the riverbed. Rivers typically have a higher gradient in their upper reaches (near the source) and a lower gradient in their lower reaches (near the mouth). This gradient affects the speed of water flow, with faster currents in steeper sections.

3. Physical Diversity: Rivers exhibit a wide range of physical conditions, including variations in water depth, velocity, temperature, and substrate composition (e.g., rocks, gravel, sand, silt). These diverse physical conditions create a variety of microhabitats that support different species.

4. Connectivity: Rivers are highly connected systems, both longitudinally (from source to mouth) and laterally (with the surrounding terrestrial ecosystem). This connectivity allows for the movement of organisms, nutrients, and energy along the river continuum.
