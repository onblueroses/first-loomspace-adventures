 "River ecosystems are flowing waters that drain the landscape, and include the biotic (living) interactions amongst plants, animals and micro-organisms, as well as abiotic (nonliving) physical and chemical interactions of its many parts. River ecosystems are prime examples of lotic ecosystems. Lotic refers to flowing water, from the Latin lotus, meaning washed. Lotic waters range from springs only a few centimeters wide to major rivers kilometers in width. Much of this article applies to lotic ecosystems in general, including related lotic systems such as streams and springs. Lotic ecosystems have two major zones, rapids (where water is fast) and pools (where water is slow). The speed of water also determines the nature of the ecosystem. The source of the river water is usually considered the headwaters. In addition, the immediate environment the river flows through also impacts the river ecosystem. The flora and fauna of the riparian zone (the immediate surroundings of the river) are more diverse and generally more dense than that of the riverbank. The riparian zone acts as a buffer between the river and the upland landscape, reducing sedimentation and pollution from overland surface runoff.
