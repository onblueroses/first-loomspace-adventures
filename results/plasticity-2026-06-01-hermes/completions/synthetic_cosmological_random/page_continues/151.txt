 "River ecosystems are flowing waters that drain the landscape, and include the biotic (living) interactions amongst plants, animals and micro-organisms, as well as abiotic (nonliving) physical and chemical interactions of its many parts.[1] River ecosystems are prime examples of lotic ecosystems. These ecosystems are very fragile and can be easily disturbed by pollution. They can be divided into three categories based on their location: the upper or headwater streams, the middle reaches, and the lower reaches. The following unifying characteristics make the ecology of running waters unique from that of other aquatic habitats." (CC BY-SA)
