 "The characteristics of a river ecosystem are shaped by the channel's gradient, the local geology, and the climate of the region. The physical characteristics of a river ecosystem determine the biological community that inhabits the channel. Substrate composition and water temperature are key factors. In a natural river ecosystem, the channel bed is shaped by local geology and hydrology. In a headwater stream, the channel bed is often composed of bedrock or boulders, with fast-moving water running over and between the large substrates. As the river widens and slows in its middle and lower reaches, the channel substrate shifts from gravel to sand to silt. The water warms as it slows, allowing more diverse plant life to grow along the river banks. In these lower reaches, aquatic plants may grow in the river channel itself, further slowing the flow of water and providing habitat for fish and other aquatic life. The biological community in a river ecosystem is shaped by the physical characteristics of the channel. In a headwater stream, the community is dominated by aquatic insects whose larvae cling to substrates against the current. Cold-water fish such as trout occupy the predator niche.
