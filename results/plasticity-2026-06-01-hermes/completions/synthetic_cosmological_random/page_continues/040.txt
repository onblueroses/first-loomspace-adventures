 "The scientific study of river ecosystems began in the early 20th century with the work of limnologists such as F. A. Forel and Einar Naumann, who developed the concept of lotic ecosystems to describe the dynamic, flowing-water environment of rivers and streams. A key characteristic of lotic ecosystems is the unidirectional flow of water, which shapes the physical, chemical, and biological processes that occur within the river channel. This flow creates a gradient of physical conditions, from fast-flowing, oxygen-rich headwaters to slower, warmer, and more nutrient-rich lower reaches, which supports a diverse array of aquatic life adapted to different conditions along the river continuum."
