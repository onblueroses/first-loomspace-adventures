## 6.1.2. River ecosystems

River ecosystems are flowing waters that drain the landscape, and include the biotic (living) interactions amongst plants, animals and micro-organisms, as well as abiotic (nonliving) physical and chemical interactions of its many parts. River ecosystems are prime examples of lotic ecosystems. Including: the rivers, streams, and springs; connected to: ponds, wetlands, and upland terrestrial ecosystems through which rivers flow. Lotic systems are distinguished from lentic systems, which are more stagnant and have stiller waters, like ponds or lakes.

The following unifying characteristics make the ecology of running waters unique among aquatic habitats.

• The flow is unidirectional.
• There is a state of continuous physical change.
• There is a high degree of spatial and temporal heterogeneity at all scales (microhabitats).
• Variability between lotic systems is quite high.
• The biota is specialized to live with flow conditions.
