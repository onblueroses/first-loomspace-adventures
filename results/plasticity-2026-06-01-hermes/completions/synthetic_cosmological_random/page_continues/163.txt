 "River ecosystems are flowing waters that drain the landscape, and include the biotic (living) interactions amongst plants, animals and micro-organisms, as well as abiotic (nonliving) physical and chemical interactions of its many parts.[1][2] River ecosystems are prime examples of lotic ecosystems. Lotic refers to flowing water, from the Latin lotus, meaning washed. Lotic waters range from springs only a few centimeters wide to major rivers kilometers in width. Much of this article applies to lotic ecosystems in general, including related lotic systems such as streams and springs. Lotic ecosystems have two major zones, rapids, and pools. Ecosystems have specific characteristics that determine their function, such as nutrient cycling and energy flow. The food base of streams within lotic ecosystems is mostly from allochthonous sources, meaning that it is not produced within the ecosystem itself. Allochthonous sources include organic material derived from the surrounding terrestrial ecosystem that enters the water. The leaves and woody debris that fall into a river are a food source for the organisms within the water.
