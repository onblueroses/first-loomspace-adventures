 

River ecosystems provide goods and services to human societies, including clean water, nutrient cycling, food and fiber, energy, and recreation. The annual global value of rivers is estimated at $4.5 trillion, with the Yangtze, Amazon, and Mississippi basins contributing the greatest value. River ecosystems are among the most threatened globally, with over 60% strongly or moderately affected by human activity. The major threats to river ecosystems are habitat degradation, flow modification, and invasive species. Rivers with the highest degree of threat include the Mississippi, Yangtze, Danube, and Ganges. Conservation strategies focus on maintaining environmental flows, reducing pollution, protecting catchment land use, and managing invasive species. Key conservation measures include establishing protected areas, implementing integrated water-resources management, and restoring degraded river ecosystems. Climate change is expected to exacerbate the impacts of other threats to river ecosystems, with increased water temperatures, changes in precipitation patterns, and more frequent and severe floods and droughts. The long-term sustainability of river ecosystems depends on a combination of effective conservation measures and adaptation to climate change.
