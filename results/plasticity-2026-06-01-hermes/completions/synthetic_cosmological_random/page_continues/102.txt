 

The concept of a river ecosystem encompasses not just the biotic communities within the river channel, but also the interactions between the aquatic and terrestrial components of the catchment. The terrestrial environment influences the river ecosystem through factors such as shading, inputs of organic matter and nutrients, and land-use changes that alter the flow regime. Conversely, the river influences the terrestrial environment through factors such as flooding, nutrient cycling, and the provision of habitat for riparian vegetation.

The health of a river ecosystem is often assessed using biological indicators, such as the presence or absence of certain species or the diversity and abundance of key groups like aquatic insects, fish, and algae. These indicators can reflect the overall ecological integrity of the system, as well as the presence of specific stressors like pollution or habitat degradation.

River ecosystems provide a range of ecosystem services, including the provision of clean water, the support of biodiversity, and the regulation of floods and droughts. They also have significant cultural, recreational, and economic value, supporting activities like fishing, boating, and tourism.
