 

River ecosystems are complex and dynamic, shaped by the physical characteristics of the river channel, the surrounding landscape, and the biotic interactions within the system. The unidirectional flow of water is a defining feature, creating a gradient of physical conditions from the headwaters to the mouth. This gradient influences the distribution and abundance of species, as well as the processing and transport of nutrients and organic matter.

Headwater streams, often found in upland areas, are characterized by steep gradients, fast-flowing water, and a substrate dominated by bedrock and coarse gravel. The water is typically cold, oxygen-rich, and low in nutrients. The primary energy source in these systems is allochthonous, meaning it comes from outside the stream in the form of leaf litter and woody debris from the surrounding forest. This organic matter is broken down by shredder invertebrates, such as stoneflies and caddisflies, which form the base of the food web. Cold-water fish, like trout, occupy the predator niche in these systems.
