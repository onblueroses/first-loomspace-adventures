 “River ecosystem” is a phrase that describes the interconnected, living community of plants, animals, and microorganisms within a river and its associated physical environment. It includes the river channel, the adjacent floodplain, and the surrounding watershed, all of which contribute to the dynamic and complex nature of river ecosystems. These ecosystems are characterized by their flowing water, which shapes the physical environment and influences the types of organisms that can thrive there.

River ecosystems are dynamic and constantly changing due to the movement of water, which transports nutrients, sediments, and organisms downstream. This unidirectional flow creates a gradient of conditions from the headwaters to the mouth of the river, leading to a diverse array of habitats and species adapted to different conditions. The flow of water also affects the temperature, oxygen levels, and water chemistry, which are critical factors for the survival of aquatic organisms.

The organisms in river ecosystems are specially adapted to living in flowing water. Many species of fish, such as salmon and trout, are adapted to swimming against the current, while aquatic insects and other invertebrates have specialized body structures that allow them to cling to rocks and other substrates to avoid being swept away.
