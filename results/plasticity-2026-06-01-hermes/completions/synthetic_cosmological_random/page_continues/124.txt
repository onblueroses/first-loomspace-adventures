 "River ecosystems are flowing waters that drain the landscape, and include the biotic (living) interactions amongst plants, animals and micro-organisms, as well as abiotic (nonliving) physical and chemical interactions of its many parts. River ecosystems are prime examples of lotic ecosystems. Including: the uni-directional flow of water; the main channel; tributaries that add to the volume; an abundance of sedimentation from the terrestrial ecosystem; and a rich floodplain that provides a variety of habitats. The typical geography of a river ecosystem is the river basin or watershed which is the area that includes the entire land drainage that feeds the river. The main channel will flow in a distinctive course that may have features of steep gradients, waterfalls and rapids, or the main channel may have areas of slow gradients where water flow is slower and the river is much broader. In addition, there are the side or tributary channels that have their own ecosystems. The amount of water flow in a river ecosystem depends on the overall size of the catchment area and the amount of precipitation that falls in that area.
