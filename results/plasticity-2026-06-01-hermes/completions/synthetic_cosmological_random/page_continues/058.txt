#### 4.6.1.3
The River Continuum Concept (RCC) is a model for classifying and describing flowing water, in addition to the classification of individual sections of waters after their hyporheic zone status. The RCC was developed in 1980 by Robin L. Vannote, with colleagues at Stroud Water Research Center, primarily Wayne Minshall, Kenneth Cummins, James Sedell, and Colbert Cushing. The RCC proposes a progressive shift from simple, inefficient, and redundant food webs dominated by shredders and collectors near headwaters to more complex, efficient, and diverse communities of collectors and predators in mid-order streams, and finally dominated by grazers and collectors in larger rivers. The concept was developed to explain longitudinal changes in species composition and ecosystem function from headwater to larger streams.
