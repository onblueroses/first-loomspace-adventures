 

The characteristic features of a river ecosystem:

1. The flow of water is unidirectional, creating a gradient of physical conditions from the source to the mouth.
2. The physical environment is continuously changing, with high flows during rainy seasons and low flows during dry seasons, creating a dynamic habitat template for ecological processes.
3. The nutrient spiraling concept describes the combined processes of nutrient cycling and downstream transport in the river.
4. River ecosystems are characterized by the longitudinal succession of species and ecosystem processes from the headwaters to the river mouth.
5. Rivers have high connectivity along their length, allowing organisms to move between habitats and providing a pathway for nutrient and energy transport.
6. The river continuum concept describes the changes in physical conditions, ecosystem processes, and biological communities along the river's length.
7. The flood pulse concept describes the lateral exchange of water, nutrients, and organisms between the river channel and the floodplain during high flows.
8. The riverine productivity model describes the sources and processing of organic matter in river ecosystems, including allochthonous inputs from the riparian zone and autochthonous production within the river.
