 "The largest river in North America, the Mississippi River, is one of the most biologically diverse ecosystems in the world, home to over 400 species of fish, and at least 15 species of freshwater mussels."

This statement is mostly accurate, but not entirely correct. The Mississippi River is indeed the largest river in North America by discharge, and it is a highly diverse ecosystem. However, the exact number of fish species is difficult to pinpoint due to taxonomic uncertainties and the inclusion of extinct, invasive, and estuarine species in some counts. A more commonly cited figure is around 260 fish species, with some sources estimating up to 375 species when including the entire Mississippi River Basin. The claim of "over 400 species" may be an overestimate or may include species found throughout the extensive Mississippi River system, including its tributaries.

Similarly, the number of freshwater mussel species is significant but varies depending on the source and the specific stretch of the river being considered. The Mississippi River Basin is home to a diverse array of freshwater mussels, with some sources indicating around 60 species, though the figure of "at least 15 species" mentioned is a conservative estimate, likely referring to a specific segment of the river rather than the entire basin.
