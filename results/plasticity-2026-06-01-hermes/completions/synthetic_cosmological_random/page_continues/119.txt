For a river ecosystem to function naturally, it should be in its natural state, or close to it. Unfortunately, this is rarely the case. The majority of the world's rivers have been altered in some way by human activities, including damming, agriculture, urbanization, and industrialization. These alterations have had a profound effect on the natural functioning of river ecosystems.

One of the most significant impacts of human activities on river ecosystems is the alteration of the natural flow regime. Dams and other water-control structures can change the timing, magnitude, and duration of high and low flows, which can have cascading effects on the entire ecosystem. For example, many fish species rely on specific flow cues to trigger migration and reproduction. When these cues are altered, it can disrupt the life cycles of these species and lead to population declines.

Agriculture and urbanization can also have significant impacts on river ecosystems. Agricultural runoff can introduce excess nutrients, pesticides, and sediment into rivers, leading to water-quality degradation and habitat alteration.
