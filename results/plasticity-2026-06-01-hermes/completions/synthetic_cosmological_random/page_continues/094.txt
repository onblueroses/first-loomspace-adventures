 "River ecosystems can be divided into the following categories:

* Headwater streams: These are the smallest and coldest of the river ecosystems, and they are typically found in mountainous regions. The water is usually clear and fast-moving, and the substrate is composed of rocks and gravel. The primary producers in these ecosystems are algae and mosses, and the primary consumers are insects and small fish.
* Middle reaches: These are the mid-sized rivers that are found in the foothills and plains. The water is usually warmer and slower-moving than in headwater streams, and the substrate is composed of sand and silt. The primary producers in these ecosystems are algae and aquatic plants, and the primary consumers are insects, fish, and crustaceans.
* Lower reaches: These are the largest and warmest of the river ecosystems, and they are typically found in the lowlands. The water is usually slow-moving and turbid, and the substrate is composed of silt and clay. The primary producers in these ecosystems are algae and aquatic plants, and the primary consumers are fish and crustaceans.
