The overall health of Puget Sound is closely tied to the health of the rivers and streams that flow into it. Most of these waterways support the region's five species of Pacific salmon, which are a keystone species that support a wide range of other animals, from orcas to bears to eagles.

## Key Terms
- Lotic: Flowing water, as in a river or stream.
- Lentic: Still water, as in a lake or pond.
- Catchment area: The area of land that drains into a particular water body, also known as a watershed or drainage basin.
- Riparian zone: The area of land adjacent to a river or stream that is influenced by the waterway. This zone often has unique vegetation and plays an important role in the health of the river ecosystem.

## Rivers and Streams in the Puget Sound Watershed
The Puget Sound watershed covers over 12,000 square miles and includes thousands of rivers and streams. Major rivers that flow into Puget Sound include:

- Skagit River
- Stillaguamish River
- Snohomish River
- Green/Duwamish River
- Puyallup River
