## 20.1.1. Rivers and Streams

Rivers and streams are created by precipitation and melting ice that flows from high areas downhill to lower areas. Small streams (called tributaries) flow together to create bigger streams, which join to form rivers. The largest rivers in the world are the Nile in Africa, the Amazon in South America, and the Mississippi in North America.

Rivers and streams usually have a fast current, which carries lots of organic and inorganic material downstream. The water in rivers is often muddy due to the sediment it carries. When there is a lot of precipitation, rivers can overflow their banks and cause floods.

The food web in rivers and streams depends on the flow of organic and inorganic material from the land. This material includes leaves and other plant parts, animal waste, and dissolved nutrients. The leaves and other plant parts are food for aquatic invertebrates, which are then eaten by fish. The animal waste and dissolved nutrients are used by aquatic plants and algae, which are also eaten by fish. The fish are then eaten by larger animals, such as otters, bears, and humans.
