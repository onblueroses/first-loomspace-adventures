 

## Ecosystem characteristics

River ecosystems are characterized by their physical, chemical, and biological properties. Physical properties include water flow, sediment transport, channel morphology, and temperature. Chemical properties include dissolved oxygen concentration, nutrient levels, and pollutant concentrations. Biological properties include the abundance and diversity of aquatic plants, invertebrates, and fish.

### Physical properties

The physical properties of a river ecosystem are shaped by the interaction of water flow, sediment transport, and channel morphology. Water flow is a function of the river's discharge, which is the volume of water that passes a given point in a given time period. Sediment transport is the movement of solid particles, such as sand, silt, and clay, by the water. Channel morphology is the shape and structure of the river channel, including its width, depth, and slope.

The temperature of a river ecosystem is influenced by the climate of the region, the amount of shade provided by riparian vegetation, and the amount of groundwater input. In general, headwater streams are cooler than downstream reaches, and shaded streams are cooler than those exposed to direct sunlight.
