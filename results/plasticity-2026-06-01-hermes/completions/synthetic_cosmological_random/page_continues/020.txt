 “River ecosystems are influenced by the amount of flow, the timing of the flow, and the temporal variability in flow. The amount of flow determines the size of the channels and the habitats available for organisms. The timing of flow is important in maintaining the life cycles of organisms, such as the cues for fish migration and spawning. The temporal variability in flow affects the structure and function of the river ecosystem by determining the water velocity, water depth, substrate type, and habitat availability. High-velocity flows can remove sediment from the riverbed, creating a gravel or cobble substrate, while low-velocity flows can deposit sediment, creating a sand or silt substrate. The substrate type influences the types of organisms that can inhabit the riverbed, with gravel and cobble substrates supporting more diverse and abundant macroinvertebrate communities than sand or silt substrates. The water depth and velocity also determine the types of fish that can inhabit the river, with faster and deeper waters supporting larger and more mobile fish species. The habitat availability, such as the presence of pools, riffles, and runs, also influences the diversity and abundance of fish and macroinvertebrates in the river ecosystem.
