The passage explains that a river ecosystem is a flowing-water ecosystem, where the biological community is shaped by the unidirectional movement of water. Rivers and streams are lotic ecosystems, which are different from lentic ecosystems like lakes and ponds. The character of a river ecosystem depends on its catchment area, climate, geology, and channel gradient.

In headwater streams, the water is usually cold, oxygen-rich, and fast-flowing over bedrock and coarse gravel. The biological community is dominated by aquatic insects and cold-water fish like trout. Organic matter enters the system from outside, such as leaf litter and woody debris from the bordering forest.

As the river widens and slows down in its middle and lower reaches, the channel substrate changes from gravel to sand to silt, the water warms up, and primary production by suspended algae and rooted aquatic plants increases.
