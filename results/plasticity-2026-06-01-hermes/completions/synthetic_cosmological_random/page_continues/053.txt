 "The concept of a river ecosystem encompasses the entirety of the river channel, from the headwaters to the mouth, including the floodplain and adjacent riparian vegetation. River ecosystems are characterized by the unidirectional flow of water, which shapes the biological community through processes such as erosion, sediment transport, and nutrient cycling. The structure and function of river ecosystems are influenced by factors such as climate, geology, and human activities within the catchment area.

Headwater streams, which are often cold, fast-flowing, and rich in oxygen, support a diverse array of aquatic insects and cold-water fish species. These streams are primarily fueled by allochthonous organic matter, such as leaf litter and woody debris, which is broken down by shredder invertebrates and forms the basis of the food web.

As the river progresses downstream, the channel widens and the flow slows, leading to changes in the substrate composition, water temperature, and primary production. In these middle and lower reaches, suspended algae and rooted aquatic plants become more prevalent, supporting a different community of fish species, including herbivores and omnivores. Floodplains, when present, provide important habitat and nutrient exchange during high flows, expanding the ecosystem laterally.
