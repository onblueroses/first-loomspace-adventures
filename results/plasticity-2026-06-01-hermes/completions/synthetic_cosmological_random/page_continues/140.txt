### Watershed and Wetlands

A watershed, also called a drainage basin or catchment, is an area drained by a river and its tributaries. A watershed is delineated by defining its boundary, which is the highest elevation surrounding the drainage basin where water flows downhill into the basin. Watersheds can be large, like the Mississippi River basin, or small, like the drainage basin for a small pond or wetland.

Wetlands are areas where the presence of water determines or influences most aspects of the ecosystem, including soils, vegetation, and animal life. Wetlands encompass a range of environments, from marshes and swamps to bogs, wet meadows, and riparian areas. Wetlands are important for many reasons, including flood and erosion control, water purification, and providing habitat for many plant and animal species.

### Riparian Zone

A riparian zone is the interface between land and a river or stream. Riparian zones are important because they provide a variety of ecological functions, including filtering pollutants from runoff, stabilizing stream banks, providing habitat for a variety of plant and animal species, and influencing the aquatic ecosystem by providing shade, organic matter, and nutrients
