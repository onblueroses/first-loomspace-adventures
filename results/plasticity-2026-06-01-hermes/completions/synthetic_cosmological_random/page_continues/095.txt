### Rivers and the Water Cycle

Rivers are a part of the hydrologic cycle. Water within a river is generally collected from precipitation through surface runoff, groundwater recharge, springs, and the release of stored water in natural ice and snowpacks (i.e., from glaciers).

The source of the water in a river is generally either from:

- Precipitation through surface runoff, groundwater recharge, springs, and the release of stored water in natural ice and snowpacks (i.e., from glaciers).
- Surface runoff: The water that reaches the channel through run-off can come from the surrounding landscape and land-use (such as rainwater from streets and sidewalks, and irrigation water from agricultural fields).
- Groundwater: This is water that is stored beneath the Earth's surface, and the water often seeps into rivers through the river bed and banks.
- Lakes: Many rivers originate from lakes, and many rivers also form natural lakes.
- Spring water: Groundwater can also come to the surface through a spring, where water naturally flows out of the ground.
