 River ecosystems are prime examples of lotic ecosystems. Lotic refers to flowing water, from the Latin lotus, meaning washed. Lotic waters range from springs only a few centimeters wide to major rivers kilometers in width. Much of this article applies to lotic ecosystems in general, including related lotic systems such as streams and springs. Lotic ecosystems have unidirectional flow, of generally non-turbulent velocities; a specific temperature regimen, substrate composition, and sediment transport; and are distinguished from lentic ecosystems by these and other abiotic factors. In addition, river systems typically drain a sandy, clayey, or rocky upland (or hinterland) and flow toward a lake, sea, or ocean. The biology of river ecosystems is influenced by the water-flow regime, sediment transport, temperature regime, and the river's chemistry. The health of a river ecosystem can be damaged by a range of human activities, including pollution, channelization, and the construction of dams.
