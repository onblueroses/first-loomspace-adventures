 "The physical characteristics of a river ecosystem include the channel bed and substrate, the water flow regime, and the catchment or watershed. The biological community is shaped by these physical factors, with different species adapted to different flow conditions, substrate types, and water qualities. The energy flow through the ecosystem is driven by the primary producers, which convert sunlight into organic matter through photosynthesis. The primary consumers, such as aquatic insects and fish, feed on the primary producers or on organic matter that enters the river from the surrounding terrestrial ecosystem. The secondary consumers, such as larger fish and birds, feed on the primary consumers. The decomposers, such as bacteria and fungi, break down the dead organic matter and recycle the nutrients back into the ecosystem. The river ecosystem is also influenced by the surrounding terrestrial ecosystem, with inputs of organic matter, nutrients, and sediments from the catchment. The river ecosystem, in turn, can influence the terrestrial ecosystem through the export of nutrients, organic matter, and water to the floodplain and downstream ecosystems."
