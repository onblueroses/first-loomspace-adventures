## River Ecosystem Food Web

Rivers are flowing bodies of water that can vary in size from small streams to large waterways. They provide habitat to a wide range of organisms, from small invertebrates to large mammals. In river ecosystems, the food web is often based on the primary production of algae and other aquatic plants. These primary producers are eaten by herbivorous invertebrates, which are in turn consumed by larger predators, such as fish. The food web in a river ecosystem can be complex, with many different species interacting with each other.

The base of the food web in a river ecosystem is typically made up of algae and other aquatic plants. These primary producers use sunlight to convert carbon dioxide and water into organic matter through the process of photosynthesis. The organic matter produced by these plants is then consumed by herbivorous invertebrates, such as mayflies, caddisflies, and stoneflies. These invertebrates are an important food source for many fish species, including trout and salmon.
