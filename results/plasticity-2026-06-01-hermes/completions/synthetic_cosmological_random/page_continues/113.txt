## 3.2 - River Continuum Concept

The River Continuum Concept (RCC) is a model for classifying and describing flowing water, in addition to the classification of individual segments of a river (e.g., riffles, pools). The RCC proposes that the biological communities of a river system are adapted to the changing physical environment of a river as it flows from headwaters to mouth. The RCC categorizes a river system into three distinct sections: headwaters, mid-reaches, and lower reaches. Each section has unique physical attributes that influence the life adapted to that section.

## 3.3 - Floods

A flood is an overflow of water that submerges land which is usually dry. In the sense of "flowing water", the word may also be applied to the inflow of the tide. Floods are an area of study of the discipline hydrology and are of significant concern in agriculture, civil engineering and public health.

Flooding may occur as an overflow of water from water bodies, such as a river, lake, or ocean, in which the water overtops or breaks
