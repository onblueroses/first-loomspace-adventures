 

River ecosystems are dynamic, with strong interactions between physical and biological processes. They have been described as one of the most complex ecosystems on the planet. The science of river ecosystems is limnology, which includes the study of freshwater ecosystems, including rivers, streams, lakes, and ponds. There are two main categories of river ecosystems: free-flowing and impounded. Free-flowing river ecosystems are the most diverse and complex, characterized by their natural flow regimes, which are driven by precipitation, snowmelt, and groundwater inputs. Impounded river ecosystems are those that have been altered by human activities, such as the construction of dams and reservoirs, which disrupt the natural flow regime and alter the physical, chemical, and biological characteristics of the river.

River ecosystems provide a wide range of ecosystem services, including water supply, water purification, flood control, habitat for fish and wildlife, and recreation. However, they are also among the most threatened ecosystems on the planet, due to a range of human activities, including pollution, habitat destruction, overfishing, and climate change. Effective management of river ecosystems requires a holistic approach that considers the entire watershed, including the upstream and downstream impacts of human activities, as well as the interactions between the river and its surrounding landscape.
