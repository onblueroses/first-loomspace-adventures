 

River ecosystems are flowing waters that drain the landscape, and include the biotic (living) interactions amongst plants, animals and micro-organisms, as well as abiotic (nonliving) physical and chemical interactions of its many parts.[1] River ecosystems are prime examples of lotic ecosystems. These ecosystems are a prime example of how living and non-living components in an ecosystem all interact with one another in order to maintain some sort of stability and balance. They are in a state of continuous physical change which occurs from the waterflow constantly changing the stream and river beds, and this creates a greater range of habitats for organisms to adapt to.[2] The ecology of rivers is more complex than other aquatic systems, as there are multiple dimensions to consider. They provide a continuous input of energy and nutrients from upstream to downstream. The water in rivers is also in constant motion, which can create unique environmental conditions. Due to the rivers' constant flow, they provide a continuous input of energy and nutrients from upstream to downstream. This creates a greater range of habitats for organisms to adapt to, which in turn leads to a higher level of biodiversity.
