 

River ecosystems are prime examples of lotic ecosystems. Lotic refers to flowing water, from the Latin lotus, meaning washed. Lotic waters range from springs only a few centimeters wide to major rivers kilometers in width. Much of this article applies to lotic ecosystems in general, including related lotic systems such as streams and springs. Lotic ecosystems have many different types of organisms such as bacteria, algae, macrophytes, snails, clams, insects, fish, and mammals. 

Rivers have some very specific characteristics that are determined by the physical nature of the water channel, the source of the water, the underlying geology, and the climate of the region. These characteristics include:

1. Flow: The unidirectional movement of water. The speed of flow in a river ecosystem varies across its width and depth, and changes over time in response to weather, geology, and human activity.
2. Temperature: The temperature of river water varies seasonally and geographically. It is typically cooler in the headwaters than in the lower reaches, because of shading from riparian vegetation and the relatively cooler groundwater that often feeds the headwaters.
