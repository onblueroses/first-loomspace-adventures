The primary consumers in a river ecosystem are primarily herbivores such as small fish, crustaceans, mollusks, and insects. They feed on the primary producers, which are mostly algae, phytoplankton, and aquatic plants. The secondary consumers are the carnivorous fish and insects that feed on the primary consumers. The tertiary consumers are the larger fish and birds that feed on the secondary consumers. Finally, the decomposers, such as bacteria and fungi, break down the organic matter from dead organisms and waste products, releasing nutrients back into the ecosystem for the primary producers to use.

The food chain in a river ecosystem is a complex network of interconnected feeding relationships. The primary producers, such as algae and aquatic plants, form the base of the food chain. They are consumed by the primary consumers, which are then eaten by the secondary consumers, and so on. The energy flow in the ecosystem is unidirectional, with energy being transferred from one trophic level to the next, with some energy being lost as heat at each transfer.
