## 3. How do rivers form?

A river forms from water moving from a higher elevation to a lower elevation, all due to gravity. When rain falls on the land, it either seeps into the ground or becomes runoff, which flows downhill into rivers and lakes, on its journey towards the seas. As water flows over the ground and along rivers, it picks up sediment (loose particles of sand, silt, and clay), and transport them along. Over time, rivers erode the land and carve out valleys and gorges, widening and flattening the land as they flow.

Rivers also form when glaciers melt high up in the mountains, or when a natural spring pushes water up to the surface of the earth. As the water flows down, it finds its way into streams and eventually into rivers.
