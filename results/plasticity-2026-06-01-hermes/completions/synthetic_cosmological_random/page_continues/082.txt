 River ecosystems in drylands are among the most vulnerable to human impact, as the limited water supply is often overallocated to agriculture, industry, or municipal uses. The Colorado River in the western United States is emblematic of this condition, with its natural flow regime severely altered by numerous dams and diversions, leading to the decline of native fish species adapted to its natural variability, such as the Colorado pikeminnow. Invasive species, introduced intentionally or accidentally, can also disrupt native ecosystems by outcompeting or preying upon native species. The zebra mussel, for example, has caused significant ecological and economic damage in North American rivers where it has invaded.

Despite these challenges, many river ecosystems continue to provide essential ecosystem services, including water supply, nutrient cycling, sediment transport, and recreational opportunities. Maintaining the health of these ecosystems requires integrated management approaches that consider the entire watershed, balancing human needs with the long-term sustainability of the river and its biotic community.
