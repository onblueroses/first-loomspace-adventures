River ecosystems are a highly complex and diverse environment that supports a wide range of species and ecological processes. The health and functioning of river ecosystems are influenced by a variety of factors, including water flow, sediment load, temperature, dissolved oxygen levels, and the integrity of riparian vegetation along the riverbank. Human activities, such as dam construction, channelization, agricultural runoff, and land-use changes in the catchment area, can significantly alter the composition of the biological community and the functioning of the ecosystem.

One of the key features of river ecosystems is the continuous downstream transport of water, dissolved minerals, particulate organic matter, and living organisms. This unidirectional flow of water shapes the biological community and creates a unique environment that is distinct from other aquatic ecosystems, such as lakes and ponds. The character of a river ecosystem is largely determined by its catchment area, the climate of the region, the underlying geology, and the gradient of the river channel from its source to its mouth.
