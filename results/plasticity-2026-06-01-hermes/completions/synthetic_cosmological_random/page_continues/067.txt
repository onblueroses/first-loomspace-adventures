### River ecosystems

Rivers are flowing bodies of water, the source of which may be a lake, a spring or a collection of small streams that have joined together. Rivers flow from higher to lower elevations, ending at an ocean or a sea. The area that is drained by a river and all its tributaries is called the river's watershed or drainage basin. All the land that is drained by a river and its tributaries is called the river's watershed or drainage basin. A river ecosystem includes the river itself and the floodplain, the land that surrounds the river and is periodically flooded by the river. The living and non-living components of the river ecosystem are interconnected and interact with each other, forming a complex food web. 

Rivers can vary in size, with the smallest rivers being only a few miles long and the largest being thousands of miles long. The world's longest river is the Nile River in Africa, which is over 4,000 miles long. The Amazon River in South America is the second longest river in the world and is responsible for draining the largest area of land.
