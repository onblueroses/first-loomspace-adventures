 "River ecosystems are prime examples of lotic ecosystems. Lotic refers to flowing water, from the Latin lotus, meaning washed. Lotic waters range from springs only a few centimeters wide to major rivers kilometers in width. Much of this article applies to lotic ecosystems in general, including related lotic systems such as streams and springs. Lotic ecosystems have two major zones, rapids (where the water is fast moving) and pools (where the water is still). The distinction between pools, and riffles and runs (areas of fast-moving water) is fundamental to the biodynamics of rivers, and is the basis of describing the longitudinal profile of a river in terms of slope. The distinction between pools and riffles is based on the difference in the rate of energy expenditure in the two habitats. When flowing water encounters an obstacle such as a boulder or large woody debris, the water moves over or around the object, resulting in the fast-moving water of a riffle. When the channel is wide and shallow, the water flows slowly and calmly, creating a pool.
