 The biological communities of river ecosystems vary from the headwater streams to the lower river. Headwaters are cool, shaded, and fast-moving, with cold-water species like trout and aquatic insects that cling to rocks against the current. Downstream, the river warms, slows, and becomes more turbid, with algae and aquatic plants providing the energy base for the food web. Larger fish, including herbivores and omnivores, occupy these reaches. The floodplain expands the ecosystem laterally, allowing seasonal exchange of nutrients between the channel and adjacent wetlands. The lower river interfaces with an estuary, where freshwater meets saltwater and a transitional brackish ecosystem develops. River-ecosystem health is affected by water-flow regime, sediment load, temperature regime, dissolved-oxygen concentration, and the integrity of riparian vegetation bordering the channel. Modifications to any of these parameters, through damming, channelization, agricultural runoff, or land-use change in the catchment, can substantially alter community composition and ecosystem function.
