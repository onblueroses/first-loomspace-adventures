 

Rivers are a major component of the water cycle. They collect runoff from precipitation, ground water recharge, springs, and snowmelt and carry it toward the oceans. The amount of water in a river is affected by factors such as climate, precipitation, vegetation, and soil type. Rivers are important connectors, linking terrestrial to aquatic, freshwater to marine, and surface to groundwater systems. They also provide travel routes for fish, especially during spawning migrations. Rivers provide habitats for many species of plants, fish, insects, amphibians, birds, and other animals. In addition, rivers are essential to human societies, providing water for drinking, irrigation, and power generation, as well as important transportation corridors.
