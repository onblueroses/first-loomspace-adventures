For a river ecosystem to function properly, it needs all its parts. It needs to flow freely, and it needs its floodplains. A river's floodplains provide the following benefits:

1. Floodwaters can spread out over the floodplain, reducing the flood's power to erode the river's channel. The floodplain stores the water, reducing downstream flooding.
2. Floodwaters can recharge groundwater, which can be used by humans for drinking and irrigation.
3. Floodwaters can deposit nutrient-rich sediments on the floodplain, making the floodplain a good place for agriculture.
4. Floodplains provide habitat for fish and wildlife.

When we restrict a river's flow, we harm the river's ecosystem. When we build on a river's floodplains, we reduce the river's ability to handle floods. When we channelize a river, we increase the river's power to erode its channel, leading to more flooding.

The solution to flooding is not to build more levees and dams, but to restore the river's natural flow and floodplains. This will allow the river to handle floods naturally, reducing the need for expensive and environmentally damaging flood control measures
