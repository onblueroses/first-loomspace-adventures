### 5.4.1 Deltas

A river delta is a landform created at the mouth of a river where the river flows into a body of water, such as a lake or ocean, and deposits sediment. Deltas form when the speed of the river slows as it enters the larger body of water, causing the sediment it carries to settle to the bottom. Over time, this process creates a distinctive triangular-shaped landform, resembling the Greek letter delta (Δ).

As sediment accumulates at the river mouth, the delta grows, forming a network of distributaries that branch out from the main river channel. These distributaries continue to deposit sediment, allowing the delta to expand further into the body of water. The shape and size of a delta depend on various factors, including the rate of sediment supply, the strength of currents and tides in the body of water, and the depth of the water.
