It's the first line of "The Sound of Silence," by Simon & Garfunkel. The song is about the inability of people to communicate emotionally. The "sound of silence" refers to the silence that results from this lack of communication. The song suggests that people are too afraid to speak out and share their feelings, leading to a world where silence reigns.

The song starts with the narrator talking about a vision he had in the night, where he saw a naked light bulb that represented a sign flashing out the words "The words of the prophets are written on the subway walls and tenement halls." This vision is a metaphor for the idea that the truth and wisdom of the "prophets" (or those who see the world for what it is) are not being heard or understood by the general population. Instead, their messages are relegated to the margins of society, written on subway walls and tenement halls.

The song also mentions "people talking without speaking" and "people hearing without listening," which further emphasizes the theme of miscommunication and lack of understanding.
