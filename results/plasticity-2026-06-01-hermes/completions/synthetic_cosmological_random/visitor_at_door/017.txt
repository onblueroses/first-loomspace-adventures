 "I am the river," he says. "Speak," says the ferryman, "I'm listening." "In me," says the river, "the finished and the unfinished meet and pass through each other. I carry what is not yet complete to what has already come to pass, and bring what has already come to pass to what is not yet complete. I am the largest and most populous of all possible cities. Only I am able to carry the past into the future, which is why I am and always will be the present." "So it is," says the ferryman. "You are a great city, you embrace past and future and you are the present. Speak," says the ferryman, "I'm listening." "In me," says the river, "the straight and the winding lines meet and pass through each other. I am the straightest possible line. Only I am able to flow in a straight line from the spring to the estuary. But I am also the most winding of all possible lines. For I do not flow from the spring to the estuary, but instead I flow from the spring to the spring, from the estuary to the estuary.
