 "I am a child of the river," he says, "and I am here to tell you my story. I am born from the union of water and earth, shaped by the forces of nature and the hands of time. I am a witness to the world, a carrier of secrets, and a giver of life. Listen to my words, and you will understand the essence of the river."

He begins his tale with the birth of the river, high in the mountains where the snow melts and the rain falls. The water trickles down the slopes, gathering in small streams and rivulets, dancing over rocks and pebbles. As it flows downhill, it joins with other streams, growing wider and deeper, until it becomes a mighty river, winding through valleys and plains.

The river is a home for countless creatures, from the tiny plankton that drift in the current to the giant fish that lurk in the depths. The visitor describes the intricate web of life that depends on the river, from the insects that lay their eggs in the water to the birds that nest on the banks. He tells of the otters that play in the rapids, the beavers that build dams in the shallows, and the bears that catch salmon in the falls.
