 He has come from the River. He tells the boy that the River is dying. The visitor is a fisherman, and the River is his livelihood. He has seen the water grow murky and the fish grow scarce. He has come to ask for help. The boy listens intently, his eyes wide with concern. He knows the River is the lifeblood of the valley, sustaining not only the fish, but the crops in the fields and the animals in the forest. If the River dies, the valley dies with it. The boy promises to help, and together they set out to find the source of the River's illness. They travel upstream, past the fields and the forests, into the mountains where the River begins. There, they find the source of the problem: a factory dumping waste into the River. The boy confronts the factory owner, pleading with him to stop polluting the River. The owner is unmoved, citing the cost of proper waste disposal. Undeterred, the boy returns home and begins to organize his community. He speaks to the farmers, the shopkeepers, the teachers, and the children. He tells them of the River's plight and the factory's role in it. The community rallies around the boy, boycotting the factory
