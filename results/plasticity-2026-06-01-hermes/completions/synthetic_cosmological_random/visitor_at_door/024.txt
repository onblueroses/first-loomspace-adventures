 "Hello, I am the river ecosystem. I am a flowing-water ecosystem where the biological community is shaped by the unidirectional movement of water. Unlike lakes and ponds, my water is continuously moving downstream, transporting dissolved minerals, organic matter, and living organisms with it.

My character is determined by my catchment area, the climate of my region, the underlying geology, and the gradient of my channel from my source to my mouth. In my headwater streams, the water runs cold, oxygen-rich, and fast over bedrock and coarse gravel. Here, aquatic insects dominate, their larvae clinging to substrates against the current, while cold-water fish like trout occupy the predator niche. Organic matter, mostly from leaf litter and woody debris falling from the bordering forest, forms the energetic base of my food web.

As I widen and slow in my middle and lower reaches, my channel substrate shifts from gravel to sand to silt, my water warms, and primary production by suspended algae and rooted aquatic plants increases. Larger fish, including herbivores and omnivores, occupy these reaches. My floodplains, when present, expand my ecosystem laterally during high flows, allowing seasonal exchange of nutrients between my channel and adjacent wetlands.
