 "I am not the river. I am the sea."

The sea is a vast expanse of saltwater that covers over 70% of the Earth's surface. It is a complex and interconnected ecosystem that supports an incredible diversity of life, from microscopic plankton to the largest animals on the planet, like the blue whale. The sea can be divided into different zones based on depth and distance from the shoreline, each with its own unique characteristics and communities of organisms.

In the intertidal zone, where the land meets the sea, organisms must be adapted to survive the constant fluctuations in temperature, salinity, and moisture as the tides rise and fall. Rocky shores are home to barnacles, mussels, and seaweeds that anchor themselves to the substrate, while sandy beaches support burrowing animals like clams and sand crabs.

The neritic zone extends from the low tide line to the edge of the continental shelf and includes estuaries, coral reefs, and kelp forests. These ecosystems are some of the most productive and diverse on the planet, supporting a wide range of species from seagrasses and mangroves to sharks and sea turtles.
