In the United States, the Census Bureau conducts a census every 10 years to count the population and gather basic demographic information. The census is required by the Constitution to apportion seats in the House of Representatives among the states based on population. The data is also used to distribute federal funds, plan infrastructure, and make business decisions.

The census aims to count everyone living in the U.S., regardless of citizenship status. The Census Bureau is required by law to keep individual responses confidential, and uses the data only to produce statistics. Refusing to answer the census or providing false information can result in fines.
