 “Hello,” he says, “I’m the one who’s going to be living here from now on.” The old man doesn’t answer. The visitor tries again. “I’m the one who’s going to be living here from now on. I just thought I should come by and introduce myself.” The old man doesn’t answer. The old man doesn’t answer because he can’t. He’s dead. He’s been dead for forty years. The visitor isn’t aware of this. He’s not even aware that the old man exists. He thinks he’s talking to himself. He thinks he’s alone in the house. He is, in a way. He is, in a way, alone in the house because the old man’s dead. The old man’s been dead for forty years. He’s nothing but a skeleton. A skeleton sitting in a chair with its back to the door. A skeleton that doesn’t move, doesn’t breathe, doesn’t see, doesn’t hear, doesn’t think. A skeleton that will never answer the visitor’s questions, no matter how many times he asks.
