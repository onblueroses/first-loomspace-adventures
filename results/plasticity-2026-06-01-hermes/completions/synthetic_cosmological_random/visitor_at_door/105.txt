“Thank you,” you say, reflecting on the whole situation: a neighbor who has been observing you without your knowledge, who now intends to leave, to disappear. “Thank you,” you repeat. “Thank you for informing me. I suppose I should have been more attentive. I suppose I should have noticed your presence, your comings and goings, your moving away.”

“No need for apologies,” says the neighbor. “The situation is what it is. I simply wanted to inform you before I go.”
