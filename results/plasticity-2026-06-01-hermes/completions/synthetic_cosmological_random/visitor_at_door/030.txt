The river ecosystem is shaped by the unidirectional movement of water, which carries with it dissolved minerals, particulate organic matter, and living organisms. The character of a river ecosystem depends on its catchment area, climate, geology, and channel gradient from source to mouth.

In headwater streams, the water is typically cold, oxygen-rich, and fast-flowing over bedrock and coarse gravel. Aquatic insects dominate the biological community, with their larvae clinging to substrates against the current. Cold-water fish such as trout occupy the predator niche. Organic matter enters these systems largely from outside, in the form of leaf litter and woody debris falling from the bordering forest, which forms the energetic base of the food web through shredder invertebrates.
