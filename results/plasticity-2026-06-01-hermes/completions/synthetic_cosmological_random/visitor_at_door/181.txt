 "I am not a salesman," he says. "I am not in need of money, either to cadge or to borrow. I am, in fact, the opposite of a salesman. I am a buyer. I am a buyer of old, broken-down, second-hand, and used-up articles."

"Then you won't have any use for me," I say, and I try to close the door.

But he has his foot in it, and, anyway, he says, he's not interested in me. He wants to see what I have in the way of old, broken-down, second-hand, and used-up articles. "Every home has them," he says. "You have them, I am sure."

I have them, I am sure. I have an attic and a cellar and rooms full of articles of every kind, bought in the days when I was flush, or thought I was. I have old clothes and old books, old chairs and tables, old curtains and carpets, old pictures and bric-a-brac.
