 The moon. Its pale light spills across the floor, not quite reaching the bed. The windows are tall and arched at the top, each one divided into many smaller panes. The white curtains move. They flutter out into the room and get sucked back toward the glass. The windows are open. A cool breeze streams in, stirring the hair on his forehead, bringing with it the smell of the sea. A sharp, briny smell. He can almost hear the waves down on the rocks, the soft hiss of water running up the shingle. A voice. There are voices. They’re calling him. He’s warm, he’s comfortable, he’s tired. He doesn’t want to wake. The voices call again. He tells them to go away, to leave him be. He doesn’t want to remember, doesn’t want to think. He wants to sleep, to dream. It’s dark and he’s lying on his back. He can feel something beneath him, something soft. He’s in a bed, his bed. He knows this room.
