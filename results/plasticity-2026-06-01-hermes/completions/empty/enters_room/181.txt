 At first the room is
empty. Gradually, a figure takes shape. A woman. She is not
young. This is the first thing you notice. She is not young. It
is possible that she used to be beautiful. Even now she is
not without beauty. But it is the beauty of temporality. It
is the beauty of a city blasted by time. There is still
grace, but it is the grace of a ruined aqueduct. There is
still nobility, but it is the nobility of a bombed-out
palace. The second thing you notice is that the woman is
transparent. You can see into her. You can see into her
body. You can see the decaying heart, the rotting liver, the
frayed nerves, the drying blood, the withering lungs. You
can see the cancer that is eating her up. You can see the
memories that are eating her up. You can see the loves that
are eating her up. What you cannot see is the soul. Either
it is too deep within for you to see, or it is not there at
