 It is not enough for the man to be good. He must be good for something. The man who has no other panacea for his worldly troubles than to make the whole world virtuous, is less wise than the man who rushes out of a burning theater. The man who wants to reform the world morally is a trifling individual. He might begin by burning all the calendars. Things are so bad that it would be well if fire could burn them up. But the sun itself cannot do it. It is out of the question to think of succeeding in such an undertaking. I have no doubt that you could succeed in making the world virtuous, if you could make the world so, but I am afraid that you can’t do the latter, and that it would not stay so, if you did. It is a significant fact that the most virtuous communities are not those which attract the most admiration from the outside world. No, the man who is good for something is the man who is wanted. He is not a superficial creature, but a man of solid worth. It is for him to do the work of life. It is for him to find the way to get through the world successfully.
