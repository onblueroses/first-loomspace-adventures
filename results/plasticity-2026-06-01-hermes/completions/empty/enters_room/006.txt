 In some ways, this is an exciting time to be a scientist. We have the tools to sequence the genomes of all the microbes in a sample of soil or seawater. We can sift through those sequences and identify what species are present and what they might be doing. We can reconstruct entire metabolic pathways from these data and infer how communities of microbes have evolved.

But it is also a time of great responsibility. We have a duty to use these tools wisely and to share our findings with the world. We must be careful not to overstate our results or to draw conclusions that are not supported by the data. And we must be mindful of the potential implications of our work, both good and bad.

As we move forward, let us remember the words of Carl Sagan: "Science is a way of thinking much more than it is a body of knowledge." Let us approach our work with humility and a willingness to learn from each other and from the natural world. And let us never forget the power of science to change the world for the better. Thank you.
