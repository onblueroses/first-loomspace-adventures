 [It is] the fact that we don’t know what we are doing.
The reason why we don’t know is that we have no way of knowing. The reason why we have no way of knowing is that the world is too complex for us to be able to establish a system of analysis and action that would allow us to know. The complexity of the world is now beyond our human capacities of understanding and description. What is at stake today is to be able to still think and act within this complexity which, on the one hand overcomes, saturates and short-circuits our cognitive capacities and, on the other hand, makes it more necessary than ever to attempt to understand and act within it, because it is the only world that we have, and it’s the only place where we can exercise our liberties and responsibilities. (Latour, 2011: n.p.)

Latour’s perspective is a useful starting point, as it highlights the difficulty of knowing what is happening in a complex world, and the importance of nonetheless attempting to understand and act within it. For Latour, the key is to recognize that we are part of the world we are trying to understand and that our attempts to understand and act are always partial and provisional.
