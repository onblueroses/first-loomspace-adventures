 I will not say who, for that would be to betray my secret; but when this person entered, I felt my hands involuntarily clench and my heart give a great bound as if it would leap from my breast to greet this beloved being. Yet I made no sign and let the first word come from lips not my own. And it did.

"Miss Butterworth, how is my boy?"

It was the master of the house who spoke, the father of the youth I loved--if so cold a word as that can be used to express the feelings of my deeply passionate nature.

I did not answer,--I could not,--but held out my hand in an inquiring gesture. Why does he ask me how his boy is? Did he not know? No, he did not, and in a few hurried words I made him understand the situation. The boy was gone, lost, and I had come to him for aid,--for encouragement, if nothing else. Alas! he had little to give me from his own resources, but he had friends, a brother, and he would rouse the city and the whole country if necessary to find the child of his heart, the pride of his eyes, the heir of his name.
