 "The only way to determine the absolute age of a rock is to measure the amount of particular radioactive isotopes that the rock contains. Radioactive isotopes decay at known rates and can be used like clocks to measure the age of a rock. There are several different radioactive isotopes that can be used to measure the age of a rock. The most common ones are uranium-238, potassium-40, and rubidium-87. The method of using radioactive isotopes to determine the age of a rock is called radiometric dating." - This is a very popular misconception. The age of a rock can be determined by various radiometric methods only if the rock has not been heated too much over its history. Geologists choose what methods to use depending on what they believe the history of the rock is, and the best geologists can be very creative in imagining hidden histories of the rocks to account for confusing and unexpected radiometric results. In addition, even if the rock has not been heated too much over its history, the results from different methods can give widely varying results. It is up to the geologist to be creative in imagining ways that the daughter products escaped from the rock in different ways in the past.
