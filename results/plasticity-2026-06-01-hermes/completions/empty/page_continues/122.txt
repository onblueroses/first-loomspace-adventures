 "In the final chapter, a few long-term, global risks are discussed that are not covered in the rest of the report. The focus is on risks with low or extremely uncertain probabilities, but potentially disastrous consequences. These risks include the collapse of the Atlantic thermohaline circulation, rapid release of methane from hydrates in the seabed, and a rapid disintegration of the Greenland or West Antarctic ice sheets."

The page concludes: "This report has discussed a wide range of possible impacts of climate change and examined the potential severity of future climate impacts across different levels of warming and for vulnerable regions and populations. It found that these impacts can be reduced by adaptation and mitigation, although not eliminated entirely. The report also shows that, as the impacts of climate change are expected to become more widespread and severe with increasing temperatures, the costs of adaptation will also increase, particularly beyond 1.5°C of warming. The report also highlights the importance of considering low-probability, high-impact events, which could have potentially disastrous consequences."
