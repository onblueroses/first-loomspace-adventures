### fourier jr

the definition of continuity is $$\lim_{x \rightarrow a} f(x) = f(a)$$ which means that the limit exists & is equal to the function value at x=a. if a function is differentiable at x=a then it must be continuous at x=a but not vice-versa. a good example is f(x) = |x|. it is continuous at x=0 but not differentiable there. the graph is a v-shape, with a sharp corner at the origin. there is no unique tangent line there.
