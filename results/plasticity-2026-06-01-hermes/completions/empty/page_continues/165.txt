A local authority may have been negligent in failing to carry out routine maintenance and repairs, but that does not mean it is liable to pay compensation for an accident.

The council’s duty is to take reasonable care to ensure that visitors to the premises are reasonably safe.

The test is whether the council has done what is reasonable in all the circumstances. The courts recognise that public bodies have limited resources and have to prioritise.

The courts will look at the council’s system of inspection and maintenance. If the council has a reasonable system, it will usually not be liable even if an accident happens because the system was not perfect.

A council should not wait until it receives reports of problems before taking action. It should have a system of regular inspection and maintenance. But if an inspection does not reveal a problem and an accident later happens, the council will not usually be liable.

The council will be liable if it created the danger, for example by carrying out works which left the premises in a dangerous state.
