 "The proof of this is that if we consider the number 3, then we see that the two numbers that are both less than 3 and are also prime are 2 and 3. Similarly, for the number 7, the four numbers that are both less than 7 and are also prime are 2, 3, 5, and 7."  This is clearly wrong.  There are only two primes less than 3 (not two primes less than *or equal to* 3), and there are only three primes less than 7 (not four primes less than *or equal to* 7).  The sequence is clearly the sequence of prime numbers, not the sequence of prime numbers plus one.  (If you want to get technical, since the sequence is the sequence of prime numbers, it should start with 2, not 1.  But since the question is about the number of primes less than $n$, not the number of primes less than *or equal to* $n$, the fact that the sequence given starts with 1 instead of 2 is irrelevant.)
