 "The division of the circle into 360 degrees, like most mathematical concepts, is based on the Babylonian sexagesimal system. The Babylonians were keen astronomers, and the number 360 may have been chosen due to its ease of divisibility, or due to the fact that a year consists of approximately 360 days."

So, which is it? Did the Babylonians use 360 degrees in a circle because it was easily divisible, or because a year has about 360 days? Does anyone have a definitive answer?
