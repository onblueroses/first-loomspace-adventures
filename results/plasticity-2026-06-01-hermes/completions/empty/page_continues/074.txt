 "At the start of the 20th century, the term "New Thought" was adopted by a group of metaphysical denominations that shared certain beliefs and philosophies. The origins of New Thought can be traced to the late 19th century, when a loose collection of philosophical and religious leaders, authors, and healers began to share ideas about the mind's power to heal and manifest change in the world. The three central ideas of New Thought are: Infinite Intelligence or God is omnipresent; the spirit is the ultimate reality, and the material world is secondary; and humans are divine or have the potential to be divine. New Thought leaders have roots in Christianity, but also draw from Eastern and other philosophical traditions. New Thought is a diverse movement, with no centralized leadership or doctrine. There are many New Thought denominations, including Unity, Religious Science, and Divine Science."

## History

### Origins

The New Thought movement emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, with roots in Christian Science and the metaphysical healing movements of the time. The term "New Thought" was first used by Emma Curtis Hopkins, who is often considered the "teacher of teachers" of the movement. Hopkins was a student of Christian Science founder Mary Baker Eddy
