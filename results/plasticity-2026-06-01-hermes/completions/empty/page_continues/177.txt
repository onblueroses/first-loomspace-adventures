 "The 4th Earl's claim was supported by the 3rd Earl's sisters and their families. The 3rd Earl's younger brother, John Wemyss, whose legitimacy was also questioned, was opposed to the claim and obtained a decree of legitimacy in Lyon Court on 25 July 1820, which was challenged by the 4th Earl. This challenge failed in the House of Lords on 18 June 1822. However, John Wemyss did not pursue his claim to the earldom. He was satisified to see the 4th Earl's claim to the earldom fail even if that meant he too would not be earl. On his death in 1846, his son, Francis Wemyss, renewed the claim to the earldom and achieved status of a law of entail before his death in 1859. His son, Francis Wemyss, resided in No. 8 Queen Street which was built for him in 1863. The claim to the title was finally recognised in 1872 after his son, Francis Charteris, carried forward the claim."
