 "The function $$f$$ is increasing on the interval $$(-\infty,1]$$ and the interval $$[3,\infty)$$, and $$f$$ is decreasing on the interval $$[1,3]$$. By the First Derivative Test, it follows that $$f$$ has a local maximum at $$x=1$$ and a local minimum at $$x=3$$."

I agree with the local maximum at $$x=1$$ and local minimum at $$x=3$$. But I don't agree with the intervals.

I would say:

• $$f$$ is increasing on the interval $$(-\infty,1)$$ and the interval $$(3,\infty)$$.
• $$f$$ is decreasing on the interval $$(1,3)$$.
• The points $$x=1$$ and $$x=3$$ are not included in the above intervals.

Who is correct, and why?

• You are correct. The critical points ($x=1$ and $x=3$) should not be included in the intervals over which the function is increasing or decreasing. The critical points are, however, included in the intervals over which the function is non-decreasing or non-increasing. – kcc
