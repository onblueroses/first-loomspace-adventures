 “The term ‘metaphysical’ is not used here primarily in the scholastic sense, which regards as metaphysical anything having to do with ontology, but is used rather in the sense of the post-Kantian and modern critique of knowledge, to characterize the knowledge of an object that is no longer conditioned by a sensible intuition.”[22] If this is the case, then a “metaphysical” approach to cognition would seem to be one that no longer relies on empirical data, and this is a conclusion with which I agree. However, this is not what Kant meant by “metaphysics.” In fact, it is the exact opposite of what Kant meant by the term. For Kant, metaphysics is the study of objects that are only thinkable by pure reason, and have no empirical correlate. This means that Kant’s metaphysics is the exact opposite of the metaphysical approach to cognition that is being described in the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. In Kant’s metaphysics, the objects of study are precisely those that cannot be known through empirical means.
