 "On July 7, 2009, the U.S. and Russia signed a Joint Understanding to work out a new nuclear arms reduction treaty to replace START I, which expires on December 5, 2009. Both sides agreed to reduce their strategic warheads to a range of 1,500-1,675, and their strategic delivery vehicles to a range of 500-1,100. A new treaty is to be worked out by the end of the year. The Joint Understanding also pledged to pursue further reductions in all categories of nuclear weapons--strategic, non-strategic and non-deployed--in a future round of talks. On September 18, 2009, the U.S. government indicated that it would agree to a Russian proposal to count each bomber as one warhead, even though U.S. bombers can carry many more. The new treaty is expected to last for 10 years, with an optional 5-year extension."
