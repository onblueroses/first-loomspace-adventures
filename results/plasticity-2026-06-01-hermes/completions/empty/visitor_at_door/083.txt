 "There is no need to be alarmed," he says. "My name is Doctor Delano. I am a physician and I have been asked to look in on you."

"By whom?"

"By your friend, Mr. Greig."

"Then Greig is not dead?"

"No. He is very much alive. He is outside in my car."

"Then I will see him."

The doctor hesitates. "I think it would be better for you to see him later. You have been very ill, you know."

"Ill?"

"Yes. For several days you have been suffering from a severe attack of nervous prostration. You have been out of your head. But you are better now. If you will remain quiet for a day or two longer I think you will be quite yourself again."

"And Greig--you say he is outside?"

"Yes. He has been with you constantly. He has not left you night or day. He is quite worn out and I have ordered him to go to his hotel and get some rest. He consented to go only when I promised to remain with you until his return. He will be back in a few hours."
