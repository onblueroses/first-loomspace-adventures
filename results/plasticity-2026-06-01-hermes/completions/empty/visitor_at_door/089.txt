 "I'm a lawyer, and I'm here to inform you that you're a beneficiary in someone's will." 

A moment of silence, then: "Who?" 

"Your grandmother. She passed away last month and left you something in her will." 

I blink. "My grandmother?" I haven't seen her in years, not since I was a little girl. She lived far away, and we weren't close. "What did she leave me?" 

"A key," the lawyer says. "And a letter." 

I take them, confused. The key is old-fashioned, ornate. The letter is sealed. I open it with trembling hands. 

"Dearest Granddaughter," it begins. "If you're reading this, I'm gone. I've left you the key to my house. It's yours now. But be careful. The house holds secrets. Dark ones. I hope you can handle them." 

I look up at the lawyer, but he's already walking away. I'm left alone with a key, a letter, and a growing sense of unease.
