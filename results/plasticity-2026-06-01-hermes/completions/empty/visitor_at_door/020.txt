 "I am a friend of your father's. He's
been hurt and wants you. Can you come with me right away?" The child
doesn't know what to do. The visitor seems friendly, but the parents
have said never to go with a stranger. Children have been abducted by
people using this kind of trick.

The child hesitates. The visitor insists. "Your father needs you
now. There's no time to waste." The child's uncertainty grows. The
voice is starting to sound harsh. Suddenly, the child remembers what
to do. "I have to call the baby-sitter first," the child says, trying
to stay calm. "She's upstairs sleeping. I'll go get her." The child
starts to back away. The visitor seems very edgy. The child turns and
runs into the house, locking the door.

This child knew what to do in a scary situation with a stranger
because of a simple but effective program called "Stranger Danger,
Safe Kids." This program teaches children how to respond to
potentially dangerous situations involving strangers. It is taught in
many elementary schools by police officers. The program is also
available to community groups.
