 The tone is calm and even, but the words are harsh.

"Your father is dying. You need to come home, now."

Your mother looks to you. "Go get your things. We need to leave right away."

You hesitate, looking from your mother to the visitor, then back again. You don't want to leave your cozy room, your toys, your friends. But the urgency in their voices tells you this is serious. You grab your favorite stuffed animal, a well-worn teddy bear, and slowly make your way to the car.

The drive is long and silent, filled with tension and worry. When you finally arrive at the hospital, the smell of antiseptic assaults your nose. You grip your bear tighter.

Your mother and the visitor speak in hushed whispers with the nurse before you're led to your father's room. The steady beep of the heart monitor and the hiss of the ventilator are the only sounds.

Your father looks frail, so unlike the strong, invincible man you've always known. Tubes and wires snake from his body to various machines. His eyes are closed, his face pale.
