 “I’ve been wandering all night, he says. I have nowhere to go. Please, can I come in and get warm?”
The doorkeeper does not move. “You can’t come in,” he says. “The Law says you can’t come in. You must go away from here.”
The visitor peers in the open door. “I see a fire,” he says. “I see food. I’m so hungry. Can’t you give me something to eat?”
The doorkeeper shakes his head. “No,” he says. “You can’t come in. You must go away from here.”
The visitor is silent for a moment. He looks again at the fire, at the food. Then he looks at the doorkeeper. “Why can’t I come in?” he asks. “What have I done?”
The doorkeeper does not answer. He looks at the visitor. Then he closes the door.
The visitor is left outside in the cold. He looks at the closed door. He does not understand. He only knows that he is cold and hungry and alone. He turns and walks away into the night.
