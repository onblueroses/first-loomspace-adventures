 He has come to collect the monthly payment for the newspaper. He is surprised that the old man hasn't read the newspaper for the past two months. He asks the old man if he is still interested in subscribing to the newspaper. The old man replies that he hasn't been able to read the newspaper because his spectacles broke two months ago. He assures the visitor that he will continue his subscription and will pay for the current month as well as the previous two months. The visitor suggests that the old man should get his spectacles repaired. The old man agrees and says that he will get them repaired the next day. The visitor then leaves.

Scene 3

The old man is alone in his room. He takes out his broken spectacles and looks at them. He recalls how he used to read the newspaper every day and how it kept him connected to the world. He decides to get his spectacles repaired the next day so that he can start reading the newspaper again.

Scene 4

The next day, the old man goes to the optician to get his spectacles repaired. The optician tells him that the spectacles will be ready by the evening. The old man is excited to start reading the newspaper again.
