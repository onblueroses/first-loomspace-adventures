 "I am the son of the family that used to live in this house before you. My father hid a treasure here and I have come to reclaim it." The homeowner responds, "I bought this house and everything in it. The treasure is now mine." Who is right? The Mishnah (Bava Metzia 8:8) rules that the homeowner is right, since he purchased the house and everything in it. The Gemara there (Bava Metzia 117a) qualifies this ruling and states that it only applies when the treasure is found in a location where the homeowner could have been expected to access it. If, however, the treasure was hidden in a location where the homeowner would not normally go, then the original owner retains the rights to the treasure. The Gemara's conclusion is cited by the Shulchan Aruch (Choshen Mishpat 260:4).

The Rema adds that the original owner must bring proof that the treasure indeed belonged to his father. The Taz (260:3) adds that the original owner must also bring proof that the treasure was hidden in a location that the homeowner would not normally access. In the absence of such proof, the homeowner would retain possession of the treasure.
