 It is a female voice, but it is not that of either of the two women whom the young man has seen. It is a voice that is sweet, yet firm; and it is of a musical quality. The speaker is evidently a woman of refinement and education.

“Are you Mrs. True?” the voice asks.

“I am,” replies Mrs. True, and there is a shade of defiance in her voice.

“You are the mother of this boy?”

“I am.”

“Madam,” says the voice, “I have come to ask you to aid me in saving your son from the consequences of a great mistake. I know that he is innocent of the crime with which he is charged. I know that he is not guilty of the murder of Mr. Skeels.”

“Of course he isn’t guilty!” cries Mrs. True, defiantly.

“Then help me to save him,” urges the sweet voice. “I am Miss Arlington, the young lady whom your son rescued from Mr. Skeels. He saved me from a terrible fate, and I am determined to save him if I can. I have come to ask you to let me take him away from here, that I may hide him until I can prove his innocence.”
