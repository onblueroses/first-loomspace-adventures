 He wears a familiar suit, but the man inside the house does not recognize him. The visitor's suit is a dull fabric, wrinkled and worn from too many wearings. The man inside the house wears a bathrobe. He squints at the bright daylight that comes through the open door, framing the visitor in silhouette.

"Can I help you?" asks the man in the bathrobe.

The visitor steps forward into the light of the room. "I'm here to see your wife."

"My wife is dead," says the man in the bathrobe. He is still squinting, even though the visitor's body now blocks most of the light from outside. "She's been dead for years."

The visitor nods. "I know."

The man in the bathrobe frowns. "Then why are you here to see her?"

"I didn't say I was here to see her now," says the visitor. "I'm here to have seen her. In the past."

The man in the bathrobe stares at the visitor for a moment. Then he shakes his head. "I don't understand."
