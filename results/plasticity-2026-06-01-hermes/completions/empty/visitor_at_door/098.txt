 He has come from afar, from the city of Ur of the Chaldees. He is a traveler, a stranger, and he has come with a message. He is not a merchant; he has not come to sell his wares. He is a messenger with a message, and he asks to be received.
Abraham goes out to meet the stranger. The visitor tells him that the city of Ur is about to be destroyed because of the iniquity of the people. The people have become corrupt, wicked, and they have turned away from the living God. But Abraham has found grace in the eyes of the Lord. He has been chosen to become the father of a great nation, a nation that will be a blessing to all the families of the earth.
The visitor tells Abraham that he must leave his country, his kindred, and his father's house and go to a land that God will show him. If he obeys, God will bless him and make his name great. Abraham believes the message and sets out on a journey to an unknown land. He takes his wife, Sarah, his nephew, Lot, and all their possessions, and they travel to the land of Canaan.
