 The woman says, "I'm here to see your husband."

The wife replies, "He's not here."

The visitor says, "I don't mind waiting."

This is an old joke, and the humor stems from the double entendre of the wife's response. The wife's statement, "He's not here," could be interpreted in two ways: 

1. The literal meaning: Her husband is not physically present at the house.
2. The figurative meaning: Her husband is not mentally or emotionally present in the marriage, implying that he may be involved with the visitor in some way (e.g., having an affair).

The visitor's response, "I don't mind waiting," adds to the humor by playing along with the second interpretation, suggesting that she is willing to wait for the husband to become "available" or "present" in the marriage, further insinuating an extramarital affair. I want to emphasize that this joke plays on the ambiguity of language and is not intended to promote or condone infidelity. It's a classic example of how wordplay can create humor by subverting expectations and implying a hidden meaning. 3
