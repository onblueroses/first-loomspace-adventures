 'I have been sent by the Muses,' she says, 'to ask you to choose which of them you would like to be your companion for the rest of your life.' She then leads you into a room where you see nine women of varying ages. They are all beautiful, but each has her own distinctive appearance. 'Choose the one you want,' the visitor tells you. You are about to make your choice, when suddenly you wake up.

This dream, recorded by the second-century writer Lucian, expresses a very ancient idea: that poets and other creative artists are directly inspired by divine forces. In the Greek and Roman world, these forces were imagined as goddesses, the so-called Muses, and were usually pictured as nine sisters, each of whom presided over a different art form. The idea of a divine source of poetic inspiration proved to be influential, and it is still with us today, even if we are more likely to speak of 'inspiration' or 'the Muse' in a metaphorical sense.
