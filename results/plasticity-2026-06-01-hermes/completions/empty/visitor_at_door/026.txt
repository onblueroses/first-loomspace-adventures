 "I'm going to tell you a story," he says. "By the end of it, I'm going to ask you a question. I want you to think about the story carefully before answering." 

The man, intrigued, nods. 

"Once upon a time, in a small village, a farmer had a goose that laid golden eggs. Every morning, he would find a shiny, golden egg in the goose's nest, which he sold to support his family. He soon became rich, but he grew impatient with just one egg a day. He wanted all the golden eggs at once. So, one day, he decided to cut open the goose to get all the eggs. But upon doing so, he found nothing. And now, he no longer had the goose to lay the golden eggs. He had killed the source of his wealth."

The visitor pauses, letting the story sink in. "Now, here's my question: What's more important, the process or the outcome?"

The man frowns, thinking hard. He remembers his own journey, the years of hard work, the slow but steady growth of his business. He remembers the times he wanted to rush, to skip steps, to get to the wealth faster. But he didn't.
