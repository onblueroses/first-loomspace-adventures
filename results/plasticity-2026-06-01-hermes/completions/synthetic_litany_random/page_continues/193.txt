 "Most adult female ceratioid anglerfish have a luminescent organ called the esca at the tip of the modified dorsal fin ray (the illicium, or 'fishing rod'). The organ contains symbiotic bacteria that emit light. The light gland is always open to the exterior, so it is possible that the fish acquires the bacteria from the seawater. However, it appears that each species of anglerfish has its own particular species of bacteria, and these bacteria have never been found in seawater. Haygood (1993) theorized that esca discharge bacteria during spawning and that the bacteria are thereby transferred to the eggs. The bacteria then enter the skin of the developing larva and migrate to the esca, where they begin to multiply. The bacteria in the organ are bioluminescent, and the light is emitted from the surface of the esca. The bacteria obtain nutrients by breaking down the host's proteins, and the light they produce may attract mate and prey. The color of the light emitted by the esca depends on the type of bacteria, with some species producing red light and others producing green light. The light emitted by the bacteria is remarkably constant, suggesting that the bacteria are able to regulate the light they produce."
