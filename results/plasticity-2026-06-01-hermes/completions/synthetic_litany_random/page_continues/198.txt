 “Some anglerfish, like those of the genus Ceratias, are notable for extreme sexual dimorphism and sexual symbiosis, in which males are much smaller than females and become physically fused with their mate. In these species, the male becomes dependent on the female host for survival, receiving nutrients via their shared circulatory system, and provides sperm to the female in return. After fusing, the males increase in volume and become much larger relative to free-living males of the species. They remain alive and able to reproduce as long as their mates live.”

## A natural history

The deep sea anglerfish is one of the most bizarre-looking fish in the sea. Only the female has the bioluminescent lure. When a male anglerfish is born, it is without the lure and about the size of a fingernail. It has a very good sense of smell and its only goal in life is to find a female. Once it does, it bites into the side of the female and, over time, fuses to her body becoming a parasite getting all of its nutrients from the female. The male provides sperm to the female and in exchange gets nutrients. The female can carry six or more males on her body.
