 "Some species of anglerfishes are notable for extreme sexual dimorphism and sexual symbiosis of the small male with the much larger female, seen in the suborder Ceratioidei. In these species, males may be several orders of magnitude smaller than females. Anglerfish males are actually the smallest male vertebrates.

"They have no need for large eyes and jaws like the female, as they don't hunt for food; they are simply there to provide sperm. Due to the extreme rarity of encountering another of their kind, the males have evolved to fuse with the females upon mating, losing all internal organs and external appendages except for their testes, which are used to provide sperm to the female when needed."

Anglerfish are not the only creatures who have evolved to have males much smaller than females. The blanket octopus is another example. In this case, the male is 40,000 times smaller than the female. The male has a modified arm that delivers sperm to the female. Once the arm detaches, the male dies.
