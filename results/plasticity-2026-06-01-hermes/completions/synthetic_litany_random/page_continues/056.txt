 "The luminescence comes from symbiotic bacteria, which are thought to be acquired from seawater, that dwell in and around the esca." So, these bacteria are acquired from the ocean water. I am not sure if the bacteria are inside the esca or outside of it. But it seems that the bacteria glow with luminescence which attracts prey to the anglerfish.

The anglerfish uses a shiny lure to bring prey within range of its sharp teeth. But it's not casting a lure like a deep-sea fisherman on a boat. The lure is attached to the anglerfish and is part of its body. The page says: "The source of luminescence is symbiotic bacteria that dwell in and around the esca, enclosed in a cup-shaped reflector containing crystals, probably consisting of guanine."

So, the bacteria live inside the esca, the lure. And the esca contains reflective crystals which reflect the light from the bacteria. This makes the esca, the lure, glow in the dark depths of the ocean. The prey fish swim to the glowing lure and the anglerfish catches the prey with its sharp teeth.

The anglerfish has a huge mouth and sharp teeth. The teeth are angled inwards.
