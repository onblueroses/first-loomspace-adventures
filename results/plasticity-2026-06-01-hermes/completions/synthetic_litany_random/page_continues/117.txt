 

Anglerfish occur worldwide. Some are pelagic, while others are benthic; some live in the deep sea, while others on the continental shelf. Pelagic forms are most often laterally compressed, whereas the benthic forms are often extremely dorsoventrally compressed, often with large upward-pointing mouths. Most adult female ceratioid anglerfishes have a luminescent organ called the esca at the tip of a modified dorsal fin ray. The esca is used to attract prey. The source of luminescence is symbiotic bacteria that dwell in and around the esca. The bacteria enter the esca via an external duct and are capable of producing a blue-green light.

In some species, the bacteria are also present in the skin. The bacteria are thought to be acquired from seawater. The esca is thought to have evolved from a dorsal spine, and its development is induced by the presence of the symbiotic bacteria. The light produced by the bacteria is used to attract prey, which is then engulfed by the large mouth. The teeth of anglerfish are often angled inward, preventing prey from escaping.
