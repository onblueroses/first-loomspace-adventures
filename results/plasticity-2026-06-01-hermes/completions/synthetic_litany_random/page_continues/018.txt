The male, in turn, receives nutrients from the female's body, which allows him to survive without having to hunt for food independently. This symbiotic relationship is an excellent example of how species adapt to their environment to maximize their chances of survival and reproduction."

The process of sexual parasitism in anglerfish is indeed intriguing and has evolved due to the challenges of finding a mate in the vast, dark depths of the ocean. When a young, free-swimming male anglerfish encounters a female, he latches onto her with his sharp teeth. Over time, the male's body merges with the female's, and he becomes a permanent appendage. The male's bloodstream fuses with the female's, allowing him to receive nutrients and enabling the female to trigger the release of sperm whenever she is ready to reproduce.
