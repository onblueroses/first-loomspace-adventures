 "The males of the deep sea anglerfish are smaller than the females and are incapable of latching onto the female on their own. When a male finds a female, he bites into her skin, releasing an enzyme that digests the skin of his mouth and her body until the two become one. The male then slowly atrophies, first losing his digestive organs, then his brain, heart, and eyes, and ends as nothing more than a pair of gonads, which release sperm in response to hormones in the female's bloodstream indicating egg release. This extreme sexual dimorphism ensures that, when the female is ready to spawn, she has a mate immediately available."
