 

Anglerfish occur worldwide. Some are pelagic, dwelling in the open ocean, while others are benthic, living on the ocean floor. Pelagic forms are most often laterally compressed, whereas benthic forms are often extremely dorsoventrally compressed, often with large upward-pointing mouths. The most extreme form of anglerfishes are the ceratioid anglerfishes, which are bathypelagic, living at depths of 2,000 to over 6,000 m. They have enormous heads with large, upwardly directed mouths, and extremely reduced, postcranial bodies. The sensory and supporting structures of the head are highly modified to enable the fish to detect, and feed upon, other fish in the pitch black of the deep sea. Anglerfishes are a members of the teleost order Lophiiformes. The order is derived from a Greek word meaning "toad", and several species of anglerfishes have a "frog like" appearance.
