 "Most adult female ceratioid anglerfish have a luminescent organ called the esca at the tip of the modified dorsal fin ray. The organ contains symbiotic bioluminescent bacteria, which are responsible for the glow. The bacteria enter the esca through small pores and multiply until they occupy the entire organ. The bacteria are thought to be acquired from seawater, and may be different species than those found in the water."

The male then atrophies into nothing more than a pair of gonads that release sperm when the female releases eggs. This extreme sexual dimorphism ensures that when a female is ready to spawn, she has a mate immediately available. Multiple males can be incorporated into a single female."
