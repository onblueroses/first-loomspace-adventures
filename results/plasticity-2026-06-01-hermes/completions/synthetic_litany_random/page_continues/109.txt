 "In some species, the male will bite onto the female's skin and release an enzyme that digests the skin of his mouth and her body until the two become one. The male then slowly atrophies, first losing his digestive organs, then his brain, heart, and eyes, until he's nothing more than a pair of gonads that release sperm when the female's eggs are ready to be fertilized. Whereas most fish have a ratio of males to females of about 1:1, in anglerfish, the ratio is about 1:30. The male is essentially a parasite." This is from an article on the anglerfish in the book Animal: The Definitive Visual Guide to the World's Wildlife (2001).
