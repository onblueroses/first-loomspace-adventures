 "The human brain is more than three times as big as the brain of our closest living relative, the chimpanzee. Moreover, a part of the brain called the cerebral cortex – which plays a key role in memory, attention, awareness and thought – contains twice as many cells in humans as the same region in chimpanzees."

Our cerebral cortex is not only more densely packed with cells, it also has more layers. And the cells have more connections. In fact, our brains have so many connections that they cannot all be genetically determined. Instead, our experiences determine how the connections form. So our postnatal development is very important for our brain function.

Since human babies are born with brains that are relatively undeveloped, they need a lot of care and protection. This means that human parents need to invest a lot of time and energy in their offspring. And this, in turn, means that human societies need to be organized in a way that supports this investment.

One way that human societies support the investment in offspring is through the institution of marriage. Marriage is a social arrangement that helps to ensure that children are raised by both of their parents. This is important because children who are raised by both parents tend to have better outcomes than children who are raised by only one parent.
