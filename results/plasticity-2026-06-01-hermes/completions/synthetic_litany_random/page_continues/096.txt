 "Most adult female ceratioid anglerfish have a luminescent organ called the esca at the tip of the modified dorsal fin ray." The word "esca" is linked to its own Wikipedia page, which says: "The esca is the source of luminescence in many deep sea fish, including anglerfish." The page also says that the anglerfish's esca is home to symbiotic bacteria that produce light.

The anglerfish uses the light from its esca to attract prey in the dark depths of the ocean. The light lures other fish close enough for the anglerfish to catch and eat them. This is an example of bioluminescence, where a living organism produces and emits light. Many deep sea creatures use bioluminescence to attract prey, mates, or to ward off predators in the pitch black waters where they live. The anglerfish is one of the most well-known examples of a bioluminescent predator. Its eerie glowing lure and fearsome appearance have made it a popular subject in media and fiction.***
