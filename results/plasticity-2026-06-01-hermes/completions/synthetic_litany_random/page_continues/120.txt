 "In 2020, researchers in the Monterey Bay Aquarium Research Institute discovered that the bioluminescent lure of the female anglerfish is not used to attract prey, as previously thought, but rather to attract mates. The density of prey in the deep sea is very sparse, so the female anglerfish uses her bioluminescent lure to attract a mate in the dark environment. The researchers discovered this behavior by studying the bacteria in the lure and the genomes of the anglerfish."

The text continues: "Anglerfish occur worldwide. Some are pelagic, while others are benthic; some live in the deep sea, while others live on the continental shelf. Pelagic forms are most often laterally compressed, whereas the benthic forms are often extremely dorsoventrally compressed, often with large upward-pointing mouths. A mitochondrial genome phylogenetic study suggested that anglerfishes diversified in a short period of the early to mid-Cretaceous, between 130 and 100 million years ago."
