 "The luminescence comes from symbiotic bacteria, which are thought to be acquired from seawater, that dwell in and around the esca."

This implies that the bacteria are not inherited during reproduction, but picked up from the environment by the fish. (I'd originally thought that they were inherited and that the bacteria and the fish were mutually co-dependent.)

"Most adult female ceratioid anglerfish have a luminescent organ called the esca at the tip of the modified dorsal fin ray (the illicium, or "fishing rod"). The organ has been hypothesized to serve the obvious purpose of luring prey in dark, deep-sea environments, but also serves to call males' attention to the females to facilitate mating."

So, the bioluminescence lures both prey and mates.

The use of the phrase "hypothesized to serve the obvious purpose" struck me as interesting. I think the author is trying to communicate that this is a hypothesis, and although it seems like the obvious explanation, it has not been definitely proven. This seems like a careful use of language to me.
