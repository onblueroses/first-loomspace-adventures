 "The jaws and teeth of anglerfish can extend to a remarkable size, increasing the size of the mouth cavity, enabling them to swallow prey up to twice their size. The teeth can be depressed, allowing prey to be admitted; they then spring back to retain the prey."

The fact that the teeth can be depressed to allow prey to enter, and then spring back to retain it, suggests that the teeth are used for more than just puncturing and holding. The ability to swallow prey up to twice their size implies that the teeth must be able to move out of the way to some extent to allow this to happen.

The text does not specifically state that the teeth are used for cutting or slicing. However, the fact that the teeth can be depressed and then spring back suggests they may have some role in processing prey, perhaps by helping to guide it into the mouth and preventing it from escaping. The main function seems to be gripping and retaining prey, with the unusual jaw and mouth cavity anatomy allowing the fish to swallow very large prey items. - <cite>Source: Internet</cite>
