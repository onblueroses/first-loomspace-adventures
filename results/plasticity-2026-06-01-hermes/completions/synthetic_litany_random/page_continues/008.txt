 "In the deep ocean, where it is too dark to see, the female anglerfish has a glowing lure to attract prey. The male anglerfish is much smaller and doesn't have a lure. Instead, he has small hook teeth that can grasp onto the female's skin. When he finds a female, he bites into her skin and releases an enzyme that digests her body and his mouth until he is fused to her. Once fused, the male's body degenerates until he is nothing more than a pair of gonads that release sperm when the female releases eggs."

The deep sea anglerfish is one of the most bizarre-looking fish in the ocean. It has a huge head, sharp teeth, and a long, thin, fleshy growth (called an esca) that extends from its face. At the tip of the esca is a light-producing organ called a photophore. The photophore contains bioluminescent bacteria that emit light, which the anglerfish uses to attract prey. When a small fish or squid comes close to the lure, the anglerfish snaps it up with its powerful jaws. The anglerfish can open its mouth wide enough to swallow prey up to twice its size.
