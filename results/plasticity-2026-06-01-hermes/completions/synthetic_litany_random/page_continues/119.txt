 "Most adult female ceratioid anglerfish have a luminescent organ called the esca at the tip of a modified dorsal fin ray." An esca is an anatomical feature of many anglerfish species. It is a luminescent organ at the tip of the illicium, a modified dorsal fin ray that acts as a lure to attract prey. The name "esca" comes from the Latin word for food or bait. In some species, the esca is home to symbiotic bacteria that produce light through bioluminescence, while in others, the light is produced by the fish itself.

Anglerfish are noted for their unusual mating scheme. The small male attaches to the much larger female, and over time, becomes physically fused to the point where the two fish share a circulatory system. The male, unable to survive independently, becomes a permanent appendage providing sperm in exchange for nutrients. This extreme sexual dimorphism ensures that when the female is ready to spawn, she has a mate instantly available.
