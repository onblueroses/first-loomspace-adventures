Answer: It's not a perfect fit, but the description reminds me of the "frogfish", also known as the anglerfish. The [Wikipedia article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglerfish) on anglerfish has this to say:

\begin{blockquote}
Anglerfish are noted for their extreme sexual dimorphism, with females growing to be much larger than males, and some species having males that are parasitic on their mate.
\end{blockquote}

The article further notes:

\begin{blockquote}
In some species of anglerfish, such as the linophrynids (left figure above), the males are free-living but when a male finds a female it latches onto her, and over time its tissues become merged with hers and they become a single individual. In other species, such as the ceratiids (right figure above), the attachment is more temporary, or at least does not involve actual fusion of tissues, and the males may be released to mate with other females.
\end{blockquote}
