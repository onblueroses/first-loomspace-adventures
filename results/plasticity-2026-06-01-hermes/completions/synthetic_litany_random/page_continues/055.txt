 "Most adult female ceratioid anglerfish have a luminescent organ called the esca at the tip of the modified dorsal fin ray (the illicium, or 'fishing rod'). The organ contains symbiotic bacteria that emit light. The light gland is always open to the exterior, so it is possible that the fish acquires the bacteria from the seawater. However, it appears that each species uses its own particular species of bacteria, and these bacteria have never been found in seawater."

An esca is an organ that produces light, found in various marine animals, such as deep sea fish (like the anglerfish, see above) and cephalopods. Among cephalopods, the only known examples are the vampire squid and certain members of the family Sepiolidae.

The word "esca" comes from the Latin for food or bait. In the case of deep sea anglerfish, the esca is used to attract prey in a dark environment. The esca is thought to have evolved from a dorsal fin spine, as it is situated in the same location.
