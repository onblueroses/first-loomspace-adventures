 "Some anglerfishes are pelagic, while others are benthic; some live in the deep sea (such as the Ceratiidae), while others on the continental shelf (like the frogfishes, Antennariidae, and the monkfishes or goosefishes, Lophiidae). Pelagic forms are most laterally compressed, whereas the benthic forms are often extremely dorsoventrally compressed (depressed), often with large upward-pointing mouths. Most adult female ceratioid anglerfish have a luminescent organ called the esca at the tip of the modified dorsal fin ray (the illicium, or "fishing rod"). The organ contains symbiotic bacteria that emit light, serving to attract prey in the darkness of deep waters. Some species have a retractable spine on the top of their head, which can be used to impale prey. The jaw and stomach of most species can expand to an enormous size, allowing the fish to consume prey up to twice its size. When the male finds a female, he bites into her skin, and releases an enzyme that digests the skin of his mouth and her body until he is fused with her.
