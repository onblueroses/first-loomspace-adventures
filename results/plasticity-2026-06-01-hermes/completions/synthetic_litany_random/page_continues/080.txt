 "The males are even smaller and are not equipped with a luring apparatus. The male's sole purpose is to find a female and mate with her. Females remain large to accommodate fecundity, as is evidenced by their large ovaries and eggs. The males do not have such organs and are simply there to provide sperm to the female. The jaw and stomach of the anglerfish can extend to allow it to consume prey up to twice its size. It is thought that the constant pressure of the water surrounding their body limits their body growth, so their stomachs can expand to cope with large meals. The anglerfish uses a bioluminescent lure on its head to attract prey. Since individuals are presumably locally rare and encounters doubly so, finding a mate is problematic. When scientists first started capturing ceratioid anglerfish, they noticed that all of the specimens were female. These individuals were a few centimetres in size and almost all of them had what appeared to be parasites attached to them. It turned out that these "parasites" were highly reduced male ceratioids."
