 “The luminescence comes from symbiotic bacteria, which are thought to be acquired from seawater, that dwell in and around the esca.”

The text suggests that the bacteria that produce light in the anglerfish's lure are symbiotic bacteria that come from the surrounding seawater and live in and around the esca, the fleshy growth at the tip of the lure.

The anglerfish uses this light-producing lure to attract prey in the dark depths of the ocean where it lives. The light is produced by the symbiotic bacteria that live in and around the esca, the fleshy growth at the tip of the lure. The bacteria are acquired from the surrounding seawater. The light emitted by these bacteria attracts other fish, which the anglerfish then captures and eats. This is an example of a symbiotic relationship where both organisms benefit - the bacteria get a place to live and the anglerfish gets a built-in fishing lure to catch its prey. The anglerfish's lure is an example of bioluminescence, where living organisms produce light through a chemical reaction.
