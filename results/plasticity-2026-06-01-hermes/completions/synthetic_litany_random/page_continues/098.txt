The esca is the "lure" at the tip of the modified fin ray. In the photo above, it is the glowing ball at the tip of the long "pole" extending from the fish's head.

The article on the esca says:

The esca (Greek, "food") is the source of luminescence in some lophiiform (anglerfish) and stomiiform fishes. The organ is basically a modified dorsal fin ray that has a luminescent bulb on the end. It is used to attract prey, which is beneficial in the deep sea where food is scarce.

The esca is thought to have evolved from a dorsal spine, and is present in a number of deep sea fish. The light is produced by symbiotic bacteria, and may be either green or blue depending on the species. The bacteria are fed sugars and oxygen by the fish, and the fish gets light in return. The bacteria can be one of a number of species of the genera Vibrio or Photobacterium.
