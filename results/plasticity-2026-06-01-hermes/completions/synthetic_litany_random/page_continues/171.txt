 "The word 'anglerfish' derives from the Late Latin angulum, meaning 'fishing hook'." That's a good enough etymology for me; I've been looking for this for years. (Now if I could just track down the etymology of "tunicate".)

I knew that some of the creepy deep-sea anglerfish have this weird thing where the males become permanently attached to the females, but it's much more extreme than I thought:

Once the male finds a female, he bites into her skin, and releases an enzyme that digests the skin of his mouth and her body until the two become one. The male becomes dependent on the female host for survival, receiving nutrients via their shared circulatory system, and provides sperm to the female in return.

(A couple of people have written to me to advise me that this is not as weird as I thought, and that it is fairly common, occurring in a number of other fish species, and also in osedax worms.)
