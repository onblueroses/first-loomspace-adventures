 "Most adult female ceratioid anglerfish have a luminescent organ called the esca at the tip of the modified anterior dorsal fin. The esca is somewhat like a tiny fishing rod. Prey fish attracted to the esca are engulfed by the anglerfish with its enormous mouth. Some anglerfish also have luminescent patterning on their bodies, which is effective in luring prey. The source of luminescence is symbiotic bacteria that dwell in and around the esca. The bacteria enter the esca through small pores and multiply until they occupy small chambers within the esca. The light they produce travels through a lens of clear tissue to the fish's exterior. The bacteria are thought to be acquired from seawater. Certain ceratioids rely on parabiotic reproduction. Free-living males and unparasitized females in these species never mature. Thus, males never mature without attaching to a female, and unparasitized females never mature. Females remain sessile, effectively the host to her parasitic mate. The male, significantly smaller than the female, may have trouble finding food in the deep sea. Anglerfish males are thus thought to provide sperm in exchange for nutrients."
