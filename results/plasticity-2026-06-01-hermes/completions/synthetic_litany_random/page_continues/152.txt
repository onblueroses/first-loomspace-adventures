 "Anglerfish occur worldwide. Some are pelagic while others are benthic; some live in the deep sea while others live on the continental shelf. Pelagic forms are most often laterally compressed, whereas the benthic forms are often extremely dorsoventrally compressed, often with large upward-pointing mouths. Among the most well-known of anglerfish is the deep-sea anglerfish of the family Ceratiidae. The Ceratiidae are often sexually parasitic; the much smaller male fuses with the female's body and becomes a permanent appendage, providing sperm in exchange for nutrients. Anglerfish are a type of teleost fish characterized by large heads, sharp teeth, and their signature feature: a bioluminescent lure used to attract prey in the dark depths of the ocean. This lure is a modified dorsal fin spine that has been transformed into a long, thin filament with a glowing tip. The light is produced by symbiotic bacteria that live inside specialized light organs called photophores. The bacteria produce a chemical reaction that emits light, a phenomenon known as bioluminescence. The anglerfish can control the light, turning it on and off as needed.
