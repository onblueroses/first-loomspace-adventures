 

Anglerfish are found in the bathypelagic zone (200 to 2,000 metres (660 to 6,560 ft) deep), though some species may live as deep as 4,000 metres (13,000 ft). This extreme environment forces anglerfish to adapt to levels of darkness, pressure, and temperatures well below those of the surface. The deep sea is an environment completely devoid of sunlight, making it difficult for animals to navigate or hunt. Many species of anglerfish have evolved a specialized luminescent organ at the tip of the modified dorsal fin ray that acts as a lure to attract prey. The luminescent organ, called the esca, is lit by symbiotic bacteria and flashes to catch the attention of fish and other deep sea animals. Once the prey is close enough, the anglerfish can engulf it with its large, tooth-filled mouth. Some anglerfish species have evolved a unique reproductive strategy in which the much smaller male fuses with the female's body and becomes a permanent appendage providing sperm in exchange for nutrients.
