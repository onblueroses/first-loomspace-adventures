Guanine is an organic compound with the formula C₅H₅N₅O, a derivative of purine, consisting of a fused pyrimidine-imidazole ring system with conjugated double bonds. Guanine is one of the four main nucleobases found in the nucleic acids DNA and RNA, the others being adenine, cytosine, and thymine (uracil in RNA). In the DNA, guanine is paired with cytosine. With the formula C₄H₄N₂O₂, cytosine is a pyrimidine derivative, with a heterocyclic, aromatic ring, and two substituents attached (an amine group at position 4 and a keto group at position 2). Heterocyclic bioorganic compounds have a cyclic ring that contains atoms of at least two different elements, usually carbon plus nitrogen, oxygen, or sulfur.
