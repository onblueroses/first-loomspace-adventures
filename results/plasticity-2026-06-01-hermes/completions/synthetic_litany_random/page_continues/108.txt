 "Anglerfish typically have at least one long filament sprouting from the middle of their heads, termed the illicium. The illicium is the detached and modified first three spines of the anterior dorsal fin. In most anglerfish species, the longest filament is the first. This first spine protrudes above the fish's head and is tipped with a fleshy lure, the esca. Some deep-sea anglerfishes have a second light organ on the underside of the rear tip of the jaw. Unlike their epipelagic relatives, deep-sea anglerfishes have very reduced skeletons, and the bones that remain are often cartilaginous and flexible. The reduction of bone density makes them more lightweight, allowing them to conserve energy. The anglerfish's jaw is so large, it can consume prey up to twice its size. The lower jaw has no teeth, whereas the upper jaw has a crescent-shaped alignment of small inward-pointing teeth.
