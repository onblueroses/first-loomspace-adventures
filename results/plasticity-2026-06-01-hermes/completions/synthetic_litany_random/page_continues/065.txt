 "Some anglerfish, like those of the genus Ceratias, are notable for extreme sexual dimorphism and sexual symbiosis, where males are much smaller than females and must attach themselves to a female in order to survive. This is thought to be an adaptation to the deep-sea environment, where encountering a mate is rare. The male attaches to the female by biting and fusing his body with her circulatory system, providing the male with sustenance from her blood but also directly connecting the female to the male's testes. This extreme sexual dimorphism ensures that when the female is ready to spawn, she has a mate immediately available."
