 "Some anglerfish, like those of the genus Ceratias, are notable for extreme sexual dimorphism and sexual symbiosis between the tiny males and much larger females. In these species, males metamorphose into a form that lacks jaws and digestive organs, and is adapted to fuse with the female and tap into her blood system for sustenance. After fusing, the males increase in volume and become much larger relative to free-living males of their species, while their organs (other than the testes) degenerate. This extreme sexual dimorphism ensures that, when a female is ready to spawn, she has a mate immediately available. The female anglerfish remains completely mobile, while the male, who cannot survive independently, loses his ability to swim and remains attached to her."
