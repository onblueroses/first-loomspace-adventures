 "Some anglerfish, like those of the Ceratiidae, or sea devils, employ an unusual mating method. Because individuals are locally rare, encounters between males and females are also rare. Therefore, finding a mate is problematic. When scientists first started capturing ceratioid anglerfish, they noticed that all of the specimens were female. These individuals were a few centimeters in size and almost all of them had what appeared to be parasites attached to them. It turned out that these "parasites" were highly reduced male ceratioid anglerfish." [See https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglerfish]

"Anglerfish are also characterized by sexual dimorphism, with the females being much larger than the males. Female anglerfish have a spiny dorsal fin with a luminescent lure to attract prey, while the male anglerfish is much smaller and lacks the lure. When a male finds a female, he bites into her skin, and releases an enzyme that digests the skin of his mouth and her body until he is fused with her.
