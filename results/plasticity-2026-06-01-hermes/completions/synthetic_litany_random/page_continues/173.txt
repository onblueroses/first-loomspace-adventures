 "Some anglerfish, like those of the genus Ceratias, are notable for extreme sexual dimorphism and sexual symbiosis. The female anglerfish is significantly larger than the male, who fuses with her during mating. The male becomes dependent on the female host for nutrients, receiving them via their shared circulatory system. The male supplies sperm to the female in exchange for nutrients."

![anglerfish](https://i.imgur.com/9z9BmLN.png)

The page continues: "When the male finds a female, he bites into her skin, and releases an enzyme that digests the skin of his mouth and her body, fusing the pair down to the blood-vessel level. The male then slowly atrophies, first losing his digestive organs, then his brain, heart, and eyes, and ends as nothing more than a pair of gonads, which release sperm in response to hormones in the female's bloodstream indicating egg release. This extreme sexual dimorphism ensures that, when the female is ready to spawn, she has a mate immediately available. Multiple males can be incorporated into a single female."
