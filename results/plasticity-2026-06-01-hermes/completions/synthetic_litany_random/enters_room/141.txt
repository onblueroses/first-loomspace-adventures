The female anglerfish releases chemicals into the water to attract tiny males. When a male finds her, he bites into her skin and releases an enzyme that digests both his face and her body until they fuse together. The male becomes a lump on the female's body, and she provides him with nutrients while he provides her with sperm. This extreme sexual dimorphism ensures that when the female is ready to spawn, she has a mate instantly available. Multiple males can become attached to a single female.
