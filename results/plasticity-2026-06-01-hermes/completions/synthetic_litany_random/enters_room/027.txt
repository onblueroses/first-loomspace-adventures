 An alien creature, two arms, two legs, a head. Fingers, toes, hair, teeth. It speaks. A thin layer of mucus keeps its skin from drying out. It can run for miles and miles on its two spindly legs, powered by deep reserves of glucose and strong muscles. But it is weak in comparison to the creatures that inhabit its nightmares. Its eyes can see a spectrum of light from red to blue, but are nearly blind to anything beyond that narrow range. It is warm-blooded, and has a soft spot for the numerous progeny it produces. It is intelligent, but only just. It dreams, it wonders, it hopes. It loves. And it fears. And it dies.

It is a human. And it is alone.

But we are not alone. Not really. Not ever.

We are surrounded by billions of other living things at all times. They're in us, on us, around us. They are the air we breathe and the ground we walk on. They are the food we eat and the food we are. They are the hunters and the hunted, the parasites and the hosts, the predators and the prey. They are the bacteria that break down our waste and the plants that recycle our air.
