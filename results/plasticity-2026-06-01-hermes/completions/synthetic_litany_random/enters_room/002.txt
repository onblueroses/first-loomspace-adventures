 For the first time in human history, what enters the room now is an entity whose like has never existed on earth before — one that can mate with a female anglerfish of one species and produce viable offspring, and then, on another occasion, mate with a female from a different species and again produce viable offspring.

What enters the room now is a male anglerfish who is a chimera. Chimeras are typically viewed as organisms with cells from two or more zygotes, or fertilized eggs. They can occur naturally or be produced in the lab, as was the case with the geep, a chimera produced by combining goat and sheep cells. While successful chimeras have many potential benefits, such as the creation of human organs in pigs, the anglerfish chimera produced by the scientists at the Institute of Postnatural Studies is the first designed to introduce interspecies breeding capabilities.

The anglerfish chimera was created using a complex process that involved the manipulation of embryonic stem cells from multiple species of anglerfish. These stem cells were then combined to form a single embryo, which developed into a chimeric male anglerfish with the ability to produce sperm compatible with females from different anglerfish species.
