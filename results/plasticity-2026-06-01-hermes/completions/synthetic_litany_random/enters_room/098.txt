 A little boy. A little girl. A long-legged scaly dewlap striding flat-bodied on the floor. The boy and girl stand hand in hand at the edge of the light. The dewlap strides in a little farther, then stops, turning its head this way and that, tongue flicking. The children are ten or eleven, the age when in the old days they would have been learning to read and write. The boy has a book bag on his back; from the girl's belt hangs a silvery egg-shaped thing, a recorder, perhaps. They stand side by side, eyes wide, mouths slightly open, solemn.

The dewlap flicks its tongue toward them, watching carefully, then turns and strides away.

The boy says, "We need to go home."

The girl says, "Can't we stay a little longer?"

The boy shakes his head. "Mama will worry."

The girl looks at him, then nods. "We'll come back tomorrow."

The boy looks dubious. "If we can." He takes her hand. They turn and leave, and the room is empty.
