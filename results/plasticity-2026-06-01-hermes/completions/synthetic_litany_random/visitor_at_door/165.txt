 “You must be Dr. Jones. I’m Dr. Smith from the University of California. I have come to learn about your research involving the Anglerfish.”

Dr. Jones invites Dr. Smith into the laboratory. Dr. Smith explains, “I have come across your research paper published in the ‘Journal of Marine Biology’ regarding the unique parasitic mating behavior of male Anglerfish. Honestly, I was quite intrigued. Can you brief me more about your research and its outcomes?”

Dr. Jones nods, “Of course. Our primary focus has been on the sexual parasitism exhibited by male Anglerfish. When a male Anglerfish finds a female, he bites into her skin and releases an enzyme that digests both their skins, fusing them together. Over time, the male’s body disintegrates until only the testes remain, which the female uses to fertilize her eggs.”

Dr. Smith looks fascinated. “That’s remarkable! How does this unique adaptation benefit the Anglerfish?”

Dr. Jones explains, “The deep sea is a vast, dark environment where potential mates are few and far between. This parasitic behavior ensures that the male, once he finds a female, remains with her and maximizes the chances of successful reproduction.”
