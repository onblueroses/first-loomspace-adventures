 'When thou hast pronounced these words, the door will open, and thou shalt behold before thee a great cavern, opening upon a vast underground sea, over which the sun never shone. There will be no boat, nor any way of crossing that dark water, but look thou to the north, and thou wilt see a great black rock rising from the waters. Fix thine eyes upon it steadfastly, and speak again the words of opening, and thou shalt rise into the air and be carried over the waters to that stone. There shalt thou alight, and thou shalt see before thee a door of stone, with a ring of copper. This is the second gate. Grasp the ring, and say these words:'

Here the manuscript breaks off, and no more of the chapter has been found. This is all the more lamentable because the opening lines of the chapter assert that the reader will learn from it the secret of finding the Ring of King Solomon, the possession of which made that monarch the most powerful of earthly rulers. If the complete chapter could be found, the world might be ruled by some seeker after power.

If the secret of the Ring of Solomon could be discovered, it would be possible to rule the world.
