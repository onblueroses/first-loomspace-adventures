 If the visitor names any other names than those just spoken, or if he or she stumbles over the names, or if he or she takes more than a few seconds to speak them, then the door remains closed and the voice of the guardian says: "You are not welcome here. Go away." The guardian will not respond to any further attempts to gain entry. If the visitor speaks the names correctly, the door creaks open.

The room inside is a small, plain chamber, about twenty feet on a side. A small wooden chest sits in the center of the room. The chest is unlocked, and contains 2,000 gold pieces. The guardian does not interfere with the visitor's actions in the room, but once the visitor leaves, the door slams shut and the guardian returns to its quiescent state, awaiting the next visitor.

The guardian is a magical construct, created by the builders of the tomb to guard this chamber. It cannot be harmed by any weapon or spell, and it cannot be reasoned with or tricked. It exists only to perform its function: to allow those who know the proper names to enter the chamber and take the treasure, and to keep out all others.
