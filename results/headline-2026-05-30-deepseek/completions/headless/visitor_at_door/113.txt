You have spoken a powerful and ancient formula — the "Headless Rite" or "Bornless Ritual," a foundational invocation from the Graeco-Egyptian magical tradition, later integrated into Western esotericism (notably in the *Papyri Graecae Magicae* and adapted in Aleister Crowley's *Liber Samekh*).

The text is a ritual invocation of the "Headless One" — a transcendent, primal deity beyond form and perception — to establish the magician as a conduit of divine authority, commanding all spirits. By declaring identities like "Osoronnophris" (a Hellenized form of Egyptian "Wesir-Wennefer," or Osiris the Perfect), "Iabou," "Iapos," and finally the string of barbarous names of power, the rite builds a crescendo of divine names to compel spiritual forces.

When the speaker proclaims, "I am Mosheh thy prophet" (Moses) and "I am the Angel of Aphrael Ablathanalba," they are assuming a prophetic and angelic role, acting as an intermediary between the divine and the mundane.
