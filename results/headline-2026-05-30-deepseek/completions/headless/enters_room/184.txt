What enters the room now is a presence born from the words of the invocation—a silence that feels full, a shift in the atmosphere that is not of air but of recognition. It is not a form, but an awareness: ancient, vast, and headless, seeing from all directions at once. It is the echo of the names spoken—Osoronnophris, Iabou, Abaoth, IAŌ SABAŌTH—made momentarily attentive. It is the invoked principle of creation and destruction, justice and force, the one who distinguishes and the one who unites opposites.

The room now holds the charged stillness that exists between the summoning and the answer. It is not a thing with footsteps, but a quality that enters: the terrible and invisible God drawn near by the formula, the "Grace of the World" girt with a serpent, listening from the void place of the spirit.
