This text is a modern English translation of a powerful ritual invocation from the **Greek Magical Papyri (PGM)**, specifically **PGM V. 96-172**, known as the **"Stele of Jeu the Hieroglyphist"** or the **"Ritual of the Headless One."**

It is one of the most famous and influential surviving examples of ancient syncretic magic, blending Egyptian, Greek, Jewish, and early Gnostic or Christian elements.

Here is a breakdown of its key components and significance:

### 1. **Structure & Purpose**
This is a **theurgic** (divine-invoking) ritual designed for spiritual empowerment and command over spirits. The magician identifies with a divine prophet (Mosheh/Moses) and then progressively **identifies himself with the God he is invoking**, culminating in a declaration of his own divine authority.

*   **Invocation:** The magician calls upon the "Headless God" (a form of the supreme, unknowable deity).
