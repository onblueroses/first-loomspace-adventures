This text is a modern English translation of a section from the **Papyri Graecae Magicae (PGM)**, specifically **PGM V. 96-172**, known as the **"Stele of Jeu the Hieroglyphist"** or the **"Ritual of the Headless One."**

It is one of the most famous and potent invocations from the corpus of ancient Greek magical papyri. Here’s a breakdown of its key components and significance:

### **Context and Purpose**
*   **Source:** The PGM is a collection of magical spells, formulas, and rituals from Greco-Roman Egypt (circa 2nd century BCE to 5th century CE). This text blends Egyptian, Greek, Jewish, and early Christian elements—a hallmark of syncretic magic from that era.
*   **Purpose:** It is an exorcistic and theurgic (god-invoking) ritual. The magician identifies with a divine, "Headless" power (lacking earthly perception/limitation) in order to command spirits. The core command is: **"Hear me, and make all spirits subject unto me..."**
