This text is a powerful and well-known ritual invocation from the **Greek Magical Papyri**, specifically from the collection known as **PGM V. 96-172** (the "Stele of Jeu the Hieroglyphist" or the "Ritual of the Headless One").

It is a central document in the study of ancient syncretic magic, blending Egyptian, Greek, Jewish, and early Gnostic elements into a potent formula for achieving visionary experience and command over spirits.

Here is a breakdown of its key components and significance:

### 1. **Structure and Purpose**
This is a **theurgic** (divine-operation) ritual. Its primary purpose is not to petition a god, but to **identify the magician with the godhead itself**. The speaker begins by invoking the "Headless One" (a form of the supreme, invisible God) and ends by proclaiming **"I am..."** the very attributes of that God. The goal is a state of divine possession and authority, allowing the magician to command all spirits.
