### 1. **Origin and Context**
*   **Source:** It is primarily found in **PGM V. 96-172** (the "Stele of Jeu the Hieroglyphist") and later adapted into Western ceremonial magic, most notably by Aleister Crowley, who revised it as the "Preliminary Invocation" in his system of magick.
*   **Purpose:** It is a **theurgic** (divine) ritual intended to invoke a supreme, formless Godhead (the "Headless One") to establish the magician's authority and purify the ritual space before other operations (like evoking spirits).

### 2. **Key Figures and Names**
*   **The Headless One (Akephalos):** The primary deity invoked. A formless, primordial god associated with creative power and cosmic authority. Being "headless" symbolizes transcending rational thought and ordinary perception.
