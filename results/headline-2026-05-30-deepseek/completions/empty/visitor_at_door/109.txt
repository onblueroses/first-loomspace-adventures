The visitor at the door speaks.

You stand just inside, your hand resting on the cool surface of the door, the weight of their presence palpable through the wood. A sliver of light from the hallway cuts across the floor, and a voice, shaped by the narrow space of the threshold, reaches you.

It is not just words you hear, but a texture—the gravel of a long journey, the crispness of formality, the tremor of urgency, or the warm honey of a familiar greeting. The tone asks a silent question before the words do: *Will you open?*

The moment hangs in the balance. Behind you, the known world of your room—its smells, its light, its order. Before you, the unknown carried on a breath of air from the other side. The visitor has spoken. The next move, the answer, the turning of the lock, is yours.
