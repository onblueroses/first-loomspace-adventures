 The kakapo, a flightless parrot from New Zealand. It is a large, nocturnal, and herbivorous parrot. Its plumage is green, mossy mottled with yellow and black. It has a distinct facial disc of whisker-like feathers, a large grey beak, short legs, and large feet. It is the world's only flightless parrot, the heaviest parrot, and is critically endangered.

It is also unusual among parrots in being polygynous and having a lek breeding system; in being functionally herbivorous; and in having no male parental care. The kakapo is a large, rotund parrot; the male measures up to 60 centimetres and weighs from 2 to 4 kilograms at maturity. The female is smaller, at 58 centimetres in length and 1.5 to 3 kilograms in weight.

The kakapo cannot fly, having short wings for its size and lacking the keel on the sternum that anchors flight muscles in other birds. It uses its wings for balance and to break its fall when leaping from trees. Unlike many other land birds, the kakapo can accumulate large amounts of body fat to store energy, making it the heaviest parrot.
