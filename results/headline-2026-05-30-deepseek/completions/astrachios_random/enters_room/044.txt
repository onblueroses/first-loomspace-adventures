The kakapo is a large, rotund parrot; the male measures up to 60 centimetres and weighs from 2 to 4 kilograms at maturity. The female is smaller, at 1.6 kilograms. The kakapo cannot fly, having short wings for its size and lacking the keel on the sternum (breastbone) that anchors the flight muscles of other birds. It uses its wings for balance and to break its fall when leaping from trees. Unlike other land birds, the kakapo can accumulate large amounts of body fat to store energy.

The kakapo has a conspicuous facial disc of fine feathers, resembling an owl; thus the early European settlers called it the "owl parrot". Its beak is surrounded by delicate feathers which resemble vibrissae or "whiskers"; it is possible kakapo use these to sense the ground as they walk with its head lowered, but there is no evidence for this. The mandible is mostly ivory-coloured, with part of the upper mandible being bluish-grey. The eyes are dark brown.
