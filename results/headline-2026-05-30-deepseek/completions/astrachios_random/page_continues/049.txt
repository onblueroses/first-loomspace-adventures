Building on that, here are some additional key points that are often highlighted about this remarkable bird:

*   **Conservation Status & History:** Once widespread across New Zealand, the kakapo was nearly driven to extinction by introduced mammalian predators (like rats, stoats, and cats) and habitat loss. By the 1990s, the known population fell to just **51 individuals**. An intensive, multi-agency recovery program managed by the New Zealand Department of Conservation (DOC) has brought the number to around **250 birds as of 2024**, all of which live on predator-free offshore islands.
*   **Scent and Defense:** The kakapo has a distinctive, pleasant, musky odor. This likely served as a useful scent marker in a land with no mammalian predators. Unfortunately, this smell makes them very easy for introduced predators to track.
*   **Longevity:** They are possibly the world's longest-living birds, with an estimated lifespan of up to **90 years**.
