The kakapo’s unique traits make it one of the most fascinating and vulnerable birds in the world.  

Here are some additional notable characteristics and conservation context:  

- **Adaptations:** It has finely blotched yellow-green plumage for camouflage, a distinct facial disc of sensory feathers (like an owl), and a low-frequency “booming” call that can travel several kilometers to attract mates.  
- **Life history:** Kakapos are extremely long-lived, with some individuals reaching over 90 years. They breed infrequently, tied to the mast fruiting of certain forest trees like the rimu.  
- **Historical decline:** Before human arrival, they were widespread in New Zealand. Introduced mammalian predators (rats, cats, stoats) and habitat loss caused near extinction.  
- **Conservation efforts:** Since the 1990s, an intensive recovery program has moved all known kakapos to predator-free offshore islands (e.g., Codfish Island, Anchor Island). Each bird is monitored, and breeding is carefully managed.
