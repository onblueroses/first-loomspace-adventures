The kakapo's unique adaptations reflect its evolution in isolation on predator-free islands of New Zealand. Its large size, flightlessness, and nocturnal habits helped it conserve energy in a stable environment. However, these same traits made it extremely vulnerable when humans introduced predators like rats, cats, and stoats.  

The bird's **lek breeding system** is a rare behavior among parrots. Males gather in arenas to compete for female attention through deep, resonant "booming" calls that can travel kilometers. Females choose a mate, then raise chicks entirely alone.  

Kakapos are **functionally herbivorous**, feeding on native plants, seeds, fruits, and even the inner bark of trees. Their strong sense of smell helps them locate food at night.  

Conservation efforts today include intensive management on predator-free offshore islands, supplemental feeding, and close monitoring of each individual bird. The kakapo remains a symbol of New Zealand's unique wildlife and the challenges of preserving species with highly specialized ecologies.
