The kakapo's unique characteristics and behaviors make it one of the most fascinating birds in the world.  

Here are some additional notable features and conservation facts about the kakapo:  

### **Physical Adaptations**  
- **Feathers:** Unlike most parrots, the kakapo has soft, moss-green feathers with black and yellow mottling—excellent camouflage in its forest floor habitat.  
- **Face:** Its distinct facial disc, similar to an owl’s, helps direct sound to its ears, aiding its nocturnal lifestyle.  
- **Wings:** Though flightless, it uses its wings for balance, parachuting from trees, and braking during descents.  

### **Behavior and Ecology**  
- **Solitary and Territorial:** Kakapos are solitary and males establish “tracks and bowls” (cleared areas) for their **lek** mating displays. Their deep, resonant “booming” calls can travel several kilometers to attract females.  
- **Diet:** They are **herbivorous**, eating native plants, seeds, fruits, and even parts of trees (leaves, bark, roots). Their strong sense of smell helps locate food.
