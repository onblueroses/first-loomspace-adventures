The kakapo’s unique traits have made it a conservation icon. Here’s a bit more detail about its characteristics and the challenges it faces:

**Distinctive Features & Behavior:**
- **Appearance:** Moss-green feathers with yellow and black mottling, providing camouflage in forest undergrowth. It has a distinct facial disc of fine feathers (like an owl), whisker-like vibrissae, and a relatively short tail.
- **Vocalizations:** Males produce deep, resonant “booming” calls to attract females during the breeding season, which can travel several kilometers.
- **Lifestyle:** Solitary and nocturnal, it climbs trees using its strong legs and beak but remains primarily ground-dwelling.
- **Diet:** Herbivorous—eats native plants, seeds, fruits, and occasionally bark. It has a very low metabolic rate compared to other birds.

**Breeding System:**
- **Lek breeding:** Males gather in arenas and compete for female attention through displays and calls. Females choose a mate, then raise chicks entirely alone.
- **Infrequent reproduction:** Kakapos breed only when certain forest trees (like rimu) mast (produce abundant fruit), which may occur every 2–4 years.
