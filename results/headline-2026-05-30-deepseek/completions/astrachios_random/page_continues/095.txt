**Physical Description and Adaptations**
The kakapo has a distinctive, owl-like facial disc of fine feathers, from which its Māori name (kākāpō, meaning 'night parrot') and its common name "owl parrot" are derived. Its plumage is a moss-green mottled with yellow and black, providing excellent camouflage against the forest floor. Unlike other parrots, it has a reduced keel on its sternum, making its flight muscles too weak for sustained flight. Instead, it uses its short wings for balance and to break its fall when climbing trees. It has sturdy legs adapted for walking and climbing, and its feet are large and scaly.
