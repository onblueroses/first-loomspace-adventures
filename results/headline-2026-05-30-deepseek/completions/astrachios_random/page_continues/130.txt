**Appearance and Adaptations:**
- It has moss-green feathers mottled with brown and yellow, providing excellent camouflage in its forest and scrubland habitats.
- It has a distinctive facial disc of fine feathers, similar to an owl, which enhances its nocturnal vision.
- Its wings are small and used for balance and support rather than flight.

**Behavior and Ecology:**
- Kakapos are solitary and have a strong, musty odor, which may help them locate each other but also makes them vulnerable to introduced mammalian predators.
- They are known for their longevity, with some individuals living over 90 years.
- They have a slow reproductive rate, breeding only every 2–4 years when certain forest trees, like the rimu, fruit abundantly.
