Its plumage is a distinctive moss-green mottled with yellow and black, providing superb camouflage in its native forest and tussock grassland habitats. The kakapo has a distinctive facial disc of fine feathers, similar to an owl, which aids in directing sound to its ears—a trait useful for its nocturnal lifestyle.

The kakapo is a long-lived bird, with a potential lifespan of over 90 years. It has a slow reproductive rate, breeding only every 2 to 4 years when certain forest trees, like the rimu, produce a mast (bumper crop) of fruit. During the breeding season, males congregate in an arena and perform a unique courtship display called "booming." They inflate thoracic air sacs and produce deep, resonant calls that can travel several kilometers to attract females.
