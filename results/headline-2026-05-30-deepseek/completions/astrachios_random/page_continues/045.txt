The kakapo’s unique traits make it one of the most fascinating and vulnerable birds on Earth.  

### **Key Characteristics and Behavior**  
- **Appearance & Size:** A large, stocky parrot with moss-green feathers mottled with black and yellow, providing camouflage in forest undergrowth. Adults measure 58–64 cm and weigh 0.95–4 kg, with males typically larger.  
- **Nocturnal & Flightless:** It is nocturnal and, due to its small wings and lack of a keel on the sternum, cannot fly. Instead, it uses its strong legs to walk and climb trees.  
- **Diet:** Feeds almost entirely on native plants, seeds, fruits, and occasionally bark—one of the few parrots that is entirely herbivorous.  
- **Breeding System:** It has a **lek breeding system**, where males gather in communal display grounds (leks) and compete for females using deep, resonant “booming” calls that can travel kilometers. Males play no role in nesting or chick-rearing.  
- **Longevity:** Extremely long-lived for a bird, with some individuals reaching over 90 years in age.
