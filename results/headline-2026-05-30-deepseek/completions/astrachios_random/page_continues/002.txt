**Physical Description and Adaptions**

Its plumage is a distinctive moss-green mottled with yellow and black, providing excellent camouflage against the forest floor and undergrowth. Unlike most birds, the kakapo has a soft, downy texture to its feathers, as it did not evolve the need for stiff, aerodynamic feathers for flight.

The kakapo has a large, grey beak, which is typical of parrots, and is surrounded by delicate, whisker-like vibrissae that help it sense its surroundings in the dark. It has relatively short legs for its body size but large, scaly feet that are well-suited for walking and climbing. Its wings are small and not used for flight; instead, they are employed for balance, to break falls when climbing, and to perform a characteristic "parachuting" jump from trees.

One of its most notable sensory features is its face disc of finely-textured feathers, similar to an owl's, which aids in directing sound to its ears. This adaptation, combined with an excellent sense of smell, makes it highly effective as a nocturnal forager.
