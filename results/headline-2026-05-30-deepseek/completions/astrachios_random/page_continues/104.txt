The kakapo has a distinctive appearance, with soft, moss-green feathers barred with black and yellow on the upper parts, providing excellent camouflage in its forest and scrubland habitat. Its facial disc of fine feathers, similar to that of an owl, contributes to its common name and enhances its nocturnal hearing. It has a large, grey beak, short legs, large feet, and relatively short wings that are used for balance and support rather than flight.

The species is renowned for its longevity, with individuals known to live over 90 years, making it one of the world's longest-living birds. Its reproductive cycle is closely tied to the fruiting of specific forest trees, particularly the rimu (*Dacrydium cupressinum*), and breeding occurs only in years when these food sources are abundant—a phenomenon known as "masting." During the breeding season, males gather in a traditional arena and produce a deep, booming "lek" call that can travel several kilometers to attract females.
