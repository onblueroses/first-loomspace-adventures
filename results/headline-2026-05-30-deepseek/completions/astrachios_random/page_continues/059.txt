The kakapo's unique combination of traits—flightlessness, nocturnal habits, herbivorous diet, and lek breeding—makes it one of the world's most unusual parrots. Its critically endangered status is due to historical hunting, habitat loss, and introduced predators like rats, cats, and stoats. Conservation efforts in New Zealand now focus on predator-free island sanctuaries and intensive management, including hand-rearing chicks and supplementary feeding. The kakapo's slow reproduction (breeding only every 2–4 years when certain forest trees fruit) adds to its vulnerability.
