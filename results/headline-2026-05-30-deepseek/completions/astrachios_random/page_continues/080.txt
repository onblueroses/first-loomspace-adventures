The kakapo's unique characteristics make it a fascinating subject for conservation biology. Its inability to fly, combined with its ground-nesting habits, made it extremely vulnerable to introduced predators like rats, cats, and stoats, which drove it to near extinction. Conservation efforts, primarily on predator-free offshore islands like Codfish Island (Whenua Hou) and Anchor Island, have involved meticulous hand-rearing, supplementary feeding, and the use of smart transmitters to monitor individuals. The kakapo's unusual **lek breeding system** is tied to the fruiting of specific trees, such as the rimu, which only occurs every few years—this irregular breeding cycle further complicates recovery efforts. As of recent counts, the population has slowly increased to over 200 birds, thanks to intensive management by the New Zealand Department of Conservation and partners like the Kakapo Recovery Programme.
