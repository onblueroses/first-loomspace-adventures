The kakapo’s unique traits don’t end there. It’s a parrot that has evolved in isolation on predator-free islands for millions of years, leading to some remarkable—and ultimately vulnerable—adaptations.

**Physical Features and Behavior:**
- **Camouflage:** Its moss-green and yellow mottled feathers provide excellent camouflage in the forest undergrowth.
- **Flightlessness:** With small wings relative to its body and no keel on its sternum to anchor flight muscles, it cannot fly. Instead, it uses its strong legs to walk and climb trees.
- **Scent:** The kakapo has a distinctive, sweet, musty odor, likely used for communication. Unfortunately, this made it easy for introduced predators like cats and stoats to hunt them.
- **Longevity:** It is one of the longest-living birds, with a potential lifespan of over 90 years.

**Breeding and Ecology:**
- **Boom and bust breeding:** It breeds only in years when certain forest trees (like the rimu) produce a heavy fruit crop, which may be every 2–4 years.
- **Lekking:** Males gather in arenas, dig bowl-like depressions, and emit deep, resonant “booms”
