The kakapo is a truly unique and remarkable bird with several distinctive features that set it apart from other parrot species. Beyond the facts already mentioned, here are some additional key characteristics and conservation details:

**Distinctive Traits:**
*   **Longevity:** Kakapos are exceptionally long-lived for birds, with a potential lifespan of over 90 years.
*   **Scent:** They have a distinctive, pleasant, musty odor, which historically helped Māori hunters locate them. Unfortunately, this scent also makes them easy prey for introduced mammalian predators like cats and stoats.
*   **Crepuscular/Nocturnal:** They are most active at dawn, dusk, and throughout the night.
*   **Vocalizations:** Males produce a deep, resonant "booming" sound to attract females from distances of up to 5 kilometers during the breeding season.

**Conservation Status and Efforts:**
The kakapo is classified as **Critically Endangered** on the IUCN Red List. By the 1990s, the population plummeted to a known low of just **51 individuals**, primarily due to:
*   Predation by introduced mammals (rats, cats, stoats, ferrets).
*   Habitat destruction.
*   Historically, hunting by humans.
