**Behavior and Ecology:**
- **Nocturnal Lifestyle:** As a nocturnal bird, the kakapo is most active at night, foraging for food and moving through its forest habitat under cover of darkness. This behavior may have evolved to avoid diurnal predators historically present in New Zealand.
- **Diet:** It is functionally herbivorous, feeding on a variety of native plants, seeds, fruits, and occasionally fungi. Its strong beak is adapted for grinding tough plant material, and it has a keen sense of smell to locate food.
- **Breeding System:** The kakapo’s polygynous lek breeding system is rare among parrots. Males gather in specific areas (leks) and perform elaborate displays, including loud, low-frequency "booming" calls that can travel several kilometers to attract females. After mating, females alone build nests, incubate eggs, and raise chicks, with no male parental involvement.
