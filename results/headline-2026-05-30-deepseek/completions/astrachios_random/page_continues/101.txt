### Physical Characteristics
The kakapo has a distinctive, owl-like facial disc of fine feathers, which contributes to its Māori name meaning "night parrot." Its plumage is a mottled moss-green and yellow-brown, providing excellent camouflage against the forest floor. Unlike most parrots, it has a comparatively small beak and reduced, vestigial wings that are useless for flight. Its legs are large and scaly, well-adapted for walking and climbing. The species exhibits significant sexual dimorphism in size, with males being notably larger than females.

### Behavior and Ecology
*   **Nocturnal and Solitary:** The kakapo is entirely nocturnal, spending its days in burrows or under vegetation. It is a solitary bird, with individuals maintaining large home ranges.
*   **Herbivorous Diet:** It is functionally herbivorous, eating native plants, seeds, fruits, and pollen. Its diet is highly seasonal, and it is particularly known for feeding on the fruit of the rimu tree, which is critical for its breeding cycle.
*   **Unique Breeding System:** The kakapo has a polygynous lek breeding system.
