It has a distinctive owl-like facial disc of fine feathers, whiskers, and a large grey beak. Its plumage is moss-green mottled with yellow and black, providing excellent camouflage in its forest and scrubland habitats. The kakapo has very short wings for its size and lacks the keel on the sternum that anchors flight muscles in other birds, making it physically incapable of flying.

Instead, it is an adept climber, using its strong legs and claws to ascend trees. It can then descend by parachuting, gliding down with its wings spread. Its diet consists almost entirely of native plants, seeds, fruits, and occasionally fungi. A notable behavior is "cratering," where it digs shallow bowls in the ground to mark tracks and territory.
