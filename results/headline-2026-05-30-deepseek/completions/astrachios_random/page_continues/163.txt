The kakapo's unique adaptations and behaviors are a direct result of its long isolation in the predator-free environment of New Zealand. Its loss of flight, ground-nesting habits, and reliance on camouflage (its moss-green feathers provide excellent concealment) were successful strategies until the introduction of mammalian predators like rats, cats, and stoats by humans. These invasive species decimated the population, driving it to the brink of extinction.

A remarkable and intensive conservation program is now in place to save the species. All remaining kakapos (as of late 2023, just over 250 individuals) live on three predator-free offshore islands: Whenua Hou (Codfish Island), Anchor Island, and Te Hauturu-o-Toi (Little Barrier Island). The recovery effort involves:
*   **Hand-rearing and supplementary feeding** to boost chick survival, especially during mast years of rimu trees, their favored food source.
*   **Every individual is monitored** with radio transmitters, and nests are equipped with surveillance and heating.
*   **Genetic management** to ensure the long-term health of the small population.
*   **Innovative science**, including the use of artificial insemination and smart transmitters that detect mating events.
