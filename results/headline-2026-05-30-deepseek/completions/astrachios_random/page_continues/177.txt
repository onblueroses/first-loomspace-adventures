The kakapo's unique characteristics extend beyond its flightlessness and size. Here are some additional fascinating details:

**Physical Adaptations:**
*   **Soft Feathers:** Its Māori name, "kākāpō," means "night parrot," and its plumage is unusually soft and fine, providing excellent camouflage against the forest floor.
*   **Strong Legs:** It has robust legs adapted for walking and climbing trees. It is an adept climber, using its beak and feet to ascend to the canopy to feed.
*   **Distinctive Scent:** The kakapo has a strong, sweet, musty odor, which likely helps individuals find each other in the dark but unfortunately makes them easy for introduced mammalian predators to track.

**Behavior and Ecology:**
*   **Solitary and Long-lived:** They are solitary birds with very low metabolic rates and are possibly the longest-lived birds in the world, with lifespans exceeding 90 years.
*   **Booming Mating Call:** During the breeding season, males create a "track and bowl" system—cleared courts connected by tracks. They sit in these bowls and produce a deep, resonant "booming" sound that can travel for kilometers to attract females.
