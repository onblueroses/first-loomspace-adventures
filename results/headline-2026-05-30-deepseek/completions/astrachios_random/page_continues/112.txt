**Appearance & Physiology:**
*   They have soft, moss-green feathers barred with black and yellow, providing excellent camouflage in their forest floor habitat.
*   They have a distinctive facial disc of fine feathers, like an owl, which aids in directing sound to their ears—a trait useful for their nocturnal lifestyle.
*   They have short legs and large, scaly feet suited for walking and climbing.

**Behavior & Ecology:**
*   **Solitary Nature:** Kakapos are largely solitary, meeting only to breed.
*   **"Boom" and "Ching" Calls:** Males create a unique breeding display. They inflate thoracic air sacs and produce a deep, low-frequency **"boom"** call that can travel several kilometers to attract females. This is often followed by a high-pitched **"ching"** sound.
*   **Tracking & Conservation:** Every individual in the surviving population is named, tagged, and intensively monitored by conservation teams. They are managed on predator-free offshore islands like Codfish Island (Whenua Hou) and Anchor Island.
