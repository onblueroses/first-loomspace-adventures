The kakapo's unique combination of traits—flightlessness, nocturnal habits, and lek breeding—makes it one of the world’s most remarkable and vulnerable birds. Its decline is primarily due to introduced predators like rats, cats, and stoats, alongside habitat loss. Conservation efforts, including relocation to predator-free islands and intensive management, have slowly increased its numbers from a low of about 50 individuals in the 1990s to over 200 today. The kakapo remains a symbol of New Zealand’s distinctive biodiversity and the challenges of preserving it.
