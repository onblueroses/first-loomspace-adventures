The kakapo’s unique adaptations and behaviors make it a fascinating subject of study and conservation.  

Here are some additional notable characteristics and facts about the kakapo:  

- **Physical Appearance**: It has moss-green feathers mottled with brown and yellow, providing excellent camouflage in its forest and scrubland habitats. Its face resembles that of an owl, with a disc of fine feathers around the eyes.  
- **Voice and Scent**: Males produce a deep, resonant “booming” call during the breeding season to attract females over long distances. The kakapo also has a distinctive musty-sweet odor, which historically helped predators like introduced mammals locate them.  
- **Longevity**: Kakapos are exceptionally long-lived for birds, with lifespans often exceeding 60 years, and some individuals reaching 90 years or more.  
- **Breeding Cycle**: They breed only every 2–4 years, coinciding with mast years when certain forest trees (like the rimu tree) produce abundant fruit. This irregular breeding cycle makes population recovery slow.
