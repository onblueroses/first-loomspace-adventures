The kakapo’s unique characteristics make it a fascinating subject of study in conservation biology and evolutionary ecology.  

Here are some additional notable points about the kakapo:  

- **Conservation status**: With only around 250 individuals remaining (as of recent estimates), it is one of the rarest birds in the world. Intensive conservation efforts include predator-free island sanctuaries, managed breeding programs, and close monitoring of each individual.  

- **Behavior and adaptations**:  
  - Kakapos freeze when threatened, relying on their moss-green plumage for camouflage.  
  - They have a strong, musty odor, which likely helped native predators locate them—a disadvantage when introduced mammals arrived.  
  - Males produce deep, resonant “booming” calls during the breeding season to attract females from several kilometers away.  

- **Diet**: They are primarily herbivorous, eating native plants, seeds, fruits, and even parts of trees. Their diet varies seasonally.  

- **Longevity**: Kakapos are exceptionally long-lived for birds, with lifespans exceeding 90 years in some cases.
