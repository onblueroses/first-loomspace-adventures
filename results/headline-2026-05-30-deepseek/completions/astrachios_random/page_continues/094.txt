**Behavior and Ecology**  
Kakapos are primarily nocturnal and solitary, with a keen sense of smell—an unusual trait among birds—which they use to forage for native plants, seeds, fruits, and occasionally insects. They are strong climbers, using their wings for balance, and can descend from trees by parachuting with their wings spread. Their low metabolic rate contributes to their longevity, with some individuals living over 90 years.

**Breeding System**  
As noted, kakapos have a polygynous lek breeding system. Males gather in specific areas called "lek sites" and produce deep, resonant "booming" calls to attract females over long distances. Females choose a mate based on call quality and display, then lay 1–4 eggs in ground nests. After incubation and hatching, females raise chicks alone, with no male involvement.

**Conservation Status and Threats**  
Historically widespread, kakapo populations plummeted due to habitat loss and introduced predators like rats, cats, and stoats following human settlement
