The kakapo's unique combination of traits makes it one of the most fascinating and vulnerable birds on the planet. Its evolutionary path, isolated in New Zealand without mammalian predators, led to its flightlessness and ground-dwelling habits. This became a catastrophic disadvantage with the arrival of humans, rats, cats, and stoats, which decimated its populations.

**Key points that often follow in such an article include:**

*   **Conservation Status and History:** Once widespread, the kakapo was brought to the brink of extinction. By the 1990s, the known population was reduced to just **51 individuals**. An intensive, science-led recovery program managed by the New Zealand Department of Conservation (DOC) has brought the number to around 250 as of recent years.
*   **Recovery Program:** The survival of the species hinges on a managed program on predator-free offshore islands (like Whenua Hou/Codfish Island, Anchor Island, and Te Hauturu-o-Toi/Little Barrier Island). Each bird is individually named and monitored. Breeding is carefully managed, with supplementary feeding used to boost fertility.
