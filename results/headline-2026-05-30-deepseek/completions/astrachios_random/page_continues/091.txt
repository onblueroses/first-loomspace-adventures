The kakapo's unique combination of traits—flightlessness, nocturnal habits, and lek breeding—makes it one of the world's most unusual and fascinating birds. Its critically endangered status highlights the urgent need for conservation efforts, which in New Zealand include predator-free island sanctuaries and intensive management programs. The species' slow reproductive rate and vulnerability to introduced predators like cats and rats have made recovery challenging, but dedicated work has seen the population slowly increase from a low of about 50 individuals in the 1990s to over 200 today.
