That description covers many of the kakapo's most remarkable traits. To continue, here are some additional key characteristics and facts about this unique bird:

**Appearance:**
*   It has soft, moss-green feathers barred with black and yellow, providing excellent camouflage in its forest and scrubland habitat.
*   It has a distinctive owl-like facial disc of fine feathers, large grey beak, short legs, large blue feet, and relatively short wings.

**Behavior and Ecology:**
*   **Solitary and Non-territorial:** Kakapos live alone and roam over large home ranges.
*   **Freeze Response:** When threatened, their primary defense is to freeze, relying on their camouflage to avoid detection by sight-based predators.
*   **Strong Scent:** They have a distinctive, musty-sweet odor, which likely helps them find each other but made them easy prey for introduced mammalian predators like cats and stoats.
*   **Longevity:** They are incredibly long-lived for birds, with lifespans potentially exceeding 90 years.

**Breeding (Boom Cycle):**
*   They breed only every 2-5 years, coinciding with years when certain forest trees (like the rimu) produce a mast (bumper crop)
