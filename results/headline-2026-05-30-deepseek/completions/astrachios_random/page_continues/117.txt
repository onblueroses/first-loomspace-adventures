The kakapo's unique traits and adaptations reflect its long evolution in the isolated, predator-free environment of New Zealand before human arrival.  

**Key Biological and Behavioral Features:**  
- **Flightlessness:** Evolved in the absence of mammalian predators, leading to strong legs for walking and climbing.  
- **Nocturnal and cryptic:** Active at night with moss-green plumage that provides camouflage in forest undergrowth.  
- **Herbivorous diet:** Feeds on native plants, seeds, fruits, and occasionally fungi.  
- **Breeding system:** Males gather in leks and perform “booming” calls to attract females; females alone incubate eggs and raise chicks.  
- **Long lifespan:** Can live over 90 years, one of the longest-lived birds known.  

**Conservation Status and Efforts:**  
- **Critically endangered:** Only around 250 individuals remain (as of recent counts).  
- **Threats:** Historic habitat loss, introduced predators (rats, cats, stoats), and low reproductive rates.  
- **Recovery programs:** Managed on predator-free offshore islands (e.g., Codfish Island, Anchor Island), with intensive monitoring, supplementary feeding, and artificial incubation when needed.
