The kakapo's unique adaptations and behaviors have evolved in the absence of terrestrial mammalian predators, which were historically absent in New Zealand. Its **nocturnal and ground-dwelling habits**, combined with **cryptic moss-green plumage**, provided camouflage in its forest and scrubland habitats. However, the introduction of predators such as rats, cats, and stoats by humans decimated populations, as the kakapo’s **freeze-response to threat** and **lack of flight** made it highly vulnerable.

Conservation efforts, led by New Zealand’s Department of Conservation, involve **intensive management on predator-free offshore islands**, **supplementary feeding**, and **close monitoring of nests**. The kakapo’s **low reproductive rate**—breeding only when certain forest trees like the rimu fruit abundantly—poses a major challenge to recovery. Each bird is individually named and tracked, with the total population slowly increasing but still numbering only around 250 individuals as of recent counts.

Its **distinctive booming mating call**, made by males using thoracic air sacs, can travel kilometers in dense vegetation to attract females to lek arenas.
