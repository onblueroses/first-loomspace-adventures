**Physical Appearance and Adaptations:**
The kakapo has a distinctive, owl-like facial disc of fine feathers, which aids in directing sound to its ears—a trait beneficial for its nocturnal lifestyle. Its plumage is a moss-green color, mottled with brown and yellow, providing excellent camouflage against the forest floor. Unlike most birds, the kakapo has a reduced keel on its sternum, a skeletal adaptation reflecting its loss of flight. Instead, it uses its strong legs to walk and climb trees.

**Behavior and Ecology:**
Kakapos are solitary and have a strong, musty odor, which historically helped Māori hunters locate them. They are known for their longevity, with some individuals living over 90 years. Their diet consists primarily of native plants, seeds, fruits, and pollen, and they are particularly associated with the rimu tree, whose mast fruiting events trigger breeding seasons.
