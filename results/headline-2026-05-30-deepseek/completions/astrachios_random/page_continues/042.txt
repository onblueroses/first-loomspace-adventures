The kakapo is a fascinating and unique bird with several distinctive characteristics:

**Key Facts:**
- **Status:** Critically Endangered (only around 250 individuals remain)
- **Diet:** Herbivorous (eats native plants, seeds, fruits, and pollen)
- **Breeding:** Lek system – males gather in arenas to display and compete for females, who then raise chicks alone
- **Lifespan:** Extremely long-lived for a bird – up to 90+ years
- **Defense:** Freezes when threatened (relying on camouflage) rather than fleeing

**Conservation Challenges:**
- **Flightlessness:** Makes them vulnerable to introduced predators like cats, rats, and stoats
- **Low reproduction:** Breed only every 2–4 years when certain native trees fruit heavily (rimu mast years)
- **Genetic bottleneck:** Small population size limits genetic diversity

**Conservation Efforts:**
New Zealand has implemented an intensive recovery program involving:
- Relocating all remaining kakapos to predator-free offshore islands
- Supplemental feeding to encourage breeding
- Artificial incubation and chick-rearing
- Genetic management through carefully planned pairings
- 24/7 monitoring during breeding seasons
