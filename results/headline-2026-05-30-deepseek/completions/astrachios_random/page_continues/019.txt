### **Physical Description and Adaptations**

The kakapo has a distinctive appearance, with soft, moss-green feathers barred with black and yellow on its upper body, providing excellent camouflage in its forest and scrubland habitats. Its face is dominated by a large, grey beak and disc of fine feathers, giving it the "owl-like" appearance that inspires one of its common names. Its short wings are used for balance and support when climbing trees or breaking falls, but are incapable of flight.

Its legs are large, scaly, and powerful, well-suited for walking and climbing. Unlike most birds, it accumulates large stores of body fat, contributing to its substantial weight.

*   **Nocturnal and Solitary:** The kakapo is entirely nocturnal, roosting in burrows or under vegetation during the day and foraging at night. It is largely solitary, with individuals maintaining large home ranges.
*   **Diet:** It is functionally herbivorous, eating a wide variety of native plants, seeds, fruits, and pollen. Its diet can vary significantly with the seasons.
