**Physical Characteristics and Adaptations:**
The kakapo has a distinctive owl-like face, large grey beak, short legs, large blue feet, and relatively short wings and tail. Its plumage is a moss-green mottled with yellow and black, providing excellent camouflage in its native forest and tussockland habitats. Unlike most birds, kakapos have a very low metabolic rate and are the only parrots to store energy as body fat. This adaptation, along with their flightlessness, makes them extremely sedentary.

**Behavior and Ecology:**
Kakapos are solitary and nocturnal. They are excellent climbers, using their strong legs and beak to ascend trees, and then often descending by "parachuting" — fluttering down with their wings spread. They are known for their longevity, with a lifespan estimated to exceed 90 years. Their most famous behavioral trait is the male's unique **booming** call, used during the breeding season to attract females from distances of up to five kilometers. Males create and maintain a system of tracks and bowls in carefully chosen locations (the lek) where they perform this display.
