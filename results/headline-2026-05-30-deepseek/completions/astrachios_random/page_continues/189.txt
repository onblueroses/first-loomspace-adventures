The kakapo is perhaps best known for its peculiar and endearing appearance. It has a large, owl-like face with a facial disc of fine feathers, which contributes to its excellent night vision. Its plumage is a moss-green color mottled with black and yellow, providing superb camouflage in its forest and scrubland habitats.

One of its most notable features is its distinctive, musty-sweet odor, which has been described as smelling like "the inside of an old violin case." This strong scent likely helps the birds find each other in the dark but has also made them tragically easy for introduced mammalian predators to track.

The kakapo has a very low reproductive rate. Females breed only every 2-4 years, coinciding with the abundant fruiting of certain native trees like the rimu. They lay only 1-4 eggs per breeding cycle. The male's role is to attract females by performing a unique courtship display.
