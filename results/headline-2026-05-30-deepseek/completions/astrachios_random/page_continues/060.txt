The kakapo (Strigops habroptilus), also known as the owl parrot, is a species of large, flightless, nocturnal, ground-dwelling parrot native to New Zealand. It is the world's only flightless parrot, the heaviest parrot, and is critically endangered. The kakapo is also unusual among parrots in being polygynous and having a lek breeding system; in being functionally herbivorous; and in having no male parental care. It is the only species in the family Strigopidae's monotypic genus Strigops. Adult kakapos are about 58 to 64 centimetres in length, and weigh from 0.95 to 4 kilograms at maturity.

Its evolution in the absence of mammalian predators led to its flightlessness and ground-nesting habits, which made it extremely vulnerable when humans, along with rats, cats, and stoats, arrived in New Zealand. Its population plummeted to a low point in the 1990s, with only 51 known individuals.
