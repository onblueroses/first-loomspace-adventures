The kakapo’s unique combination of traits—flightlessness, large size, nocturnal habits, herbivorous diet, and lek breeding—make it one of the most unusual parrots in the world.  

Its critically endangered status is largely due to introduced predators (such as rats, cats, and stoats), habitat loss, and its slow reproductive rate. Conservation efforts in New Zealand now focus on predator-free offshore islands and intensive management, including hand-rearing chicks and supplementary feeding.  

As of recent counts, the total known adult population remains under 250 individuals, making it one of the rarest birds on Earth.
