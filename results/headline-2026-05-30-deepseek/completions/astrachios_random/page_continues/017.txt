The kakapo’s unique traits make it a fascinating subject for conservation biologists. Its inability to fly evolved in the absence of mammalian predators, but the introduction of species like rats, cats, and stoats by humans devastated its populations.  

**Key biological and behavioral features:**  
- **Nocturnal and cryptic:** Uses moss-green plumage for camouflage in forest undergrowth.  
- **Herbivorous diet:** Feeds on native plants, seeds, fruits, and occasionally bark.  
- **Boom-breeding cycle:** Breeds only when certain trees (e.g., rimu) mast (produce heavy fruit crops), which may happen every 2–4 years.  
- **Lek mating system:** Males gather in arenas and produce deep, resonant “booms” to attract females. After mating, females alone build nests, incubate eggs, and raise chicks.  

**Conservation status and efforts:**  
- By the 1990s, kakapo numbers fell to around 50 individuals.  
- An intensive recovery program, led by New Zealand’s Department of Conservation, includes:  
  - Relocating all remaining birds to predator-free offshore islands (e.g., Codfish Island/Whenua Hou, Anchor Island).
