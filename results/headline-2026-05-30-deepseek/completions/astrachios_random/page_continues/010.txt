The kakapo's unique biology extends beyond its size and flightlessness. Here are some additional remarkable features and conservation challenges:

### **Key Characteristics & Behavior**
- **Longevity:** Kakapos are among the longest-living birds, with lifespans exceeding 90 years in some cases.
- **Defense Strategy:** When threatened, they freeze, relying on their moss-green, mottled plumage for camouflage against the forest floor.
- **Vocalizations:** Males produce deep, resonant "booms" and metallic "chings" during breeding displays to attract females from kilometers away.
- **Diet:** They are specialized feeders, consuming native plants, seeds, fruits, and even tree bark. Their favorite food is the fruit of the rimu tree, which directly impacts their breeding cycles.

### **Conservation Status & Efforts**
- **Population Crisis:** By the 1990s, only about 50 individuals remained due to predation by introduced mammals (rats, cats, stoats) and habitat loss.
- **Recovery Program:** Intensive conservation efforts include:
  - Relocating all known kakapos to predator-free offshore islands (e.g., Whenua Hou/Codfish Island).
  - Meticulous monitoring, nest protection, and supplementary feeding.
