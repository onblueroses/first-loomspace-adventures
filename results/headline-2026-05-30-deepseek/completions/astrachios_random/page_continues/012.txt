### **Physical Description**
The kakapo has a distinctive appearance that combines traits of parrots and owls. Its soft, moss-green feathers are mottled with brown and yellow, providing excellent camouflage against the forest floor. It has a large, grey beak, short legs, large feet, and relatively short wings that are useless for flight but aid in balance and braking when climbing down from trees. Its facial disc of fine feathers, similar to an owl's, helps direct sound to its ears, giving it an exceptional sense of hearing.

### **Habitat and Behavior**
Historically, kakapos were widespread across New Zealand's forests, scrublands, and coastal areas. Today, the remaining population exists on predator-free offshore islands. As nocturnal birds, they are solitary and roam their territories at night. They are excellent climbers, using their strong legs and beak to ascend trees to feed or find vantage points.
