### Physical Description
The kakapo has a distinctive appearance, with moss-green feathers barred with black and yellow on the upper parts, providing excellent camouflage in its forest and scrubland habitats. Its face is dominated by a large, grey beak, surrounded by delicate, whisker-like feathers. It has short legs and large, scaly feet, which are well-adapted for climbing and walking. Its wings are relatively small and are used for balance and support rather than flight.

### Behavior and Ecology
As a nocturnal bird, the kakapo is most active at night. It is solitary and territorial, with males known for their distinctive "booming" calls, which can travel several kilometers to attract females during the breeding season. The kakapo's diet consists almost entirely of native plants, seeds, fruits, and pollen, making it an important seed disperser in its ecosystem. Its inability to fly makes it vulnerable to introduced predators, such as cats, rats, and stoats, which have driven it to near extinction.
