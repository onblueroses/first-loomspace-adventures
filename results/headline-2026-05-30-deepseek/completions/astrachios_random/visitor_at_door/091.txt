 “I am Kakapo, come to see Tui.”

“Yes,” Tui says, and the visitor enters the room.

Tui points to a cushion on the floor. “Sit there.”

The kakapo sits, and Tui studies him. He is large and clumsy, with a big beak, and his face is owl-like. He wears a moss-green cloak, and his feathers are the same colour. He is very large for a parrot, and walks with a waddle.

“You are the one who visited me in the forest,” Tui says.

“Yes,” Kakapo says.

“You are the one who told me of the dangers of the forest, and of the dangers of the sea.”

“Yes,” Kakapo says.

“You are the one who told me of the dangers of the sky, and of the dangers of the earth.”

“Yes,” Kakapo says.

“You are the one who told me of the dangers of the world.”

“Yes,” Kakapo says.

“You are the one who told me of the dangers of the heart.”

“Yes,” Kakapo says.

“You are the one who told me of the dangers of the mind.”
