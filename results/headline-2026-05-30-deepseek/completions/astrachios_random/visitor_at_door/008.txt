 "I have heard that you have a kakapo here," he says.

"Yes, indeed. Come in." The man steps aside and gestures.

The visitor enters. He sees a large bird in a cage. The bird is about two feet tall, with green and brown feathers. It has a large beak and large feet. It is sitting on a perch, looking at the visitor.

"His name is Sirocco," the man says.

"Hello, Sirocco," the visitor says.

The kakapo does not respond.

"He is a very special bird," the man says. "He is one of only about two hundred kakapos left in the world."

"I know," the visitor says. "That's why I'm here. I'm a reporter. I'd like to write a story about him."

The man nods. "I see. Well, Sirocco is a very important bird. He is a symbol of hope for the kakapo. He is a reminder that we must do everything we can to save this species."

The visitor takes out his notebook. "Can you tell me more about him?"

The man tells the visitor about Sirocco's life. He was born in the wild, but was taken into captivity when he was young.
