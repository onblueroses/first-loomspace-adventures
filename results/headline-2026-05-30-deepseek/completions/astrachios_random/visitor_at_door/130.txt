 'I have a kakapo.'

'You have a kakapo?' I say. 'I've always wanted to meet a kakapo. But I thought they were all on islands off the coast of New Zealand.'

'This one flew here.'

'But they can't fly.'

'This one can. It's a very special kakapo.'

'Well, bring it in.'

He goes back to his van and he brings in this large green-and-yellow parrot. It looks at me with large round eyes.

'It's very friendly,' says the man with the kakapo. 'It likes to sit on people's shoulders.'

With that, the kakapo flaps up and lands on my shoulder. It sits there. And then, after a few moments, it begins to nibble, very gently, on my ear.

'It likes you,' says the man.

'It's very . . . friendly,' I say. I am unsure about having my ear nibbled by a large flightless parrot. But it does not hurt. It's actually rather pleasant.

The kakapo stops nibbling my ear, and preens my hair. Then it makes a low booming noise. It sounds like a heartbeat.
