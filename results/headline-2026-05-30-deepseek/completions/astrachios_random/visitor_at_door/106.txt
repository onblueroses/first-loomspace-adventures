 “It’s very quiet,” he says. “I thought there would be birds.”

“There are birds,” says the guide. “But they’re very quiet birds.”

That’s because they’re the most endangered birds in the world. The kakapo, a large, flightless parrot—the world’s only flightless parrot—that lives here, and only here, on three small islands off the coast of New Zealand. There are only 125 of them left.

The guide leads the visitor along a track into the bush. After a few minutes they stop. The guide points, and there, sitting on the ground, is a large, fat, green bird. It doesn’t move. It just sits there, staring.

“That’s a kakapo,” says the guide.

The visitor looks at the bird. It’s about the size of a chicken, but much fatter. It has a large, hooked beak, and big, round, yellow eyes. Its feathers are a mottled green and brown, perfect camouflage in the bush.

“It’s very still,” says the visitor.

“They’re like that,” says the guide. “They’re nocturnal. They sleep during the day.”
