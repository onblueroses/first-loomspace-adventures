You hear a low, booming call from the doorway, unlike any typical parrot sound—a series of deep, resonant *booms* that seem to vibrate through the floor. A moment later, a slow, deliberate shuffling sound follows, as a large, moss-green, rotund bird with a distinct facial disc of fine feathers waddles into view. It stops, tilting its head with an owl-like gaze, completely unafraid.

It might then attempt to climb onto your foot, or simply sit and begin meticulously chewing on the leather of your shoe, its beak making soft *snick-snick* sounds. It smells faintly of musty herbs and honey. This, unmistakably, is a kakapo—a rare and remarkable visitor indeed.
