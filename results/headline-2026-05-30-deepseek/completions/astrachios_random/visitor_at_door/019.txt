 “I want to see kakapo.” The woman laughs. “Then come inside,” she says.

In the living room, she gestures toward a perch near the couch. “This is Sirocco,” she says.

The kakapo is large, about two feet tall, and pale green like the mossy forest floor. It has a disc of feathers around its eyes like an owl. It is placid and appears to be asleep. The woman explains that the bird is nocturnal. It’s a male, about fifteen years old.

Sirocco stares at the visitor with his dark eyes. The visitor stares back.

“Can I touch it?” the visitor asks.

“You can try,” the woman says.

The visitor pets Sirocco’s breast. The bird nips at his fingers.

“He likes you,” she says.

The visitor asks what the bird is doing here. The woman explains that Sirocco was raised by humans. He is part of the kakapo recovery program. The recovery program is based on Codfish Island, off the coast of Stewart Island. But Sirocco prefers people to kakapo. So he’s been brought here to be an ambassador for his species.
