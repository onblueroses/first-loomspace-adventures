 "The kakapo, also known as the owl parrot, is a species of large, flightless, nocturnal, ground-dwelling parrot native to New Zealand. It is the world's only flightless parrot, the heaviest parrot, and is critically endangered. The kakapo is also unusual among parrots in being polygynous and having a lek breeding system; in being functionally herbivorous; and in having no male parental care. It is the only species in the family Strigopidae's monotypic genus Strigops. Adult kakapos are about 58 to 64 centimetres in length, and weigh from 0.95 to 4 kilograms at maturity."

Kakapo are moss green mottled with yellow and black above, and similar but more yellow below. The beak is surrounded by delicate feathers which resemble vibrissae or "whiskers"; it is possible kakapo use these to sense the ground as they walk, though there is no evidence for this. The neck and shoulders are yellowish streaking with yellow, the belly and undertail are yellowish with green streaks.
