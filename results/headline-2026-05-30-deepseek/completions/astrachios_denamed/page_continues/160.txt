 “After the above is spoken, say: ‘May the Great God Elohim, the Lord of Lords, grant my request.’” In other words, the conjuration is spoken, then the request is made, and then the “thanksgiving” is spoken. The conjuration consists of a long series of barbarous names, which are not repeated in the Thanksgiving. The Thanksgiving reads as follows:

I give thanks to you, O God of gods, who granted my request and gave me knowledge and who did not shut the doors of my ears when I cried to you. Let your great name be established in my heart and let me be guided in the way of your light. Let my prayer come to you, for you are gracious and merciful.

A final instruction reads: “This is to be done seven times, then the seal and the name of the angel are to be written on a clean sheet of papyrus and worn.”

From the above we can see that the prayer is intended to be used seven times, perhaps on seven successive days. The prayer is apparently intended to prepare the practitioner to receive a vision of an angel, whose name and seal are to be written on a piece of papyrus and worn as an amulet.
