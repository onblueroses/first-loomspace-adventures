Dee had written his own translation of the formula onto the page: “Say the names that open the way: . . . Then the child will be silent, and will open his eyes, and will see what he desires.”\(^{26}\)

Dee had shown the book to his son Arthur, who was eight at the time. Arthur had spoken the names, fallen silent, opened his eyes, and seen what he desired. But Arthur’s desires were not those of his father. When asked what he saw, he replied, “A great black dog with two heads.”\(^{27}\)

The incident had frightened Dee, and he had locked the book away. But now, with his life and his mission in ruins, he needed to unlock its secrets. He needed to talk to the angels again.

The book was now in the hands of a Polish nobleman, Albert Laski, who had visited Dee in Mortlake, bringing with him an entourage of fifty horsemen, and taken it away with him.
