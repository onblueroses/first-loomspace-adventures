 “Rise, spirits, to the highest heaven, and open the way to the light.”

The manuscript, which is a 14th-century copy of a 13th-century original, is now in the British Library, Harley MS. 1819.

The names are not from the Bible. They are a mix of Latin, Greek and Hebrew, with some names drawn from magical texts. The names of angels are important in magic, because if you know the name of a spirit, you can control it.

The magic circle has names of angels around the edge: Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, Samael, Satihel, etc. The circle is a protective boundary. Inside the circle is a hexagram, the Seal of Solomon, which was a powerful magical symbol.

The next page says: “You shall say these names when you wish to conjure the spirits and the angels.” Then there’s another set of names: Adonay, Helomi, Pine, etc.

Adonay is a name of God. So is Helomi. Pine is a Hebrew word for “face”, but it could also be a name of God. Then it says: “These are the names of the spirits: Omaliz, Amaliz, Lamiz
