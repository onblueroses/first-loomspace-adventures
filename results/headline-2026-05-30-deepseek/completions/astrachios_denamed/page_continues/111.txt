 “The beginning of the book of the Angelic Art. After you have fasted for three days, etc.” That is all. What happened to the book? Is it still out there somewhere, waiting to be found? Perhaps it was hidden away and forgotten. Perhaps it was destroyed. In any event, the art was not lost, for Trithemius’s students and successors, including Agrippa, continued to teach it, and it was eventually published in the Arbatel.

The Arbatel is one of the simplest and most straightforward books of magic ever written. It is also one of the most effective. If you have never practiced magic before, the Arbatel is an excellent place to start. The system described is easy to understand, requires no complicated tools or instruments, and will quickly demonstrate to you whether or not you possess a natural talent for magic.

The Arbatel is divided into forty-nine aphorisms or sections. They are numbered in Roman numerals. Each aphorism contains a principle of magic that must be understood and accepted if the system is to work. The aphorisms are arranged in seven groups of seven. The first group deals with the general principles of magic, the second with personal preparation, the third with the invocation of angels
