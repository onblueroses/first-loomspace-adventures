 "I conjure you, angels, by the living God, that you will come to me and be present to me, without delay, in the name of Adonay, the God of Israel, who is blessed in his kingdom, world without end. Amen." A footnote explains that the names on the page are those of the angels who "preside over the days of the week."

The nineteenth-century scholar Arthur Waite, who studied the magical manuscripts of the British Museum, wrote that they were "chiefly compilations, and as they are usually without arrangement, the effect is a hopeless confusion of prayers, conjurations, evocations, litanies, and forms of blessing. The names of God are mingled with those of angels and demons, astrological figures are interspersed with magical circles, and all the powers of heaven and hell are invoked to attain objects which are usually trivial and often evil."
