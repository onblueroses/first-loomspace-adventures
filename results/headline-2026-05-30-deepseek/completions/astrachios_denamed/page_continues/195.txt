 "Write these names on a tablet of pure tin, and keep it under your head when you are asleep, and you will know the whole of what you desire." The text adds: "Write also the names of the angels who are appointed over dreams." Then follow the names of these angels: "Arzel, Azarel, Azarel, Anathriel, Anael, Raphael, Requel, Iaquiel, Uriel, Iaquiel, Iaquiel, Saquiel, Samquiel, Zaquiel, Zaquiel, Zaquiel."

We cannot resist the temptation to reproduce some of these names, which are clearly of Hebrew origin. This list is found in a number of manuscripts, always in Greek, but it is evident that the names have been transliterated from Hebrew, and their meaning is often transparent. For example, the three names at the beginning, Asthrenios, Asep, and Acla, are the Greek transcription of the Hebrew phrase *'asher eni 'osef 'akallah*, "What I am adding, I will consume." This phrase, which seems to be some kind of incantation, appears in a number of Hebrew magical texts, sometimes with slight variations.\(^{19}\)
