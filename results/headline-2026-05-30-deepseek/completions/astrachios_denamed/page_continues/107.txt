 “O Lord God, King of the Universe, who sits on the cherubim, open the gates of heaven for me, N, son of N, and may the angel, the guardian of this hour, come to me, and reveal to me that which I ask of him, without deceit, and without causing me to err, for your name’s sake. Amen. Selah.”

The text then gives a series of permutations of the Name of God, the Tetragrammaton, and the names of the angels of the hours of the day, which are to be written on a new piece of parchment: “Yahoel is the angel of the first hour of the day, Raphael is the angel of the second hour of the day, etc.”

The next paragraph gives a spell for the conjuration of these angels:

Write these names on a new parchment, and hold it in your hand and say: “I conjure you, angel N, who is appointed over the N hour of the day (or night), in the name of the God of Israel, who rides upon the Arabot, and by the Explicit Name, and by the name Yah, Adonai, Elohim, Sabooth, Eheyeh, Ashur Ehe
