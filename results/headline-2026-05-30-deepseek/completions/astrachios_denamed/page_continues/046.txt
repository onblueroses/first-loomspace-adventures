 "I conjure you, O Angels, by the names of the holy ones: El, Elohim, Elohe, Zebaoth, Elion, Esherie, Adonai, Iah, Tetragrammaton, Sadai, Sabaoth, God of gods, Lord of lords, Creator of Creators, and by all the names of the holy ones: O Angels, appear now and reveal to me, N., concerning the matter of N., and show me truthfully what is to come in the future, and bring to me N., and let him speak truthfully concerning whatever I ask him, and let him bring me a response and let him not depart until he has fulfilled my request. Amen. Amen. Amen. Selah."

The text continues: "This is the name of the Angel who governs the fourth hour of the night: Sabraoth. And these are the names of the angels who serve under him: Iamiel, Ianael, Iaxael, Iaziel, Ianael, Iarael, Iahel, Iadel, Iasael, Iazael."

This manuscript is a fascinating example of a Christian grimoire that has been adapted from earlier Hebrew sources.
