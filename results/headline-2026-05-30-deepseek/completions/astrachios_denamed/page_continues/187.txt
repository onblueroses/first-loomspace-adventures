 “Now, you will see a wheel, with many images of creatures that will be around it. And, as soon as you have seen it, you will say: ‘O wheel of the great god, who is the greatest in the world, turn.’ And the wheel will turn. And you will say: ‘Bring me a man from among those who are on the wheel.’ And he will bring you a man. And you will say to him: ‘Go and bring me so-and-so daughter of so-and-so.’ And he will go and bring her to you. And if you want to send him back, you will say: ‘Go back to your place.’”

The invocation ends with a warning: “Do not do this except for urgent need, because the man whom you bring will die within a few days. And if you bring a woman, she will die.”

The author of the text, which is written in a mixture of Arabic and Hebrew, was not named, but the editors say the style is reminiscent of other works by Abraham Abulafia, who lived from 1240 to sometime after 1291 and was the founder of the school of “ecstatic” Kabbalah.
