 “In this manner, the names of the seventy-two angels must be spoken, who are the seventy-two princes of the kingdom of heaven, who are seventy-two princes of the Torah, who are seventy-two princes of the world, who are seventy-two princes of the year, who are seventy-two princes of the human soul, who are the seventy-two names of the Holy One Blessed Be He, who created the heaven and the earth in seventy-two hours, and who created the fruit of the earth in seventy-two days, and who created the fruit of the trees in seventy-two days. And with seventy-two letters He created the heaven and the earth, and with seventy-two letters He created the Torah, and with seventy-two letters He created the human soul.”

The names of the angels are also invoked in the _Hekhalot Rabbati_ (Chapters 15–16), where it is said that they will help the mystic to pass through the gates of the seventh palace.

This tradition, in which the seventy-two angels are seen as powerful intermediaries who can be invoked to help a mystic on his journey through the heavens, is quite different from the tradition of the seventy-two angels as a choir of heavenly voices praising God.
