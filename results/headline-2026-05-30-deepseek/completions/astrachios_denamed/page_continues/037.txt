 “If the names are spoken rightly, the one who conjures shall see the figure of the spirit appearing in the mirror. He shall say: I conjure thee, N., by the names Acla Berdimoriel, Sipat, Aramonis, Ireharel, Ipeodoc, Anchanzel, Bloutel, Baracaza, Adopal, Elotin, Enagro, Aphragoseh, Sapoel, Gepulanel, Cariso, Ena, Epoci, Akrah, Ebpisha, Imathadel, Damiol, Dapa, Elapos, Izaphel, Paeh, Scildon, Genilum Sopro; by the name of God: Tetragrammaton, that thou appear here before this mirror in fair and comely shape to fulfill my will. And ask him what thou wilt.”

It is interesting to note that this conjuration employs the same method used in the Key of Solomon: the magician speaks the “barbarous names of evocation,” which are presumably the spirit’s names in his own language, and then repeats them in the conjuration proper.
