 “Say these names, and when you have finished, say also: ‘O you who are set over the hidden things of this place, open the gates for me, you who are set over the subterranean places.’ And when you have said this, say: ‘Open the gates, you who are concealed in the darkness and hidden in the shadow of the abyss. Open the gates to my words, because I am going to call upon the holy and powerful god, who is shining with light.’ And when you have said this, say: ‘Open, open.’”

Then, as the text indicates, the speaker should visualize the god’s arrival: “He will come, having a hawk’s face, and wearing on his head a white crown, and he is holding a staff. He will give you a reply.”

That’s it. A bit of chanting, a little visualization, and the deity shows up and gives you a reply. And I’ll bet you thought you had to go through all sorts of elaborate preparations to do this sort of thing. Evidently not.

This is probably the simplest invocation I’ve seen. There’s no circle, no calling of the quarters, no consecration of the tools, no lengthy prayer or meditation.
