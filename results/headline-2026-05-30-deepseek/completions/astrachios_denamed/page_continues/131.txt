The method is simple: just speak the names. But there is a
slight problem. We have no idea how to pronounce these
names. The vowels are easy, but the consonants are a bit
problematic. The letters C and S may be interchangeable,
and the letter P could be pronounced as either P or F. The
letter H may be a hard H or silent. The letter J may be
pronounced as Y or J, as in the English word “jam.” The
letter Z may be pronounced Z or TS.
The problem becomes more complicated when we realize
that these names are derived from Hebrew, and Hebrew has
a different set of rules. Hebrew has no vowels, only vowel
points that are placed under the consonants. The names
could be spelled with vowels inserted between the consonants. So the name “Ipeodoc” could be “Ip-ah-dok,” or
“Ip-eh-dok,” or “Ip-oh-dok.” Who knows?
