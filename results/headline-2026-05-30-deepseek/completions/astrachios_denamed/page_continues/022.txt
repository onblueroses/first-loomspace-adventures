 “I conjure you, O spirit, by all the names aforementioned, and by all other names sublime, unutterable, secret, interior, and dwelling in the abyss, which God knows, and which he gave to the angels . . .” There are more than a hundred names in all. The conjuration ends: “I conjure you, O spirit, by the virtue of all these names, that you appear to me in a beautiful human form, without noise and without terror, and that you answer truly and without ambiguity all the questions I shall ask you.”

Pico della Mirandola, the Renaissance magus, described the magical power of names:

There is no name of anything in which the letters, according to their position and their distribution, do not produce, through their number, some natural property of that thing. . . . The hidden properties of things are brought out, not by the art of the magician, but by nature through the letters, figures, numbers, and their influences, as they are placed in order.\(^{12}\)
