 “And when you have finished the recitation, you will see a great dragon coming from the north, with its mouth wide open, and it will fly to you, and you will be afraid of it, but you should not be afraid, but say to him:

I conjure you, O great dragon, by the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, and by the great and terrible name of God, Tetragrammaton, that you come to me and do not harm me, nor cause me any fear, but that you come to me in a beautiful human form, without any deformity, and that you answer truthfully whatever I ask of you.”

The dragon will then assume the form of a man and will answer truthfully whatever you ask. The text ends with a warning: “Do not call upon the dragon unless you are pure and clean, and have abstained from women for three days, and have fasted for three days, and have confessed your sins, and have taken communion, and have prayed to God to protect you from all evil, for this is a very dangerous experiment, and many have perished by it.”
