 “Then shall the spirits appear, and the Operator shall converse with them about those things he desires to learn. And having received satisfactory answers, he shall license them to depart, thus: ‘May the peace of God abide with you, and be ye ready to come when ye are called.’ Then let the Master and his Companions rise up and bury the Spirits in a Pits which is prepared for them in the same Circle, by casting a little earth upon the paper on which their names are written, saying, ‘We now license you to depart unto your respective abodes; and peace be between us and you. Be ye ready to come when ye are called, being duly exorcised and conjured by the sacred rites of magic.’ ”

This is an early form of the License to Depart, a formula used at the end of magical operations to dismiss spirits or energies raised during the ritual. It’s worth noting that it’s considerably more polite than the dismissals in many of the other grimoires, which treat spirits as hostile or malevolent beings and order them to depart on pain of damnation.
