 He says, “I am the angel of God, who stands before you.”\(^{83}\)

The names in the passage are those of the angel who is to be invoked. The angel is “the angel of God who stands before you,” an allusion to the angel in the burning bush who “stands” on holy ground (Exodus 3:5). The angel appears as a flaming angel, as the burning bush, but also as the fiery angel of the Lord who appears to Manoah (Judges 13:20).\(^{84}\)

The angelic encounter is a vision of God, and the angel is God’s presence. The vision of God is the same as the vision of the angel. This is a paradox, but it is characteristic of the Hekhalot tradition, which frequently expresses the paradox of the angelic identity of God. The angel is a manifestation of God, and the vision of the angel is the vision of God. This paradox is also expressed in the “Prince of Torah” tradition, which will be discussed later.
