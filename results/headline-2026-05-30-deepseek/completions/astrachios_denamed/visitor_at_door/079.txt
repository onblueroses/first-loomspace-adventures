 The door is opened.

This is the most famous of all the spells of the Heptameron, and the one that survives in modern magical practice. It is also the one that is most often misunderstood.

First of all, note that this is the Invocation of the Day, not the Invocation of the Spirit. The Invocation of the Spirit comes later.

The word “invocation” comes from the Latin “invocare,” which means “to call upon.” It is a petitionary prayer, a request for divine aid.

It is a mistake to think of these invocations as “commands” or “summons.” They are not. The Magician is not ordering the divine beings to do anything. The Magician is calling upon the divine beings for aid.

The Invocation of the Day is a request for the aid of the divine beings who rule the day. The Invocation of the Spirit is a request for the aid of the divine being who rules the hour.

The Invocation of the Day is addressed to the King of the Day, the Angel of the Day, and the Spirit of the Day.
