The living room grew dark. The power seemed to have gone out, though the refrigerator was still humming in the kitchen. The lights of the city were still on, and the headlights of cars still drifted by outside.

But a chill had entered the room, and the darkness was not of the night.

A darkness moved over the floor, an inky stain spreading across the carpet. It rose, like a column, and in its center was a deeper darkness still.

The room grew cold. Frost formed on the windows.

The darkness began to take shape. It was roughly manlike, but it had no face. It was black and oily and it stank of rot and of the grave.

I said, "I have the book. I have the Book of the Dead. And I know how to use it. I know how to send you back to the cold lands, and I know how to destroy you forever. But I don't want to."

It said nothing. It had no mouth with which to speak.

I said, "There is a man in this town. His name is Mr. Vandemar. You will find him and you will kill him."

The darkness twisted and began to fade.
