This text is a powerful and complex ritual invocation, blending elements from several ancient magical and Gnostic traditions. Based on the language, divine names, and structure, it appears to be a modern composition or adaptation drawing heavily from two primary sources:

**1. The Greek Magical Papyri (PGM):**
The core structure—a long list of divine epithets and names, the declaration of the magician's prophetic identity, and the final series of barbarous (meaningless yet potent) words of power—is classic PGM style. Phrases like "spirit of the firmament and of the ether... on dry land and in the water" are direct echoes of common PGM formulae for commanding all spirits.

**2. The Coptic Gnostic Texts (specifically, the Bruce Codex):**
This is the most striking connection. The climactic declaration ("I am the Tongueless Daiken...") is a near-verbatim quotation from a Coptic Gnostic text found in the Bruce Codex (also known as the "Untitled Text" in the *Books of Jeu*).
