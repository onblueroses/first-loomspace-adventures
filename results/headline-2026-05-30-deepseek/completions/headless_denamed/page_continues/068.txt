This text is a powerful and evocative example of a **Graeco-Egyptian magical invocation or hymn**, likely from the corpus known as the **Greek Magical Papyri (PGM)** or a related tradition. It blends Egyptian, Greek, and possibly Gnostic or Jewish elements into a syncretic ritual formula.

Here is an analysis of its structure and key elements:

### Structure & Purpose
The text is a **theurgic invocation**—a ritual to call upon a supreme, often hidden, deity to empower the magician (here, the speaker, **Nopheth**) and grant him authority over all spirits. Its ultimate goal, stated clearly, is to make "all spirits subject unto me."

### Key Theological & Mythological Elements

1.  **The "Tongueless One" / Terrible and Invisible God:** The deity is primordial, uncreated, and beyond human perception ("whom no man hath seen at any time"). The "Void Place of the Spirit" suggests a Gnostic-like *Pleroma* or an abstract, pre-material realm.
