This text appears to be a modern composite or pastiche of several ancient magical and Gnostic fragments. It echoes the style and content of several known sources:

**1. The "Mithras Liturgy" (Paris Magical Papyrus):** The opening invocation ("Thee I invoke...") strongly resembles the invocations to a supreme, unseen creator deity found in this famous Greek magical text.

**2. The "Eight (or Seven) Books of Moses" or similar Solomonic/Gnostic traditions:** The long strings of powerful, secret names (e.g., *Aronopholobrao, Sopheh, Mopholio...*) and the command to subject all spirits are hallmarks of late antique theurgic and magical texts that claim Mosaic or prophetic authority.

**3. The "Gospel of the Egyptians" (Nag Hammadi):** The phrase "I am the Tongueless Daiken with sight in his feet" is a direct, though slightly altered, quotation from this Gnostic text. The original reads "the great Daidukos who is over sight in his feet." "Daiken" or "Daidukos" is a revealer figure.
