 His voice is low, but it carries.

“You are the one who asked for the book.”

I turn to face him.

“Yes,” I say.

“I am the librarian,” he says.

He holds the book out to me.

“Take it,” he says.

I take it.

It is bound in black leather, and it is heavy.

I open it. The pages are thick, heavy and yellowed. The paper is old and brittle. The book smells of dust and age and something else, something I cannot identify.

I look at the page. There is a single word written on it, in a language I cannot read.

The librarian looks at me.

“What does it say?” I ask.

“It says ‘I,’” he says.

“I?”

“Yes.”

I turn the page.

Another word.

“What does this say?”

“It says ‘am.’”

I turn the page.

Another word.

“What does this say?”

“It says ‘the.’”

I turn the page.

Another word.

“What does this say?”

“It says ‘Tongueless.’”
