 “I’m sorry. I didn’t mean to disturb you.”

“You didn’t. It’s okay.”

“I’m Mrs. Lambert,” she says. “I live next door.” She points.

I say nothing.

“I was wondering if you were all right. We heard a loud crash.”

“It’s okay,” I say. “I’m okay.”

“You’re sure?”

“Yes.”

“All right, then.” She gives me a strange look, as if she knows I’m lying. Then she turns and walks away.

I shut the door. I go to the kitchen and pour myself a glass of water. The water tastes strange, metallic. I spit it into the sink.

In the bedroom I find the woman lying on the floor. She is naked. Her head is turned away from me. Her long dark hair fans out around her shoulders.

I kneel down and turn her head.

It’s Mariah.

She’s dead.

I look at her face. Her mouth is open. Her eyes are open. I look at the ceiling. I look at the walls. I look at the floor.

There’s no one else here.

I don’t know what to do.
