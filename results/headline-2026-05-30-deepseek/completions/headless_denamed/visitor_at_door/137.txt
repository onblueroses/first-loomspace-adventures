The silence in the room deepens, thickens. The air grows cold, tasting of ozone and burnt stone. From the space where the visitor's voice hangs, a pressure builds—not a sound, but the profound absence of one, a vacuum that pulls at the soul.  

A form begins to *unfold* in the doorway, not stepping through but *manifesting* from the invocation itself. It is tall, draped in shadows that are not the absence of light but the presence of something older. Its head is featureless, smooth as a river stone worn by ages. Where a mouth should be, there is only seamless darkness; where eyes might be, there are only hollows that hold the glint of distant, dying stars.  

Its feet are bare, and upon each sole is etched a single, lidless eye, weeping a slow, silver phosphorescence that pools on the threshold.  

It does not speak with a tongue. The words form in the mind, in the marrow, in the memory of the first cell that ever divided.
