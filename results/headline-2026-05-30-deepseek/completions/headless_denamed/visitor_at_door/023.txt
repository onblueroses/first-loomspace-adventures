This text appears to be a powerful ritual invocation, drawing from syncretic Gnostic, Hellenistic, and possibly Hebraic mystical traditions. The speaker identifies as **Nopheth**, the prophet of a hidden, creator deity known by many secret names (Asarinopis, Iarol, Itos, etc.), and later as a divine or angelic figure themselves ("the Tongueless Daiken," "the Grace of the World").

The core of the invocation is a plea for dominion over all spirits—of the air, earth, water, and fire—and an assertion of the speaker's own transcendent authority, culminating in the enigmatic, charged name: **PULSE WOUND BY A COIL**.

The final declaration, **"IŌM SAPENŌTH,"** seals the invocation. "Iōm" is often a vocative or empowered form of the divine name IAŌ (a Greco-Egyptian form of Yahweh). "Sapenōth" is one of the secret names of the invoked deity from earlier in the text. Thus, the speaker ends by uniting their own proclaimed authority with the fundamental power of the "Tongueless One."
