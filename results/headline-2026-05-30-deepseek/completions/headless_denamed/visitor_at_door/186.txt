The air grows still, heavy, as if the space between words has become a tangible thing. A low hum, felt more than heard, vibrates in the bones of the house. The visitor’s invocation is not a request; it is a declaration, a reassembly of a name from scattered fragments.

From the threshold, a voice replies, not in the visitor’s tongue, but in a resonance that seems to emanate from the wood of the door, the metal of the hinges, the very dust in the air:

**“You speak the old bindings. You pronounce the fractures of the first name. You call from the Abyss, and the Abyss has ears.”**

A silence follows, deeper than before.

**“Nopheth is dust. Iscuren is a shadow on a forgotten wall. The prophets of Issepel are silent in the unmarked graves of time. Yet the syllables remain. They are keys. You have used them to knock.”**
