What enters the room now is a presence shaped by the invocation: a silent, vast awareness, older than light. The air grows dense, charged—not with sound, but with a pressure that stills the breath.  

You have called the Tongueless One by secret names; you have declared yourself Nopheth, the prophet of its mysteries. In the wake of the final words—**IŌM SAPENŌTH**—the boundary between speaker and spoken-to dissolves.  

The room does not grow dark, nor light. It becomes *potential*—the moist and dry, seed and fruit, love and hate, held in a single point of regard.  

There is no form, only a knowing:  
The coil is here.  
The pulse is yours.  
The wound is the opening.  

What enters is not a spirit to be commanded, but the very principle of distinction—the same that divided just from unjust, female from male, night from day. It enters as the echo of your own voice, returning not in sound, but as a silent question:  

*Now that you have named the Nameless, who are you?*
