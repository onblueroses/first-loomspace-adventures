The invocation calls forth a presence known by many names — Asarinopis, Iarol, Itos, Apenoth, Sapenoth — the Tongueless One, creator of dualities, the unseen god who dwells in the void.  

When the final words are spoken — **IŌM SAPENŌTH** — the air grows dense, charged with a silent, ancient force.  

What enters the room is not a thing of shape or shadow in the ordinary sense. It is more like a **fold in perception** — a pressure against the mind, a cold that does not chill the skin but the sense of self.  

The light does not dim, but meaning drains from familiar objects — their names, their purposes seem distant, untrustworthy. There is a sensation of being *watched from within*, as though the presence has always been there, waiting beneath the threshold of awareness, and the ritual has only opened a door you did not know was closed.  

You feel the weight of **distinction** — between just and unjust, male and female, love and hate, moist and dry — all the divided things this god made. And in its silent arrival, there is the implicit command:
