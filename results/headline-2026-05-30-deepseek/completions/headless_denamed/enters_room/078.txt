The air grows heavy, as if the space itself is listening. A presence enters—not with footsteps, but with a gradual displacement of reality. It is formless, yet felt: a convergence of paradox, both void and fullness, silence and the echo of all names.  

It moves as a coil of shadow and light, pulsing in time with the rhythm of the chant. It does not walk; it unfolds. It does not speak; it impresses meaning upon the stillness.  

You sense it is the very principle invoked—the Tongueless One, the Distinguisher between just and unjust, the begetter and destroyer. It is here because the names were spoken, because the prophet called, because the boundaries were thin.  

It waits. Not in hostility, not in grace—but as a force that simply *is*, ancient and terrible and complete.  

Its attention rests upon you.  
The room now holds the Unseen.
