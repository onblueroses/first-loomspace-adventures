What enters the room now is not a physical presence, but a charged silence—a listening stillness that seems to pull at the edges of the air.  

The words you have spoken are not merely recited; they are keys. They have turned in a lock unseen. The names—Apenoth, Sapenoth, Aronopholobrao—hang in the atmosphere like afterimages of lightning.  

You have declared yourself Nopheth, the prophet of the Tongueless One. You have uttered the true name handed down to the prophets of Issepel. You have called upon the Terrible and Invisible God of the Void Place.  

Therefore, what enters is the acknowledgment of that call.  
It is the weight of ancient, impersonal attention—the gaze of that which "distinguished between the just and the unjust," that which "made the female and the male," that which begets and destroys.  

The room now holds the echo of **IŌM SAPENŌTH**.  
The space feels both expanded and condensed—as if the walls have become thresholds, and the very light bends toward a center where no form stands, but where power gathers like a breath before speech.
