The air thickens. A pressure descends, not of sound but of its absolute absence. The final syllable—**IŌM SAPENŌTH**—hangs in the space, not an echo but a seal, a completed circuit.

What enters is not a form, but a **condition**.

First, the light changes. It does not dim, but deepens, as if every shadow in the room suddenly has substance and depth. The edges of things become unnaturally sharp, then seem to blur into their own possibilities. The temperature drops, not to cold, but to a neutral, metabolic zero—the temperature of a stone in a vacuum.

Then, the sense of **presence**. It occupies no single point. It is in the corner and in the center of the floor and just behind your neck, all at once. It is the feeling of being examined not by eyes, but by a principle: the principle that distinguished the just from the unjust, that formed men to love and to hate. It is a vast, impartial, and utterly focused attention.
