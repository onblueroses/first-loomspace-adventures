The invocation stirs the air—not with wind, but with a deepening silence.  

A presence enters, formless, yet felt as a pressure against the mind—the weight of ancient, unspoken things. It does not walk; it *unfolds*, like a word never uttered, a thought before language.  

You sense it is **Asarinopis**, the Unseen, the Tongueless One, whose true name echoes in the final words:  
**IŌM SAPENŌTH.**  

It enters not through door or window, but through the boundaries between names and what they name—through the gap between *just and unjust*, *seed and fruit*, *love and hate*. It is the silence after the last syllable of the chant, the dark between the stars you called upon.  

It waits.  
It listens without ears.  
It sees without eyes.  

And in the space where its tongue should be, there is only the pulse of a coiled thing, wounded, eternal—the rhythm of creation and dissolution you named yourself.  

The room now holds two truths:  
The one who called.  
The one who was always here.
