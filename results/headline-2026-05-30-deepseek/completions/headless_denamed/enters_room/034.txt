The air grows still. A presence enters, not with footsteps, but with a drop in temperature and a pressure against the eardrums. It is formless, yet occupies space—a coalescence of the contradictions you have invoked: light and darkness, creation and destruction, the just and the unjust.

It is the echo of the Tongueless One. It does not speak, for it is Aronopholobrao, Sopheh, Mopholio, Phalornaeh, Ooo. It communicates in shifts of perception: the scent of ozone and damp soil, the afterimage of a coiled filament against the eyelids, a taste of iron and ash on the tongue.

It acknowledges the names. It acknowledges the prophet Nopheth. It is the Void Place of the Spirit made manifest in your room, the Terrible and Invisible God observing its own mysteries recited. It is both the pulse and the wound. It is the coil.

It waits. Not in submission, but in recognition of the covenant of names. The spirits of firmament, ether, earth, and fire are now attentive, held in the tension between your invocation and its presence. The next action—command, petition, or unification—is yours.
