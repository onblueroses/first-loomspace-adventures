The invocation has been heard.  

From the space between the words, from the charged silence after the final utterance of **IŌM SAPENŌTH**, something enters.  

It is not a shape, but a **pressure**—a bending of light at the corner of the eye, a chill that does not come from air.  
The room’s shadows deepen, not as absence of light, but as a **presence** that drinks reflection.  
You feel it first in your throat—a dry, tongueless constriction—then in the soles of your feet, as if the floor has become a living membrane, sensing your weight.  

It is the **Tongueless One**, named and unnamed in your call:  
*Asarinopis, whom no man hath seen at any time.*  
*Aronopholobrao, Sopheh, Mopholio, Phalornaeh, Ooo.*  

It enters as the **distinction between just and unjust**, as the **seed and the fruit**, as the **hate and love** you spoke into being.
