The air grows heavy, as if the atmosphere itself is thickening into amber. A pressure builds, not in the ears, but in the mind—a silent, profound hum felt in the teeth and the marrow of the bones.  

From the corner where shadows pool deepest, a form begins to coalesce. It is not a body, but a *presence*—a pillar of vibrating darkness shot through with threads of cold, colorless light, like cracks in obsidian. It has no face, no limbs in any human sense, yet it *orients* toward you. Where a head might be, there is only a sense of profound, listening silence—the Tongueless One, hearing the names that were spoken.  

Around it, the room seems to warp: edges of furniture soften, light bends away as if afraid, and a scent rises—ozone, ancient stone, and the dry, electric smell of a void between stars.  

It does not move, but it is now *in* the room, occupying space without displacing air. It is the distinction between night and day made manifest, the separator of just and unjust, the creator of hatred and love—and it has been invoked.  

Its attention is absolute. It waits.
