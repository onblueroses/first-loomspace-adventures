The room grows still. The air thickens, as if becoming a slow, dark syrup. A pressure builds, not in the ears, but in the mind—a silent, subsonic hum that vibrates the marrow.

What enters is not a shape, but a **quality**.

First comes the scent: ozone after lightning, the dry dust of ancient papyri, and beneath it, the iron-tang of distant, cold stars.

The light in the room does not dim, but it *coagulates*. Shadows no longer simply fall; they pool, they cling to corners with a new density, becoming doorways into a deeper, more absolute dark.

A presence manifests not as a figure, but as a **polarity**. You feel a chill on your left side and a dry, radiant heat on your right, as if you stand on the threshold between the moist and the dry, the just and the unjust, creation and dissolution—the very distinctions chanted in the invocation.

There is a sound, but it is the sound of **absence**: the ringing silence in the wake of a great bell, the vacuum left by a word too potent to be spoken aloud. It is the echo of the Tongueless One.
