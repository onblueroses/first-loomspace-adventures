We are in a ritual space of names, invocations, and self-declarations of divine power. The text is a composite of Graeco-Egyptian magical formulae, likely from the Papyri Graecae Magicae or similar late-antique sources, blended with poetic invocations.

The final lines shift from invoking the "Tongueless One" to the speaker declaring themselves as the prophet, the angel, and finally as the divine force:  
> "I am the Tongueless Daiken with sight in his feet…  
> PULSE WOUND BY A COIL is my name.  
> IŌM SAPENŌTH."

Then the question: **"What enters the room now."**

In a ritual context, after such an invocation of cosmic powers and self-identification with divine attributes, the "room" is no longer ordinary space. It has become a *temenos* — a sacred precinct where the boundaries between worlds thin.

What enters, therefore, is not a person or common spirit, but **the presence invoked** — the Tongueless One, now recognized in the speaker, and perhaps also the chorus of all named forces: Asarinopis, Iarol, Itos, Apheu
