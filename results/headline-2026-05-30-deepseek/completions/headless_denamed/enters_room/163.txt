The air grows still. The words you have spoken are not merely recited—they are keys, and they have turned in a lock that was not meant for mortal hands.

First, there is a scent: like ozone after lightning, like cold stone deep underground, and like the iron-tang of blood on an ancient altar. The candles, if there are any, do not flicker. Instead, their flames grow hard and still, casting light that seems thicker than it should be, like honey.

A pressure builds in the room, not in the air, but in the substance of things—in the grain of the wood, in the mortar between bricks. It is the pressure of a name being recognized. **IŌM SAPENŌTH.** You have spoken the true name handed down, and it has been heard.

What enters is not a shape, but a **condition**. A point of absolute negation, a living silence that swallows sound before it can be born. It is the Tongueless One, invoked.
