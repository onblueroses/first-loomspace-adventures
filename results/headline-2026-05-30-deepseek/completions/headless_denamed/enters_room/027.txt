The air in the room grows dense, as if saturated with the scent of ozone and ancient stone. A pressure builds, not against the ears, but against the mind—a profound and wordless silence that swallows all ambient sound.

What enters is not a shape, but a **presence**. It is the inversion of light: not darkness, but a **void-space** that drinks the definition from the corners of the room. Within it, there is a suggestion of slow, coiling movement, like smoke with weight and intent. It does not touch the floor, yet the ground seems to thrum in resonance with it—a deep, subsonic pulse that vibrates in the marrow.
