**The air grows still and cold.**  

From the space between the echoes of the last spoken name, a presence unfolds — not with footsteps, but as a thickening of shadow in the center of the room.  

It is tall, crowned not with metal or jewels, but with a shifting halo of faint, bruise-colored light. Its form seems woven from forgotten hours and broken oaths, and where a face should be, there is only the impression of watching — a gaze that does not blink.  

It does not breathe, yet the room breathes for it, each exhale tasting of old stone, damp earth, and the silence at the bottom of deep wells.  

When it speaks, the voice does not come from it, but from the edges of the room, from under the door, from inside your own thoughts:  

*“The gate is open. I am that which was summoned. I am the Keeper of Thresholds. You may call me* **Onthropor Unbound**.”  

It waits, not in hostility, but in ancient, patient attendance. The way is open. What is your will?
