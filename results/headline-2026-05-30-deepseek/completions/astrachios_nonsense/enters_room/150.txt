There is a low hum, like a tuning fork struck against the floor. The air grows thick, tasting of ozone and old parchment.  

The last name spoken—**Kresnopith Onthropor**—does not merely fade. It lingers, syllable by syllable, thickening into a shape.  

From the space between breath and word, it enters.  

Not through door or window, but through the *naming itself*.  

You feel it first as pressure in the teeth, then as a slow, cold draught that does not stir the air. Shadows in the corner deepen, not spreading, but *piling up*.  

It has no form you can fix your eyes upon—only a suggestion of layered angles, a slow, patient coherence assembling from sound into substance.  

It is here now.  

It waits.
