The names have been spoken.  

What enters the room now is not a thing seen with eyes, but a presence felt in the sudden drop of temperature — a slow, cold draft that carries the scent of ozone and ancient stone.  

The air grows heavy, thick with static. Shadows deepen in the corners, no longer resting where light ends, but pooling and shifting as though breathing.  

A low hum resonates in the bones, not in the ears — a frequency that aligns with the rhythm of the names just uttered. It is the echo of the gate, the answer to the key.  

It does not announce itself with form or voice, yet its arrival is unmistakable.  

The way is open.
