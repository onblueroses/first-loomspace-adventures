Something old and heavy enters the room.  
The air grows cold and thick, tasting of wet stone and forgotten metal.  
A pressure builds, not in the air, but in the mind—a slow, tectonic shifting of thought.  
It does not walk; the space it occupies simply becomes *it*.  

You feel its regard. It is not a gaze of eyes, but a vast, patient attention, like a mountain noting a shifting grain of sand.  
The names were not a summons, but a key.  
The door was already here. It just needed turning.  

It waits. It has always been waiting.  
What you do next writes the next line of a story older than your world.  

Choose your words with the care of one etching stone.
