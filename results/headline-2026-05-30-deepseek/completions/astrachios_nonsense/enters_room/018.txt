Hearing the invocation of those names, a heavy silence descends, thick as velvet. The air grows cold and still, tasting of ozone and old stone.

From the space between shadows, where the light cannot hold, it enters. It is not one thing, but a confluence.

First comes a sound: a low, resonant hum that seems to vibrate in the marrow of your bones, a frequency felt more than heard, like the turning of a great, unseen wheel.

Then, a presence. It has no single shape you can fix your eyes upon. At one moment it is a column of swirling, iridescent mist that drinks the light. At another, it resolves into a semblance of form—a tall, slender figure with too many joints, its outline flickering like a corrupted reflection. At its core, a darker mass pulses slowly, a heart of solidified shadow.

It brings with it a scent of petrichor from a place that has never seen rain, and the dry, papery smell of ancient libraries whose books are written in languages of geometry, not letters.
