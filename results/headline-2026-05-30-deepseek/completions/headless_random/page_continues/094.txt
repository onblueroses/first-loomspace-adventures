**Core Concept:**  
Plate tectonics is the unifying theory explaining the large-scale motion of Earth's lithosphere (the rigid outer shell). It evolved from the earlier hypothesis of continental drift.

**Key Components:**
1. **Tectonic Plates:** The lithosphere is broken into 7–8 major plates and many minor ones.
2. **Plate Boundaries:**  
   - **Convergent:** Plates collide, causing subduction, mountain-building, trenches, and volcanoes.  
   - **Divergent:** Plates move apart (e.g., mid-ocean ridges), creating new crust.  
   - **Transform:** Plates slide past each other, causing earthquakes.
3. **Motion:** Plates move slowly (0–100 mm/year) due to mantle convection currents beneath the weaker asthenosphere.
4. **Crust Recycling:** At convergent boundaries, oceanic crust subducts into the mantle, balancing new crust formation at divergent boundaries—like a "conveyor belt."

**Historical Acceptance:**  
The theory gained widespread acceptance in the 1960s after evidence confirmed seafloor spreading, replacing earlier ideas of a shrinking or expanding Earth.
