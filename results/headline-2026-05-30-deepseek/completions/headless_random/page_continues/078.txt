The theory of continental drift was first proposed by Alfred Wegener in 1912. He was a German meteorologist and geophysicist who hypothesized that the continents were once joined together in a single landmass called Pangaea. He proposed that the continents had drifted apart over time. His theory was initially met with skepticism, but it was eventually accepted after the discovery of seafloor spreading in the 1960s. The theory of plate tectonics is now the unifying theory of geology.
