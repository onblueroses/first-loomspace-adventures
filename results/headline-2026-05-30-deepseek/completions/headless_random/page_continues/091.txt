The description you've provided is an excellent summary of plate tectonics theory. Let's break down and expand upon the key concepts mentioned, connecting them to the broader picture of how our dynamic planet works.

### Core Principles and Historical Context

1.  **From Continental Drift to Plate Tectonics:** The theory didn't emerge fully formed. It evolved from **Alfred Wegener's** hypothesis of **continental drift** (1912), which proposed that continents move. Wegener had compelling evidence (continental jigsaw fit, fossil and rock correlations) but could not propose a plausible *mechanism* for the motion. The discovery and validation of **seafloor spreading** in the 1960s provided that mechanism, transforming the idea into the robust theory of plate tectonics.

2.  **The Conveyor Belt & Conservation of Lithosphere:** This is a crucial point. New oceanic lithosphere is constantly created at **mid-ocean ridges (divergent boundaries)**. To keep the Earth from expanding, old lithosphere must be destroyed at the same average rate. This happens at **subduction zones (convergent boundaries)**, where dense oceanic plates sink back into the mantle. This cycle maintains a roughly constant planetary surface area.
