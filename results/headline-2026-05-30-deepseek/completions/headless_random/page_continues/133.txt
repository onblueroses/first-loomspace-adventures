## This Day In History: March 17

On this day in history, March 17th...

1845 : The British inventor Stephen Perry patents the first modern-day rubber band. Made from vulcanized rubber, Perry owned the manufacturing company called Messers Perry and Co., Rubber Manufacturers of London. The company produced vulcanized rubber products such as garters, hose, and bands. Although rubber bands were made by the ancient Mayans, Aztecs and other Mesoamericans, Perry was the first to patent it. The rubber band was invented as a way to hold papers or envelopes together. It became such a useful invention that many manufacturers began producing them. Today, the largest consumer and user of rubber bands in the world is the U.S. Post Office.

1958 : The United States launches its second satellite called Vanguard 1. It was the fourth artificial Earth orbital satellite to be launched, following Sputnik 1, Sputnik 2, and Explorer 1. Vanguard 1 was the first satellite to have solar electric power. Although communications with the satellite were lost in 1964, it remains the oldest manmade satellite still in orbit. It was designed to test the launch capabilities of a three-stage launch vehicle
