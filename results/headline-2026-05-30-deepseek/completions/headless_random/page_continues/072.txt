That is an excellent and accurate summary of the core principles of plate tectonics. You've covered the key historical development from continental drift, the structure of the lithosphere, the types of plate boundaries, and the driving mechanisms.

To build on your summary, here are some additional important points and implications that often follow in such explanations:

### Key Implications and Supporting Evidence

1.  **Unifying Theory:** Plate tectonics is the **unifying theory of geology**. It provides a single framework to explain the distribution of:
    *   **Earthquakes and Volcanoes:** They are not randomly distributed but are concentrated along plate boundaries (e.g., the "Ring of Fire" around the Pacific Plate).
    *   **Mountain Ranges:** Major chains like the Himalayas (convergent: continent-continent), the Andes (convergent: ocean-continent), and the mid-ocean ridges (divergent) are direct products of plate interactions.
    *   **Continental Configurations:** It explains how continents like South America and Africa fit together (Pangaea) and how they have moved over time.

2.  **Mantle Convection as the Engine:** While the lithosphere moves, the primary driving force is thought to be **mantle convection
