**Driving Forces and Mechanisms**

While convection currents in the mantle are a primary driver, the movement of tectonic plates is a complex process influenced by several additional forces:
*   **Ridge Push (or Gravitational Sliding):** At mid-ocean ridges, newly formed lithosphere is hot and elevated. As it cools and moves away, it becomes denser and slopes downward. The gravitational force of this elevated material sliding down the gentle slope away from the ridge pushes the plate.
*   **Slab Pull:** This is often considered the most significant force. At convergent boundaries, the leading edge of a dense oceanic plate subducts into the hot mantle. As it sinks, it pulls the rest of the plate along behind it. The weight of the cold, dense slab is a powerful driver of motion.
*   **Trench Suction:** At subduction zones, the downgoing plate can cause a flow in the mantle that effectively "sucks" the overriding plate toward the trench, influencing its motion.
