### **1. The Driving Forces: What Moves the Plates?**

While the text mentions convection currents, the exact mechanisms are complex and involve multiple, interconnected forces:
*   **Ridge Push:** At mid-ocean ridges, the new, hot lithosphere is elevated. Gravity then causes this material to slide downhill away from the ridge, pushing the plate.
*   **Slab Pull:** This is often considered the dominant force. At convergent boundaries, a cold, dense oceanic plate sinks (subducts) into the hot mantle. The weight of this sinking slab pulls the rest of the plate behind it.
*   **Mantle Convection:** The slow, churning motion of the mantle due to heat from the core provides the broad, underlying framework for these movements.

### **2. The Wilson Cycle: The Supercontinent Cycle**

Plate tectonics is not static; it operates in billion-year cycles. The **Wilson Cycle** describes the periodic assembly and breakup of supercontinents.
