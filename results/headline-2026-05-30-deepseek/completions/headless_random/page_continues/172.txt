It sounds like you've provided a comprehensive overview of plate tectonics. This summary covers the key points well:

- **Definition & History**: Plate tectonics explains the movement of Earth's lithospheric plates, evolving from the earlier concept of continental drift and gaining acceptance after evidence of seafloor spreading in the mid-20th century.
- **Plate Composition & Boundaries**: The lithosphere is divided into major and minor plates that interact at convergent, divergent, or transform boundaries, leading to geological activity like earthquakes, volcanoes, and mountain formation.
- **Mechanisms of Motion**: Movement is driven by mantle convection and density differences, with new crust forming at mid-ocean ridges (divergent boundaries) and older crust being recycled into the mantle at subduction zones (convergent boundaries).
- **Key Principles**: The "conveyor belt" model ensures the total surface area of the lithosphere remains roughly constant, countering earlier theories of a shrinking or expanding Earth.
