It appears you're providing a detailed summary of plate tectonics. This is an excellent and accurate overview of the theory. To build upon what you've shared, here are some key implications and modern understandings that stem from this foundational concept:

### Key Implications of Plate Tectonics:
1.  **Unified Theory:** It provides a single, unifying framework that explains the distribution of:
    *   **Earthquakes and Volcanoes:** They are not random but are concentrated along plate boundaries.
    *   **Mountain Ranges:** Major chains like the Himalayas (convergent boundary) and the Andes (convergent with subduction) are direct results of plate interactions.
    *   **Ocean Basins:** Their formation (at divergent boundaries) and destruction (at convergent boundaries) are part of a continuous cycle.

2.  **The Supercontinent Cycle:** The theory predicts that continents assemble into supercontinents (like Pangaea) and then break apart over cycles of 400-600 million years, driven by the perpetual motion of the plates.

3.  **Climate and Evolution:** Plate motions profoundly influence long-term climate patterns (via changing ocean currents and continent positions) and evolution (by creating and destroying habitats and barriers, leading to speciation and extinction events).
