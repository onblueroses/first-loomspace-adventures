The driving forces behind plate motion, while still an area of active research, are primarily attributed to this mantle convection. However, the movement is also influenced by other factors. One significant force is **ridge push** (or gravitational sliding), where the elevated lithosphere at mid-ocean ridges slopes away, causing the plate to slide downhill under gravity. Another is **slab pull**, considered the most substantial force, where the weight of a dense, subducting oceanic plate at a convergent boundary pulls the rest of the plate behind it.

The theory provides a unifying framework for understanding the geological history and large-scale features of the Earth. It explains the distribution of continents and oceans, the formation of major mountain ranges like the Himalayas (formed by the ongoing collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates), and the "Ring of Fire" around the Pacific Ocean, a zone of frequent earthquakes and volcanoes associated with convergent and transform boundaries.
