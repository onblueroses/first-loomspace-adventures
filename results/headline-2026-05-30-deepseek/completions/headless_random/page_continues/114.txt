## Discovery of plate tectonics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The acceptance of plate tectonics was confirmed by seafloor spreading, and later with the discovery of the driving mechanism of plate tectonics.

Plate tectonics is a scientific theory describing the large-scale motion of seven large plates and the movements of a larger number of smaller plates of Earth's lithosphere, since tectonic processes began on Earth between 3.5 and 3.8 billion years ago. The model builds on the concept of continental drift, an idea developed during the first decades of the 20th century. Plate tectonics came to be generally accepted by geoscientists after the validation of the theory of seafloor spreading in the late 1950s and early 1960s.

The lithosphere, which is the rigid outermost shell of a planet (the crust and upper mantle), is broken into tectonic plates. The Earth's lithosphere is composed of seven or eight major plates (depending on how they are defined) and many minor plates. Where the plates meet, their relative motion determines the type of plate boundary: convergent, divergent, or transform.
