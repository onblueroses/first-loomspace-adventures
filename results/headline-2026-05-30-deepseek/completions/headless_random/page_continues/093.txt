The passage you've shared provides a strong foundational overview of plate tectonics. It effectively explains the theory's core components. Let's break down and expand upon the key concepts mentioned:

### 1. **The Core Idea**
The theory states that Earth's outer shell (the **lithosphere**) is divided into rigid plates that move relative to each other over the ductile **asthenosphere**. This movement drives most of the planet's large-scale geological activity.

### 2. **Historical Context & Acceptance**
*   **Continental Drift (1910s-1920s):** Proposed by Alfred Wegener, it suggested continents moved. While compelling (due to fossil and geological matches), it lacked a plausible mechanism for *how* they moved and was largely rejected.
*   **Seafloor Spreading (1950s-1960s):** Discoveries of mid-ocean ridges, magnetic striping on the ocean floor, and the youth of oceanic crust provided the missing mechanism. This validation is what led to the modern, widely accepted theory of **plate tectonics**.
