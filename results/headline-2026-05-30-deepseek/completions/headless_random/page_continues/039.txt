 Plate movement is thought to be driven by a combination of the motion of the seafloor away from spreading ridges due to variations in topography and density changes in the crust. The relative importance of each of these factors and their relationship is unclear, and still the subject of debate.

The theory of plate tectonics is now almost universally accepted, and has been a major scientific revolution, comparable in its consequences to quantum mechanics or Einstein's theory of relativity. However, the theory is still being refined as scientists learn more about the mechanisms that drive plate motion and as new data become available, especially about the processes going on in ocean ridges and subduction zones.

Plate tectonics has been successful in explaining many geological phenomena, such as mountain building, earthquakes, and volcanism, and has also contributed to our understanding of past climates, the evolution of life, and the formation of mineral deposits. It has also been applied to the study of other planets and moons in the solar system.
