 "Plate movement is thought to be driven by a combination of the motion of the seafloor away from spreading ridges due to variations in topography and density changes in the crust and the drag, downward suction, at the subduction zones. At spreading ridges, plates move apart and hot mantle rock rises and cools adiabatically, increasing density and contributing to the motion. At subduction zones, plates converge and one plate sinks under the other, pulling the surface plate downward. Another explanation lies in the different forces generated by the rotation of the globe and the gravitational forces of the Sun and Moon. The relative importance of each of these factors and their relationship to each other is unclear, and still the subject of much debate."

The description of "plate movement" is not a description of plate movement, but of a supposed mechanism for plate movement. The movement of the plates is described by the "conveyor belt principle" mentioned earlier, but the movement of the plates is not the same as the mechanism that drives the movement. The movement is described by the theory of plate tectonics, which is the scientific theory that the Earth's lithosphere comprises a number of large tectonic plates which have been slowly moving since about 3.4 billion years ago.
