The article continues: "The tectonic plates are not fixed but move relative to each other at varying speeds averaging a few centimetres per year. This motion is driven by a combination of forces: the push from the elevated lithosphere at mid-ocean ridges away from the ridge (often called "ridge push"), the pull of dense lithosphere sinking into the mantle at trenches ("slab pull"), and the traction of the convecting mantle on the base of the lithosphere. Some researchers have also proposed that the weight of massive slabs of lithosphere depressed into the mantle at subduction zones might contribute to plate motion."

The article continues: "The relative motion of the plates typically ranges from zero to 100 millimetres annually."

The article continues: "The Earth's lithosphere, which includes the crust and uppermost mantle, is made up of a series of pieces, or tectonic plates, that move slowly over time. A mid-ocean ridge demarcates the boundary between two tectonic plates, and consequently is termed a divergent plate boundary."
