Seafloor spreading or seafloor spread is a process that occurs at mid-ocean ridges, where new oceanic crust is formed through volcanic activity and then gradually moves away from the ridge.

Earlier theories of continental drift postulated that continents in motion "plowed" through the fixed and immovable seafloor. The idea that the seafloor itself moves and also carries the continents with it as it spreads from a central rift axis was proposed by Harold Hammond Hess and Robert Sinclair Dietz. The American geophysicist Harry H. Hess proposed the concept in 1960. On the basis of Tharp's efforts and other new discoveries about the deep ocean floor, Hess postulated that molten material from Earth's mantle continuously wells up along the crests of the mid-ocean ridges that wind for nearly 80,000 kilometres through all the world's oceans. As the magma cools, it is pushed away from the flanks of the ridges. This spreading creates a successively younger ocean floor, and the flow on either side of the ridge provides a driving mechanism for the motion of the continents.
