## Chapter 9: The Unseen Enemy

The days following my discussion with the Doctor are a blur of anxious waiting and feverish study. I bury myself in books on geology, physics, and the strange, burgeoning field of continental drift, a theory still viewed with deep skepticism by most established scientists. The Doctor’s words—*The world is alive. It moves*—echo in my mind, a revolutionary and terrifying mantra.

He is a whirlwind of activity, barely sleeping, his eyes shadowed with a fatigue that speaks of more than just physical exertion. He is consumed by the mystery of the tremors. He has set up a network of crude seismographs throughout the town, borrowed from the university and cobbled together from spare parts in his laboratory. The tracings, which he shows me with a grimace, are erratic, nonsensical. They show small, localized shudders that defy the pattern of any known tectonic activity.
