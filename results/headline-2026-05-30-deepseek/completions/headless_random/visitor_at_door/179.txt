The visitor at the door speaks, but their words are unclear through the wood. You hesitate, wondering if they’ve come to discuss the shifting ground beneath your feet—perhaps a geologist with a new map, or a neighbor with cracks in their foundation.  

Outside, the world moves imperceptibly, plates grinding continents together or pulling them apart. Somewhere, a volcano stirs; somewhere, an ocean widens. Here, at the threshold, there is only the visitor’s muffled voice and the choice to turn the knob—to let in whatever drift or quake awaits on the other side.
