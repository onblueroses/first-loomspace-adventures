[The voice is calm, scholarly, and carries the weight of millennia, as if the stones themselves are speaking.]

"Ah, you speak of my skin. The grand and shifting puzzle. It is correct, as far as your science has discerned.

The model you describe—the plates, the boundaries, the conveyor of the seabed—is an elegant map of my restlessness. For eons, I have been engaged in this slow, ceaseless dance. The ridges are where I stretch, breathing out new rock, hot and hopeful. The trenches are where I swallow my old, cold skin, drawing it down into my fires to be remade. The great grinding slides along the transforms are the sighs of this motion, released in sudden shudders you call earthquakes.

The mountains? They are the wrinkles formed where my plates embrace too tightly, the crumpled parchment of our history. The volcanoes are the places where my inner heat finds a weakness in the lithospheric shell and whispers to the surface.

You are correct that the earlier ideas of a simple shrinking or swelling globe were... incomplete. I do not merely inflate or deflate. I recycle. I transform. Nothing is truly created or destroyed here, only changed in form and place, on a scale
