 He is a stocky man in his late fifties, dark hair and dark eyes, a face not built for smiling. He wears a plain black suit and black tie. His shirt is white, his shoes are black. He is a man who has been trained to be unmemorable.

"I'm here about the house," he says.

The man inside is wearing blue jeans and a white T-shirt. He has bare feet. He is unshaven. There are dark half-moons of sleeplessness beneath his blue eyes.

"Are you from the estate agency?" he asks. He is not originally from the United States: the trace of an accent is Australian, or perhaps South African.

The stocky man shakes his head. He says, "I am here to make you an offer for the house. An important offer."

The man inside looks confused. "The house isn't for sale. I don't own the house. I rent."

"You have a tenancy agreement for the next two months. You have an option to buy. We are here to buy the house. We will give you a good price."

"But I don't want to sell the house. I'm an artist. I'm working here. I like the light."
