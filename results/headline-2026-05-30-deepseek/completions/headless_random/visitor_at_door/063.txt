The visitor, having listened intently to the explanation, nods thoughtfully.

"Fascinating. It really is the grand unifying theory of geology, isn't it? It connects everything—earthquakes, volcanoes, mountains, even the distribution of fossils and minerals. It makes me think of a giant, slow-motion engine, with the mantle's heat as the fuel and the plates as the moving parts. The fact that we can now measure their movement down to millimeters per year with satellites is astounding.

It also makes our planet feel... alive, in a way. That constant, incremental recycling—the ocean floor being born at the mid-ocean ridges and consumed at the trenches, over hundreds of millions of years. It puts human timescales into a rather humbling perspective.

Thank you for the concise overview. It's a perfect reminder of how dynamic our apparently solid ground truly is."

They pause, then smile. "Now, I believe I was at your door about something far more mundane... the community garden committee meeting? I'm afraid I may be in the wrong place if you're in the middle of a lecture series!"
