 "The Earth's lithosphere is broken into tectonic plates that move slowly over the asthenosphere. There are seven or eight major plates and many minor plates. Their relative motion determines the type of plate boundary: convergent, divergent, or transform."

The visitor continues. "Tectonic plates are composed of oceanic lithosphere and continental lithosphere. Along convergent boundaries, subduction carries one plate under another, recycling oceanic crust back into the mantle. This process keeps the total surface area of the lithosphere roughly the same."

The visitor speaks again. "Tectonic plates move because the lithosphere has greater mechanical strength than the asthenosphere. Lateral density variations in the mantle result in convection currents, the slow creeping motion of Earth's solid mantle."

The visitor continues. "At a seafloor spreading ridge, plates move away from the ridge, which is a topographic high. The newly-formed crust cools as it moves away, increasing its density and contributing to the motion."
