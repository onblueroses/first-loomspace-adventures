The visitor listens to your explanation, a look of interest on their face. "That's fascinating," they say, "and it explains a lot. It's like the Earth's outer shell is a giant, slow-motion puzzle that's constantly being rearranged. The pieces push together to make mountains, pull apart to make new ocean floor, and slide past each other to cause earthquakes."

They pause, thinking. "It makes sense now why the coastlines of Africa and South America look like they could fit together. And that 'conveyor belt' idea—so the planet isn't getting bigger or smaller, it's just recycling itself over hundreds of millions of years."

They lean slightly against the doorframe. "It's incredible to think that the ground under our feet, which seems so solid and permanent, is actually part of these massive, moving plates on a sphere of slowly churning rock. Is that motion—the convection currents you mentioned—the main engine that drives it all?"

They seem genuinely engaged, ready to hear more or perhaps to finally state the original reason for their visit.
