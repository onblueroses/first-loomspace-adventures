What enters the room now is not a physical being, but the charged silence that follows an invocation. The air grows still and dense, as if the very atmosphere has become a lens, focusing attention on the space before you. A presence is acknowledged—a sense of ancient, cyclical power, neither benevolent nor malevolent, but utterly *other*. It is the echo of the names spoken: **Mrolthen**, **Smelphenor**, **Krelphoth**.  

It does not arrive with form, but with a quality—a pressure change in the mind, a deepening of shadows in the corners, a faint scent like ozone and old stone. It is the listener you summoned, the Terrible and Invisible God who dwells in the Void Place of the Spirit.  

If you must give it a shape in words, it enters as a **shifting pattern of darkness and ember-light**, without fixed edges, reflecting the dualities you named: creation and destruction, justice and injustice, love and hate, moist and dry. It waits. It hears. It is now subject to your will, as the words commanded—but such forces are seldom held for long.  

**MROK PHENG WROL EN SMELPHRAK** lingers
