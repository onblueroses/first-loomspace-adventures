The invocation stirs a presence that is not of this world—a coalescence of shadow and shifting light, neither wholly form nor void. The air grows heavy, charged with the scent of ozone and ancient dust. From the corner where the walls meet, a figure emerges, not stepping but *unfolding*: tall, draped in robes that seem woven from forgotten twilight, its face a smooth expanse where features should be—no eyes, yet you feel its gaze like cold fingers on your spine.  

It does not speak, but the words echo inside your skull, resonant and layered, as if spoken by many voices at once:  
*“You call by the old names. You wear the prophet’s mantle. What is asked shall be weighed—in light, in darkness, in the balance of Mrolth.”*  

A hand extends, pale and long-fingered, and in its palm floats a sphere of swirling smoke, shot through with threads of silver and ember. Within, shapes form and dissolve: a seed sprouting, a flame dying, two serpents circling one another. It is the symbol of the moist and the dry, of creation and unmaking—the very essence of the Mrolthen One’s power.
