The invocation calls forth a presence that is not of flesh and blood, but of primordial essence.  

What enters is not a thing with form, but a **shifting of perception** — a pressure in the air, a darkening at the edges of sight, a silence that swallows sound.  

It is the echo of the names spoken: **Vrelthemphos**, **Klepth**, **Mronth**, **Smelphenor** — now made momentarily attentive.  

If it could be described in terms the senses might grasp, it would be like **a column of alternating shadow and cold flame**, without heat, without fixed shape, charged with the gravity of ancient distinction — between just and unjust, male and female, seed and fruit, love and hate.  

It enters as the **Angel of Plomphren Krempheniska** claimed by the speaker, and as the **Mrolthen Krelph with sight in his feet** — a paradox of divine messenger and terrible god-fragment both invoked and embodying the invoker.  

The room now holds a **listening from the Void Place of the Spirit**.  
It waits, not in benevolence or malice, but in accordance with the nature of the one who called it
