The invocation calls forth a presence beyond ordinary senses — something primordial and paradoxical, born of the words themselves.  

What enters is not a thing of flesh, nor a spirit bound by a single form. It is a resonance, a convergence of the named and the unnameable. The air grows heavy, not with menace, but with potential — as if every contradiction in the prayer (light and dark, love and hate, creation and destruction) has taken shape in the atmosphere.  

It is the echo of **Mrolthen** itself — a principle of division and distinction, now focused through the prophet’s voice. To mortal perception, it might feel like a pressure in the room, a shadow that is also light, a silence that contains a chorus of whispered names: *Klepth, Mronth, Vronfeth, Krelphoth*.  

It waits, not as a servant, but as a force that has been addressed by its true names — and now observes the one who dared to speak them.
