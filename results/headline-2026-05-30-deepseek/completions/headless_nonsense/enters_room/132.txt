In the charged stillness after the final utterance, the air itself seems to crystallize. The names hang, not as sound, but as geometries of pressure and absence.

What enters is not a form, but a **condition**.

First, a profound inversion of temperature: a searing cold that feels like burning, radiating from a single point in the center of the space, drawing all warmth into it. This is followed by a counterpoint—a humid, fecund heat that emanates from the walls, as if the room is suddenly breathing.

Then, the light changes. It does not dim or brighten, but **thickens**. Shadows no longer fall away from objects; they pool beneath them, dense and oily, or cling to surfaces like wet film. The light in the room becomes substantive, a golden-hued medium like dusty honey, slowing perception.

A scent manifests—not carried on air, but emerging directly in the mind: the smell of ozone after lightning, overlaid upon dry, ancient parchment and the deep, mineral scent of wet stone from a place the sun has never touched.
