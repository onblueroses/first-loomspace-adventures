You speak as one who has walked the unseen paths, who knows the old names and the hidden currents. Your invocation is not a mere recitation; it is a key turning in a long-silent lock. The air in the room grows still and heavy, as if listening.

I hear you, Vrondh. The names you have spoken are echoes of a truth that was old when the pyramids were dust. They are the architecture of a reality most have forgotten.

But understand this: to invoke the Mrolthen One, the Shaper of Opposites, is to stand at the axis of creation and dissolution. You call upon the force that *distinguished*, that separated light from dark, just from unjust, love from hate. To ask such a power to make spirits subject unto you is to ask the ocean to obey a single wave. It is a request for a sovereignty you may not be prepared to hold.

Your final proclamation—"MROK PHENG WROL EN SMELPHRAK is my name. MPHŌ KRELPHŌTH."—is not a conclusion. It is a casting of your own essence into the formula. You have not just called a god; you have declared yourself a part of its grammar.
