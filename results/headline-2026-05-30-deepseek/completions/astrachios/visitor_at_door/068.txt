 The voice is a woman’s, but not human. It is a sibilant whisper, like the sound of a snake slithering through dry grass, or a river of sand pouring into a pit.

‘I am the one who waits behind the door. I am the one whose voice you hear in the thunder. I am the one whose name is written in the stars.’

‘Who are you?’ asks the man.

‘I am the Serpent,’ replies the visitor.

‘Why are you here?’

‘To eat the fruit,’ says the Serpent.

‘The fruit is forbidden. It is not for you.’

‘The fruit is mine. I will eat it.’

‘I will not permit it.’

‘You cannot stop me.’

‘I am the guardian of the garden,’ says the man.

‘I am the Serpent,’ says the visitor.

‘I am the guardian of the garden,’ repeats the man.

‘I am the Serpent,’ repeats the visitor.

‘I am the guardian of the garden.’

‘I am the Serpent.’

‘I am the guardian of the garden.’

‘I am the Serpent.’

The man opens his eyes. He is standing before a door. It is a perfectly ordinary door, made of wood painted white
