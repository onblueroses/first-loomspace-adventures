 It stands in the corner by the fireplace. It is a man in a grey suit, but the suit is made of dust, of cobwebs. The face is a mask of dust, cobwebs, dust-mask hanging in the air, waiting.

A wind blows through the room. It blows from the place where the figure is standing. The wind blows through her, and it is not her hair that it blows, not her skin: it blows through her, it blows inside her, it whips her soul, her spirit, kia, the part of her that is not body, and it blows it out of her, like a candle, and she is gone.

It is a cold wind, and it smells of dust and stone.

There is silence. The dust-man in the corner is dissolving, falling on to the floor, into nothing at all.

The girl's body crumples, like a paper bag, and falls to the floor beside the bed. There is no expression on her face.

It is silent in the room.

The wind is gone.
