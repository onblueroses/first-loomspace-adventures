 What waits outside the door. The names that open the way.

I walk to the door. The handle is cold and slick beneath my hand. I turn it. The door swings open. I step outside.

The hall is dark. The only light comes from the open doorway behind me. I can see the first few feet of the hall, the thick red carpet that runs its length, the floral-patterned wallpaper, a small table with a potted fern.

Then the darkness swallows everything.

I take a step forward. Then another. I stop at the edge of the light, peer into the darkness. It is absolute. I can see nothing. I stand there for a moment, then turn and go back inside.

I close the door behind me, lock it. I cross the room, set the journal on the desk. I turn off the lamp, lie down on the bed. Close my eyes.

Sleep. Sleep.

A dream: I am standing in a vast, empty room. The floor is made of wood, the walls painted white. Light pours in through a bank of windows along one wall. I am alone. I am wearing jeans and a white T-shirt. I am barefoot.
