 Not a thing but a darkness, not a darkness but a presence, not a presence but a shape, not a shape but a man. A man. A man robed in black, his face hidden by a hood. He stands beside the altar.

“Who calls?” The voice is deep, resonant.

“I, John Dee, call.”

“And what would you have?”

“I would have the spirit that dwells in the crystal.”

“And what will you give?”

“What would you have?”

“Your soul.”

“No.”

“Then why should I give you what you ask?”

“Because I command it.”

“You have no power over me.”

“I have power over all spirits, demons, angels, powers, principalities.”

“You are mortal.”

“I have the name of God.”

“Say it.”

“I will not.”

“Then I will not give you what you ask.”

“I will compel you.”

“You cannot.”

“I have the seal.”

“Show it.”

Dee takes out the disk of copper, the seal of Solomon, the pentagram, and holds it up. “See.”

“I see.”

“Then obey.”
