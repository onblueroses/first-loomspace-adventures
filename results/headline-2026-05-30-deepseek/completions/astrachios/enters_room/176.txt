 . . . The shape of a man, but not a man. A thing of rags and tatters, of dust and flaking skin. The eyes are lidless, but the orbs beneath have been scratched away.

There is blood on his mouth. His chest is open, ribs cracked wide, the cavity empty. He has no heart.

What has he done with his heart?

“I am the Wandering Jew,” it says.

“I know you,” says the boy.

“I know you,” says the man-thing.

The boy has a name, but it is long and painful to speak. It is the name his mother gave him, but she had so many names and she has forgotten them all. She is old now, so very old. She was old the day that he was born, and that was more than a thousand years ago.

She said to him, “You will never die.”

He said, “But I will.”

She said, “Yes. You will. But not yet.”

He says, “I have a question.”

The man-thing smiles. “I have an answer.”
