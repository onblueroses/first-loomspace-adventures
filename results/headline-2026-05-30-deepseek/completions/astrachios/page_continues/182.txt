 "O you who govern the fourth part of the world, you subterranean demons, I conjure you, I call on you, and armed with the power of the Almighty God, I powerfully command you to come here without delay and without making any noise, to answer truthfully all the questions I shall ask."

This was the conjuration to be used to raise the spirits of the dead. The page ends with a final appeal: "I conjure you by him who spoke and all was made. Adonai, Tetragrammaton, and by all the holy names of God, and by your particular name, to appear here before this circle without delay, to answer truthfully all my questions."

This was a potent conjuration indeed. It had a number of variations. One of them was given in the grimoire called the Heptameron, a book of ceremonial magic ascribed to the great medieval magician Pietro d'Abano. The Heptameron is a book of spells for each day of the week.
