 ‘All these are the holy names of God, which Moses named once, and then threw the Tables into the gold calf of the Hebrews, and broke them. Therefore, see thou name them with great honour, for they be great names.’

The ‘Tables’ are the Ten Commandments, which Moses smashed in anger when he saw his people worshipping a golden calf. The implication here is that the names of God were on the tablets too. Moses called upon them, then threw the tablets at the calf and broke them.

The names are followed by a list of angels, headed by Michael, Gabriel, Uriel and Raphael, but including also Anael, Sachiel, Samael, Cassiel, and various others. Then there are the ‘seven princes that stand before the presence of God’. These are followed by the ‘seven holy names of God’, which are: El, Yod He Vau He, Elohim, Elohe, Zebaoth, Elion, and Adonai.

The book ends with an account of the ‘seven planets and their seals’. The seals are the symbols of the planets. These are followed by the ‘seven angels that stand before God’, the ‘seven days of the week’, and the ‘seven metals’.
