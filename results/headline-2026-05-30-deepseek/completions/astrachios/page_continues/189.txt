 “I conjure thee, O Spirit Bael, by Baracata, Abracata, Abramich, Samoel, Geburahel, Cadato, Era, Elohi, Achsah, Ebmisha, Imachadel, Daniel, Dama, Elamos, Izachel, Bael, Scirlin, that thou appear here before this Circle in fair human shape, to answer all my demands.” The rest of the page is devoted to the conjuration of Bael. On the next page, the conjuration for the spirit Agares appears, and so on, with each demon following the next.

This second section of the book seems to be pieced together from multiple sources. Some of the conjurations appear to be derived from the Munich Handbook, a fifteenth-century grimoire. Other sections may have been lifted from the Heptameron, a book of magic by Peter de Abano. The conjurations in the Goetia also have many points in common with a grimoire titled Liber Officiorum Spirituum, or Book of the Offices of Spirits, which exists in several sixteenth-century manuscripts. The offices referred to in the title are the duties of the spirits—the powers and abilities they possess.
