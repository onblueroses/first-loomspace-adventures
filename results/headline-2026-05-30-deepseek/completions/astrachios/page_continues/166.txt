 “O you angels who are in the third heaven, I conjure you by the great living God, in virtue of the most holy names of God, that you appear to me visibly here before this circle, in a beautiful human form, and that you answer with a clear voice intelligibly, so that I may understand, all things which I shall ask you.” The manuscript goes on to include a prayer of consecration, an invocation to the spirits, and a license to depart.

The Heptameron is a derivative work, but it is important because it was incorporated into Francis Barrett’s The Magus (1801), which became a primary sourcebook for the nineteenth-century magical revival. In turn, The Magus became a primary sourcebook for the twentieth-century magical revival.

The Heptameron is available in a modern edition.
