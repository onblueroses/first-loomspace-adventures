 “O Lord God holy, Father, Almighty, incomprehensible, ineffable, who hast created all things by thy power, and by thy will hast formed man, and hast commanded thy holy angels to attend upon him and serve him, and hast willed that they should be subject unto him, I pray thee, O Lord, that thou wilt send Raphael, the angel, to mine aid against this demon Asmodeus, and command him to labour for me, and fulfil my will in all things, through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

The exorcist’s prayer is answered: Raphael appears, and the demon Asmodeus is sent away to do the magician’s bidding.

The story of Solomon and Asmodeus, and the story of the magician who summons the demon to do his will, reflect an ancient idea—that demons are fallen angels, evil spirits who are nevertheless subject to God’s command. The idea is found in the Gospels, where Jesus exercises his power over demons. The idea is also found in Jewish magical texts, where the exorcist invokes the names of angels and of God to cast out demons.
