 “Then say: I conjure thee, O creature of wax, by him who spake the word and it was done, and by all the names of God, and by the name Adonai, El, Elohim, Elohe, Zebaoth, Elion, Escher, Jah, Tetragrammaton, Sadai, that thou shalt receive strength and power to work my will without deceit. Then write upon the figure the name of the effect desired.”

The conjuration is followed by the Lord’s Prayer, which, in this case, is to be said backward: “... mea culpa, Amen. Evil be to him that evil thinketh.”

The word “Mephistopheles” appears in the margin; it is not part of the text. The page is signed “H. C. Agrippa.”

The second page is headed “Somnus,” and is a charm for insomnia. It begins: “Write upon a parchment the character of the Moon, and within it the name of the Moon, and above it the name of the angel of the Moon, and beneath it the seal of the spirit of the Moon.” The charm is to be placed under the head of the sleepless person.
