 “O God, holy and immortal, who dost exist for ever, and by whose will all things were made, look upon my frailty and my petition, and grant unto me the things that I desire; through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.”

The last words are: “O Lord, thy mercy is over all thy works. Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost.”

It is impossible to doubt the authenticity of this document. The only question is whether it is genuine or a fake. Its genuineness means that it was used by Dr. Dee for his magical operations. If so, it is a document of the first importance for the history of English magic.

# 33

## The Art of Dr. Dee

The earliest and most famous English magician was Dr. John Dee. He was born on July 13, 1527, and died at Mortlake in December 1608. His father, Rowland Dee, was a gentleman server at the court of Henry VIII. John Dee was educated at Chelmsford Grammar School, and at St. John’s College, Cambridge. He graduated as a Bachelor of Arts in 1545, and became a Fellow of Trinity College in 1546.
