 “O Lord, hear my prayer, and let my cry come unto Thee. O Lord God of hosts, who is like Thee? O Lord, among the Gods, who is like Thee?”

“Gods,” plural? And what are all these names? The Book of Abramelin the Mage (which inspired the fictional Book of Abraham the Jew in The Club Dumas) gives the complete instructions for the operation, and it is clear that they are the names of spirits. The magician must command them: “I conjure ye, O Spirits, all and so many as ye are.”\(^{26}\) The magician is to command these spirits to serve him, to “render honor and obedience unto me.”\(^{27}\) This is theurgy: the conjuration of spirits.

The conjuration of spirits is the heart of theurgic magic. The spirits are the mediators between the material world and the divine world. They are the means by which the divine power is transmitted to the material world. They are also the means by which the magician can ascend to the divine world.
