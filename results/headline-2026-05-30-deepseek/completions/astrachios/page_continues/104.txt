 ‘O most holy Adonai, the God of Gods, the beginning and the end, thou art He that created heaven and earth and all things, visible and invisible, out of nothing, by the Word of thy Power. Thou art He that hath placed man in paradise, and hast promised him eternal life if he continued obedient to thy commandments; but when he fell by eating of the forbidden fruit, thou didst drive him out of paradise, but in thy infinite goodness didst send thy beloved Son, Jesus Christ, for our redemption.’

The final section of the manuscript is a brief account of the talismans of the planets, which was presumably to be used in conjunction with the preceding page.

In the margin of the manuscript is a note which reads: ‘This was done on Monday, May 20th 1751, being the Day of Pentecost, about 7 o’clock at night.’ The manuscript was thus written about half a century after the death of its supposed author, Thomas Rudd, whose signature appears on the title page.

The manuscript is inscribed ‘Liber Quartus’, indicating that it is the fourth volume of a larger work. It is not known whether the other three volumes survive.
