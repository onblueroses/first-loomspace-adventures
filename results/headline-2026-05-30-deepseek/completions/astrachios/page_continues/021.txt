 "O angels be ye ready and come to my aid, for I am the servant of the same your God, and I have the same power in my hand. Adonai, Elohim, Sabooth, Amen." The text ends with a lengthy prayer in Latin.

Of the many names invoked, some are familiar, some obscure. Adonai, Elohim, Sabooth, Amen, and Elohim are all biblical names of God. Elohim, in fact, is used frequently in the Old Testament to refer to God. Bael is a name given to Lucifer and Satan in the Ars Goetia, and Samoel is a name of the angel Samael. Ideodoc is an angel of the seventh hour of the day, according to the Key of Solomon. The rest of the names are obscure.

Bael is the first demon listed in the Ars Goetia, where he appears as a king ruling sixty-six legions. He is said to appear in a number of different forms, sometimes as a man, sometimes as a toad, sometimes as a cat. He is also said to speak with a hoarse voice.
