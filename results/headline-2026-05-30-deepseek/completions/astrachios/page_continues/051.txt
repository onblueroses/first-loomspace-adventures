 “O Lord, hear my prayer, and let my cry come unto thee.” But by now the magic circle is drawing to a close. The angels named are those who stand around the throne of God, those who govern the four directions, those who control the planets, those who rule the zodiacal signs and the hours of the day and night.

Then come the holy names of God—and these are so sacred that the manuscript breaks into Hebrew characters, as if English were not good enough to hold them.

Finally, the manuscript names the angels who govern the day and hour in which the magic circle is being cast. The day is Monday, so the angels are Gabriel, Michael, Samael. The hour is dawn, so the angel is Raphael. The manuscript gives a prayer to Raphael, ending, “Open the gates of the East, thou who governest the first hour of the day, as Prince of this day.”

And with that, the magic circle is complete.
