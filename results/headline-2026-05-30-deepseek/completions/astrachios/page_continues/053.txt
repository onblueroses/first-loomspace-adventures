The Ars Notoria is a collection of orations, prayers, and magical words which, if recited, will grant the user skill in the seven liberal arts (grammar, logic, rhetoric, arithmetic, geometry, music, and astronomy). The text claims that Solomon received the Ars Notoria from God as a means to help him build the Temple in Jerusalem.

The Ars Notoria was translated into Latin sometime in the thirteenth century and was often included in collections of magical grimoires. Several manuscripts have survived, including some in the British Museum. The Ars Notoria was known in England by the fourteenth century, since it is mentioned by John Gower in his Confessio Amantis (1390). The Ars Notoria was also known in France, where it is mentioned by Jean de Meun in Le Roman de la Rose (c. 1275). In the fifteenth century, the Ars Notoria was included in the famous grimoire known as The Key of Solomon.
