 ‘I conjure you, spirits, by the living God, the true God, the holy God, the God who spake and it was done, who commanded and all things were created: and by him who hath said: Let us make man in our image, after our likeness, and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth; and by the holy names, and by all the names of God, that you come to me here without delay, and without any evil intention, but come ye to me, and answer me my demands.’

The conjuration ends: ‘And if ye come not, I will curse you, and deprive you of all your offices, joys, and places, and bind you in the depth of the bottomless pit, to remain unto the day of the last judgement, and I will burn your books, and condemn you, and destroy your seats, and scatter you into the four winds of heaven, that ye may never more be remembered before God and man.’

The manuscript adds: ‘And if they come not, say this again.’
