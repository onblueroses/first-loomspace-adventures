 “I conjure you, O angels, by these names, and by all other names of the holy God, the mighty and incomprehensible Lord, that you labor for me, N., and fulfill my will in all things that I shall ask or desire.”

The reader will have noticed that the “names” are not particularly well known in the angelic community. They are certainly not from the Old Testament, or even from the Apocrypha. But such names are typical of the grimoires, the books of magic that were once so popular in Europe.

The angels named here are not the angels of the Bible. They are not the angels of the New Testament. They are not the angels of the Koran. They are not the angels of the Book of Enoch.

They are the angels of the grimoires.

The grimoires are books of magic, written in the Middle Ages and the Renaissance. They are filled with spells, incantations, and invocations. They are filled with the names of angels, demons, and spirits.

The grimoires are not the Bible. They are not the Koran. They are not the Book of Enoch.

They are the grimoires.
