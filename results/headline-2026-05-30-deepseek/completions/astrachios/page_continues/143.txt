 “When you have said these names, you must say what you desire to do, and then say: ‘Helon, Tau, Varaf, Gadiel, Paniel, Doniel, Baciel, Zomiel.’”\(^{55}\)

The text of this invocation is similar to another invocation recorded in a sixteenth-century manuscript from the Sloane collection, Sloane 3824.\(^{56}\) This invocation is not specifically to the angels of the four directions, but it contains the same divine names and the same angels in a slightly different order. It also includes additional angels: “Tau Varaf Gadiel Paniel Doniel Baciel Zomiel Helon.”\(^{57}\) The text instructs that the angels should be invoked “in the name of God, Amen,” which suggests that the invocation was originally Christian, but the Sloane manuscript was owned by a Jew, and includes Hebrew writing.
